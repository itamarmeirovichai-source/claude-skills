Method lessons recorded by that program (apply all of them):
- Trade-day filters that read the trade day's own bar count or its 15:59 bar leak the future (IBS was inflated by 0.22R). Run the future-truncation harness on every intraday test before reading a result.
- Bars are stamped at their open. A 15:58 flatten cannot use the 15:55 five-minute bar's close. Define the last usable bar explicitly.
- Trailing stops must be sampled the way the bot samples (its scan interval), never on bar highs and lows.
- Count detection horizons in days with a trade (about 14 a month), not 21 trading days.
- Cluster standard errors by trading day. A 5,000-draw bootstrap cannot resolve a 0.05/N quantile; use at least 50/alpha draws.
- Scale by past volatility only; full-sample scaling understates drawdowns by about a third.
- Compare every long-biased result to buy-and-hold of the same basket at the same exposure; beta is never edge. Remove the 20 best days as a fragility check.
- The worse of two execution-lag variants decides (signal at close vs fill at next open; lag 1 vs lag 2; as-written vs ex-ante eligibility).
- Choose the headline variant by in-sample Sharpe before any out-of-sample number is read.
- Clocks: HistData 1-minute files are New York local time with DST; Oanda is UTC; Databento ts_event is UTC nanoseconds; FirstRate is New York naive. Verify every new file's clock with an outcome-free diagnostic (mean absolute 1-minute return by minute of day around known opens, winter vs summer).
- Rolls: Databento `.c.0` (calendar) has no RTH bars on quarterly expiry Fridays and sits in the illiquid expiring contract for about six sessions; FirstRate-style data rolls one to three days earlier. Decide in the pre-registration whether roll weeks are dropped or spliced; never change it after results. Prefer volume-rolled `.v.0` or explicit contracts for intraday tests.
- Never mix dividend-adjusted and raw price files. Index opens before 2010 are stale. SPXUSD 1m misses RTH minutes on most 2012–2013 days.
- SqueezeMetrics GEX/DIX publishes after the close (median 17:52 ET), so it is usable only for next-day signals.

<standards_gate_v2>
A variant passes Gate v2 only if all of the following hold. Print every criterion with its number in the verdict.

1. Pre-registration: its registry row exists in a commit older than any backtest code or result for it; the rules were not changed after any data was read.
2. Out of sample: a period or market set never used to choose any rule; the split is in the row. Minimum OOS: 5 years for daily or multi-day strategies, 3 years with ≥ 300 trades for intraday; the OOS must include 2020 and 2022 unless the data does not exist, in which case say so.
3. Costs: realistic one-way costs per instrument plus a 2× cost run reported beside it. Floors unless measured better: ES 0.6 points round trip (or 0.30 per side), MES $1.30 round trip plus 0.25 points slippage, NQ 0.375 points per unit traded, SPY 0.5 bp per unit; CFDs per side 0.5–1.5 bp for FX and indices, 1 bp gold, 2.5 bp WTI, 8 bp natural gas, plus FTMO swaps when held overnight (index long about −8% a year US, −6.5% EU, −4.9% JP225; crypto −30% a year both sides; FX markup about 1% a year); crypto perps 7 bp per side plus actual funding. Nothing passes "on 1× cost only".
4. Significance: OOS net mean > 0 with t = min(iid t, Newey-West lag-10 t) ≥ the fixed bar z(0.05/N_total); day-clustered bootstrap 5th percentile of the mean > 0 with ≥ 50/alpha draws (and ≥ 10,000 block-20 draws for daily series).
5. Multiple testing: Deflated Sharpe Ratio (Bailey and López de Prado 2014) computed with the number of trials in the family and across the program ≥ 0.95; Probability of Backtest Overfitting (CSCV, 16 splits) ≤ 0.10.
6. Null models: alpha t against buy-and-hold of the same basket at the same exposure ≥ the bar; the strategy beats 95% of random-entry strategies with the same holding-time distribution and trade count; a shuffled-signal permutation test p < 0.05/N_family.
7. Stability: positive net in at least 70% of calendar years; no single year or single month holds more than 50% of the OOS profit; profitable in at least two of the three volatility terciles; the parameter neighbourhood (±25% on every parameter) stays positive (a plateau, not a spike).
8. Leaks: the future-truncation harness (delete every bar after the entry minute; nothing about that day may change) passes; the perturbation test passes; the worse of the execution-lag variants is the one reported; bar-timestamp, roll and adjustment audits are documented.
9. Independent re-implementation: the Re-implementer's OOS mean matches within the pre-stated tolerance (default 10% of the mean or 0.02R, whichever is larger).
10. Red Team: no unrefuted material objection remains; the strongest one is quoted in the verdict.
11. Prop-fit: at the volatility the target needs (≤ MAX_ANNUAL_VOL_ALLOWED), the worst day stays inside the firm's daily-loss limit with the per-firm guard (60–80% of the limit), the maximum drawdown inside the maximum-loss rule, and breach counts over rolling windows are reported; holding times and order patterns comply with the clauses.
12. Reporting: the result file has N trials, the bar, OOS period, costs, trades per year and per day, R per trade or bp per day, gross and net, Sharpe with a confidence interval (Lo 2002 standard error with skew and kurtosis adjustment), maximum drawdown, worst day, by-year table, turnover, regime table, the 20-best-days fragility check, the random-entry and buy-and-hold comparisons, and the economics at each eligible firm.

Translate every result into Itamar's unit in the same breath: $/slot-year at the firm, with p10 and p90, and the probability of passing the evaluation.
</standards_gate_v2>

<hypothesis_lanes>
