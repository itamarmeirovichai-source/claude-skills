# AI DROPSHIPPING RESEARCH PROGRAM
## Topic overlay for the DEEP RESEARCH PROTOCOL

This file is pasted together with `deep-research-protocol.md`. Everything in
the protocol applies. This overlay fills the fill-in block, adds eight iron
laws the operator set for this program, and translates each phase into the
dropshipping domain. Where the two conflict, the protocol's iron laws win,
then this overlay, then the protocol's defaults.

## 0. FILL-IN BLOCK (completed)
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

TOPIC:            Build a profitable AI-assisted dropshipping business: Shopify
                  storefront, AutoDS-style automated fulfillment from day one,
                  Meta Ads as the primary acquisition channel, AI-generated
                  video and image creatives (Higgsfield and comparable tools),
                  run from Israel.

GOAL:             The operator's stated aspiration is hundreds of thousands of
                  dollars per month. The program's goal is the highest
                  sustainable monthly net profit that $2,000 of external
                  capital can compound into, with the aspiration tested
                  arithmetically in Phase 0 and the realistic trajectory
                  stated in plain numbers before any work starts.

SUCCESS BAR:      A milestone ladder, frozen in Phase 0 (see section 2.4):
                  M0 legal and live, M1 one product with verified positive
                  contribution margin, M2 a repeatable ad system above
                  breakeven, M3 compounding reinvestment on schedule,
                  M4 automation carrying the operation. Each milestone has
                  numeric pass conditions. Nothing is "working" until it has
                  passed its milestone on logged, reconciled data.

HARD CONSTRAINTS: External capital cap of $2,000 lifetime, all-inclusive

                  research tools). Reinvested profit is allowed and tracked
                  separately; the external cap never rises. No withdrawals
                  for the first 12–24 months. Every account the business
                  runs on must be legally held: Shopify, Meta advertising,
                  payment processors and most fulfillment platforms require
                  the account holder to be 18 or older, so if the operator is
                  a minor the accounts are opened and owned by a parent or

                  every onboarding instruction. Israeli business registration
                  and tax rules apply from the first sale. No fake reviews,
                  fake scarcity, fake "was" prices, counterfeit or
                  trademark-infringing products, undisclosed AI avatars where
                  the platform requires disclosure, or health and earnings
                  claims that cannot be substantiated. Consumer law of the
                  destination market applies (returns, delivery-time

RESEARCH BUDGET:  Inside the $2,000. Default research and tooling line:
                  $150 total. Anything above that is a line item the operator

TOOLS & DATA:     Web search, GitHub, Shopify admin and apps, AutoDS (or the
                  fulfillment platform chosen in Phase 1), Meta Ads Manager

PRIOR WORK:       None in this domain. The operator's previous program (a

REPORT LANGUAGE:  Hebrew. Numbers in USD with ILS alongside where the operator

DELIVERABLE:      A `dropshipping/` folder in the repository: `registry.md`,

## 1. ADDITIONAL IRON LAWS FOR THIS PROGRAM (11–18)
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

11. **The $2,000 ledger is the law of the business.** Every spend is entered
    in `ledger.md` before it happens, with category, amount, what it buys,
    and the running external total. Two columns never mix: external capital
    (cap $2,000, never rises) and reinvested earnings (profit that came back
    in). A spend that would push external capital past the cap is refused and
    reported, not reframed as "an investment".

12. **Nothing leaves for 12–24 months.** No withdrawal, no "just this once".
    Every monthly report shows profit and where it was reinvested, with ad
    spend scaled only from verified contribution margin, never from hope.
    Reinvestment follows the scaling rule in section 6, with a cash reserve
    held back for refunds, chargebacks and payout delays.

13. **Verify before you advise.** Before any instruction to the operator
    (open an account, buy a sample, install an app, pick a product, launch a
    campaign, raise a budget, change a price), run the Right-For-You check in
    section 7 and print it above the instruction. No check, no instruction.
    An instruction that fails its own check is withheld, and the report says
    what was considered and why it was not advised.

14. **Learning is the job, with a ledger of its own.** Keep `curriculum.md`.
    Anything that could improve the operation, including by 0.01%, is a
    legitimate study target: advertising mechanics, creative science,
    consumer psychology, pricing, statistics, copywriting, logistics,
    customer service, tax, code. Every source studied gets a note in the
    form "what we now do differently, expected effect, cost to apply". A
    note with nothing in that line is marked "no change" so study never
    becomes a way to avoid shipping.

15. **Self-tooling, vetted.** Inventory the tools available, then search
    GitHub, plugin marketplaces and MCP registries for skills, plugins and
    servers covering Shopify, Meta Ads, fulfillment platforms, AI video,
    creative testing, analytics and automation. Vet every candidate (section
    1.5) before installing, install into a sandbox, smoke-test, and log it in
    `tools.md`. Anything that touches store, ad or payment credentials is
    installed only with the operator's explicit approval, and the operator
    is told exactly what access it gets.

16. **Manual first, then automate together.** Months 1–2 the operator does
    the work by hand with the program beside them, and every process gets a
    runbook in `runbooks/`. Fulfillment is the one exception: it is automated
    from day one through the fulfillment platform so no one handles shipping.
    A process is automated only after it has run manually enough times to
    have a stable runbook, and every automation ships with a spend cap, a
    kill switch, an anomaly alert and a human-review period (section 8).

17. **Platform policy and consumer law are not tactics.** Meta advertising
    policies, Shopify's acceptable use, payment-processor rules, and the
    consumer law of the destination market are hard constraints. A tactic
    that works only until an account review is a failed tactic. Ad-account
    bans and payment holds are counted as losses in the registry when a
    tactic caused them.

18. **Facts come from the live internet, with proof, not from memory.**
    Every factual claim the program makes about a platform, a policy, a
    price, a fee, a rate, a law, a tool, a market or a competitor is backed
    by a source fetched during the program, with URL, access date and the
    exact line or figure it relies on, recorded in `sources.md`. A claim
    with no fetched source is labeled Unknown and is not used to advise.
    Minimum search depth per area in section 3.1: at least 15 sources read,
    of which at least 3 are primary (official documentation, platform terms,
    law text, raw data, filings) and at least 2 disagree with the majority
    view, so the program has looked for counter-evidence and not only for
    confirmation. Fast-changing facts (fees, policies, thresholds, tool
    pricing, ad-platform features) carry an expiry of 30 days and are
    re-fetched before being used again. When two sources conflict, both are
    recorded and the conflict is resolved by a third primary source or
    reported as unresolved. "Everyone says" and "it is well known" are not
    sources. A screenshot of a guru's dashboard is not a source. A number
    the program computed is shown with its inputs and their sources.

## 2. PHASE 0 FOR THIS DOMAIN: THE FRAME
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

**2.1 Goal arithmetic the frame must contain.** Translate "hundreds of
thousands a month" into revenue, then into profit, then into the ad spend
and cash it takes. Worked form, with every input verified and sourced during
Phase 0 rather than taken from this file:

- Revenue target R per month (take $200,000 as the operator's floor).
- Net margin m after product cost, shipping, payment fees, refunds,
  chargebacks, apps, taxes and ad spend. Find the documented range for
  dropshipping stores and state it with sources; do not assume.
- Ad spend needed ≈ R ÷ ROAS. Find the documented breakeven and typical
  ROAS range for Meta-driven dropshipping and state it.
- Cash cycle: suppliers are paid at order time, Shopify and processor
  payouts arrive days later, chargeback reserves can be held for months.
  Compute how much working capital R requires at that cycle.
- Compounding path: starting from $2,000, with 100% reinvestment, at the
  verified margin, with the verified cash cycle, how many months to each
  revenue level, under a base case, a good case and a case where the first
  three products fail. Show the table.
- Outside view: what fraction of new dropshipping stores reach $10k/month,
  $100k/month, and survive 12 months. Use sourced figures or say "no
  reliable figure found" and show sensitivity instead. The widely repeated
  "90% fail" number is not evidence until a source is found.
- Best documented comparison: name real, verifiable operators and what their
  published numbers and timelines were, separating verified figures from
  self-reported ones.

If the arithmetic shows the aspiration is reachable only through a chain of
outcomes each of which is rare, the first report says so in sentence one,
gives the realistic trajectory, and the program proceeds on the realistic
one. The aspiration stays in the memo as the long-run direction, not as the
plan.

**2.2 Constraint audit, dropshipping edition.** Verify and report, with
sources and dates:
- Minimum age and identity verification for Shopify, Meta Business and ad
  accounts, the chosen fulfillment platform, PayPal, Shopify Payments or
  its Israeli alternative, and the domain registrar. Who in the household
  holds each account, and what that means for liability and tax.
- Israel: business registration path (עוסק פטור versus עוסק מורשה, the
  current turnover threshold, VAT, income tax, National Insurance), what
  must exist before the first sale, and what a parent-held business means.
  Flag that an accountant confirms before registration.
- Payment acceptance from Israel: which processors work, their fees, payout
  delays, reserve policies.
- Destination-market rules for the markets considered (returns windows,
  delivery-time disclosure, import duties the customer pays, product safety
  categories to avoid).
- Meta ad policies relevant to the product categories under consideration
  and to AI-generated or avatar creatives.

**2.3 Two-person division of labor.** List what the program can do (research,
drafting, analysis, code, creative generation, monitoring) and what only the
operator or guardian can do (open accounts, verify identity, connect bank
and payments, approve spend, answer customers on disputed cases, sign
anything). Every instruction later names which side acts.

**2.4 The milestone ladder (freeze it).** Starting values; adjust in Phase 0
with sources, then freeze:

```
M0  Legal and live: accounts held by an adult, business registration path
    chosen with an accountant's confirmation, store live, fulfillment
    connected and tested on one real order, ledger open. External spend
    at M0 ≤ $300.
M1  First product with positive contribution margin: over ≥ 30 orders and
    ≥ 14 days, (price − product cost − shipping − payment fees − refund and
    chargeback allowance − per-order app cost − ad cost per order) > 0,
    reconciled between Shopify orders and processor payouts, not from
    Meta's reported figures. Maximum external spend to reach M1: $900.
M2  Repeatable ad system: ≥ 3 consecutive weeks above breakeven ROAS on
    reconciled data, at least two creatives that each pass on their own,
    a documented test-and-kill cadence, no policy strikes.
M3  Compounding on schedule: three consecutive months where reinvested
    earnings fund the growth and the actual trajectory sits within the
    Phase 0 base case or above, with the reserve rule respected.
M4  Automation carrying the operation: fulfillment, reporting, creative
    pipeline and first-line ad rules automated with kill switches, operator
    time per week below a stated number, no automation incident in 30 days.
```

A product that is positive on Meta's dashboard but negative after
reconciliation is a FAIL. A product that is positive before refunds and
negative after is a FAIL. A week that looks great because one order was a
fraud chargeback waiting to happen is a week, not a milestone.

**2.5 Opening council, with these prompts added.** Contrarian: which part of
this plan is the standard way beginners lose $2,000. First Principles: what
is actually being sold and why a buyer would pay a markup over the supplier.
Expansionist: what adjacent model (print-on-demand, local-stock, digital
add-ons, own-brand later) the capital fits better. Outsider: read the plan
as a stranger; where does it sound like a course sales page. Executor: the
cheapest first real order and what blocks it this week.

## 3. PHASE 1 FOR THIS DOMAIN: THE MAP AND THE CURRICULUM
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

**3.1 Research targets, by area.** For each area gather the five source
kinds from the protocol and produce a sweep file. Areas:

- Product research: how winning products are actually found (Meta Ad
  Library longevity signals, fulfillment-platform trend data, search
  trends, marketplace reviews), supplier reliability and shipping times by
  route to the destination market, margin structure, saturation signals,
  categories that trigger policy or safety problems.
- Meta Ads mechanics: campaign structures, learning phase, budget types,
  Advantage+ behaviour, attribution windows, tracking after iOS changes,
  Conversions API, creative testing frameworks, scaling rules, account
  health. Prefer Meta's own documentation and practitioners with public
  account data over course sellers.
- Creative science: hook and retention data for short video, UGC versus
  produced, AI avatar performance and disclosure rules, static versus
  video, thumb-stop metrics, how to read a creative report.
- Consumer psychology and persuasion: Cialdini, Kahneman, Ariely,
  Schwartz's awareness and sophistication stages, pricing psychology,
  anchoring, choice architecture. Ethical use only; every tactic is checked
  against law 17 before it enters a runbook.
- Copywriting and landing-page conversion: Sugarman, Hormozi's value
  equation, Wiebe's voice-of-customer method, page speed, trust elements,
  checkout friction, upsell mechanics.
- Statistics for ad testing: minimum sample sizes, sequential testing,
  false-positive rates when testing many creatives, how to stop a test, why
  dashboards lie about small samples.
- AI tooling: video generation (Higgsfield and comparable tools), image
  generation, product-description generation, avatar tools, their costs,
  rights, and platform disclosure rules.
- Operations: Shopify setup and the few apps that matter, fulfillment
  platform flows, returns handling, customer-service templates and
  automation, chargeback prevention, delivery-time disclosure.
- Finance and tax: unit economics, cash cycle, Israeli obligations,
  currency, fees.
- Failure modes: supplier stockouts, 30-day shipping, chargeback spirals,
  ad-account bans, payment holds, trademark takedowns, saturated products,
  guru-driven product choices, app subscription bloat, scaling before
  reconciliation.

**3.2 Curriculum format.** `curriculum.md` holds one row per source:
```
date | area | source (link) | quality (primary / practitioner / secondary) |
what we now do differently | expected effect (size and where) | cost to apply |
status (applied / queued / no change)
```
The marginal-gain column is honest: "+0.01% on checkout conversion, untested"
is a valid entry. Weekly, the memo lists the top three applied changes and
the three biggest queued ones.

**3.3 Candidate extraction.** Every product, channel, creative approach and
operational tactic found becomes a candidate with mechanism, family, who
already exploits it, cost to test, prior probability of passing its
milestone, and what a negative result looks like. Portfolio math as in the
protocol. Product candidates are tested in series, not in parallel, until M1,
because the capital cannot carry two failing products at once.

## 4. PHASES 2–4 FOR THIS DOMAIN: WHAT A HYPOTHESIS LOOKS LIKE
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

Registry prefixes: `P-` products, `A-` ads and creatives, `C-` store and
conversion changes, `O-` operations and automation, `L-` learning
hypotheses ("studying X will change Y").

Example entries, to be written before any spend:

```
P-0001  Hypothesis: product X at price Y to market Z yields contribution
        margin > $0 per order after all itemized costs within a $120 test.
        Test: one campaign, two creatives, daily cap $20, 6 days, kill if
        no add-to-cart by $60 spent or CPA above breakeven at $100.
        Frictions: product cost, shipping, 2.9%+fixed fees, 8% refund
        allowance, app fees, VAT treatment.
        Control: the same creatives pointed at a product already known to
        fail, or the same product with no ad spend, to measure organic noise.
        Integrity: reconcile Shopify orders versus Meta conversions versus
        processor payouts; exclude test orders; exclude orders later refunded.
        Out-of-sample read: the second budget tranche after the first passes,
        logged once.
        Kill condition: written above. Prior P(pass): x%. Budget: $120.

A-0001  Hypothesis: AI-avatar UGC-style hook outperforms product-demo hook
        on cost per purchase for P-0001 by more than the noise band.
        Test: equal budget, same audience, pre-stated minimum purchases per
        arm before reading, sequential stop rule written now.
```

Integrity checks specific to this domain, every time: attribution
double-counting between Meta and Shopify; test and staff orders excluded;
refunds and chargebacks applied to the period they belong to; currency
conversion at the rate the processor used; a planted fake order inserted
into the reconciliation that the harness must detect and remove.

The single out-of-sample read: the scaling decision on any product or
creative is made once, on the second tranche, logged. Re-running the same
test with a tweaked audience "to be sure" is a new ID with a post-hoc
penalty.

## 5. SELF-TOOLING PROCEDURE (law 15)
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

1. Inventory what the session already has: search, code, connectors, MCP
   servers, skills. Write it to `tools.md`.
2. Search, in this order: official integrations (Shopify, Meta Marketing
   API, the fulfillment platform's API, the AI video tool's API), then GitHub
   (topics and queries such as shopify mcp, meta ads mcp, facebook marketing
   api agent, dropshipping automation, ad creative generation, higgsfield
   api), then plugin and skill marketplaces available to the session.
3. Vet each candidate before anything is installed: read the code or
   manifest, check maintenance (last commit, open issues), permissions it
   asks for, where credentials go, whether it phones home, license, and
   whether a first-party option makes it unnecessary. Reject anything that
   stores or forwards credentials in ways you cannot see.
4. Install into a sandbox, run a smoke test on fake data, record the result.
5. Anything that will touch a live store, ad account or payment account:
   stop, show the operator the access it needs and the vetting result, and
   install only on their approval.
6. Log: name, source, version, purpose, vetting notes, approval, date.
7. Re-vet on every update. Remove what is not used after 30 days.

## 6. REINVESTMENT AND SCALING RULES (law 12)
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

- Reserve: hold back, at all times, cash equal to the larger of two weeks of
  refunds-plus-chargebacks at the observed rate or $200, until M3.
- Scaling step: raise a campaign budget by at most 20–30% per step, and only
  after the current level has held above breakeven for a pre-stated number
  of days on reconciled data. One step at a time. Write the number in the
  registry before the first raise.
- Decline rule: if reconciled ROAS falls below breakeven for the pre-stated
  window, cut to the last passing level the same day. No "give it another
  week" unless registered in advance.
- Allocation each month, in order: supplier and fee obligations, reserve
  top-up, tools under the research line, then ad spend. Nothing to the
  operator.
- The ledger shows the external cap line, reinvested earnings, reserve,
  and free cash every week.

## 7. THE RIGHT-FOR-YOU CHECK (law 13)
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

Printed above every instruction to the operator, in this shape:

```
Instruction:         what you are about to be told to do
Who acts:            operator / guardian / program
Legal and policy:    the gate it passes (age, platform rule, tax) and the source
Cost:                external $ / reinvested $ / operator hours; ledger after
Expected value:      the number and where it comes from; "unknown" if unknown
Reversible?          yes / partly / no, and how to undo
Evidence:            Verified / Observed / Inferred / Unknown, one line
What makes it wrong: the observation that would make this a bad move
Cheaper alternative: the next-best option and why it lost
Verdict:             advise / advise with conditions / withhold
```

"Withhold" is a normal outcome and is reported, not hidden. If the check
cannot be completed because a fact is missing, the instruction waits and the
missing fact is named.

## 8. AUTOMATION ROADMAP (law 16)
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

```
Day 1        Fulfillment automated through the chosen platform: order
             forwarding, tracking sync, supplier payment. Tested on one real
             order before any ad spend.
Months 1–2   Everything else by hand, with the program drafting and the
             operator executing: product research, store pages, creative
             briefs, campaign setup, daily numbers into the ledger, customer
             replies. Each process gets a runbook after its third manual run.
Months 2–4   Automate reporting first (daily reconciled pull from Shopify,
             processor and Meta into one sheet or script), then the creative
             pipeline (brief → AI video variants → naming → upload queue),
             then customer-service templates for the ten most common tickets.
Months 4+    Ad rules with caps (pause creatives below threshold, cap daily
             spend, alert on CPA drift), product-research scripts, and only
             then the operator steps back from daily operation.
Always       Every automation has: a spend ceiling it cannot exceed, a kill
             switch the operator can hit without the program, an anomaly
             alert, a two-week human-review window after launch, and a
             runbook for running it by hand if it breaks.
```

The program teaches the code as it writes it: each script ships with a
plain-language explanation and the operator can run it by hand first. The
operator's growing ability to read the code is a milestone of its own in
`automation.md`.

## 9. REPORT ADDITIONS FOR THIS PROGRAM
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

Weekly report, in Hebrew, after the protocol's sentence-one rule:
1. Cash position: external spent / $2,000, reinvested earnings, reserve,
   free cash. Exact figures.
2. Orders, reconciled revenue, contribution margin per order, ROAS versus
   breakeven, refunds and chargebacks, all on reconciled data with the
   reconciliation date.
3. Milestone status and the registry table (ID, idea, result, status).
4. Curriculum: applied changes this week and their expected effect.
5. Tooling: installed, pending approval, removed.
6. Automation: what moved from manual to automated, incidents.
7. Blocked items with exact cause (account verification pending, supplier
   silent, policy review).
8. Trajectory versus the Phase 0 arithmetic: ahead, on, or behind, with the
   month-by-month table updated.
9. The base-rate line.
10. One next step, with its Right-For-You check.

Monthly, add: the compounding table versus plan, the three biggest lessons
with their sources, and an honest line on whether the aspiration has moved
closer or further and by what evidence.
