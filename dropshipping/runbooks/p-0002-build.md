## 0. What the reviews changed (read before anything else)

- **Targeting restriction, added 2026-11-11.** According to PubMed, Glickman et al. 2000 (JAVMA 217(10):1492–9, prospective cohort of 1,637 large- and giant-breed dogs) found that "having a raised feeding bowl" was significantly associated with increased risk of gastric dilatation-volvulus (bloat), with "approximately 20 and 52% of cases of GDV among the large breed and giant breed dogs, respectively, ... attributed to having a raised feed bowl" ([DOI 10.2460/javma.2000.217.1492](https://doi.org/10.2460/javma.2000.217.1492), S-205). Label: Observed (abstract; one cohort; later papers dispute the size of the effect but none reverse it). Consequence: the product is sold and advertised **for small and medium dogs and for cats only**; the page carries a plain line asking owners of large or giant breeds to ask their vet; no ad or page sentence claims any health benefit (digestion, neck, posture, bloat). This is also what Meta's health-claim standard and the FTC substantiation rule require (S-08, S-94), so the restriction costs nothing we were allowed to use.
- **Market reality.** The Zooplus bowl-stand page (1,185 reviews, 3.5/5: 515 five-star, 245 one-star; S-202) and the retailer snippets (S-203) show that the category's cheap end fails on four things: wobble, rattle, rust and bowls that are hard to lift out. The personalised bowls (S-204) are praised for a cleanly printed, centred name and criticised for smeared names and small capacity. The product that wins this test is therefore not "an elevated bowl"; it is a stable, lift-out, non-rusting stand that carries the pet's name correctly. The supplier spec must prove each of those before the claim is used (section 3).

- Who it is for: small and medium dogs (under ~50 lb / 23 kg) and cats. Who it is not for: large and giant breeds (see §0); the page says so.
- Price $49.90, shipping included (LP1). No compare-at price (D3). No timer or counter (S-117).
