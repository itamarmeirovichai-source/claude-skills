# P-0003 build package: timer-based 6-meal automatic pet feeder (no app) at $59.90 (written 2026-11-11, $0 spent)

Third in the test order. Registry: `registry.md` P-0003. Stop rule: T1 $120 over 6 days at $20/day, continue only if purchases ≥ 4; T2 $120, pass if ≥ 3. Prior 10%. Kill condition: US-warehouse SKU without a UL/ETL (or equivalent NRTL) mark on the power adapter → kill; electronics refund allowance 9%. **[M0]** marks what needs an adult account or a real SKU. סיכום בעברית בסוף.

## 0. What the reviews changed

- 47 real reviews read (S-210..S-212): Trixie TX2 twin-tray dispenser on Zooplus UK (36 reviews, **2.8/5**, 14 one-star), Catit Pixi 6-meal on Zooplus (11 reviews, 2.8/5), PetSafe 6-meal on Chewy (3.9/5, 73 ratings, "Not reliable!", listing discontinued), Cat Mate C500 (4.1/5, 230 ratings, no texts visible), Petzilla and other snippets. **The category's whole problem is one word: reliability.** The dominant failure is the lid or tray not opening at the set time ("The feeder didn't open even once"; "Opens at random"; "Lid stays shut or opens hours off schedule"; "worked once, then failed after 16 days"), followed by the animal defeating the device ("Cat figured out the knob within a day"; "a cat forced it open to steal food"), clock drift (15–30 min/day), beeping that cannot be silenced, and cooling packs that last 3 hours so wet food spoils. Praise is conditional: "difficult to break into and is very reliable", "easy to programme", "Suits shift work", "useful at night and while away".
- Consequence for the hypothesis: a $59.90 timer feeder is sellable only if the page and ads sell **proof of opening on time**, a **locking lid a cat cannot lift**, and an honest **dry-food-only** scope (no wet-food or ice-pack claim unless the supplier gives hours and the sample is tested). The ad's hero shot is the tray opening on schedule on camera with a clock in frame. Anything less repeats the category's 1-star reviews with our name on them.
- Scope and policy: no claim that the feeder keeps a pet alive, "safe to leave for days", medication dosing, or weight management (health claim, S-08; FTC substantiation, S-94). Say "for dry kibble, up to [N] days of meals, a backup when you are late, not a replacement for care".

## 2. Positioning (Schwartz, S-102)

- Awareness: **solution-aware and burned** (many have owned one that failed). Sophistication: 4–5: the mechanism must be shown; the claim is proof, not adjectives.
- One-line promise (only if C1–C3 are proven on the sample): *Six dry-food meals that open on time, under a lid your cat can't lift. No app, no Wi-Fi, no subscription.*
- Who it is for: cat and small-dog owners with shift work, late evenings, or a greedy pet; dry kibble only. Who it is not for: wet food, multi-day absences, medication.
- Price $59.90 shipping included (observed market: Taavita 6-slot $96.95 dropship-style, S-170; Walmart $31–34, S-171; PetSafe/Cat Mate branded $40–70). No compare-at; no "Sale ends soon" (Taavita's badge, S-170, FTC-risky S-117).
- "No app" is the differentiator against Petlibro and PawsPik (S-183), and it is also what the burned buyers ask for (phrases 12–13, 18).
