# 09 — Customer Interview Guide

**No one has been contacted. This is an instrument, not a record.**

## Design principles

1. **Ask about past behaviour, not future intention.** "Would you buy this?" is worthless as
   demand evidence — people are agreeable, especially to someone who has been pleasant for
   twenty minutes. "What did you last pay for, and what made you do it that week?" is evidence.
2. **Never describe the product before the problem section is finished.** Once you have
   described it, every subsequent answer is contaminated by politeness.
3. **Ask for the specific instance, then the general pattern** — never the reverse. "Tell me
   about the last time" before "how often do you usually."
4. **Silence is an instrument.** After an answer, wait. The second thing they say is usually
   truer than the first.
5. **You are not selling.** If they ask to buy during the interview, say: "I'll send you
   details after — right now I'd rather not bias what you tell me." Then actually send them.
6. **Write verbatim quotes, not summaries.** A paraphrase loses the language we need for
   [20](20-brand-voice-and-copy.md). Record with permission; transcribe the key lines exactly.

## Recruitment criteria

See [42](42-validation-plan.md) for how recruitment happens without cold outreach during this
project (it does not — no contact occurs in this assignment).

Target 12 interviews: 7× S3 (property managers 6–25 units), 5× S2 (owners 2–5 units). Screen on
Q1/Q2 from [08](08-segment-selection-matrix.md).

## Opening (60 seconds)

> Thanks for the time. I'm researching how small vacation rental operators handle marketing
> content — I'm not selling anything today and I'm not going to pitch you at the end. I'm trying
> to understand what actually happens in your week. There are no right answers and "we don't do
> that" is one of the most useful things you can tell me. Mind if I record so I don't have to
> take notes? I'll delete it after I've written it up, and I won't quote you by name anywhere
> without asking.

## Section A — Their operation (orientation, 5 min)

- **A1.** How many units do you manage, and where?
- **A2.** Walk me through where your bookings came from last season, roughly by channel.
- **A3.** Do you have a direct booking site? *(If yes)* What share of bookings comes through it?
  *(This is the single most important qualifying answer — E-28 says most will say "not much")*
- **A4.** Who handles marketing? Is it you, or someone else?
- **A5.** What does your week look like in the four weeks before your high season?

## Section B — Past behaviour around marketing assets (the core, 15 min)

This section produces the evidence. Everything else is context.

- **B1.** **Tell me about the last time you paid someone for anything to do with how your
  properties look online.** Photography, a website, a designer, anything.
  - Follow-ups: What did it cost? What made you do it *that* month? Who decided? How did you
    find them? Would you do it again?
- **B2.** What did you get, and what happened to it? Where did those files end up?
  - *(Listen for: assets bought and never used. Very common, and it is the single strongest
    argument for our "delivered in the shapes you actually publish" positioning.)*
- **B3.** **Have you ever made or commissioned a video of a property?** Tell me about it.
  - Follow-ups: What did you do with it? Where did you post it? Did you ever make a second one?
    Why / why not?
- **B4.** **Have you ever tried an AI tool for images or video of your properties?** What
  happened?
  - *(Critical. Tests the "AI is a liability word" hypothesis in [07](07-customer-research.md).
    Listen for artifact complaints in their own words.)*
- **B5.** Walk me through what you posted on social in the last month. *(Ask them to open it.
  Reported posting frequency is almost always higher than actual.)*
- **B6.** What's the last thing you meant to post and didn't? What stopped you?

## Section C — Platform experience and risk (5 min)

Tests **A-02**, the assumption our entire accuracy positioning rests on.

- **C1.** Has a platform ever rejected or asked you to change any of your listing photos?
  What happened?
- **C2.** Have you ever had a guest complain that the place didn't match the photos?
  - Follow-up: What did that cost you — refund, review, anything?
- **C3.** Do you know what the rules are about editing listing photos? *(Do not educate them.
  Record what they believe. If most say "no idea," that is the finding.)*
- **C4.** *(If they have a Vrbo listing)* Have you put a video on it? *(Tests whether E-03's
  channel is actually used.)*

## Section D — The direct booking problem (5 min)

- **D1.** *(If they have a direct site)* What made you build it? What did you expect?
- **D2.** What's working and what isn't on it?
- **D3.** If direct bookings doubled next season, what would that be worth to you?
  *(Frames budget without asking about our price.)*
- **D4.** *(S3 only)* When you pitch a new owner for their property, what do you show them?
  - *(Tests the owner-acquisition angle from [07](07-customer-research.md) §S3 — potentially our
    strongest message and the cheapest thing to learn here.)*

## Section E — Only now, the concept (5 min)

Introduce it flatly, with no enthusiasm, and explicitly invite rejection.

> Here's what I'm considering building, and I'd rather you tell me it's a bad idea than be
> polite. Someone sends me the photos they already have. I turn them into about seven short
> videos — versions for Instagram, for a website header, one that meets Vrbo's rules, one for
> Google. No site visit. Three to five days. Somewhere around $279. I wouldn't be able to tell
> you it gets you more bookings, because nobody actually knows that.

- **E1.** What's your first reaction?
- **E2.** What's wrong with it?
- **E3.** What would you expect that to cost? *(Before revealing reaction to the number. If they
  have already heard $279, ask instead: "what would make that feel expensive?")*
- **E4.** Who would you have to ask before buying it?
- **E5.** What would have to be true for you to try it this season?
- **E6.** What would make you not trust it?

### Questions we deliberately do **not** ask
- ❌ "Would you buy this?" — agreeableness bias; no evidential value.
- ❌ "How much would you pay?" as the primary price signal — stated WTP is unreliable; the price
  test in [42](42-validation-plan.md) is the real instrument.
- ❌ "Do you think video helps bookings?" — invites speculation we would then be tempted to cite.

## Section F — Close (2 min)

- **F1.** Is there anything I should have asked and didn't?
- **F2.** Who else should I talk to? *(Only referral request. Do not push.)*
- **F3.** Can I come back to you when I have something to show? *(Consent for later contact,
  captured and honoured — [38](38-rights-and-security.md).)*

### Decision rules from the aggregate

| Observation | Action |
|---|---|
| <4 of 12 paid for any marketing asset in 12 months | **A-01 in danger.** Do not proceed to ads. Re-examine segment before anything else |
| <3 of 12 recall any platform media enforcement | **A-02 fails.** Demote accuracy from lead message to trust-builder; lead with time saved |
| >6 of 12 report negative AI-tool experience | Remove "AI" from headline copy; lead on the finished result, mention method only when asked |
| >6 of 12 have a direct site with <25% direct share | E-28 confirmed locally; direct-booking framing becomes the lead angle |
| ≥4 of 12 (S3) say they show marketing work in owner pitches | Promote the owner-acquisition angle to a primary ad concept (AD-4) |
