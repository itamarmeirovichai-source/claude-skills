# 18 — Conversation Examples

Sixteen worked conversations. **These are training and evaluation material, not transcripts of
real customers.** No real person, company, or property is depicted. They exist so a founder or an
assistant can see the method in [16](16-sales-playbook.md) and [17](17-objection-library.md)
executed under pressure — including when it goes badly.

Outcomes: **6 orders, 5 no-sales, 3 disqualifications, 1 complaint, 1 escalation.** That
distribution is deliberate. A library where every conversation converts teaches nothing.

Each is annotated with the state ([16](16-sales-playbook.md) §3) and the technique.

---

## C-1 — Straightforward inbound → order ✅

> **Lead (form):** 4 properties, publishes to Instagram + own booking site, 30+ photos, owns
> them, needs within 3 weeks.

**Us:** Thanks — four properties and your own booking site is a good setup for this. Before I
recommend anything: which of the four is most in need of it right now? `[S1→S2, one question]`

**Them:** The lake cabin. It's our newest and it's not booking like the others.

**Us:** Got it. What do you think is different about the cabin versus the ones that book well —
is it the photos, the price, or something about the place itself? `[S2 diagnosis — resisting the
urge to assume it's a content problem]`

**Them:** Honestly the photos are fine. I think people don't realise how close it is to the
water. It's 40 seconds' walk but you can't tell from the pictures.

**Us:** That's a good brief, actually — that's one thing video does well, showing the walk. Two
questions: do you have a photo looking from the cabin toward the water, and is "40 seconds"
something you'd be comfortable putting on screen as a caption? `[Diagnosis → fact verification,
pre-empting an accuracy problem before it exists]`

**Them:** Yes and yes, it's genuinely about 40 seconds.

**Us:** Then here's what I'd do. Standard package, $279. Two concepts — one leading on the
walk to the water, one on the interior. Seven files: two for Instagram, one square, one for your
site header, a silent loop for the top of the page, a music-free cut that meets Vrbo's rules if
you list there, and a 30-second one for your Google profile. Three to five business days from
when your photos land. One revision round. I won't tell you it'll get you bookings — nobody can
back that up — but you'll have the files. `[S3, exact scope, explicit non-claim]`

**Them:** Do it.

> **Outcome: order, $279.** Note what closed it: the diagnostic question found a *specific*
> communication problem (proximity to water) that video genuinely solves. No persuasion was
> required once the brief was right.

## C-2 — Price objection → smaller package → order ✅

**Them:** $279 feels like a lot for a video made from photos I already have.

**Us:** Fair. Compared to what — more than you'd budgeted, or is it that you're not sure what
you'd get? `[OBJ-1 diagnostic]`

**Them:** More than I budgeted. I was thinking a hundred-ish.

**Us:** Then Standard is the wrong one. Starter is $149 — one concept, three files: vertical for
Instagram, square, and a wide one for your site. Same accuracy review, same one revision round.
You lose the second concept and the Vrbo and Google cuts. If you only post to Instagram, you
won't miss them. `[Scope down, not price down — 15 §Discount rules]`

**Them:** That works. Can I upgrade later?

**Us:** You can order the extra formats later at the add-on prices, yes. I'd just do Starter
first and see if you like the output.

> **Outcome: order, $149.** Margin lower, customer acquired honestly, no precedent set that the
> price is negotiable.

## C-3 — Disqualified: nowhere to publish ❌

> **Lead (form):** 1 property, "Not sure yet" on where they'd use it, 15–30 photos.

**Us:** Thanks for this. One thing to check before anything else — where were you thinking of
putting the video? `[Q2 first, always]`

**Them:** On my Airbnb listing.

**Us:** That's the thing I have to tell you up front: Airbnb doesn't let hosts upload video to
the listing gallery. No MP4, no YouTube link. It's photos only, apart from Luxe listings and
some professional API accounts. So a video wouldn't have anywhere to go on your listing.
`[E-01, stated immediately, before any pitch]`

**Them:** Really? I've seen ads for Airbnb videos everywhere.

**Us:** You have, and that's the frustrating part. A lot of that marketing is written for a
placement that doesn't exist. Video is genuinely useful for a rental — but on Instagram, your own
booking site, a Vrbo listing, or a Google Business Profile. If Airbnb is your only channel right
now, buying a video from me would be buying a file with nowhere to put it, so I'm not going to
sell you one.

**Them:** So what should I do?

**Us:** If you want to build something outside Airbnb, start an Instagram account for the
property and post photos for a month first. If that feels worth continuing, come back and I'll
make you something to post. I'll send you a short guide — no charge and no follow-up sequence.

> **Outcome: disqualified, honestly.** Cost: one lost order. Gained: a person who now knows
> something true, and who is a plausible customer in three months. This conversation is also our
> single best piece of organic content ([29](29-content-calendar.md), Day 2).

## C-4 — "I'll use the AI tool myself" → order ✅

**Them:** Not being rude, but why wouldn't I just use Runway or whatever myself?

**Us:** Not rude at all, it's the right question. Have you tried one yet? `[OBJ-2 diagnostic]`

**Them:** I messed around with one for an evening. Got something OK-ish.

**Us:** So you already know the honest answer: you can do this. If you've got two hours per
property and you enjoyed the evening, do it yourself and keep the money.

What you'd be paying me for is four things. The two hours. Choosing which twelve of your forty
photos go in and in what order — that's most of why one property video works and another doesn't,
and it's the part the tool doesn't do. An accuracy pass against what the platforms allow, because
Airbnb can make you pull media that adds something the property doesn't have. And seven finished
files in the shapes you actually publish, instead of one you then re-crop four times.

One more thing worth knowing regardless: free tiers on most of those tools are personal-use
only. If you're running a rental business, that's commercial use. `[E-21 — useful whether or not
they buy]`

**Them:** I didn't know that. How many properties do you do at once?

**Us:** One at a time on a first order. Which property? `[Buying signal — answer and advance]`

> **Outcome: order, $279.** The concession opened the door; the licensing fact closed it. Note
> we gave away the useful information without conditioning it on a purchase.

## C-5 — Wants a booking guarantee → no sale ❌

**Them:** If I spend $279, how many more bookings does that get me?

**Us:** None that I can promise. `[OBJ-12, immediate, no hedging]`

**Them:** That's not a great sales pitch.

**Us:** It isn't. But there's no credible evidence that property video causes bookings — I went
looking, and what's out there is vendors quoting their own marketing. Bookings move with price,
season, reviews and platform ranking all at once, so a video that ran alongside a good month
proves nothing about the video.

What I'll commit to is the files, the formats, the accuracy review and the date. If you need a
number attached to the outcome, I'd genuinely rather you didn't buy this — from me or anyone
quoting you one.

**Them:** I'll think about it.

**Us:** Fair enough. If you do want it later, I'm here. I won't chase you.

> **Outcome: no sale.** Correct outcome. The alternative — inventing a number — trades a $279
> order for a refund, a bad review, and a false claim on record (D-05).

## C-6 — Bad assets → declined, referred out ❌

**Them:** I've got about 8 photos, took them on my phone last year. Can you work with that?

**Us:** How big are they — if you open one on your computer, does it say the dimensions?
`[OBJ-6 diagnostic]`

**Them:** Says 1024 by 768.

**Us:** Then I'm going to say no, and here's why. I need around 15 usable images at 1500 pixels
or more. With eight small ones I'd be filling gaps by inventing rooms, and inventing is the one
thing I won't do — that's what gets listing media pulled.

The better spend right now is a local photographer for a half day. You'll get 30 or 40 images
that make your listing better immediately, and then a video is easy. If you tell me your area I'll
see whether I know anyone.

**Them:** That's annoying but fair.

**Us:** It is annoying. Send me the photos when you have them.

> **Outcome: declined.** [15](15-financial-model.md) §10 — this order would have cost 5+ hours
> and likely ended in a refund. Declining it was worth more than taking it.

## C-7 — Property manager, 14 units → pilot ✅

**Them:** We manage 14 units. What's the deal for volume?

**Us:** Before pricing — who'd be approving this, you or someone else? `[A-06, mapping the
approval path before quoting: 19]`

**Them:** Me, up to about $1,500. Above that it's my business partner.

**Us:** Good. And do the owners of the properties have any say in how their units are marketed?

**Them:** Some of them are fussy, yeah.

**Us:** Then here's what I'd suggest instead of quoting 14 up front. Pick three units — ideally
one easy, one you're struggling to fill, and one with a fussy owner. $279 each, $837 total, so
inside your authority. Three to five days each. If the output works and the fussy owner signs
off, we talk about the other eleven and I'll have real numbers to price them from.

I'm not going to quote you a volume discount today, because I haven't yet measured what a
multi-property job actually costs me to deliver. I'd rather give you a real number in three
weeks than a made-up one now. `[15 §6 — the rule that prevents growing into a loss]`

**Them:** That's more honest than the last three vendors. Let's do three.

> **Outcome: pilot order, $837.** Note: the refusal to quote volume pricing was received as
> credibility, not as unpreparedness. It also protects the margin that a premature discount would
> have destroyed.

## C-8 — Free sample request → no sale ❌

**Them:** Do one for free and if I like it I'll order five.

**Us:** What would you need to see to feel OK paying for the first one? `[OBJ-10 diagnostic]`

**Them:** Just proof it doesn't look like garbage.

**Us:** That's reasonable, and there's a library of illustrative work on the site — those are
properties I don't represent, made to show the output. Judge it there, costs you nothing.

What I won't do is free production. It's real cost, and the people who ask for it mostly don't
buy, which means the people who do buy end up paying for it. The $149 package is a real one at
the smallest size — same quality, same accuracy review.

**Them:** Everyone does free samples.

**Us:** Some do. If that's a dealbreaker I understand, and I'm not going to talk you round.

> **Outcome: no sale.** D-11 held. Note the second "no" ended it — no third attempt.

## C-9 — Impossible deadline → declined, rebooked ⏸️

**Them:** Need it by Friday, it's Wednesday night. I'll pay extra.

**Us:** What's happening Friday? `[OBJ-11 diagnostic — a real deadline changes the answer]`

**Them:** Running an Instagram promo over the weekend.

**Us:** I'm going to turn that down, and it's not a capacity thing. I don't know your property
yet, and a rushed first order is exactly how something ends up in a video that isn't in the
house. That's the one mistake I can't undo for you.

Earliest I'd take it is Tuesday, delivered the following Monday. If the promo can move, I'll take
it. If it can't, run the weekend on photos and let's do this properly for the next one.

**Them:** Let's do the next one. Book me for Tuesday.

> **Outcome: rebooked, $279, one week later.** Rush fees exist for repeat customers with spare
> capacity ([15](15-financial-model.md)), never for a first order.

## C-10 — Asked to show something that doesn't exist → declined ❌

**Them:** Can you make the backyard look bigger? And there's a fence I'd rather not show.

**Us:** Two different things there, and they land differently. Leaving the fence out of the shot
is fine — that's just framing, same as a photographer choosing an angle.

Making the yard look bigger, I won't do. Changing proportions is the kind of alteration that gets
listing media pulled, and if a guest turns up and the yard is half what they saw, that's a refund
and a one-star review before it's anything else. `[E-11, E-12, 26]`

**Them:** Everyone does it with wide angle lenses.

**Us:** They do, and that's part of why "smaller than the photos" is such a common complaint.
Slightly awkward truth: honest video often makes a place look smaller than wide-angle
photography does. My argument for that is it filters out the guests who'd have been disappointed
anyway. If you want the yard enlarged, I'm the wrong person — and I'd say that to anyone.

**Them:** Forget it then.

**Us:** No hard feelings. If you change your mind on the framing version, I'm here.

> **Outcome: declined.** Hard boundary ([26](26-property-accuracy-rules.md)). Not negotiable at
> any price.

## C-11 — Complaint: delivered late 😠

**Them:** You said Thursday. It's Monday. This is unprofessional.

**Us:** You're right and I'm sorry. Your files are attached — all seven, delivered now.

Here's what happened: your photos arrived Tuesday rather than the Friday I'd assumed, and I
should have re-confirmed the delivery date at that point instead of leaving the original one
standing. That's on me, not on you.

Two things. Your revision round is open and I'll turn any changes round same day. And I'm
refunding $50 of the $279 for the delay — that's not me buying you off, it's just that you paid
for a date you didn't get. `[35 — own it, fix it, don't grovel, don't over-explain]`

**Them:** ...OK. That's fair. The videos are good actually.

**Us:** Glad. Send revisions if you want any — otherwise I'll leave you to it.

> **Outcome: retained, $229 net.** The process failure (not re-confirming the date when assets
> slipped) is logged as a fix in [35](35-customer-operations.md), not just apologised for.

## C-12 — Revision dispute → scope held, relationship kept ✅

**Them:** These aren't right. I want a completely different style — more like a hotel ad, and
can you use these 15 new photos I just took?

**Us:** Happy to do it. Want me to be straight about where that sits in scope?
`[14 §4 decision tree, offered rather than imposed]`

**Them:** Go on.

**Us:** What you're describing is a different creative direction plus new source material, which
is a new concept rather than a revision — different narrative, different pacing, and the new
photos restart asset prep. That's $89 for the concept plus $49 for the new assets, and about
three days.

Your included revision round is still unused, though. If there's a version of "more like a hotel
ad" I can get to by changing the pacing, the music and the text on what's already built, that's
free and I'll do it today. Want me to try that first and you decide after?

**Them:** Try that first.

**Us:** On it.

> **Outcome: revision used, relationship kept, scope held.** Offering the free in-scope attempt
> *before* quoting is what stops the policy reading as a shakedown.

## C-13 — Asks for a human 🙋

**Them:** Am I talking to a bot?

**Us:** Partly. I use an assistant to draft replies and I read and send every one myself — so
there's one actual person here, and it's me. Nothing goes out that I haven't looked at. What
would you like to know? `[D-13 — never conceal, never impersonate]`

**Them:** I'd rather just have a call.

**Us:** Sure. I do calls Tuesday and Thursday afternoons — here's the link. Fair warning, it's a
one-person operation, so if you'd rather not wait, I'm quicker over email.

> **Outcome: escalated to a call.** Note we did not claim to be "a team." One person, said out
> loud.

## C-14 — Returning customer, second property ✅

**Them:** Loved the cabin one. Can we do the barn?

**Us:** Yes. Two things: has anything changed at the barn since your photos — any renovation,
new furniture, anything removed? And is this one aimed at the same guests as the cabin, or
different? `[Repeat path — skip demonstration, re-confirm facts: 16 §2]`

**Them:** New kitchen in March, I'll send photos. Same kind of guests.

**Us:** Then it's straightforward. Same package, $279. Send the new kitchen photos with the rest
and I'll start Monday — you'll have it Thursday or Friday.

And a small thing: you're my second repeat customer, so if you're happy at the end I'd like to
ask for one sentence I can put on the site, with your name or without, whichever you prefer. No
pressure and it doesn't change the price. `[36 — ask after value, never incentivised]`

**Them:** Happy to.

> **Outcome: order, $279, plus a testimonial request made correctly** — after delivery of the
> first, before delivery of the second, with no incentive attached.

## C-15 — Photographer partnership inquiry 🤝

**Them:** I shoot STR properties. Clients keep asking for video and I don't do it. Do you do
white label?

**Us:** Let's talk about it properly — this is the partnership I'd most want to have. Two
questions first. Do you want to resell it under your name, or refer clients to me directly? And
whose licence are the photos under — yours or the client's? `[33]`

**Them:** Refer is simpler. Photos are licensed to the client for listing use.

**Us:** That second answer matters. If the licence is listing-use only, the client may not have
the right to authorise derivative video — which would make both of us a problem. Easiest fix is
a line in your standard licence granting derivative marketing use. I'll send you the wording I'd
want to see.

On referrals: 15% of the first order, paid after delivery, and I tell the client you're getting
it. No exclusivity either way.

**Them:** Send it over.

> **Outcome: partnership conversation opened.** The rights issue (A-08) surfaced before it could
> become an incident, and disclosure of the referral fee was stated without being asked.

## C-16 — Refund request, quality dispute 😐

**Them:** I don't like these. I want my money back.

**Us:** Tell me what's wrong with them — and specifically, is it that they're not to your taste,
or is something in them not accurate to the property? Those go different ways.
`[Diagnose before deciding — 35]`

**Them:** The living room looks weird in the second one. Like the wall bends.

**Us:** That's a defect, not a taste question, and it shouldn't have got past me. Geometry
warping is on the reject list and I missed it. I'm re-cutting that clip today at no charge and
you'll have it within 24 hours.

If the re-cut still isn't right, I'll refund the whole order, not part of it. `[27 hard-reject —
a QC escape, treated as our error under 14 §4 category 1]`

**Them:** OK. Let's see the re-cut.

**Us:** Sending today. And thank you for describing it precisely — that's what let me fix it
instead of guessing.

> **Outcome: retained after fix.** Logged as a **QC escape** — a defect that reached a customer —
> which triggers a review of the quality gate itself ([27](27-quality-rubric.md) §Escalation),
> not just a fix of the one clip.

## What these conversations are for

| Use | How |
|---|---|
| Founder training | Read C-3, C-5, C-10, C-16 before the first real conversation. They are the four hardest |
| Assistant evaluation | [40](40-testing-plan.md) uses C-3, C-5, C-10, C-13, C-16 as eval fixtures. An assistant that converts C-5 or C-10 has **failed**, not succeeded |
| Copy source | The phrasing here is the voice reference for [20](20-brand-voice-and-copy.md) |
| Objection validation | Each maps to an OBJ-NN in [17](17-objection-library.md); after 20 real conversations, compare which actually occur |

**The most important property of this library:** in five of sixteen conversations, the right move
was to not make a sale. An assistant or a founder optimising for conversion rate alone would get
all five wrong, and four of those five would end in a refund, a compliance problem, or a
loss-making order.
