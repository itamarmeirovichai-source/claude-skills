# 33 — Partnerships & Community

Partnerships score highest on buying intent of any channel available to us
([28](28-channel-strategy.md)) because the partner has already done the hardest part: earned the
customer's trust and established that they spend money on how their property looks.

**Nothing in this document is sent to anyone during this project.** The materials are drafts.

---

## 1. Partner evaluation

| Partner type | Mutual value | Customer overlap | Effort | Risk | **Priority** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Property photographers** | They get an answer to "do you do video?" without buying gear or learning a craft. We get assets, rights clarity, and warm introductions | **Very high** | Low | Low | **⭐ 1st** |
| **Co-hosts / small PM services** | They add a service line without production capacity | High | Low | Low | **⭐ 2nd** |
| **Direct booking specialists** | Their clients build sites and then have nothing to put on them (E-28) | **Very high** | Medium | Low | **⭐ 3rd** |
| STR website developers | Same as above, earlier in the chain | High | Medium | Low | 4th |
| Interior designers | Portfolio content for them; a refurbishment trigger for us (A-14) | Medium | Medium | Low | 5th |
| Staging providers | Adjacent, weak overlap in STR | Low | Medium | Low | Skip |
| Hospitality marketing agencies | They subcontract video | Medium | High | **Medium — they may absorb the relationship** | 6th |
| STR consultants / coaches | Audience access | Medium | Low | **Medium — audience quality varies wildly** | Cautious |

### Why photographers are first, and by a distance

1. They already hold the customer relationship and the customer's proven willingness to pay for
   how the property looks.
2. They hold the source assets our product needs.
3. **They own the rights problem.** A-08 is a live legal exposure for us: a host may hold a
   licence covering listing use only and have no right to authorise derivative video. The
   photographer is the one person who can fix that at source, with one line in their standard
   licence. **This turns our biggest legal risk into a partnership benefit.**
4. Many do not offer video and do not want to.

## 2. Terms

| Element | Standard terms |
|---|---|
| **Model** | **Referral, not white label**, at launch. See below |
| Commission | **15% of the first order**, paid after delivery and after any refund window closes |
| Recurring | 10% of subsequent orders from the same customer for 12 months |
| Customer ownership | **The customer is ours after introduction.** We invoice, support, and hold the relationship. Stated plainly up front |
| Lead handoff | Partner sends an email introduction. We reply within 4 business hours |
| **Disclosure** | **We tell the customer the partner receives a fee.** Always. No exceptions |
| Quality responsibility | Ours, entirely. A partner is never blamed for our output |
| Payment timing | Monthly, in arrears, after refund windows close |
| Disputes | Written scope wins. If ambiguous, the partner is paid |
| Exclusivity | **None, either way** |
| Termination | Either side, any time, with outstanding commissions honoured |

### Why referral and not white label

White label pays more per job and looks like the bigger opportunity. It is rejected at launch
because:
- **Accuracy responsibility becomes unclear.** If a partner's client's video contains an error,
  and the partner's name is on it, [26](26-property-accuracy-rules.md)'s escalation path has no
  owner. That is the one failure we cannot afford to make ambiguous.
- We lose direct contact with the customer, and therefore all the validation evidence
  [42](42-validation-plan.md) depends on.
- It requires brand flexibility and capacity we do not have (A-15).

**Revisit at 90 days**, once accuracy escalation has been exercised at least once in the simple
case.

### Why disclose the commission

Because the customer would want to know, and because a customer who discovers an undisclosed
referral fee later reasonably concludes the recommendation was bought. One sentence prevents it:
*"[Partner] gets a referral fee if you go ahead — worth knowing when you're weighing their
recommendation."*

## 3. Partner materials (drafts — not sent)

### Outreach email to a photographer

> Subject: The video question your clients keep asking
>
> Hi [name],
>
> I make short marketing videos for vacation rentals, working from photography rather than
> shooting. I came across your work through [genuine, specific source].
>
> If your STR clients ask whether you do video and you'd rather not get into it, I'd be a
> reasonable place to send them. I work from the photos you've already delivered, so nothing gets
> re-shot and you stay the photographer on the job.
>
> Two things worth saying up front. First, I'd pay 15% of the first order, and I'd tell the
> client you're getting it. Second — and this is genuinely the more useful part — most of the
> photography licences I see cover listing use only, which means the client may not have the
> right to authorise a video made from those images. A line in your standard licence granting
> derivative marketing use fixes it, and it's worth having whether or not you ever send me
> anyone. Happy to send the wording I'd want to see.
>
> No exclusivity, no commitment. If it's not of interest, no follow-up from me.
>
> [Name]

### One-page partner overview

> **What I do:** short property videos made from existing photography. No site visit. Seven files
> per property, sized for Instagram, a booking site, Google Business Profile, and a Vrbo-compliant
> cut. Three to five business days.
>
> **What I don't do:** shoot, retouch stills, post to anyone's accounts, or claim video gets
> bookings. There is no credible evidence for that and I don't pretend otherwise.
>
> **What I need from a referred client:** 20+ photos at 1500px or more, a short property fact
> sheet, and confirmation they hold the rights to the images.
>
> **Terms:** 15% of the first order, 10% for 12 months after. Paid monthly once refund windows
> close. Disclosed to the client. No exclusivity. Cancel any time.
>
> **Who I'm a bad fit for:** single-property hosts with no website and no social account — Airbnb
> doesn't accept host-uploaded listing video, so there's nowhere to put it. I'll tell them that
> rather than take the order.

### Licence wording to suggest to photographers

> *Draft only. Both parties should have this reviewed by their own lawyer before adoption
> ([38](38-rights-and-security.md)).*
>
> "Client is granted the right to create and publish derivative works, including video, from the
> delivered images, for the purpose of marketing the photographed property, in perpetuity,
> worldwide. This right may be exercised by a third party acting on Client's behalf."

## 4. Community participation

Where our ICP gathers: STR Facebook groups, r/AirBnBHosts and similar, VRMA-adjacent forums,
local host meetups.

### Rules

1. **Read the rules. Follow them.** Most STR groups ban promotion outright, and most enforcement
   is permanent.
2. **Participate by being useful without linking.** E-01, E-04, the photography rights trap — all
   genuinely valuable, all free to give.
3. Answer the question asked. Do not answer a different question that lets you mention the
   service.
4. **Never** DM someone from a group thread unprompted.
5. **Never** use multiple accounts, automated posting, fabricated engagement, or bought
   followers.
6. If a group has a promo day or thread, use exactly that and nothing more.
7. If asked directly what we do, answer once, plainly, and stop.

**The measure of success is name recognition, not leads.** Treating a community as a lead source
is how people get banned and how a small industry decides someone is a nuisance.

### Useful, not spammy — worked example

> **Someone asks:** "Has anyone added a video to their Airbnb listing? Can't find the button."
>
> ✅ **Good:** "There isn't one — Airbnb doesn't take host-uploaded video on listing galleries,
> it's photos only. Vrbo does take video, and there are rules there too: no added music, no
> contact details on screen, 15 seconds to 2 minutes. Worth checking their guidelines before you
> make anything."
>
> ❌ **Bad:** the same answer plus "I actually run a service that does exactly this, DM me."

The good version is more persuasive *because* it does not sell. Anyone who wants to know who
wrote it can look.

## 5. Measurement

| Metric | Definition |
|---|---|
| Partners approached / active | Active = ≥1 referral in 90 days |
| Referrals received | Count, by partner |
| Referral → qualified rate | Tests whether a partner understands our fit criteria |
| Referral → order rate | **Expect materially higher than cold. If it isn't, the partner is sending the wrong people** |
| Commission paid | Against contribution margin ([15](15-financial-model.md)) |
| Disclosure rate | **Must be 100%** |

## 6. Acceptance criteria

- [ ] ≥3 partner conversations opened in 30 days
- [ ] 100% of referral fees disclosed to the customer
- [ ] Zero community rule violations
- [ ] Zero unsolicited DMs from community threads
- [ ] Licence wording shared with every photographer partner
- [ ] Referral orders tracked separately for conversion comparison
