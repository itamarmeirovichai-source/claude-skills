# 13 — Consumer Psychology & Sales Method

## Part 1 — Psychological principles

### The standard that governs this section

Every principle below is a **laboratory or field finding about human judgment**, not a
prediction about our business. The literature on behavioural effects has a well-documented
replication problem, and effect sizes measured in controlled settings routinely shrink or vanish
in commercial application. **We therefore treat each principle as a hypothesis generator, never
as a guaranteed purchasing outcome.** Each entry ends with something we can actually test.

**Sourcing note:** the attributions below are to well-known published work that is part of
general knowledge in this field. **No quotations are invented, and no specific effect sizes are
asserted**, because none were verified from primary sources within this project. Where
replication is contested, it is said so.

---

### 1. Attention — the sound-off default

- **Evidence:** Meta's own creator guidance advises designing Reels to be followable without
  sound, and states captions and on-screen text increase view-through (E-33, grade B).
- **Context/limits:** Platform guidance, from a party with an interest in more content being
  made. The widely-repeated "first 3 seconds" rule is practitioner folklore (E-33 grade E), not
  platform research — do not cite it as such.
- **Relevance:** Both systems. Our own ads and our clients' property videos are consumed muted.
- **Application:** Burned-in captions on every social deliverable. The first frame must carry
  meaning without audio. Property name and location legible at 1 second.
- **Misuse:** Caption-stuffing that covers the property. Clickbait text unrelated to the video.
- **Testable:** Same edit, captions vs none, on our own channel. Metric: 3-second retention. If
  the gap is under 10 percentage points over 10 pairs, stop treating it as settled.

### 2. Processing fluency — ease is mistaken for quality

- **Evidence:** A large literature (Reber, Schwarz, Winkielman and others) finds that stimuli
  which are easier to process are judged more favourably, more true, and more likeable.
- **Limits:** Mostly lab-based, often with artificial stimuli. Effects are small and can be
  swamped by real stakes.
- **Relevance:** Both systems. A confusing price table or a video with erratic pacing imposes
  processing cost that gets attributed to the *property* or the *service*.
- **Application:** One price table, three columns. Consistent shot duration within a video.
  Stable typography. No more than two fonts.
- **Misuse:** Simplifying away material terms — hiding the revision limit to make the page
  cleaner. That is a hidden-fee pattern and is forbidden.
- **Testable:** A/B the pricing page: three-tier table vs prose. Metric: qualification form
  starts per session.

### 3. Perceived risk and uncertainty

- **Evidence:** Loss aversion and ambiguity aversion (Kahneman & Tversky; Ellsberg) consistently
  show that decision-makers overweight potential losses and dislike unknown probabilities.
  **Note:** loss aversion's magnitude is actively contested in recent literature — treat the
  direction as robust and the size as unknown.
- **Relevance:** System A. Our buyer's risk is not $279; it is looking foolish and wasting time.
- **Application:** Make the downside small and legible — fixed price, written scope, a stated
  refund position ([35](35-customer-operations.md)), named turnaround. Show what happens if they
  dislike it *before* they ask.
- **Misuse:** Manufacturing fear ("your listing could be banned!") to force a sale. E-11 is real,
  but weaponising it is exactly the misuse. State it factually, once, when relevant.
- **Testable:** Add the revision and refund terms above the fold vs below. Metric: form
  completion rate.

### 4. Cognitive load and choice overload

- **Evidence:** The jam-study line of work (Iyengar & Lepper) suggested more options reduce
  purchase. **This is one of the more prominently contested findings; meta-analyses have found
  the effect is not robust and depends heavily on context.** Cited here with that caveat
  explicitly.
- **Relevance:** Our package count.
- **Application:** Three packages, one recommended. Not six.
- **Misuse:** Removing genuine options the customer needs in order to force an upsell.
- **Testable:** 3 tiers vs 2 tiers. Metric: orders per 100 sessions. **Expect a small or null
  effect** — that is the honest prior.

### 5. Anchoring

- **Evidence:** Robust and widely replicated (Tversky & Kahneman): an initial number influences
  subsequent numeric judgment, even when uninformative.
- **Relevance:** Our price is judged against whatever number is in their head.
- **Application:** Introduce the videographer range factually before our price — "a local
  videographer is typically $150 to $500 for a basic walkthrough, more with travel" (E-31). This
  is a true, sourced, publicly available fact. Stating it is honest anchoring.
- **Misuse:** Inventing a "was $599, now $279" that was never charged. Forbidden — fake
  reference pricing.
- **Testable:** Proposal with and without the market-rate context paragraph. Metric: close rate,
  discount-request rate.

### 6. Social proof

- **Evidence:** Strong and well replicated (Cialdini and successors), especially under
  uncertainty and when the referent group is similar to the observer.
- **Limits:** **We have none.** Zero customers.
- **Application at launch:** Substitute *process transparency* for social proof — the published
  accuracy checklist, the visible scope document, the stated refusal to make booking claims.
  These signal competence without borrowed credibility.
- **Misuse:** Fake testimonials, stock-photo "clients", "trusted by 500+ hosts", inflated
  follower counts, presenting illustrative work as client work. **All forbidden**
  ([21](21-claim-register.md)).
- **Testable:** Once we have 5 real customers, add named permission-cleared testimonials and
  measure close rate before/after. Until then, nothing to test.

### 7. Framing

- **Evidence:** Framing effects are robust in direction (Tversky & Kahneman); magnitude varies.
- **Application:** "Seven files ready to publish" frames the deliverable as complete work.
  "One video plus six exports" frames the same thing as padding. Both are accurate; the first is
  the honest frame of what they receive.
- **Misuse:** "Save $1,200 vs a film crew" — a comparison to a product we do not offer, against
  a price they were never going to pay. Misleading comparison, forbidden.
- **Testable:** Deliverable framing A/B on the pricing page. Metric: orders per session.

### 8. Autonomy and reactance

- **Evidence:** Reactance theory (Brehm) — pressure to comply generates resistance.
- **Relevance:** Our buyer is an owner-operator. Autonomy is their identity.
- **Application:** Give the exit explicitly. "If you've got two hours and like editing, do it
  yourself" ([12](12-positioning.md)). Recommend the *smaller* package where it fits.
- **Misuse:** Fake autonomy ("no pressure!") followed by five follow-up emails.
- **Testable:** Proposal with and without an explicit "here's when not to buy this" section.
  Metric: close rate and refund rate. **Our hypothesis is close rate holds and refund rate
  falls** — that would be the more valuable result.

### 9. Expectations and post-purchase satisfaction

- **Evidence:** Expectation-disconfirmation theory — satisfaction tracks the gap between
  expectation and experience, not absolute quality.
- **Relevance:** Our single biggest controllable lever on refunds and referrals.
- **Application:** Under-promise on turnaround (say 5 days, deliver in 3). Show a representative
  sample *before* payment, not a best-case one. State what AI-assisted output does not look like.
- **Misuse:** Setting expectations so low the customer does not buy. There is a floor.
- **Testable:** Track promised vs actual delivery day against refund and referral rate.

### 10. Memory and the peak-end rule

- **Evidence:** Retrospective evaluations weight peak and final moments disproportionately
  (Kahneman, Redelmeier and others).
- **Application:** The **delivery message** is the end. It must be the best-crafted message in
  the business — files organised, each one labelled with where it goes, one line on what to post
  first. [20](20-brand-voice-and-copy.md) treats it as the flagship copy asset.
- **Misuse:** A theatrical "reveal" that delays access to files.
- **Testable:** Referral rate before/after upgrading the delivery message.

### 11. Scarcity

- **Evidence:** Scarcity increases perceived value (well replicated).
- **Application:** We have **genuine** capacity limits — a solo operator with a real weekly
  ceiling ([37](37-capacity-and-contractors.md)). Stating a true limit is honest.
- **Misuse:** Countdown timers, "3 spots left" that reset, fake deadlines. **Forbidden.**
- **Rule:** If we state a capacity number it must be the real one, and we must actually turn
  work away when it is reached. Otherwise say nothing.

## Part 2 — Choosing one sales method

### Frameworks considered

| Framework | Core move | Fit here |
|---|---|---|
| **Consultative / solution selling** | Diagnose, then prescribe | Good fit. Matches a founder-expert selling a judgment-based service |
| **SPIN** | Situation → Problem → Implication → Need-payoff | Partial fit. **The Implication stage is the problem** — it pushes toward amplifying consequences, and our strongest consequence (booking loss) is exactly the claim D-05 forbids |
| **Jobs to Be Done** | Understand the progress the customer is trying to make and what they would fire | **Excellent fit** for *research and message construction* ([09](09-interview-guide.md)). Not a conversation script |
| **Value-based selling** | Price to quantified customer value | **Rejected as the primary frame.** It requires quantifying value, which requires booking-lift numbers we do not have (E-20). Using it would force us into fabricated ROI |
| **Objection diagnosis** | Treat objections as information, not resistance | Adopted as a component ([17](17-objection-library.md)) |
| **Negotiation (interest-based)** | Trade scope, protect price | Adopted as a component ([15](15-financial-model.md) discount rules) |
| **Customer success** | Value realised post-purchase | Adopted post-delivery ([35](35-customer-operations.md)) |

### Chosen method

> **Consultative diagnosis with JTBD-derived questions and scope-based negotiation. One method,
> not seven.**

The shape of every sales conversation:

1. **Qualify against reality** — do they have somewhere to publish this? (E-01 makes this the
   first question, not a later one.)
2. **Diagnose the actual job** — what are they trying to get done, and what have they tried?
   Past behaviour, per [09](09-interview-guide.md).
3. **Prescribe or decline** — recommend a package, a different vendor, or nothing. Declining is
   a normal outcome, not a failure ([08](08-segment-selection-matrix.md)).
4. **State scope and price plainly, once.**
5. **Diagnose objections rather than overcome them** — find the cause before responding
   ([17](17-objection-library.md)).
6. **Negotiate scope, not price** ([15](15-financial-model.md)).
7. **Stop when it is not a fit**, and say so.

### Why not a hybrid

Combining SPIN's implication-building with consultative diagnosis and value-based framing
produces a conversation that interrogates, amplifies pain, and then quantifies unverifiable
value. It reads as manipulation because it is. **A single coherent method that a founder can
actually run under time pressure beats an elaborate one that degrades into improvisation.**

### Absolute prohibitions

These are not stylistic preferences. Any of them appearing in our materials is a defect to be
fixed immediately:

- ❌ Fake scarcity or urgency of any kind
- ❌ Fabricated authority ("as featured in", invented credentials, borrowed logos)
- ❌ Misleading comparisons (against products we do not offer, or prices never charged)
- ❌ Hidden fees — every charge appears before payment ([14](14-offer-specification.md))
- ❌ Invented results, testimonials, case studies, or customer counts
- ❌ Concealed automation or impersonation of a human employee (D-13)
- ❌ Any claim that our video causes bookings (D-05)
