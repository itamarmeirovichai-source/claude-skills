# 10 — Traveler Decisions & Property Messaging Framework

This is System B ([06](06-two-marketing-systems.md)): what the property video has to accomplish
for a traveler. Getting this right is what makes the deliverable worth money; getting it wrong
produces beautiful footage that changes nothing.

## Status of evidence

**This section is reasoning, not research.** No traveler was surveyed for this project, and no
primary study of accommodation choice was located within scope. What follows is a structural
analysis of the decision — which factors exist and what medium can address them. Treat the
*taxonomy* as sound and any implied *weighting* as untested.

One piece of hard evidence does anchor it: **E-12** — guests obtain refunds when a property is
"materially different" from what was advertised, with size misrepresentation, missing amenities,
and photo discrepancies named explicitly. That tells us which mismatches travelers actually
escalate over, which is a far better signal than a stated-preference survey.

## 1. What travelers evaluate, and what video can do about it

| Factor | Video can… | Honest limit |
|---|---|---|
| **Destination** | Nothing | Chosen before the property |
| **Location within destination** | Show context: the walk to the beach, the street, the view direction | Cannot make a bad location good. Showing it honestly filters out wrong-fit guests, which *reduces* bad reviews |
| **Trip purpose fit** | **This is video's strongest job.** Show the property being used the way a specific trip would use it | Requires the property to actually suit that trip |
| **Price / total fees** | Nothing | Set elsewhere. A video cannot argue a price down |
| **Availability** | Nothing | |
| **Cancellation terms** | Nothing | |
| **Reviews** | Nothing directly | Accuracy indirectly protects reviews (E-12) |
| **Capacity** | **Show it.** The actual sleeping arrangements, not a claim | Overclaiming here is the #1 refund trigger (E-12) |
| **Layout & flow** | **The single thing video does that photos cannot.** Continuous movement between rooms answers "how do these spaces connect" | Requires real continuity — a hard constraint on generative motion ([26](26-property-accuracy-rules.md)) |
| **Scale** | Partially — movement through a space conveys size better than a wide-angle still | Wide lenses in photos *inflate* perceived size; honest video often makes rooms look **smaller**. Sell this as a feature: fewer disappointed arrivals |
| **Amenities** | Show them working | Never imply an amenity that is not there (E-11) |
| **Accessibility** | Show step counts, thresholds, bathroom access | **Never imply accessibility that does not exist.** Highest-harm category. Treat as hard-reject ([26](26-property-accuracy-rules.md)) |
| **Design & atmosphere** | **Video's second strongest job** — light, texture, mood | The most tempting place to exaggerate. Light grading is acceptable; inventing light is not |
| **Cleanliness** | Show it, honestly | Cannot fix a dirty property |
| **Safety information** | Show pool fencing, stair gates, lighting | Do not imply safety features absent in reality |
| **Arrival convenience** | **Strong, underused.** Parking, the door, the stairs, the lockbox | Best delivered as a *post-booking* arrival video (E-02), not a marketing cut |
| **Suitability for families / couples / groups / remote work** | **Strong.** A desk with real light and a real chair answers a remote worker's question instantly | Must be the actual desk |

### The three jobs video actually does well

1. **Spatial comprehension** — how rooms connect, and how big they really are.
2. **Trip-fit** — "is this for people like me, doing what I'm doing."
3. **Atmosphere** — the quality of light and texture at a time of day.

Everything else on the list is better handled by price, policy, copy, or reviews. **A video that
tries to do all fourteen does none of them.** Our storyboards commit to one primary job per video
([22](22-creative-concepts.md)).

### The counter-intuitive point worth saying to customers

Honest video often makes a property look *smaller* than its wide-angle photography does. Sellers
hate this. But E-12 says size misrepresentation is a named refund trigger, and a refund plus a
one-star review costs more than the marginal booking that the exaggeration won. **Accurate
representation is a yield strategy, not just a compliance one.** This is a genuinely useful thing
to be able to tell a skeptical operator, and it is defensible.

## 2. The property messaging framework

Every video we produce answers these six, in writing, before a frame is generated. This is a
required field set in the brief ([24](24-production-workflow.md)).

| # | Question | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Who is this property for?** | One primary guest type. If the answer is "everyone," the brief is not finished |
| 2 | **What makes it meaningfully different?** | One thing, verifiable. Not "cosy and well-appointed" |
| 3 | **What evidence supports that difference?** | A photo, a listing fact, a review quote, a measurement. **No evidence, no claim** |
| 4 | **What should be shown?** | Shot list, sourced entirely from real assets |
| 5 | **What should be explained?** | Caption/voice — things video cannot show (distance, capacity, policy) |
| 6 | **What must not be implied?** | Explicit prohibitions for this property. Signed off by the customer at intake |

Field 6 is the one that competitors do not have, and it is the one that prevents the incident
that would end the business.

### Fact vs. atmosphere — the line we hold

| Category | Definition | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| **Property fact** | Verifiable and consequential to the booking: room count, beds, capacity, distances, amenities present, accessibility | Must come from the customer's signed **Property Fact Sheet**. Never generated, never inferred, never guessed by a model ([39](39-data-and-agent-architecture.md)) |
| **Atmosphere** | Mood, time of day, colour grade, pacing, music | Creative latitude, bounded by [26](26-property-accuracy-rules.md) |
| **Artistic presentation** | Framing, transitions, typography, sequence | Full latitude |

The test: **would a guest arriving at the property feel misled by this?** If yes, it is a fact
problem regardless of how artistic the intent was.

## 3. Worked messaging examples

**These are illustrative property archetypes. None is a real customer property.** No real
address, brand, or client is referenced. They exist to show the framework working and to seed
[22](22-creative-concepts.md) and [23](23-scripts-and-storyboards.md).

### Archetype A — Two-bedroom coastal cottage, family market

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| Who for | Families with children under 10, 3–5 night summer stays |
| Difference | Enclosed garden with a gate, directly off the kitchen |
| Evidence | Photos 12, 14 show the gate and the garden; fact sheet confirms latch height |
| Show | Kitchen → garden door → enclosed garden → child's-eye level → back inside |
| Explain | "8-minute walk to the beach" (fact-sheet verified); "sleeps 4, 2 bedrooms" |
| Must not imply | Sea view (there is none). Beach access without crossing a road. That the garden is larger than it is |

### Archetype B — City studio, remote-work market

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| Who for | Solo remote workers, 1–3 week stays |
| Difference | A real desk at a window with power and a proper chair — not a dining table |
| Evidence | Photo 6; fact sheet confirms fibre broadband with a speed test screenshot |
| Show | Desk in real daylight → window → the chair → the outlet → street below |
| Explain | Measured download speed, exact figure from the customer's test. **No generic "fast wifi"** |
| Must not imply | Quiet, unless the customer states it and accepts the risk. Separate bedroom (it is a studio) |

### Archetype C — Six-bedroom mountain lodge, group market

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| Who for | Groups of 10–12, ski season, one shared kitchen |
| Difference | A single table that genuinely seats 12 |
| Evidence | Photo 3; fact sheet confirms 12 chairs |
| Show | The table with all 12 chairs, wide → the kitchen working triangle → the boot room |
| Explain | Bed configuration by room, drive time to the lift (verified) |
| Must not imply | Ski-in/ski-out. Hot tub (there is none). That all bedrooms are equal (two are bunk rooms — **show them**) |

### Archetype D — Design-led loft, couples market

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| Who for | Couples, 2–3 night city breaks |
| Difference | 4-metre windows and original brick |
| Evidence | Photos 1, 2, 9 |
| Show | Light moving across brick → the window scale against a door → the bed |
| Explain | Two flights of stairs, no lift. **Say it.** Filters out wrong-fit guests and pre-empts a refund claim (E-12) |
| Must not imply | Accessibility. A view beyond what the windows actually show |

### Archetype E — Rural barn conversion, off-season market

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| Who for | Couples and pairs, autumn/winter, dog owners |
| Difference | Wood burner with logs included; dogs genuinely welcome |
| Evidence | Photo 8; fact sheet confirms dog policy and log supply |
| Show | The burner lit → texture → the dog-friendly floor → the boot room |
| Explain | Nearest shop distance, mobile signal reality, dog fee if any |
| Must not imply | Walking access to anything not verified. Enclosed land unless it is |
