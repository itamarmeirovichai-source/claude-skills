# 19 — Selling to Property Managers

The B2B motion. This is a different sale from [16](16-sales-playbook.md), not a bigger version
of it.

## 1. How this differs from selling one video to a host

| | Individual host (S2) | Property manager (S3) |
|---|---|---|
| Buyer and user | Same person | **Different people** |
| Decision | One person, minutes to days | 2–4 people, days to weeks |
| Trigger | Their own property, their own season | Unit onboarding calendar, an owner pitch, a direct-booking push |
| Money | Personal discretionary | A marketing line item, possibly recharged to owners |
| What they're buying | A video | **Consistency across a portfolio**, and often an owner-facing capability |
| Risk they fear | Wasting $279 | Inconsistent output across 14 properties; an owner complaining |
| Proof they need | "Does it look good" | "Can you do this 14 times identically, on a schedule" |
| Failure mode for us | A refund | **Quoting portfolio pricing before knowing our delivery cost** |
| What closes it | A good example | **A pilot that works and an approver who signs off** |

**The single most expensive mistake available in this business is quoting a volume discount on
the first call.** [15](15-financial-model.md) §6 shows why: if template reuse does not reduce
per-unit time, a 5-property job at $199/unit earns **$69/hour** against $100/hour for five
separate Standard orders. That is growth that makes the business worse, and it is invisible
until the work is done.

## 2. The stakeholder map

**All of this carries A-06 — we have not mapped a real one.** The first three PM conversations
should map the actual path before quoting anything.

| Role | Who they usually are | What they care about | What they can kill |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Economic buyer** | Owner/principal of the PM company | Cost per unit, whether it wins owner contracts, whether it is a distraction | Everything |
| **Champion / operational user** | Marketing lead, ops manager, or the principal wearing a second hat | Not having to make the content themselves; something that looks consistent | Nothing — but without them nothing starts |
| **Marketing approver** | Sometimes the same as the champion; in larger PMs a separate brand owner | Brand consistency: fonts, colours, tone, logo placement | Creative direction |
| **Procurement / finance** | Usually absent below ~50 units; a bookkeeper above | Invoicing, payment terms, W-9, insurance | Timing |
| **Property owner** | The individual who owns a unit the PM manages | How *their* property is portrayed; whether they were asked | An individual property, and the PM's appetite to continue |

### The one most people miss

**The property owner.** A PM markets property they do not own. An owner who sees an AI-assisted
video of their house without being asked can generate a complaint that costs the PM far more
than the video cost. This is both a risk and a sales opportunity:

- **Risk control:** the PM must confirm, in writing at intake, that they have authority to
  market each property this way and to use its photography
  ([38](38-rights-and-security.md)).
- **Sales angle:** many PMs are actively trying to *win* owner contracts. "Here is the marketing
  we produce for every property we manage" is a competitive asset in an owner pitch. E-30 notes
  20–30% of a VR company's marketing budget goes to homeowner recruitment — **that is a
  different budget line from guest acquisition, and it may be the easier one to reach.**

This owner-acquisition angle is tested as ad concept AD-4 ([30](30-advertising-packages.md)) and
interview question D4 ([09](09-interview-guide.md)).

## 3. The pilot

Never quote a portfolio before a pilot. The pilot exists as much to measure **our** delivery cost
as to prove the product to them.

### Pilot design

| Element | Spec |
|---|---|
| Size | **3 properties**, at full Standard price ($279 each = $837) |
| Why 3 | Enough to reveal consistency problems; small enough to sit inside a typical champion's spend authority; large enough for us to measure per-unit time |
| Property selection | **One easy, one they struggle to fill, one with a demanding owner.** We ask for this mix explicitly |
| Price | **Full price. No pilot discount.** A discounted pilot proves people will take free things |
| Timeline | 10 business days for all three |
| Approver | **One named person** with sign-off authority, agreed before starting |
| What we measure internally | Per-unit hours, template reuse ratio, revision count, approval round-trips |

### Pilot success criteria — agreed in writing before it starts

| # | Criterion | Measured by | Who judges |
|---|---|---|---|
| PS-1 | All 3 delivered on or before the agreed date | Date | Both |
| PS-2 | All files match the agreed spec | Checklist | Both |
| PS-3 | Zero accuracy defects | [26](26-property-accuracy-rules.md) review | Both |
| PS-4 | Named approver signs off on all 3 within 5 business days | Sign-off | Them |
| PS-5 | The demanding owner's property is approved by that owner | Their confirmation | Them |
| PS-6 | **Our** per-unit hours ≤ 2.0h by property 3 | Our time log | **Us only** |
| PS-7 | Revisions ≤ 1 round per property | Count | Us |

**PS-6 is ours, not theirs, and it is the one that decides the rollout price.** If per-unit time
does not fall below 2.0 hours by the third property, template reuse is not working and
**no volume discount may be offered** ([15](15-financial-model.md) §6). We would quote the
portfolio at or near full Standard price, and if they decline, that is the correct outcome.

## 4. Portfolio rollout

Offered only after the pilot meets PS-1 to PS-5 **and** PS-6 determines the price.

| Portfolio size | Price/property **if PS-6 met** | Price/property **if PS-6 missed** |
|---|---|---|
| 5–9 | $229 | $279 (no discount) |
| 10–19 | $199 | $259 |
| 20+ | $179, quoted individually | $249, quoted individually |

All figures are hypotheses (A-07). **The right-hand column is not a negotiating position — it is
what we charge when the economics say so.**

### Rollout mechanics

| Element | Rule |
|---|---|
| **Brand consistency** | One template agreed at pilot: fonts, colour, caption style, end frame, music family. Template changes after sign-off are billable (category 3, [14](14-offer-specification.md) §4) |
| **Volume/schedule** | Default 5 properties per 10 business days ([37](37-capacity-and-contractors.md)). Never promise a rate that consumes all capacity — a second customer must still be servable |
| **Asset handoff** | One shared folder, one subfolder per property, one Property Fact Sheet per property. **We do not start a property until its fact sheet is signed** |
| **Approval ownership** | One named approver. Not a committee, not "whoever's around." If they insist on per-owner approval, that is their process to run, and it changes the schedule, not our price |
| **Billing** | ≤$500 fully upfront; above $500, 50% deposit / 50% on delivery ([15](15-financial-model.md) §9). Batch invoicing per 5 properties for larger rollouts |
| **Expansion** | New units onboarded at the agreed per-property rate, no re-negotiation, for 12 months |

### What we will not do for a portfolio customer

- Reduce the accuracy review to save time. It is the same review at property 40 as at property 1.
- Accept "use the same fact sheet for all units." Every property gets its own.
- Offer exclusivity in a market.
- Take on a rollout that would push another customer's delivery past its promised date.

## 5. Proposal template — multi-property

> **Video production — [Company name]**
> **Pilot: 3 properties**
>
> **What this is**
> Short marketing videos for three of your properties, made from photography you already hold.
> Delivered in the formats you publish to. No site visits.
>
> **Properties in this pilot**
> 1. [Property A] — [one line: which guest this one is for]
> 2. [Property B] — [one line]
> 3. [Property C] — [one line]
>
> **What you receive, per property**
> - 2 × vertical 9:16 (15–25s), captions burned in — Instagram, TikTok
> - 1 × square 1:1 (20–30s) — feed
> - 1 × wide 16:9 (20–30s) — your website
> - 1 × silent loop (8–12s) — website header
> - 1 × Vrbo cut (45–60s) — no added music, no on-screen contact details, per Vrbo's published
>   video guidelines
> - 1 × Google Business Profile cut (≤30s, under 75MB)
> - Caption file (.srt) and a one-page guide on where each file goes
>
> **What we need from you**
> - 20+ photos per property, 1500px or larger
> - A completed Property Fact Sheet per property (8 questions, ~5 minutes each)
> - Written confirmation that you hold or control the rights to the photography, and have
>   authority to market these properties
> - One named person with approval authority
>
> **Timeline**
> 10 business days from receipt of complete assets for all three.
>
> **Price**
> $279 per property. $837 total. Payment in full before production begins.
>
> **Revisions**
> One consolidated round per property, within 7 days of delivery. Changes of creative direction
> or new source material are quoted separately — the categories are set out in our scope
> document and we will always tell you which one applies before doing the work.
>
> **What we do not do**
> - We make no claims about bookings, occupancy or revenue. There is no credible evidence that
>   property video causes bookings, and we will not pretend otherwise.
> - We do not show rooms, views, amenities or features the property does not have. If a property
>   fact and a photograph disagree, we stop and ask.
> - We do not post or manage your accounts.
> - Airbnb does not accept host-uploaded video on listing galleries. Nothing here is for that
>   placement.
>
> **After the pilot**
> If the three work and your approver signs off, we will quote the remaining portfolio. We will
> not quote volume pricing before the pilot, because we would be guessing at our own cost of
> delivery and you would end up with a number neither of us could rely on.
>
> **Accepted by:** ______________________  **Date:** __________

## 6. Acceptance criteria for this motion

After 3 PM conversations:
- [ ] Approval path mapped in every one, before any price was quoted (A-06)
- [ ] Zero volume discounts quoted before a pilot was delivered and timed
- [ ] Per-unit hours recorded for every pilot property
- [ ] Owner-authority confirmation obtained in writing for every property
- [ ] At least one PM asked about the owner-acquisition use case, unprompted or prompted
      (tests the D4 hypothesis)
