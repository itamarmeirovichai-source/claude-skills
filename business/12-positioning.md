## 1. Positioning options considered

Each evaluated against seven criteria, scored 1–5.

| # | Option | Customer<br>relevance | Credibility<br>at launch | Differen-<br>tiation | Production<br>feasibility | Price<br>tolerance | Repeat-<br>ability | Margin | **Total /35** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| P1 | "AI video for your Airbnb listing" | 4 | 1 | 1 | 5 | 2 | 3 | 4 | **20** |
| P2 | "Cinematic property films, without the film crew" | 4 | 2 | 3 | 2 | 4 | 3 | 3 | **21** |
| P3 | "Cheapest property video online" | 3 | 4 | 1 | 4 | 1 | 2 | 1 | **16** |
| **P4** | **"Video your photos already contain — in every format you actually publish, checked against the platform rules"** | 4 | 5 | 5 | 5 | 4 | 4 | 5 | **⭐ 32** |
| P5 | "Direct booking content system for property managers" | 5 | 2 | 4 | 3 | 5 | 5 | 4 | **28** |
| P6 | "The accuracy-first property video studio" | 3 | 5 | 5 | 5 | 3 | 4 | 5 | **30** |

### Why P1 loses
It is the highest-search-volume position and it is **built on a false premise**. Airbnb does not
display host video (E-01). Selling it generates refunds, bad reviews, and a customer who feels
lied to. Credibility scores 1 because the first competent buyer who checks will discover it.

### Why P2 loses
"Cinematic" sets an expectation that image-to-video from stills will struggle to meet on
interiors (A-03), and it invites direct comparison to the exact alternative that beats us on
realism (videographers, [11](11-competitor-analysis.md) §B). Promising the thing you are weakest
at is a reliable way to lose.

### Why P3 loses
Price leadership against DIY tools at $10/month (E-32) is unwinnable — they have zero marginal
cost and we have a human in the loop. Margin scores 1 and repeatability scores 2 because a
price-led position attracts the customers most likely to consume revision hours.

### Why P5 was close, and what we took from it
P5 scores highest on relevance, price tolerance and repeatability, and it targets the strongest
evidence we have (E-28, E-29). But "content system" implies ongoing delivery we cannot yet
staff (A-15, [37](37-capacity-and-contractors.md)) and repeat demand we have not validated
(A-05, D-10). **P5 is the 90-day position, not the launch position.** We adopt its *language*
(direct bookings, portfolio) inside P4's structure.

### Why P6 was close, and what we took from it
P6 is the most defensible and the most differentiated, and it is the weakest *opener*. Accuracy
is a relief, not a desire — it answers a fear the buyer may not feel yet (A-02 is explicitly
uncertain on this). **Accuracy is our second sentence, not our first.**

### Chosen: P4, with P6 as the reason to believe
P4 leads with the concrete outcome (usable files, every format), which is relevant and
immediately credible with zero track record, then earns trust with the accuracy discipline.
**This ordering is itself a hypothesis** and it is tested directly by ad concepts AD-1 vs AD-3
in [30](30-advertising-packages.md).

## 2. The answer to the hardest question

> **"Why should I pay you instead of using an AI tool myself?"**

This question will be asked in the first five minutes of most conversations. Our answer:

> Honestly? If you've got two free hours, decent taste, and thirty good photos, you can get a
> reasonable result yourself with a $20-a-month tool. Plenty of people should.
>
> What you're paying me for is four things. **The two hours.** **The choice of which twelve of
> your forty photos go in and in what order** — that's the part the tool can't do, and it's most
> of why one property video works and another doesn't. **An accuracy check** against what the
> platforms actually allow, because Airbnb can make you pull media that adds something the
> property doesn't have. And **seven finished files** in the shapes you actually publish —
> including one that meets Vrbo's rules, which means no music and no text overlay with your
> phone number on it, because those get rejected.
>
> If what you need is one square video for Instagram and you enjoy editing, do it yourself. I'd
> rather tell you that than take your money.

**Why this works:** it concedes the strongest point of the objection immediately, which makes
everything after it credible; it disqualifies honestly, which is a filter we want; and every
claim in it is verifiable and permitted by [21](21-claim-register.md). It contains no booking
promise, no invented statistic, and no manufactured urgency.

**Why the alternatives fail:** "our quality is better" is unprovable with no portfolio and
invites a demo-reel comparison we may lose. "AI-powered workflow" is what the tool is. "We're
experts" is an assertion, not a reason.

## 3. The positioning statements

### One sentence (site hero, ad copy, bio)

> **We turn the photos you already have into short property videos — in every format you
> actually publish, checked against the platform rules before you post them.**

### Short description (50 words — directory listings, social bios, email signature)

> Short marketing videos for vacation rentals, made from the photos you already own. No site
> visit, no scheduling around guests. You get files sized for Instagram, your website, Google,
> and a music-free cut that meets Vrbo's video rules. Three to five business days.

### Detailed description (150 words — about page, proposals)

> Most vacation rental operators are sitting on forty good photographs and publishing none of
> them as video, because shooting one means booking a videographer around guest turnover, and
> editing one yourself means losing a day.
>
> We work from the photos you already have. You send us your images and a short fact sheet about
> the property. We build a set of short videos — usually seven — sized for the places you
> actually publish: Instagram and TikTok, your booking site, Google Business Profile, and a
> music-free cut that meets Vrbo's video guidelines.
>
> Before anything is delivered, every video goes through an accuracy review. We check that it
> shows rooms, views, amenities and layout as they really are. Airbnb can require hosts to
> remove listing media that has been altered to add features a property doesn't have, and we
> won't be the reason that happens to you.
>
> Three to five business days. One revision round included. We make no claims about bookings —
> nobody can honestly make those.

### Reasons to believe (all currently verifiable)

| # | Reason | Backed by |
|---|---|---|
| RTB-1 | We work from your existing photos — no site visit, no scheduling around guests | Process fact |
| RTB-2 | You get files in every publishing format, not one you have to re-crop | Deliverable spec, [14](14-offer-specification.md) |
| RTB-3 | We ship a Vrbo-compliant cut: no added music, no on-screen contact details, under 60s | E-03–E-07 |
| RTB-4 | Every video is human-reviewed for property accuracy before delivery, against a published checklist | [26](26-property-accuracy-rules.md) — checklist is public |
| RTB-5 | Fixed price, stated before you pay, with the scope written down | [14](14-offer-specification.md), [15](15-financial-model.md) |
| RTB-6 | We tell you what we cannot do, including that we make no booking claims | D-05 |
| RTB-7 | Licensed music with client rights; we never use editor-stock music that carries no commercial grant | E-18, E-24 |

**Note RTB-4 and RTB-7 are unusual moves:** publishing our accuracy checklist and naming a
licensing trap competitors fall into. Both convert internal discipline into visible proof, which
is exactly what a business with no track record needs.

## 4. Claims permitted at launch vs. claims requiring future evidence

Authoritative version: [21](21-claim-register.md). Summary:

| ✅ Permitted now | ❌ Requires evidence we do not have |
|---|---|
| "Files sized for Instagram, your site, Google, and Vrbo" | "More bookings" / "higher occupancy" / "more revenue" |
| "Three to five business days" (once measured — currently a commitment we must be able to keep) | "Proven to increase enquiries" |
| "No site visit required" | "Trusted by hundreds of hosts" |
| "Human accuracy review before delivery" | "Better than a videographer" |
| "Meets Vrbo's published video guidelines" *(after V-0 verification)* | "Ranked higher on Airbnb" |
| "Licensed music cleared for client use" *(after reading the full licence)* | Any testimonial, case study or number we have not collected |
| "Made with AI assistance and human review" | "Indistinguishable from a real shoot" |

## 5. Positioning risks

| Risk | Consequence | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| P4 is a *feature* position; features get copied | A specialist ships multi-format export and we lose gap 2 | Gap 3 (accuracy) is harder to copy because it requires accepting creative limits. Deepen it |
| "Accuracy" lands as boring or irrelevant | The second sentence does no work | A-02 is explicitly under test. Interview C1–C3 and ad test AD-1 vs AD-3 resolve it within 30 days |
| Refusing booking claims makes us look weaker than competitors who promise them | Lost deals to bolder competitors | Accept it. Make the refusal itself a credibility signal — it is the most memorable line in the pitch |
| "From your photos" reads as "slideshow" | Perceived as low-value | Show, do not tell. The portfolio must open with motion that is obviously not a slideshow |
