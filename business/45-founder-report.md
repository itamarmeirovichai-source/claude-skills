## What we know

**Three things are established well enough to build on:**

1. **Airbnb does not accept host-uploaded video on listing galleries** (E-01). This is the most
   consequential finding in the project. It kills the obvious version of this business — "AI
   videos for your Airbnb listing" — which is the version most competitors are selling. Video for
   a rental is real, but it lives on channels the operator controls: Instagram, their own booking
   site, Vrbo, Google Business Profile, email.

2. **The platforms have rules, and they are specific enough to build a product around.** Vrbo
   accepts video but rejects added music and on-screen contact details, wants 15s–2min, and caps
   area footage at about 20% (E-03–E-06). Airbnb will require removal of listing media altered to
   add amenities a property doesn't have, escalating to suspension and refunds from host payouts
   (E-11). Most competitors' default output — one cinematic file with a music bed — is ineligible
   for Vrbo and carries accuracy risk on Airbnb. **That gap is the product.**

3. **There is a buyer worth having.** 70% of operators have a direct booking site, and 62% of
   those get under 25% of bookings through it (E-28). That is a population that has already spent
   money on a channel and is dissatisfied with it. They have somewhere to put video and a live
   reason to want it.

**And one thing we know that most vendors in this space pretend not to:** there is **no credible
public evidence that property video causes bookings** (E-20). We looked. What exists is vendors
quoting their own marketing.

## What remains uncertain

| Uncertainty | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| **Will anyone pay for this at all?** (A-01) | The weakest assumption in the system. No evidence exists either way. Everything else assumes it |
| **Can we actually make it?** (A-03) | Zero videos produced. Interiors are the worst case for generative motion — architectural geometry warps and people notice instantly |
| **How long does an order really take?** (A-10) | **The dominant cost variable.** At 2.5h it's a $100/hour business. At 5h it's a $51/hour business, which is not worth doing |
| Does anyone feel the accuracy risk? (A-02) | Our second-strongest message may be solving a problem buyers have never encountered |
| Is there repeat demand? (A-05) | Determines whether this is a business or a sequence of transactions |
| **Nine platform claims are unverified** | The egress proxy blocked airbnb.com and help.vrbo.com. Everything above is graded B, not A |

## Who to target first

**US operators running 3 to 25 vacation rental units**, who already have a direct booking site or
an active social account, hold decent photography, and are within ten weeks of a high season or
onboarding new units. Small property managers and serious multi-property owners.

**Decline:** single-property Airbnb-only hosts with no site and no social. They are the largest,
cheapest-to-reach group and they have **nowhere to put the product** (E-01). That is the trap in
this market, and it will be tempting every single month.

Also decline: anyone wanting a feature shown that doesn't exist, anyone wanting a booking
guarantee, anyone with fewer than 15 usable photos, and any first order with a deadline under 48
hours.

## What to sell first

**One package. $279.** A single property, two creative concepts, seven files:

two vertical social cuts · one square · one web hero · one silent 8–12s loop · **one music-free
Vrbo-compliant cut** · one Google Business Profile cut (under 30s, under 75MB).

Three to five business days. One revision round. A human accuracy review against a published
checklist. No booking claims.

Starter at $149 exists as a de-risking option, mentioned but not advertised. Portfolio pricing is
quoted, never listed, and **never before a real multi-property job has been delivered and timed.**

## What requires real customer evidence

Nothing below can be settled by more research:

- **Whether anyone buys.** Five paid orders from strangers, at full price, in 30 days.
- **Whether $279 is right.** Only a price test with real buyers.
- **Whether accuracy is a message or just a discipline.** AD-1 vs AD-3, and asking twelve
  operators whether they have ever had listing media rejected.
- **Whether repeat demand exists.** Count unprompted repeats at 90 days. Not prompted ones —
  those measure our marketing, not their need.
- **Whether customers actually publish what we deliver.** The quiet failure: a customer who
  receives seven files and posts none got nothing, and would never complain.

## The one-paragraph summary

The obvious version of this business is broken at the premise, because the platform most of its
customers use cannot display its product. The version that works sells files for the channels an
operator actually controls, and treats platform accuracy rules as the product specification
rather than the fine print. The economics look good — roughly $253 contribution and $100 an hour
on a $279 order — but that rests almost entirely on one unmeasured number, the time an order
takes, and every component of the model can shift a little without breaking while the combination
of those shifts loses money on every order. Nobody has ever paid us anything, and no amount of
further research will change that. **Twenty hours and about $150 will tell us whether we can make
the thing. Thirty days and five strangers will tell us whether anyone wants it. Everything else
in these forty-six documents is downstream of those two answers.**
