# 07 — Customer Research

## Status of this document

**This is desk research. No customer has been contacted.** Everything below is either
(a) evidence from [02](02-evidence-ledger.md), (b) structural reasoning from that evidence, or
(c) a hypothesis to be tested by [09](09-interview-guide.md).

**Language note:** the "customer language" section below is written as *hypothesised* phrasing,
constructed from the structure of the problem and from publicly visible host-community
discussion themes. **It contains no fabricated quotations.** Where a real quotation would
normally be placed, there is an explicit gap marked `[TO COLLECT]`. Inventing host quotes to
make this document feel researched would corrupt every downstream decision that reads it.

## 1. Segment-by-segment analysis

### S1 — Individual host, one unit, Airbnb-only

| Dimension | Finding |
|---|---|
| Buying trigger | New listing launch; a slow season; seeing a competitor's polished listing |
| Budget ownership | Personal money, own pocket, no separation from household budget |
| Approval | Self, but often a spouse consulted at >$200 |
| Current alternative | Phone photos; a free Canva slideshow; nothing |
| Urgency | Low. Video is a "should" not a "must" |
| Seasonality | Sharp — a single high season |
| Asset availability | Mixed; often 15–30 phone photos, variable quality |
| Operational constraints | Time-rich or time-poor by temperament, not by structure |
| Perceived risk | Wasting money on something they can't measure |
| Repeat frequency | Once a year at best (A-05) |
| Acquisition difficulty | **Low** — huge population (E-27: 5M+ hosts), cheap to reach |
| Delivery difficulty | **High** — poor assets, high hand-holding, high revision appetite |
| Contribution margin | **Poor.** Low price tolerance meets high service cost |
| **Verdict** | **Decline at launch.** The classic trap: easiest to reach, worst to serve |

The structural problem: this host's dominant channel is Airbnb, which cannot display video
(E-01). They have nowhere to put the deliverable. Selling to them requires first convincing them
to build a social presence — that is a different, much longer sale.

### S2 — Owner of 2–5 units, some direct booking ambition

| Dimension | Finding |
|---|---|
| Buying trigger | Launching a direct site; adding a unit; a bad season prompting "I need to diversify off Airbnb" |
| Budget ownership | Treats the portfolio as a business; has a rough annual marketing number |
| Approval | Self, fast |
| Current alternative | A photographer they already use; DIY; a VA |
| Urgency | Moderate, rising when a new unit comes online |
| Seasonality | Pronounced; 6–10 week pre-season window (A-14) |
| Asset availability | **Good** — usually has professional photos per unit |
| Constraints | Does everything themselves; responds in evenings |
| Perceived risk | Quality embarrassment; wasted time managing a vendor |
| Repeat frequency | Plausibly per new unit + annual refresh |
| Acquisition difficulty | Moderate |
| Delivery difficulty | **Low** — good assets, clear briefs |
| Contribution margin | **Good** |
| **Verdict** | **Core ICP** |

### S3 — Professional property manager, 6–25 units

| Dimension | Finding |
|---|---|
| Buying trigger | **Owner acquisition pitch** (they need to show prospective owners a marketing capability); onboarding a batch of new units; a direct-booking push |
| Budget ownership | Real marketing line. E-30 suggests 2–5% of gross bookings as a benchmark; E-30 also notes 20–30% of marketing budget goes to *homeowner recruitment* |
| Approval | Owner/principal, or a marketing lead with sub-$500 authority (A-06) |
| Current alternative | In-house junior with a phone; a local videographer; an agency retainer; nothing |
| Urgency | Moderate–high, driven by onboarding calendar |
| Seasonality | Spread across a portfolio — **smoother demand than a single owner** |
| Asset availability | **Good and standardised** — they usually control photography |
| Constraints | Brand consistency matters; may need owner consent per property (A-06) |
| Perceived risk | Inconsistent output across a portfolio; vendor unreliability |
| Repeat frequency | **Best in class** — continuous onboarding creates genuine recurring need |
| Acquisition difficulty | Moderate–high |
| Delivery difficulty | Moderate — volume, brand rules, approvals |
| Contribution margin | **Best** — template reuse across a portfolio |
| **Verdict** | **Core ICP, and the highest-value half of it** |

The under-appreciated insight: a PM's strongest motivation may not be guest bookings at all. It
is **winning owner contracts**. "Here is the marketing we do for your property" is a sales asset
in their owner pitch. That is a direct, attributable, near-term value we can honestly speak to,
unlike booking lift. **This should be tested as a primary message angle** ([30](30-advertising-packages.md), AD-4).

### S4 — Luxury / high-ADR operator

Best margin per job, highest quality bar, longest trust cycle, referral-gated, and needs a
portfolio we do not have. **Verdict: decline at launch, revisit at 90 days once we have 15
delivered jobs.** Approaching them with no work is a wasted first impression in a small,
gossipy market.

### S5 — Boutique hotel / small inn (5–30 rooms)

Has a real marketing budget and a direct booking site, but buys through a hospitality agency and
expects brand-standard work, hotel-specific assets (rooms *and* F&B *and* amenity *and* location),
and a different sales cycle. **Verdict: decline at launch.** Adjacent, not same.

### S6 — New listing, no track record

A genuine trigger moment across S1–S3 rather than a segment. Treat as a **buying trigger**: the
launch of a unit is the single clearest moment of need. Targeting should key on this event.

### S7 — Established property with weak creative assets

Also a trigger, not a segment. Harder: no deadline forces action. Needs an external prompt
(a bad season, a competitor, a refurbishment).

### S8 — Operator building a direct booking channel

**The strongest cross-cutting trigger we have evidence for.** E-28: 70% have a direct site; 62%
of them get under 25% of bookings direct; 18% get none. This is a large, identifiable population
that has already spent money on a channel and is dissatisfied with its return. They have both the
*place to put video* and the *live frustration*. Every targeting decision should over-weight this.

## 3. Customer language framework

**Method note:** organised into the standard categories, with hypothesised phrasing marked `[H]`
and explicit collection gaps marked `[TO COLLECT]`. **Nothing here is a quotation from a real
person.** [09](09-interview-guide.md) is the instrument that fills these gaps.

### Desired outcomes `[H]`
- "Something I can post that doesn't look like I made it on my phone."
- "More bookings that don't come through Airbnb."
- "Stop paying 15% to a platform for guests I could reach myself."
- "Look as professional as the big management company down the road."
- "Have something to show owners when I pitch for their property." (S3 — see above)

### Current frustrations `[H]`
- "I know I should post but I have nothing to post."
- "Shooting it myself is a whole day, and then editing is another day."
- "My photos are fine but they're static and everyone's photos look the same."
- "I paid for a photographer and got 40 stills and no idea what to do with them."

### Past disappointments `[H]`
- "I hired someone on Fiverr and got a slideshow with a Ken Burns effect."
- "The videographer quoted $900 and wanted to schedule around guest turnover."
- "I tried an AI tool and everything looked melted."

The third one matters most. **If prospects have already tried a generative tool and been
disappointed by artifacts, "AI video" as a headline is a liability, not an asset.** This is a
specific, testable thing to ask about (interview Q-B4) and it could invert our ad messaging.

### Objections — full handling in [17](17-objection-library.md)
Price; "I'll use the AI tool myself"; "I already have a photographer"; "how do I know it
works"; "AI looks fake"; "I don't have good photos"; "no time to gather assets"; "need to ask my
partner/owner"; "can you do one free first"; "can you do it by Friday"; "will it guarantee
bookings"; "what if I don't like it."

### Decision criteria `[H]`
Does it look real. Does it look better than what I'd do myself. Is it fast. Is the price a
number I can decide alone. Do I have to do much work. Can I get my money back.

### Reasons to act now
Season approaching (A-14). New unit going live. Refurbishment finished. An owner pitch next
month. A competitor's listing looking better.

### Reasons to delay
Off-season ("I'll do it before summer"). Waiting on new photography. Waiting on a
refurbishment. No one internally owns marketing. Not convinced video does anything (the honest
one — and D-05 means we cannot argue them out of it with numbers we do not have).

## 4. What we still do not know, that matters most

1. **Whether this purchase exists at all as a standalone line item** (A-01). Everything else is
   secondary to this.
2. **Whether hosts have ever encountered platform enforcement on listing media** (A-02). If the
   answer is "never heard of it," our accuracy positioning is solving a problem they do not feel.
3. **What they last paid for a marketing asset, and what triggered it** — the single most
   informative question in the interview guide, because it is about behaviour, not intention.
4. **Whether prior bad experience with AI tools makes "AI" a negative word** in this segment.
5. **Whether the PM owner-acquisition angle (S3) beats the guest-booking angle.** This is cheap
   to test and could reframe the entire pitch.
