# 17 — Objection Library

## How to use this

An objection is **information about a mismatch**, not resistance to be overcome. The first move
is always a diagnostic question, because the same sentence ("it's too expensive") has four
different causes requiring four different responses — and three of them are not about price.

**Rules:**
- Diagnose before responding. One question, then listen.
- Never discount to close ([15](15-financial-model.md)).
- Never invent evidence, results, or urgency.
- **A second clear no ends the conversation.** Thank them and stop.
- Distinguish an **objection** (they want it but something blocks them) from **genuine
  incompatibility** (we are wrong for them). The second deserves an honest decline, not a
  rebuttal.

---

### OBJ-1 — "It's too expensive"

**Possible causes:** (a) no budget at all; (b) budget exists but value is unclear; (c) anchored
to a cheap Fiverr quote; (d) it is not the price, it is uncertainty about the outcome; (e) they
need to justify it to someone else.

**Diagnostic:** *"Compared to what — is it more than you'd budgeted, or is it that you're not
sure what you'd get for it?"*

**Short:** "Fair. What were you expecting to spend?"

**Detailed:** "A local videographer for a basic walkthrough is usually $150 to $500, and more
with travel, and they need the property empty. This is $279 for seven finished files, from
photos you already have, with no scheduling. If that's still more than it's worth to you right
now, that's a completely reasonable answer — I'd rather you keep the money than buy something
you're unsure about."

**Evidence:** E-31 (public videographer rates). **Never** a fabricated ROI.
**Next step:** if (b) → show the closest-match illustrative example. If (c) → OBJ-8. If (a) →
Starter at $149, or decline warmly.
**Stop selling when:** they say a number below $149. That is our floor
([15](15-financial-model.md)) and below it the work loses money.

---

### OBJ-2 — "I'll just use an AI tool myself"

**Possible causes:** (a) genuinely will, and should; (b) testing whether we can justify
ourselves; (c) they tried one and it was hard, and are hoping we say why ours is different.

**Diagnostic:** *"Have you tried one yet? How did it go?"* — this single question separates all
three causes.

**Short:** "Honestly, you might be right. What are you planning to use?"

**Detailed:** the answer in [12](12-positioning.md) §2, in full. Concede the point, then name
the four things: the two hours, the editorial choice of which twelve of forty photos and in what
order, the accuracy check against platform rules, and seven files in the right shapes rather
than one you re-crop.

**Evidence:** E-21 (free tiers prohibit commercial use — a genuinely useful thing to tell them
regardless of whether they buy); E-24 (editor-stock music carries no commercial grant);
E-03–E-05 (Vrbo's music and overlay rules, which a DIY export will violate).
**Next step:** if they tried and hit artifacts, show a clip and ask what went wrong for them.
**Stop selling when:** they have the time, the taste, and the inclination. **Tell them to do it
themselves.** That answer earns more referrals than a won deal.

### OBJ-3 — "I already have a photographer"

**Possible causes:** (a) loyalty; (b) assumes the photographer does video; (c) doesn't want to
manage two vendors.

**Diagnostic:** *"Does the photographer do video too?"*

**Short:** "Good — keep them. I work from photos, so a good photographer makes my job better."

**Detailed:** "We're not competing. Your photographer produces the source material. If they
already do video, use them — they'll get real footage of the actual space, which I can't. If
they don't, send me their photos and I'll turn them into the video formats. One thing worth
checking: many photography licences cover listing use only, so ask them in writing whether you
can make derivative video from the images."

**Evidence:** A-08, [38](38-rights-and-security.md).
**Next step:** offer the partnership route — we would rather have the photographer as a channel
([33](33-partnership-plan.md)).
**Stop selling when:** the photographer does video well and the customer is happy. **Genuine
incompatibility.** Say so.

---

### OBJ-4 — "How do I know this actually works?"

**Possible causes:** (a) wants outcome proof; (b) wants quality proof; (c) general skepticism
about AI.

**Diagnostic:** *"Do you mean whether the videos come out well, or whether video gets you
bookings? They're different questions and I have different answers."*

**Short (if quality):** "Here's the closest example to your property. Judge the output."

**Detailed (if outcome):** "I can't tell you it gets you bookings. Nobody can honestly — there's
no credible study showing property video causes bookings, and anyone quoting you a percentage is
quoting their own marketing. What I can promise is specific: seven files, in these formats, by
this date, accuracy-checked, one revision round, and a refund position if I miss the spec. If
you need a booking guarantee, don't buy this from anyone."

**Evidence:** E-20. This is where **refusing to claim becomes the claim.**
**Next step:** Starter at $149 as the low-stakes test.
**Stop selling when:** they require a performance guarantee. **Genuine incompatibility** (D-05).

### OBJ-5 — "AI video looks fake"

**Possible causes:** (a) saw bad output; (b) tried it themselves; (c) principled objection to AI.

**Diagnostic:** *"Have you seen something specific that put you off?"*

**Short:** "Often it does. That's mostly what people do with it, not the tool."

**Detailed:** "The failure you're describing is usually generative video inventing content —
furniture morphing, doorways dissolving, straight lines bending. Interiors are the worst case
for it, because people notice architectural geometry instantly. So the rule here is that motion
comes from your actual photographs and nothing gets invented. Anything with warping geometry or
an object that changes between frames is rejected before you see it — that's a published
checklist, not a promise. Look at this clip and tell me if it's the thing you're worried about."

**Evidence:** [27](27-quality-rubric.md) hard-reject criteria, published.
**Next step:** show, don't argue.
**Stop selling when:** they object to AI on principle. **Genuine incompatibility.** "That's a
fair position. A local videographer is what you want — happy to suggest what to ask them for."

### OBJ-6 — "I don't have good photos"

**Possible causes:** (a) genuinely doesn't; (b) underestimates what they have; (c) fishing for
us to fix it.

**Diagnostic:** *"How many do you have, and were they taken on a phone or by a photographer?"*

**Short:** "Send me five and I'll tell you honestly."

**Detailed:** "I need about 15 usable images at 1500 pixels or more. Below that, I'd be inventing
rooms rather than showing them, and that's exactly what I won't do. If you're short, the better
spend is a half-day with a local photographer first — genuinely, that's money better spent than
giving it to me."

**Next step:** photo assessment, free, 10 minutes. Or a photographer referral
([33](33-partnership-plan.md)).
**Stop selling when:** assets are insufficient. **Decline.** An order built on 8 bad photos
produces a 5-hour job and a refund ([15](15-financial-model.md) §10).

---

### OBJ-7 — "I don't have time to gather everything"

**Possible causes:** (a) genuinely busy; (b) the ask sounds bigger than it is; (c) low priority.

**Diagnostic:** *"What's the part that sounds like the most work?"*

**Short:** "About twenty minutes. Photos, and an eight-question form."

**Detailed:** "Upload a folder of photos, answer eight questions about the property — beds,
capacity, what's nearby, what it's good for — and confirm the photos are yours to use. That's it.
If your photos are already in a Drive folder, share the link and we're most of the way there."

**Next step:** send the fact sheet so they can see how short it is. Showing beats describing.
**Stop selling when:** twenty minutes is too much. That is a priority answer, not a time answer,
and it usually means the trigger isn't live yet. Note the trigger date and leave.

---

### OBJ-8 — "Someone on Fiverr quoted me $40"

**Possible causes:** (a) price shopping; (b) doesn't see the difference; (c) genuinely fine with
a slideshow.

**Diagnostic:** *"Did they show you an example of what you'd get?"*

**Short:** "They might do a good job. What did their sample look like?"

**Detailed:** "At $40 you'll usually get one file — a pan-and-zoom slideshow, which is a real
thing that works fine for some properties. What you won't get is someone checking that the video
doesn't imply something your property doesn't have, or exports that meet Vrbo's rules, which
reject added music and any on-screen contact details. If the slideshow is what you want, $40 is
a good price for it and I'd take it."

**Evidence:** E-04, E-05.
**Next step:** none. Let them go.
**Stop selling when:** immediately after the honest comparison. **Do not fight on price**
(D-04, [15](15-financial-model.md)).

---

### OBJ-9 — "I need to check with my partner / the owner"

**Possible causes:** (a) true; (b) soft no; (c) needs material to forward.

**Diagnostic:** *"Sure — what will they want to know?"*

**Short:** "Want me to put it on one page you can forward?"

**Detailed:** send a one-page proposal built to be forwarded: property name, file list, price,
dates, what is excluded, and an explicit "we make no booking claims" line. **The absence of hype
is what makes it survive a skeptical second reader** — the person who wasn't in the conversation
is the one most likely to react badly to a sales document.

**Next step:** one follow-up after 4 business days. **One.**
**Stop selling when:** the second follow-up would be the third contact. Stop at two.

---

### OBJ-10 — "Can you do one free so I can see?"

**Possible causes:** (a) risk aversion; (b) testing the boundary; (c) never intended to pay.

**Diagnostic:** *"What would you need to see to feel comfortable paying for the first one?"*

**Short:** "No free production, but $149 gets you a real one."

**Detailed:** "I don't do free work — it's real production cost and the people who ask for it
mostly don't become customers, so it makes the price higher for everyone who does. What I do
instead: the $149 package is the same quality and the same accuracy review, just fewer files.
And there's an illustrative library on the site so you can judge the output before you spend
anything."

**Evidence:** D-11.
**Next step:** Starter, or the illustrative library.
**Stop selling when:** they insist. **Genuine incompatibility.** Polite decline.

---

### OBJ-11 — "I need it by Friday" (48h or less, first order)

**Possible causes:** (a) real deadline; (b) testing responsiveness; (c) no real deadline.

**Diagnostic:** *"What's happening Friday?"*

**Short:** "Not on a first order. Earliest is [date from the capacity board]."

**Detailed:** "I won't take that one. I don't know your property yet, and rushing a first order
is exactly how something ends up in the video that isn't actually in the house — that's the one
mistake I can't undo for you. If the deadline is immovable, the honest answer is I'm the wrong
choice this time. If it can move to [date], I'll take it."

**Evidence:** [37](37-capacity-and-contractors.md), [08](08-segment-selection-matrix.md).
**Next step:** offer the real date. Rush pricing (+40%) is available only on **repeat** orders
with capacity.
**Stop selling when:** the deadline is immovable. **Decline.** Turning this down is cheaper than
the accuracy incident it would produce.

---

### OBJ-12 — "Will this guarantee more bookings?"

**Short:** "No. And nobody who tells you yes can back it up."

**Detailed:** "There's no credible evidence that property video causes bookings — I looked, and
what exists is vendors quoting their own marketing. Bookings move with price, season, reviews,
and platform ranking all at once, so a video that ran alongside a good month proves nothing.
What I'll guarantee is the files, the formats, the accuracy review, and the date."

**Evidence:** E-20, D-05.
**Next step:** reframe onto what is actually deliverable.
**Stop selling when:** they need the guarantee. **Genuine incompatibility.**

**Note:** this objection is a gift. It is the moment where refusing to promise is more persuasive
than promising would be, and several buyers will remember it as the reason they chose us.

---

### OBJ-13 — "What if I don't like it?"

**Diagnostic:** *"What would 'not liking it' look like — wrong style, or wrong property?"*

**Short:** "One revision round is included. If I missed the spec, I fix it free."

**Detailed:** the refund position from [35](35-customer-operations.md), stated plainly and
without hedging. Distinguish clearly: **taste disagreement** → revision round. **Spec miss or
accuracy error** → free fix, prioritised, and a refund if we cannot fix it.

**Next step:** put it in writing in the proposal, above the payment link, not below it
([13](13-consumer-psychology-and-sales-method.md) §3).

---

### OBJ-14 — "What exactly do I get?"

Not an objection — a **buying signal disguised as one.** Answer precisely with the file list
from [14](14-offer-specification.md), then ask for the order. Vagueness here loses deals that
were already won.

---

### OBJ-15 — "Can you add the hot tub we're installing next spring?"

**Short:** "No — not until it's in and photographed."

**Detailed:** "I can't show something that isn't there yet. Airbnb can require hosts to remove
listing media that adds amenities the property doesn't have, and that can go as far as pulling
reservations and refunding from your payout. When the hot tub's installed, send photos and I'll
do an updated cut at the extra-concept price."

**Evidence:** E-11.
**Next step:** book the future work, genuinely.
**Stop selling when:** they insist. **Decline the order entirely** — this is a hard boundary
([26](26-property-accuracy-rules.md)), not a negotiation.

## Negotiation rules that protect margin

| Situation | Response | Never |
|---|---|---|
| "Can you do better on price?" | Offer a smaller package, not a smaller price | Discount to close |
| "If I buy 3, what's the deal?" | Portfolio pricing **only after per-unit cost is measured** ([15](15-financial-model.md) §6) | Quote volume pricing on the call |
| "Can you match their quote?" | "No. Here's what's different, and if that doesn't matter to you, take theirs." | Match |
| "Throw in an extra format" | Only against a commitment of value — a named case study, prepayment, or a 5-property order | Free, to close |
| "We'll pay after we see it" | 100% upfront under $500. No exceptions | Deliver unpaid |
| Repeat customer asks for a discount | Offer priority scheduling or a bundled add-on instead | Reduce the base price |

**The principle: trade scope for price, never price for a signature.** A discount given to close
teaches the customer that the price is fiction, and it selects for exactly the buyers who will
consume the most revision hours — the two failure modes the financial model says we cannot
afford at once.

## Distinguishing objection from incompatibility

| It is an **objection** when | It is **incompatibility** when |
|---|---|
| They want the outcome but something blocks them | They want something we do not do |
| The block is information, money, timing, or approval | They require a performance guarantee (OBJ-12) |
| They keep asking questions | They need something shown that does not exist (OBJ-15) |
| | They object to AI on principle (OBJ-5c) |
| | Their assets cannot support the work (OBJ-6) |
| | Their deadline is unsafe (OBJ-11) |
| **Response:** diagnose and answer | **Response:** decline clearly, recommend a real alternative, stop |

Declining well is not a lost sale — it is a referral source and the reason the next thing we say
is believed.
