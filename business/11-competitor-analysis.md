# 11 — Competitor & Alternative Analysis

## The rule that governs this document

**Visual polish is not evidence of commercial success. Advertising visibility is not evidence of
profitability. A long-running ad is not a winning ad — it may be a founder's stubbornness or an
agency's autopilot.** No private pricing, revenue, close rate, or customer count is asserted
anywhere below. Where a number is public we cite it (E-NN); where it is not, the cell says
**"not public."** Any future session tempted to fill those cells must fill them from a real
source, not an estimate.

## Research limitation

Several named competitors' sites were **blocked by the egress proxy** (`tryreelestate.com`) or
otherwise not directly readable in this environment. Their per-video pricing could not be
obtained. This is registered as verification task **V-1** in [42](42-validation-plan.md):
before launch, visit each competitor site directly and record public pricing, turnaround,
revision policy and claim language. **Do not price against competitors until that is done.**

## 1. The alternative set

A buyer with $279 and a property has nine real options. We compete with all of them, and the
most dangerous one is the last.

### A. Local video agency / production company

| | |
|---|---|
| Segment | Boutique hotels, luxury operators, PMs with 25+ units |
| Promise | Brand-quality film production |
| Deliverables | 1 hero film + social cutdowns, on-site shoot, crew |
| Public price | Not public (project-quoted). Cinematic real-estate range $500–$2,500+ (E-31) is the nearest public anchor |
| Turnaround | Weeks — scheduling a shoot around guest turnover is the real bottleneck |
| Revisions | Contractual, typically 1–2 rounds |
| Proof | Strong showreels |
| Distribution | Usually hands over files; rarely publishes |
| Recurring | Retainers where they can get them |
| Likely limitation | Cost and scheduling. **Cannot shoot an occupied property on short notice** — a structural weakness in STR, where units are booked most of the season |

### B. Freelance videographer / real-estate videographer

| | |
|---|---|
| Segment | Owners 1–10 units, agents |
| Promise | A professional walkthrough of your property |
| Deliverables | 1 video, sometimes vertical cut |
| **Public price** | **$150–$500 basic 60–90s; $300–$1,500 agent-led; drone +$225–$500; travel +$50–$150 (rural $200+); urban 20–40% premium (E-31)** |
| Turnaround | Days to weeks, plus scheduling |
| Revisions | Usually 1 |
| Proof | Portfolio, local reputation, Google reviews |
| Distribution | None |
| Recurring | Per-listing, not ongoing |
| Likely limitation | Must physically attend. **Travel fees make rural STR uneconomic.** One output format is common; the buyer then has to crop it themselves |

**This is our primary price anchor.** Note the structural asymmetry: their cost scales with
travel and property size; ours does not.

### C. Freelance editor (Fiverr / Upwork tier)

### F. DIY AI tools (the real competitor)

| | |
|---|---|
| Segment | Everyone |
| Promise | Turn your photos into a video in minutes |
| Deliverables | Self-serve output, variable |
| **Public price** | **$10–$100/month typical (E-32).** Kling ~$6.99/mo; Runway $15/$35/$95; Veo via Google One from $19.99/mo (E-21, E-23) |
| Turnaround | Minutes |
| Revisions | Unlimited, by the user, at the cost of the user's time |
| Proof | Demo reels — **note these are cherry-picked outputs, not median outputs** |
| Recurring | Subscription |
| Likely limitation | **Free tiers prohibit commercial use** (E-21 explicitly for Runway). CapCut grants no rights to its built-in music at any tier (E-24). Output is one format; the user re-crops. No accuracy review. No editorial judgment about shot order or what to omit. The user still spends 2–4 hours |

**This is the alternative we must answer honestly.** See [12](12-positioning.md) §"Why not just
do it yourself."

| Segment | STR hosts, real-estate agents |

### H. In-house staff / VA

| | |
|---|---|
| Segment | PMs with 15+ units |
| Promise | Content under our own control |
| Cost | A salary or hourly fraction — usually the cheapest-looking and most expensive-in-practice option |
| Likely limitation | Quality ceiling, turnover, competing priorities. **When a PM has this, we are selling against a sunk cost and a colleague's feelings** — a harder sale than against a vendor |

### I. Doing nothing

| | |
|---|---|
| **Segment** | **The majority** |
| Promise | Zero cost, zero risk, zero effort |
| Likely limitation | None that the buyer feels acutely, which is exactly the problem |

**"Nothing" is the market-share leader and the correct default for many operators.** Any
forecast that ignores it is fiction. Most lost deals will be lost to nothing, not to a
competitor — and the correct response to that is a better trigger (season, new unit, owner
pitch), not a better argument.

## 2. Where we actually sit

| | Videographer (B) | **Us** | DIY AI tool (F) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Price per property | $150–$1,500 (E-31) | **$149–$279** | $10–100/mo + your time |
| Site visit needed | Yes | **No** | No |
| Works on an occupied property | **No** | **Yes** | Yes |
| Rural surcharge | $50–$200+ (E-31) | **None** | None |
| Output formats | Usually 1 | **3–7** | 1 |
| Vrbo-compliant cut (music-free, no CTA) | Rarely considered | **Standard** (E-03–E-07) | No |
| Accuracy review against platform rules | No | **Yes, named** | No |
| Buyer's hands-on time | ~2h (access, walkthrough) | **~20 minutes** | 2–4h |
| Real footage of the actual property | **Yes — their advantage** | Derived from stills | Derived from stills |

**The honest weakness, stated plainly:** a videographer captures the property as it is, in
motion, with real light. We derive motion from stills. For a property with genuinely exceptional
spaces and a customer with budget and scheduling flexibility, **the videographer is the better
product and we should say so.** That admission costs us deals we would have lost anyway and buys
credibility on everything else we say.

## 3. The gap we occupy

Three structural gaps, each traceable to evidence rather than to wishful differentiation:

1. **The occupied-property gap.** STR units are booked through the season. A videographer needs
   an empty, staged property. We need photographs, which already exist. **This gap is widest
   exactly when demand is highest** — pre-season, when units are already filling.
2. **The multi-format gap.** Every alternative delivers one file. The channel map in
   [06](06-two-marketing-systems.md) shows at least five incompatible destination specs. Buyers
   currently absorb that re-cropping cost themselves, usually badly, or simply never publish.
3. **The compliance gap.** E-11 makes AI-altered listing media an enforcement target, and E-04/
   E-05 make the default "cinematic with music" output ineligible for Vrbo. **No alternative in
   the set treats this as part of the job.** This is the only gap that is hard to copy, because
   copying it means accepting creative limits that look like weaknesses in a demo reel.

## 4. What would change this analysis

| Trigger | Response |
|---|---|
| Airbnb enables gallery video for general hosts | The market expands sharply and every competitor's positioning becomes viable. Our production system is still the asset. Re-run [12](12-positioning.md) |
| A specialist (G) publishes sub-$100 per-video pricing | Our Starter tier is undercut. Move to Standard-only and compete on the compliance gap, not price |
| A DIY tool ships a genuine multi-format export with a compliance preset | Gap 2 and part of gap 3 close. We would need to move up-market into judgment and volume service |
| Vrbo permits music | D-06 differentiator weakens. Gap 3 narrows to accuracy only |
| V-1 reveals a specialist already selling accuracy review | We are not differentiated. Re-position on speed and format breadth, and re-run this document |
