# PumpWatch: Customers, obligations and first targets

*Customers & Pricing team. Research date: 2026-10-04. Each claim has a source URL next to it. Anything not confirmed from a source is marked **UNVERIFIED**. Sources older than 2 years are marked (stale).*

---

## 1. Summary

- **What regulators expect has changed.** The SEC, FINRA, Nasdaq, a House committee and DOJ now all treat social-media "ramp-and-dump" in foreign small caps as a top priority. Firms are expected to have surveillance tuned to it. A broker that misses it is fined for **supervision (Rule 3110)** and **AML/SAR (Rule 3310 + Bank Secrecy Act)** failures.
- **Best first buyers:** small and mid-size **clearing and self-clearing firms** that handle foreign omnibus or low-priced flow and have been fined recently. FINRA often requires these firms to hire independent consultants and certify fixes, so they have a deadline and a budget. **Underwriters of small-cap IPOs** are under the most pressure, but they are also the most conflicted, so they are a riskier sale.
- **The problem with this business:** brokers earn money on pump volume, and retail customers bear nearly all of the loss. Brokers will pay to avoid **regulatory exposure**, not out of concern for victims. The product has to be sold and designed as evidence for regulators: alerts that are documented, escalated and ready for a SAR.

**The underlying obligations PumpWatch maps to:**
- **FINRA Rule 3110 (Supervision).** Firms must keep a supervisory system "reasonably designed" to catch manipulative trading. Nearly every case below cites it. https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/rulebooks/finra-rules/3110
- **FINRA Rule 3310 (AML program) and the Bank Secrecy Act SAR rule, 31 CFR 1023.320.** Broker-dealers must file a SAR on suspicious transactions of **$5,000 or more**. In 2025 a firm was fined $500k for using a $25k threshold instead (https://infobytes.orrick.com/2025-08-29/finra-fines-broker-dealer-500k-for-failure-to-file-suspicious-activity-reports/). Rule text: https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/rulebooks/finra-rules/3310 ; https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-31/subtitle-B/chapter-X/part-1023/subpart-C/section-1023.320
- **FINRA Rule 5270 (front-running block transactions)** is **not** a main driver. It shows up only in the 2026 report's general rule list. **Reg SHO** is indirect: it matters only to short-locate brokers (Cobra, CenterPoint), whose clients short pumped names. We should not lead with either.
- **Social-media supervision (Rule 2210 and influencer cases).** Webull and Moomoo were fined for supervision of paid "finfluencers" (see §4). This is evidence that firms already have to monitor social media to some degree.

**What the cases show:**
- Fines against small firms are usually **$250k to $1.3M** plus a mandatory consultant. A $20k-60k/yr tool is cheap by comparison.
- The failure that keeps recurring is not "no data". It is **"no tailored surveillance and no escalation to AML/SAR"**. PumpWatch has to produce an auditable escalation trail, not just alerts.
- No case found **requires social-media monitoring by name**. The regulatory link is indirect: the 2026 report names social-media scams as a typology and expects tailored surveillance. This is an **inference**, so be careful how it is pitched.

## 5. The broker conflict of interest

- **Brokers earn on the pump.** Commissions, payment for order flow, margin interest, and underwriting fees and warrants for IPO underwriters all come from pump volume. Spotting a pump early cuts their revenue. (General industry economics, not from one source; **UNVERIFIED** as a measured effect.)
- **Who bears the loss:** almost entirely **retail buyers who buy near the top**. OST lost more than $950M of market cap in days (DOJ, above), and Park Ha lost about $1B in one session (D&O Diary, above). Brokers rarely pay restitution: Robinhood's $3.75M is the exception, and it covered other harms too.
- **When brokers are hurt themselves:**
  1. Regulatory fines plus consultant costs (§4).
  2. Clearing and margin credit losses when a pumped stock that was used as collateral collapses (**UNVERIFIED**, reasoning).
  3. ATO fraud losses that the firm reimburses (FINRA 2026 report names ATO money used to fund pump buys).
  4. Customer complaints, arbitration and class actions. Underwriters are now named defendants.
  5. Gatekeeper reputation: House letters and SEC task force.
- **What this means for sales:** pitch it as protecting the firm's **regulatory record and credit exposure**. "Saving customers" works as a secondary argument at retail apps. Expect some firms to want alerts *without* acting on them. Our product should record the firm's response to each alert. That protects honest firms, and makes conflicted ones less likely to buy, which is acceptable.
