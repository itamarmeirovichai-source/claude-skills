# PumpWatch: competitor landscape

> Market & Regulation dept, Competitors team. Written 2026-10-04.
> Method: web searches and page fetches done on 2026-10-04 by the team lead. The Agent tool was not available in this session, so no worker subagents ran; all research was done directly.
> Rules: every claim has a source URL. Anything not confirmed from a page we opened or a search result we read is marked **UNVERIFIED**. Sources older than 2 years (before Oct 2024) are marked **[stale]**.

## 0. Bottom line (read this first)

- **The wedge is PARTIALLY TAKEN.** Several parties already join public social-media signals with price data to detect pump-and-dumps, but they sell to **exchanges and regulators**, or they build it **in-house**:
  - **Stockpulse** (Germany) sells exactly this kind of social-media surveillance to exchanges, and Nasdaq's US Market Surveillance team has used it since 2019.
  - **Korea Exchange**, **SEBI** (India) and the **Hong Kong SFC** run their own AI tools that watch social media.
- **The broker-dealer / trading-app version looks open** (UNVERIFIED beyond our searches). It would combine public social and Telegram signals with market data and EDGAR filings and issue a pre-dump warning per ticker, built into the broker's own surveillance. The closest product sold to brokers is the **OTC Markets Promotion flag/data file**. It is curated by staff, refreshed twice a day, focused on paid promotion, and does not use filing-event analysis or Telegram chatter as far as public docs show.
- Big trade-surveillance vendors (Nasdaq, NICE Actimize, Eventus, Trillium, Scila, KX/OneTick, b-next) watch **orders and trades**. Communications vendors (Smarsh, Global Relay, Behavox, much of SteelEye) watch **employees' own** chats, email and social accounts. **Neither group watches public promoter chatter on Telegram or X as a core feature.** SteelEye's marketing mentions "social media sources", but gives no details.
- **Confidence: medium (about 60%).** Our main blind spot is undisclosed features inside large vendors and private deals, such as Stockpulse feeds already sitting inside a broker's surveillance stack.

## 1. Key distinction: eComms surveillance is not public-social surveillance

| Type | What it watches | Who sells it | Relevant to PumpWatch? |
|---|---|---|---|
| eComms / communications surveillance | The firm's **own employees'** email, chat, voice and **corporate social accounts** (archived for SEC/FINRA record-keeping) | Smarsh, Global Relay, Behavox, SteelEye, NICE Actimize (SURVEIL-X comms) | **No.** It does not see outside promoters on public Telegram or X. |
| Trade / market surveillance | Orders, trades and market data. Detects spoofing, layering, wash trades, and pump-and-dump **trading patterns** | Nasdaq, NICE Actimize, Eventus, Trillium, Scila, KX/OneTick, b-next, Irisium, Solidus | **Partial.** It covers our S1/S2/S6 signals but not S3/S4/S5. |
| Public social / alt-data surveillance | Public posts on X, Reddit, StockTwits, Telegram, YouTube and so on | Stockpulse, Dataminr, RavenPack, SMA/Context Analytics, Accern, social-listening tools | **Yes for S4/S5.** Usually sold as sentiment or alpha data, with Stockpulse as the exception. |

Example: Smarsh "captures, preserves, and archives business communications" on LinkedIn, Instagram, TikTok, Facebook and X. This is the firm's own accounts, for record-keeping ([smarsh.com/channels/social](https://www.smarsh.com/channels/social/)).

### 4c. Academic tools (useful for backtest design; not commercial)

- "Detecting Pump&Dump Stock Market Manipulation from Online Forums" (arXiv 2301.11403, Jan 2023). Uses the language of social posts plus price/volume patterns in small caps; 85% accuracy, F1 0.62 ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.11403)) [stale but relevant].

## 5. VERDICT

**The wedge is PARTIALLY TAKEN.** The core idea (public social signals + market data = pump-and-dump detection) exists. The specific packaging is open:
- for broker-dealers, trading apps and banks;
- including Telegram and filings (EDGAR S-1/424B, name and control changes);
- as a per-ticker **pre-dump** risk score.

**Confidence: medium (about 60%).**

Reasoning:
1. **Exchanges and regulators are taken or self-served.** Stockpulse already sells social-media pump-and-dump surveillance to Deutsche Börse, Bursa Malaysia and Nasdaq US Market Surveillance. KRX, SEBI and the HK SFC built their own tools. PumpWatch should **not** lead with exchanges or regulators.
2. **Broker-dealers have trade surveillance but not public-social signals.** Trillium, Eventus, Nasdaq Trade Surveillance and NICE Actimize cover trading patterns (S1, S2, S6). Their own messaging, such as Eventus's line that "social media posts won't prove market abuse — the data will", shows they treat social media as out of scope. The broker-facing promotion signal that does exist (OTC Markets) is curated, twice-daily and about paid promotion. Neither offers S3 + S4 + S5 fusion with a lead time.
3. **Why confidence is not higher:** (a) Stockpulse could already be resold into broker stacks without press releases. (b) SteelEye says "social media sources" without detail. (c) Nasdaq has the data partner (Stockpulse), the AI pump-and-dump pilot and 3,800 clients. Adding a social module is a product decision, not a research problem. (d) We did not open every page (Nasdaq's retail-broker page returned 503).
4. **Moat warning:** the hard asset is **labelled outcomes** (which promoted tickers actually dumped, and how many days later) and **broker distribution**, not the social scraping. Our backtest (Gate A) is also our main proof of differentiation.
