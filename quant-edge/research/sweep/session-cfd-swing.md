### C-8-01 Night–day return asymmetry in non-US indices on CFD terms: long the overnight (one swap), short the cash day (no swap)
- mechanism: close-to-open returns carry most of the equity premium and intraday returns are flat to negative in most major indices; Hendershott–Livdan–Rösch explain it as a CAPM that holds at night and fails by day (beta earns its premium overnight), so the sign is not a US artefact. For a CFD trader the forced counterparties are the cash-market participants who cannot hold overnight risk (market makers flattening, intraday funds). Survival after publication: the premium is a risk premium (gap risk over the closure), not a mispricing; what kills it on CFD terms is the swap, so the trade is only live where the overnight premium exceeds swap + spread.
- evidence: peer-reviewed — Hendershott, Livdan, Rösch (2020) "Asset pricing: A tale of night and day", JFE 138(3) 635–662, https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jfinec:v:138:y:2020:i:3:p:635-662 (abstract only, fetched 2026-11-03): beta–return relation positive overnight, negative intraday, US and international. Peer-reviewed — Lachance (2023) "Night Trading: Lower Risk but Higher Returns?", Review of Financial Economics 41(4) 347–363, https://ideas.repec.org/a/wly/revfec/v41y2023i4p347-363.html (abstract only, 2026-11-03): US stocks 1995–2014. Working paper — Knuteson (2020) arXiv:2010.01727 v1, https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.01727 (fetched 2026-11-03): cumulative overnight "wildly positive" and intraday "disturbingly negative" in major world indices, figure-level only, no numbers. Secondary: J.P. Morgan note (via search snippet) that an overnight-reversal variant worked in Nasdaq and less so Nikkei, not Europe. No post-2015 out-of-sample number for DAX/Nikkei/HSI found.
- evidence window: US stocks 1995–2014 (Lachance); US + international portfolios to ~2017 (HLR); 21 indices "past few decades" to 2020 (Knuteson figure). DAX/Nikkei/HSI 2015–2026 untouched.
- instruments: GER40, JP225, HK50, UK100, EU50, AUS200 (FTMO CFD symbols; availability of each on the Swing account to be confirmed from the platform symbol list, which did not render to a fetch)
- holding period and expected trades per year: leg A long from cash close to next cash open (~16 h, crosses one swap); leg B short from cash open to cash close (6.5–8.5 h, no swap). ~250 trades/yr per leg per index.
- data needed: 1-minute index CFD proxies from HistData (GRXEUR, JPXJPY, HKXHKD, UKXGBP, ETXEUR, AUXAUD; free, 2010–2026, gappy) for the exact cash-open/close prints; daily cash index open/close from Stooq/Yahoo (free) as the cross-check; $0 in the Databento plan.
- expected gross edge per trade and cost sensitivity: if the index earns ~6 %/yr and all of it overnight, leg A ≈ +2.4 bp/night gross; swap −2.6 bp (EU) / −2.0 bp (JP) per night plus 1.2–3 bp round-trip spread → NET ≈ −1 to −3 bp/night: FAILS the economics screen on EU indices at the assumed swaps; marginal on JP225 only if the spread is ≤ 1 bp. Leg B: day-session drift ≈ −1 to −3 bp/day gross vs 1.2–3 bp round trip → also at or below cost. 2× cost run kills both legs. Only a conditional version (e.g. leg B only after an overnight up-move > 1σ, or leg A only when the short-swap is positive) can clear the floor.
- firm structure fit: leg A FTMO Swing only (crosses the swap and, on Friday, the weekend); leg B FTMO intraday CFD (no swap); neither fits a futures firm (non-CME). Clauses: none beyond the 5 % daily loss from the 00:00 CE(S)T balance including floating P&L.
- nearest closed relative: E1 (FTMO stock-CFD overnight, single stocks); C-3-04 / C-5-03 / C-5-07 (this sweep) use adjacent session windows in Nikkei futures and European opens; H0012/H0017 (gap fade) is the opposite sign on the US open. None closes the index-level night/day split on non-US CFDs.
- decay haircut: 0.5 (published). Splits: 2015 (Nikkei night session extended to 05:30 JST, July 2016), 2021 (Xetra/Eurex post-pandemic volumes), 2022-09 (OSE holiday trading).
- plausibility of net Sharpe ≥ 1.5: low — the unconditional premium is smaller than the swap on EU indices; even the best conditional version is a 0.5–0.8 Sharpe beta-like sleeve.
- original: no (published mechanism; the CFD cost accounting is new but not a mechanism)

### C-8-02 Halloween / "Sell in May" in non-US indices as a six-month long at FTMO Swing, swap-netted
- mechanism: November–April returns exceed May–October returns in nearly every market; proposed drivers are vacation-driven risk-aversion cycles and seasonal flows. Why it may survive: it is a half-year holding pattern that no fast capital arbitrages, and the FTMO vehicle only needs the Nov–Apr long to beat swap. Why it may not: the "effect" is largely the absence of return in summer, so a long-only Nov–Apr sleeve earns roughly the unconditional premium plus half the seasonal difference.
- evidence: peer-reviewed — Zhang & Jacobsen (2021) "The Halloween indicator, 'Sell in May and Go Away': Everywhere and all the time", J. Int. Money & Finance 110, https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jimfin/v110y2021ics0261560620302242.html (abstract only, fetched 2026-11-03), 62,962 monthly observations on all indices worldwide. Working paper version (2012) https://www.bnains.org/backtest/periode/The_Halloween_Indicator_everywhere_and_all_the_time_-_Ben_Jacobsen_%26_Cherry_Y._Zhang.pdf (not opened; search snippet 2026-11-03): effect present in all 37 countries out of sample Sep 1998–Apr 2011; Japan largest, Nov–Apr minus May–Oct ≈ 8.3 %/yr (CXO summary, secondary); later update: present in 34 of 35 countries May 2007–Oct 2015. Post-2015 out-of-sample: not found.
- evidence window: global indices to 2017 (2021 paper); OOS panels 1998–2011 and 2007–2015. 2016–2026 untouched.
- instruments: JP225, GER40, UK100, HK50, EU50, AUS200 (FTMO Swing); US500 only as a control (US indices are the most-mined market).
- holding period and expected trades per year: one entry on the last trading day of October, one exit on the last trading day of April; 1 round trip per index per year, 6 indices → 6 trades/yr. Sample size for a gate is therefore ~10 observations per index over a decade: it can only be judged as a pooled panel.
- data needed: daily cash index closes 1970–2026 (Stooq/Yahoo, free) plus FTMO-style swap accrual; $0.
- expected gross edge per trade and cost sensitivity: Japan ≈ +4 % per six-month hold above the unconditional half-year return (half of 8.3 %); EU/UK ≈ +2–3 %. Swap over 6 months ≈ −2.5 % (JP225 at −4.9 %/yr) to −3.3 % (EU at −6.5 %/yr); spread negligible. Net ≈ +0.5 to +2 % per hold at ~11 % half-year vol → Sharpe per sleeve ≈ 0.3–0.6 before the haircut. Passes the per-trade economics screen (edge ≫ 2 × round trip) but fails on vol-adjusted size; 2× cost run (swap doubled) turns EU legs negative.
- firm structure fit: FTMO Swing only (six-month hold, weekends, news). Clauses: 10 % static max loss forces the sleeve to run at ≤ 8–10 % annualized vol with a hard stop; the 5 % daily loss is not binding at that size.
- nearest closed relative: H0023 (S&P turn-of-month, calendar family) and P-C29 (vendor seasonality windows); neither tested Halloween on non-US indices. A-C29 (pre-sweep) covers OPEX/pre-holiday, not this.
- decay haircut: 0.5 (published 2002, re-published 2012/2021). Split: 2002 (Bouman–Jacobsen publication), 2012.
- plausibility of net Sharpe ≥ 1.5: low — a half-exposure beta with a seasonal tilt; at best a diversifier inside a stack, never a stand-alone.
- original: no

### C-8-03 Cultural New Year window in HK50 (and other Lunar-New-Year markets on the FTMO list) at FTMO Swing
- mechanism: Bergsma–Jiang document abnormal returns in the days around cultural New Year holidays that are not January 1 (Lunar New Year in Hong Kong, China, Korea, Taiwan, Singapore, Malaysia; Diwali in India; others), attributed to holiday mood plus pre-holiday cash infusions (bonuses, red packets) and strongest in stocks favoured by individuals. The forced buyer is retail with seasonal cash; the effect is annual and concentrated in a (−4, +4) day window, which few institutions bother to trade.
- evidence: peer-reviewed — Bergsma & Jiang (2016) "Cultural New Year Holidays and Stock Returns around the World", Financial Management 45(1) 3–35, https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/finmgt/v45y2016i1p3-35.html (abstract only, fetched 2026-11-03): 11 markets, six cultural New Years; an average stock earns ≈ 2.5 % higher abnormal return in the cultural-New-Year month vs other months; window studied (−4, +4). Index-level magnitude and post-2015 out-of-sample: not seen (full text subscriber-only).
- evidence window: 11 markets, sample ending before 2016 (exact years not seen). 2016–2026 untouched.
- instruments: HK50 (FTMO); CN50/CHINA50 and SG/KR/TW indices only if present on FTMO's list (not confirmed — the symbol table did not render); JP225 is NOT a cultural-New-Year market (January 1). India's Diwali has no FTMO index CFD known to us.
- holding period and expected trades per year: long from the close 4 sessions before the Lunar New Year closure to the close 4 sessions after reopening (~9 sessions incl. the 2–3 day closure, crossing 8–9 swaps and one multi-day gap); 1 trade/yr per market. Hopeless as a stand-alone gate sample; only as one leg of a pooled "cultural holiday" panel with C-8-11.
- data needed: daily HSI 1990–2026 (Stooq/Yahoo, free), Lunar New Year dates (public), HistData HKXHKD 1m for the CFD price through the closure (free); $0.
- expected gross edge per trade and cost sensitivity: if the index effect is a third of the stock-level 2.5 %/month and concentrated in the 9-day window, ≈ +0.8 % gross per trade; swap ≈ −0.16 % (9 days at −6.5 %/yr), spread ≈ 0.03 % round trip → net ≈ +0.6 %. Passes the economics screen per trade; irrelevant for Sharpe because n ≈ 1/yr.
- firm structure fit: FTMO Swing only (multi-day closure, weekend). Clauses: holding over a closure > 2 h is Swing-only; the gap at reopening counts against the daily loss from the 00:00 CE(S)T balance.
- nearest closed relative: H0023 (turn-of-month calendar); A-C29 (pre-holiday, US). Neither covers cultural New Year or Asian indices.
- decay haircut: 0.5 (published). Split: 2016 (publication), 2020 (COVID closure anomalies).
- plausibility of net Sharpe ≥ 1.5: low — one trade a year per market; a panel of all holiday windows (C-8-11) might reach testable n, but not a target Sharpe.
- original: no (twist: CFD-held through the closure, swap-netted)

### C-13-01 ECB press-conference tone change → Euro-area index continuation (EU50, GER40) on the FTMO intraday CFD and Swing accounts
- mechanism: Schmeling–Wagner measure the tone of the ECB president's press-conference language; a more positive tone raises Euro-area equity prices on the day and the effect grows until the next press conference. They attribute it to a risk-premium channel (tone is unrelated to current or future fundamentals), i.e. the central bank moves the price of risk and slow-moving investors re-price over weeks. The ECB publishes the introductory statement and Q&A transcript on ecb.europa.eu within hours, free; the tone can be scored with a fixed dictionary without look-ahead.
- evidence: peer-reviewed — Schmeling & Wagner (2025) "Does Central Bank Tone Move Asset Prices?", JFQA 60(1) 36–67, https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:cup:jfinqa:v:60:y:2025:i:1:p:36-67_2; open-access PDF https://openaccess.city.ac.uk/id/eprint/32436/10/does-central-bank-tone-move-asset-prices.pdf (not opened; abstract and VoxEU column via search, fetched 2026-11-03): return differential positive-vs-negative tone change ≈ 60 bp on press-conference days, > 100 bp cumulated to the next press conference; 209 press conferences through 2017; one-minute data; decision 13:45 CET, press conference 14:30 CET. Post-2017 out-of-sample: not found.
- evidence window: Euro Stoxx 50 / European equities 1999–2017.
- instruments: EU50, GER40 (FTMO); FRA40 as a second check
- holding period and expected trades per year: leg A 15:30 CET (presser end) → 17:30 CET cash close (intraday CFD, no swap); leg B hold to the next meeting (~6 weeks, Swing). 8 scheduled meetings/yr → 8 trades/yr per leg; a decade gives ~80 events.
- data needed: ECB transcripts 1999–2026 (ecb.europa.eu, free); 1-minute EU50/GER40 from HistData (ETXEUR, GRXEUR; free, 2010–2026); daily closes from Stooq; $0.
- expected gross edge per trade and cost sensitivity: leg A ≈ 30 bp per event if half the 60 bp day effect is realized after the tone is scorable (scoring needs the full transcript, i.e. the last 30–45 minutes of the presser), vs 1.5–3 bp round trip → passes the economics screen. Leg B ≈ +40 bp incremental over 6 weeks vs swap ≈ −75 bp (GER40 at −6.5 %/yr) → fails; leg B only as a short-tone leg (short pays ~−1 %/yr assumed).
- firm structure fit: FTMO intraday CFD (leg A, no news restriction needed on Swing; on Standard the ±2-minute window around the 13:45 decision is avoided by construction since entry is 15:30). Futures firms: no (non-CME).
- nearest closed relative: E2-01..12 (FOMC/CPI intraday ES/NQ) — different central bank, different market, text-based signal rather than price continuation; not closed.
- decay haircut: 0.5 (published 2015 working paper, 2025 journal). Splits: 2015 (working paper), 2022 (ECB hiking cycle, presser format changes).
- plausibility of net Sharpe ≥ 1.5: low — 8 events/yr, per-event edge ~30 bp at ~80 bp event vol gives a per-trade Sharpe ≈ 0.35 → annual ≈ 1.0 before haircut; honest expectation ≈ 0.5.
- original: no (venue twist: FTMO CFD on EU50/GER40; the paper used index futures/cash)

### C-13-02 FOMC press-conference vocal tone (audio emotion) → US500 drift, as a NEW-DATA row against the closed FOMC family
- mechanism: Gorodnichenko–Pham–Talavera show that the emotional tone of the Fed chair's voice during the press conference, after controlling for the policy action and the text sentiment, moves stock prices; a positive voice tone raises the S&P 500 with a magnitude comparable to a one-standard-deviation forward-guidance shock. The information is orthogonal to the statement text and to the policy surprise, so the E2 price-continuation tests did not use it. Live implementation needs an audio emotion classifier running on the public webcast (free) and acting during or right after the presser.
- evidence: peer-reviewed — Gorodnichenko, Pham, Talavera (2023) "The Voice of Monetary Policy", AER 113(2) 548–584, https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:aea:aecrev:v:113:y:2023:i:2:p:548-84 (abstract only, fetched 2026-11-03); working paper NBER w28592 / draft https://eml.berkeley.edu/~ygorodni/VoiceMP.pdf (not opened): sample 2011–June 2019, 36 press conferences; moving the tone from −1 to +1 ≈ +200 bp S&P 500 (draft figure, not verified in the published version); bond market takes few vocal cues. Post-2019 out-of-sample: none found.
- evidence window: S&P 500 around FOMC press conferences 2011–2019.
- instruments: US500, US100 (FTMO); ES/MES only inside the futures-firm day (14:30–15:10 CT window) with the MFFU/Lucid news bans in mind.
- holding period and expected trades per year: enter during the Q&A once the running tone score is stable (~15:00 ET), exit at 16:00 ET (intraday) or next day 09:30 ET (Swing, one swap). 8/yr.
- data needed: Fed webcast audio archive 2011–2026 (federalreserve.gov, free); open-source speech-emotion model; 1-minute ES or SPXUSD (HistData free 2010–2018 gappy; Databento ES 1m RTH ≈ $6–7 for one root if bought for other lanes); the authors' tone series if posted with the AER replication files (openICPSR, free).
- expected gross edge per trade and cost sensitivity: if the published 200 bp swing per 2 units of tone holds and the usable realized tone is ±0.5, ≈ ±50 bp per event gross against 1–3 bp round trip → passes the economics screen. Sensitivity is to classifier noise, not cost.
- firm structure fit: FTMO intraday CFD or Swing (news allowed on Swing; on Standard the ±2-minute ban covers the 14:00 statement, not the 14:30–15:30 presser — to be verified in the rules matrix); futures firms: Topstep/Bulenox allow news; MFFU Rapid/Pro and Lucid Daily do not.
- nearest closed relative: E2-01..12 (FOMC post-statement continuation/reversal, ES/NQ intraday). This is a new data source and a new mechanism (vocal affect), which the common brief allows as a new row; the Pre-registration Officer decides.
- decay haircut: 0.5 (published 2021 WP / 2023 AER). Splits: 2019 (presser after every meeting), 2021 (publication).
- plausibility of net Sharpe ≥ 1.5: low — 8 events/yr with a noisy classifier; even at the published magnitude the annual Sharpe of an 8-trade sleeve is ≈ 0.8–1.0 before haircut.
- original: no (published); the retail live-audio implementation is new

### C-13-03 FOMC statement cosine-similarity (text change) as a size/inactivity gate for the lane-2 Treasury event sleeves — no direction
- mechanism: the similarity between consecutive FOMC statements measures how much the committee changed its language; low similarity precedes larger moves in fed funds futures (Handlan). The gate scales the existing ZN/UB post-statement continuation sleeves (C-2-10, C-2-11) by the realized text change, or switches them off when the statement is a near-copy. Text is public at 14:00:00 ET; a diff takes milliseconds; no direction is claimed, so the common brief's bar for a directional edge is not the point — the row is a pre-registered overlay.
- evidence: working paper — Handlan, "FedSpeak Matters: Statement Similarity and Monetary Policy Expectations", https://handlanamy.github.io/MyFiles/Handlan_fedspeak.pdf (not opened; search snippet 2026-11-03): lower similarity between sequential statements → more variation in futures-implied rate expectations, R² ≈ 1.5× when similarity is added. Fed note — Acosta & Meade (2015) "Semantic analysis of the FOMC's post-meeting statement", FEDS Notes 2015-09-30, https://www.federalreserve.gov/econresdata/notes/feds-notes/2015/semantic-analysis-of-the-FOMCs-postmeeting-statement-20150930.html (search snippet): cosine similarity of raw statements, sharp drop Dec 2008. Negative: Wang (2026, MPRA 130358) argues language innovations add little once futures-based surprises are controlled for (snippet only).
- evidence window: FOMC statements 1994–2015 (Acosta), 2000s–2010s (Handlan); fed funds futures, not Treasury futures.
- instruments: ZN, UB, ZB (futures firms, overnight-session or intraday); US500 only via C-13-02
- holding period and expected trades per year: no trades of its own; 8 gate decisions/yr applied to C-2-10/C-2-11
- data needed: FOMC statements 1994–2026 (federalreserve.gov, free); the data of the sleeve it gates; $0 incremental
- expected gross edge per trade and cost sensitivity: none of its own; the test is whether sleeve Sharpe conditional on low similarity exceeds unconditional by a pre-registered margin. Cost-neutral.
- firm structure fit: inherits the gated sleeve's structure (futures firms; news windows at MFFU/Lucid still exclude ±2 min).
- nearest closed relative: E2-01..12 (closed for ES/NQ; this gate is for ZN/UB sleeves only, which are open in lane 2).
- decay haircut: 0.7 (original application of a published measure). Split: 2011 (press conferences begin), 2019 (presser after every meeting).
- plausibility of net Sharpe ≥ 1.5: low — an overlay cannot create an edge where the base sleeve has none; expected to be a modest variance-reduction tool at best.
- original: yes (the gating use; the similarity measure is published)

### C-8-05 Energy term-structure carry on FTMO's own swap sign (USOIL / UKOIL / NATGAS / HEATOIL): go with the side that receives the roll
- mechanism: the FTMO rolling contracts pass the futures roll yield through the nightly swap (USOIL long +5 %/yr and short −32 %/yr in backwardation; NATGAS short +40 %/yr and long −176 %/yr in contango, table above). Term-structure (carry) strategies — long backwardated, short contango commodities — are among the oldest documented commodity premia; the counterparties are hedgers and index rollers who hold the wrong side of the curve for insurance or mandate reasons, and that demand does not go away with publication. The twist here is implementation: the broker's swap quote IS the live carry signal, read daily from the same JSON endpoint, and the position only exists when the received swap exceeds a pre-registered threshold.
- evidence: peer-reviewed — Koijen, Moskowitz, Pedersen, Vrugt (2018) "Carry", JFE 127(2) 197–225, https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jfinec:v:127:y:2018:i:2:p:197-225 (abstract only, fetched 2026-11-03): carry predicts returns in the time series and cross-section across asset classes incl. commodities; working-paper Sharpe figures not seen. Peer-reviewed — Fuertes, Miffre, Rallis (2010) term-structure strategy ≈ 12.7 % alpha p.a. (secondary via Quantpedia "Term structure effect in commodities", https://quantpedia.com/strategies/term-structure-effect-in-commodities, search snippet 2026-11-03). Ghoddusi (energy-only conditional rollover, pre-2015) via the same snippet. Post-2015 out-of-sample numbers for a 3–4 energy TS version: not found.
- evidence window: 20–40 commodity futures 1970s/1980s–2012 (KMPV, FMR); nothing after 2015 seen; nothing on CFD financing terms.
- instruments: USOIL.cash, UKOIL.cash, NATGAS.cash, HEATOIL.c (FTMO); XAU/XAG excluded (always contango, both swaps negative).
- holding period and expected trades per year: position held while the swap sign and a slow trend filter agree; re-evaluated nightly, turnover ≈ 6–15 round trips/yr per instrument; 25–60 trades/yr for the sleeve.
- data needed: daily front/second futures settlements for CL, BZ, NG, HO 2000–2026 (free: EIA daily spot/futures tables, Stooq/Yahoo continuous — both blocked today, Databento 1d ≈ $0.05/root as the clean source); the FTMO swap JSON logged daily from now on (free; history does not exist — the back-test must reconstruct carry from the futures curve and treat the FTMO pass-through ratio as a parameter).
- expected gross edge per trade and cost sensitivity: carry collected ≈ 5–40 %/yr while on; price drift against carry historically takes back roughly half (the literature's net TS carry Sharpe ≈ 0.3–0.6 per commodity). Spread 2.5 bp WTI / 8 bp natgas per side on monthly turnover is negligible → passes the economics screen; the risk is the single-market vol (NG 60–80 %/yr) against FTMO's 5 % daily loss, which forces NG to ≤ 10 % of the account's risk budget.
- firm structure fit: FTMO Swing only (multi-week holds, weekends). Futures firms: no (overnight holds). Clauses: 5 % daily loss from the 00:00 CE(S)T balance incl. floating P&L — NG gap risk is the binding constraint.

### C-8-09 Closure premium around multi-day market holidays (Golden Week, Lunar New Year, Easter, Obon-adjacent) in JP225 / HK50 / GER40 held through the closure at FTMO Swing
- mechanism: a multi-day exchange closure is a weekend with more gap risk; holders who cannot hedge (or are forbidden to hold, like FTMO Standard accounts and most intraday prop traders) sell before it and buy back after, so the pre-closure day is weak-to-flat and the position held through the closure earns a gap-risk premium that is the multi-day analogue of the overnight premium.
