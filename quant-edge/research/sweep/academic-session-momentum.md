### C-3-01 Regime-gated overnight drift (02:00–03:00 ET European open) — extends pre-sweep A-C27 with the authors' 2026 negative update
- mechanism: liquidity providers absorb the end-of-day order imbalance at the 16:15 ET close and unwind when European liquidity arrives 02:00–03:00 ET, earning a positive return; the authors' pricing equation is E[overnight return] = closing dollar order imbalance × return variance × (risk-bearing capacity)⁻¹. NEW EVIDENCE: the same three authors report (2026-07-01) that the 02:00–03:00 window, which earned ≈ 3.7 %/yr over 1998–2020 (5,691 days; > 60 % of the E-mini's 5.9 % close-to-close return), has averaged close to zero over Jan 2021–Dec 2025 (1,245 days), and that the flat pattern also holds in NQ and YM. The only pricing input that moved is the dispersion of the closing imbalance: the std of the 15:15–16:15 ET RSV fell from 6.5 % to 2.9 % (VIX mean 20.4 → 19.4; overnight share of volume 15 → 16 %); limit orders posted at the close became smaller (algorithmic providers slice flow, pass less residual inventory). The authors predict the drift returns in the same window if closing-imbalance dispersion widens. Twist (ours): trade the window ONLY when a rolling 60-day dispersion of a bar-built closing-imbalance proxy (BVC on 15:15–16:15 1-min bars) is above a percentile fixed on 1998–2020, with the sign set by the day's own imbalance (long after negative RSV); inactive otherwise.
- evidence: peer-reviewed origin (Boyarchenko, Larsen, Whelan, "The Overnight Drift", RFS 36(9):3502–3547, 2023, DOI 10.1093/rfs/hhad020) plus the authors' post-publication update: "The Disappearing Overnight Drift", Liberty Street Economics no. 20260701, DOI 10.59576/lse.20260701, https://libertystreeteconomics.newyorkfed.org/2026/07/the-disappearing-overnight-drift/ and https://www.fedinprint.org/item/fednls/103479 (both fetched 2026-11-03). Post-2015 OOS: 2021–2025 ≈ 0 (authors' own number). Pre-sweep A-C27 rated this "medium" on the 1998–2020 sample; that rating is superseded.
- instruments: ES/MES (NQ/MNQ and YM/MYM as controls); FTMO US500 CFD (24 h, no swap intraday).
- holding period and expected trades per year: 60 min (01:30–03:30 variant 120 min); unconditional 252/yr; gated variant 30–80/yr.
- data needed: ES 1-min full session (Databento GLBX.MDP3 from 2010-06, ≈ $20 for the root; the 2008–2026 third-party ES 1m copy is under LICENCE HOLD, cross-check only); RSV proxy from the same bars (BVC); optional tbbo 3-month sample for proxy validation (outside the $94 ceiling unless small); CBOE VIX daily free.
- expected gross edge per trade and cost sensitivity: ≈ +1.5 bp per hour-trade pre-2021, ≈ 0 since. MES round trip at ≈ 6,500 index: 0.5 pt + $1.60 ≈ 1.3 bp; screen threshold max(2 ticks ≈ 0.8 bp, 2 × RT ≈ 2.5 bp) = 2.5 bp → FAIL unconditional. The gated variant passes only if its conditional mean is ≥ 2.5 bp, which nobody has measured.
- firm structure fit: overnight-session futures (entirely inside the 18:00-ET-anchored trading day, flat 13 h before the earliest deadline); FTMO intraday CFD (02:00 ET = 08:00 CE(S)T, no swap, inside one daily-loss day). No US Tier-1 release in the window; exclude ECB decision days. Inactivity clauses (MFFU 7 days, FTMO 30 days) can bind for the gated variant.
- nearest closed relative: none closed; pre-sweep A-C27 and P-C20; inherited H0009 stack carried an "overnight drift" component (Sharpe 0.65, no p5, stale opens).
- decay haircut: 0.5 (published; already observed ≈ 0 post-publication). Split dates: 2021-01 (post-publication, imbalance-dispersion collapse), 2020-03 (COVID vol), 2010-06 (Databento data start).
- plausibility of net Sharpe ≥ 1.5: low — the authors' own five post-publication years are flat; only a rarely-trading regime-gated variant keeps a mechanism argument, and it has no evidence yet.
- original: yes (the dispersion gate and sign conditioning; the bare window is A-C27)

### C-3-02 EU-open four-hour window (23:30–03:30 ET) decomposed by hour after 2021 — Bondarenko–Muravyev
- mechanism: European investors process the overnight information set at the European open and resolve uncertainty: ES returns in the four hours around the European open account for the entire average market return, while the rest of the day averages slightly negative (−0.8 %/yr); VIX-futures-implied uncertainty rises overnight and plummets around the EU open. The question for us is which part of the four hours survives now that the 02:00–03:00 core (C-3-01) is flat: 23:30–02:00 (Asian afternoon, London pre-open) and 03:00–03:30 (LSE/Xetra first half-hour) have not been reported post-2021.
- evidence: peer-reviewed: Bondarenko & Muravyev (2023), "Market Return Around the Clock: A Puzzle", JFQA 58(3):939–967, https://ideas.repec.org/a/cup/jfinqa/v58y2023i3p939-967_1.html (abstract only, fetched 2026-11-03); working-paper numbers from the Alpha Architect summary, https://alphaarchitect.com/market-return-around-the-clock/ (fetched 2026-11-03): EU-open window 7.6 % annualized, Sharpe 1.67, t = 6.4, gross. Post-2015 OOS: not reported by the authors; the BLW 2026 update implies the 02:00–03:00 share is gone since 2021.
- instruments: ES/MES; NQ/MNQ; FTMO US500.
- holding period and expected trades per year: 4 h (or three sub-windows tested separately), 252/yr.
- data needed: as C-3-01 (ES 1-min full session; ≈ $20).
- expected gross edge per trade and cost sensitivity: published ≈ 3 bp per 4-h trade gross (7.6 %/252) against MES RT ≈ 1.3 bp — passes the 2 × cost screen on the 1998–2018 sample, but with the core hour gone the plausible post-2021 edge is 0–1 bp → FAIL on current evidence; registered only as the decomposition test that settles the question.
- firm structure fit: overnight-session futures (23:30 ET entry is 5.5 h after the 18:00 reopen; flat by 03:30); FTMO intraday CFD (23:30 ET = 05:30 CE(S)T, after the 00:00 daily-loss anchor; no swap).
- nearest closed relative: none; adjacent to A-C27 / C-3-01 and P-C20.
- decay haircut: 0.5 (published). Split dates: 2018 (sample end), 2021-01.
- plausibility of net Sharpe ≥ 1.5: low — the published Sharpe 1.67 is gross and pre-2019; its main hour has since averaged zero.
- original: no (new evidence attached to a paper the pre-sweep cited but did not fetch)

### C-3-03 Gold overnight (Asian/London hours) drift versus COMEX-day weakness, built as an overnight-session hold in GC/MGC
- mechanism: Blose, Gondhalekar and Kort find overnight returns significantly positive and day returns significantly negative for COMEX front gold futures, the London fix spot market, gold miners and gold funds, in both up and down markets, "economically important after transaction costs"; they attribute it to gold being priced too high at the opening of the Western markets. A CBS thesis describes a hat-shaped intraday seasonality (appreciation in eastern hours, depreciation for the rest of the day, drops around the London fixing times in the alleged-manipulation years); Watkins documents a W-shaped intraday pattern in Tokyo and New York gold futures. A forced-flow reading: Asian physical and retail demand buys in Asian hours; Western-hours selling comes from ETF creations/redemptions, producer hedging and benchmark-linked flows around the 10:30/15:00 London auctions. The futures-firm trading day (18:00 ET reopen) makes the whole Asian-plus-London night holdable without crossing any deadline.
- evidence: peer-reviewed (abstract only): Blose, Gondhalekar, Kort (2018), "Overnight versus day returns in gold and gold related assets", Journal of Economics and Finance 42(3):526–549, https://ideas.repec.org/a/spr/jecfin/v42y2018i3d10.1007_s12197-017-9403-0.html (fetched 2026-11-03; no magnitudes in the abstract). Student thesis (not peer-reviewed, snippet only): "Gold Price Dynamics Around the Clock", CBS 2019, https://research.cbs.dk/en/studentProjects/gold-price-dynamics-around-the-clock/. Working paper (snippet only): Watkins (2017), "Intraday seasonality in efficiency, liquidity, volatility and volume: platinum and gold futures in Tokyo and New York", Kobe WP 1722, https://ideas.repec.org/p/koe/wpaper/1722.html. Post-2015 OOS: none seen.
- instruments: GC / MGC (CME); FTMO XAUUSD (1 bp per side).
- holding period and expected trades per year: 18:00 ET → 08:20 ET (COMEX floor open) or → 05:30 ET (London AM auction); ≈ 250/yr; variant: exit at the 10:30 London AM auction (05:30 ET for most of the year; schedule on the London clock because the US and UK DST switches differ by two to three weeks).
- data needed: GC 1-h bars 2010–2026 (Databento ≈ $1) for the first pass, 1-min full session (≈ $20) for timing; LBMA auction calendar; Shanghai Gold Exchange and Tokyo holidays.
- expected gross edge per trade and cost sensitivity: unmeasured per night. Gold's tick ($0.10 ≈ 0.25 bp at ≈ $4,000) gives a low screen threshold: GC RT ≈ 2 ticks + $5 ≈ 0.6 bp, threshold max(0.5, 1.3) ≈ 1.3 bp (MGC ≈ 1 bp RT, threshold ≈ 2 bp); a +3 bp/night mean would pass — plausible if the 2018 result holds, unknown post-2018. 2 × cost run cheap.
- firm structure fit: overnight-session futures (entirely inside the day; flat ≥ 6 h before every deadline); FTMO intraday CFD: 18:00 ET ≈ 00:00 CE(S)T, so the hold sits inside one FTMO day with no swap if entered after the server rollover (verify rollover minute); FTMO Swing not needed. News: no US Tier-1 release inside the window; exclude China PBoC/SGE holidays.
- nearest closed relative: none closed (D4 traded CFD baskets with intraday LHH/open-move/TWAP/ORB rules, not an overnight hold; D2 is daily FX/CFD trend).
- decay haircut: 0.5 (published). Split dates: 2015-03-20 (ICE LBMA gold auction replaces the fix), 2018 (publication), 2022–2025 (central-bank buying regime and Shanghai premium).
- plausibility of net Sharpe ≥ 1.5: low — the evidence is abstract-level and a decade old, and the "too high at the open" story is not a forced-trader mechanism; registered because the cost ratio in gold is the best of any overnight idea in this sweep.
- original: yes (futures-firm overnight-session construction with the 18:00 ET anchor and London-auction exits; the day/night split itself is published)

### C-3-04 Nikkei night-session overreaction → Tokyo day-session reversal, traded in NKD/NIY inside the futures-firm day (Miwa 2019)
- mechanism: Osaka's night session (16:30 → 06:00 JST, concurrent with Europe/US, ≈ 40 % of OSE futures volume) prices foreign news; Miwa shows night-session returns are negatively associated with subsequent day-session returns, that the overreaction strengthens with the length and activity of the night session, and that it is larger for Nikkei 225 futures (longer night session) than TOPIX futures. Domestic institutions and cash-futures arbitrageurs re-price at the 09:00 JST cash open. The day session (19:00–01:30 ET in US winter, 20:00–02:30 ET in summer) sits entirely inside the futures-firm trading day.
- evidence: peer-reviewed (abstract only): Miwa (2019), "Trading hours extension and intraday price behavior", International Review of Economics & Finance 64:572–585, https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/reveco/v64y2019icp572-585.html (fetched via search 2026-11-03); blind working-paper PDF https://acfr.aut.ac.nz/__data/assets/pdf_file/0009/291654/Intraday_Overreaction_Extended_session_201907Blind.pdf (not fetched). JPX WP no. 14 on day/night trading behaviour (2014 sample, snippet only), https://www.jpx.co.jp/english/corporate/research-study/working-paper/b5b4pj000000i468-att/E_JPX_working_paper_No14.pdf. Post-2015 OOS: none reported; see C-5-03 for a 2026 paper in the same direction.
- instruments: NKD (CME Nikkei/USD, $5 × index, tick 5 pts) or NIY (yen); FTMO JP225 CFD.
- holding period and expected trades per year: fade the night-session sign from 09:00 JST to 15:30 JST (or first 30 min); 245/yr unconditional, ≈ 60–80/yr gated on |night return| > 1 σ.
- data needed: NKD 1-h (≈ $1) then 1-min full session (≈ $20) 2010–2026; OSE session calendar (night session end 03:00 → 05:30 in Jul-2016 → 06:00 in Sep-2021; cash close 15:00 → 15:30 on 2024-11-05); JST/ET DST map.
- expected gross edge per trade and cost sensitivity: not in the abstract. NKD tick ≈ 1 bp at ≈ 50,000; RT ≈ 2 ticks + $5 ≈ 2.2 bp; threshold ≈ 4.4 bp; a partial reversal of a 1 % night move would need ≥ 5 bp mean follow-through to pass → provisional FAIL until measured; CFD JP225 spread ≈ 1–1.5 bp per side gives a similar threshold.
- firm structure fit: overnight-session futures (verify NKD/NIY are on each firm's permitted list; flat 14 h before deadlines); FTMO intraday CFD (01:00–07:30 CE(S)T, inside one day, no swap). News: BoJ decision days (around 12:00 JST) excluded.
- nearest closed relative: H0012/H0017 (S&P overnight-gap fade) in logic — different market and a session-structure mechanism; D4 used JP225_USD only as a held-out market with other rules.

- original: no

### C-5-01 VIX-futures → ES lead in high-volatility states, used only as a signal (Bangsgaard–Kokholm 2024)
- mechanism: when volatility is low the VX and ES markets are weakly connected; when volatility is high their prices are strongly negatively correlated and VX futures lead SPX futures; a market's lead strengthens when its relative liquidity improves; market makers' hedging contributes. The authors state the relation is too tight to support profitable trading once transaction costs are included. Relevance for us: VX cannot be traded at any firm in scope (not CME; FTMO has no VIX product — inherited G1 "no product"), so VX would be a 1-minute signal for MES only.
- evidence: peer-reviewed (abstract only): Bangsgaard & Kokholm (2024), "The lead–lag relation between VIX futures and SPX futures", Journal of Financial Markets 67:100851, DOI 10.1016/j.finmar.2023.100851, https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/finmar/v67y2024ics1386418123000496.html (fetched 2026-11-03). Related: Bollen, O'Neill, Whaley (2017), "Tail wags dog: intraday price discovery in VIX markets", JFM 37(5):431–451, https://ideas.repec.org/a/wly/jfutmk/v37y2017i5p431-451.html (VX futures lead the VIX index; snippet only); Frijns, Tourani-Rad, Webb (2016), snippet only. Post-2015 OOS: the 2024 paper's sample is recent but the authors find no net profit.
- instruments: signal VX (Cboe CFE); traded leg ES/MES; FTMO US500.
- holding period and expected trades per year: 1–15 min; hundreds/yr but only in high-VIX regimes (VIX > 25).
- data needed: VX 1-min (CFE — not in the Databento GLBX plan; outside budget) and ES 1-min; data-blocked within the plan.
- expected gross edge per trade and cost sensitivity: authors: none net of costs → FAIL screen.
- firm structure fit: intraday futures; FTMO intraday CFD; binding daily-loss limits in exactly the regimes where the lead exists.
- nearest closed relative: G1 (VRP/VIX, no product) and the negative-evidence item "1-minute lead-lag without a mechanism" (this one has a mechanism and a documented null net of costs).
- decay haircut: 0.5. Split dates: 2018-02 (Volmageddon), 2022-05 (daily 0DTE expirations).

### C-5-02 CL → 6C (petro-currency) at 5-minute to hourly horizons
- mechanism: commodity-price shocks move commodity-exporter exchange rates through terms of trade and hedging flows; Zhang, Dufour and Galbraith measure causality at multiple horizons with 5-minute and daily data and find causality in both directions but much stronger from commodity prices to exchange rates (ratios as high as 5–10) at short horizons, weakening with horizon; removing USD effects sharpens the commodity → FX direction. If FX dealers and corporate hedgers re-price CAD off crude with a lag of minutes, a large CL move would be followed by a same-sign 6C move.
- evidence: peer-reviewed (abstract and WP excerpt): Zhang, Dufour, Galbraith (2016), "Exchange rates and commodity prices: measuring causality at multiple horizons", Journal of Empirical Finance 36:100–120, DOI 10.1016/j.jempfin.2015.10.005, https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/empfin/v36y2016icp100-120.html; WP https://www.jeanmariedufour.research.mcgill.ca/Zhang_Dufour_Galbraith_2014_CommoditypriceXRateCausality_W.pdf (both via search, fetched 2026-11-03; sample 2000–2009; CL, GC, HG vs CAD, AUD, CLP). Ferraro, Rogoff, Rossi (2015), JIMF 54 (daily CAD predictability, in-sample; pre-sweep A-C22 mention, not fetched). Post-2015 OOS: none.
- instruments: 6C / M6C vs CL / MCL (CME); FTMO USDCAD vs USOIL.
- holding period and expected trades per year: 5–60 min after |ΔCL(5m)| > 2 σ; 100–300/yr.
- data needed: CL and 6C 1-min 2010–2026 (≈ $40 full session) or 1-h (≈ $2) as a pilot; EIA and BoC calendars.
- expected gross edge per trade and cost sensitivity: unknown; 6C tick 0.5 pip ≈ 0.7 bp, RT ≈ 1.5 bp, threshold ≈ 3 bp; the lagged (non-contemporaneous) CAD response to a 1 % oil move is plausibly < 1 bp at 1-minute sampling → FAIL unless the gated conditional mean is ≥ 3 bp.
- firm structure fit: intraday futures; FTMO intraday CFD; exclude EIA Wednesday 10:30 ET and BoC windows (MFFU ±2 min, Lucid Daily).
- nearest closed relative: negative-evidence items "FX intraday momentum 6E/6B/6J" and "1-minute lead-lag without a mechanism" (this has a mechanism); D2 (daily FX trend/carry) is a different horizon.
- decay haircut: 0.5 (published; sample ends 2009). Split dates: 2009, 2014-11 (oil crash), 2020-04.
- plausibility of net Sharpe ≥ 1.5: low — the measured causality is at 5 minutes in 2000–2009 and almost certainly contemporaneous at today's FX latency.
- original: no

### C-4-01 Cross-sectional overnight–intraday reversal among CME equity-index futures (ES, NQ, YM, EMD, NKD): long the lowest overnight return, short the highest, open → close
- mechanism: Hong–Wang (2000) periodic market closure: liquidity dries up over the close, overnight price moves overshoot, and the intraday session reverses the relative overnight winners and losers; Della Corte–Kosowski–Wang's "CO-OC" strategy (signal = close-to-open return, hold = open-to-close) earns roughly five times a conventional reversal in US stocks and is positive in every futures asset class; the reversal return is larger after a rise in the VIX over the non-trading hours (an extension of Nagel 2012), i.e. it is paid when overnight uncertainty was high. Cross-sectional among the five CME equity-index futures it is market-neutral (really Nikkei-vs-US and Nasdaq-vs-Dow relative legs), and the open-to-close hold sits inside the futures-firm day. The first-half-hour → last-half-hour family and the S&P time-series gap fade are different constructions.
- evidence: working paper, full text read (conference copy, "incomplete", Nov 2015): Della Corte, Kosowski, Wang, "Market Closure and Short-Term Reversal", https://www.mcgill.ca/desautels/files/desautels/dkw_2016.pdf (fetched 2026-11-03). Futures: 35 CME contracts, daily open/close from TickData, Jul 1982–Dec 2014; equity-index futures (DJIA, NASDAQ, NIKKEI 225, S&P400, S&P500) CO-OC: 0.25 % per day, t = 13.18, annualized Sharpe 4.08, correlation 1–4 % with conventional reversal; subperiods 1982–2006 and 2007–2014 both positive (futures Sharpe range 1.26–2.51 early, 1.30–6.68 late). Caveats in the paper itself: daily open prices, and "much of the reversal profitability is within the bid-ask bounce" in the stock literature. Follow-up "Overnight-Intraday Reversal Everywhere" (Della Corte, Kosowski) not fetchable (assets.super.so 403; cicfconf.org 503); search excerpt: equity-index futures reversal ≈ 0.354 %/day after high VIX vs ≈ 0.121 % after low VIX (unconfirmed). Post-2015 OOS: none published; the pattern "has not disappeared" in 2007–2014.
- instruments: ES/MES, NQ/MNQ, YM/MYM, EMD (S&P MidCap 400; thin, 0.10 pt tick), NKD/NIY; FTMO: US500, US100, US30, JP225 CFDs (US2000 as a sixth leg).
- holding period and expected trades per year: 09:30 → 16:00 ET (flat before 16:10 ET / 15:10 CT); 1 long + 1 short leg per day ≈ 500 round trips/yr.
- data needed: daily RTH open/close for the five roots 2010–2026 (Databento ohlcv-1d ≈ $0.05/root, or 1-h ≈ $1/root to define a 09:30–09:31 fill); SPX open/close 1990–2026 in hand; VIX daily free. Cheapest test in this file.
- expected gross edge per trade and cost sensitivity: published 25 bp/day for the long-short (12.5 bp per leg) 1982–2014 gross; leg round trips ES/NQ ≈ 1.3 bp, YM ≈ 1.5 bp, NKD ≈ 2.2 bp, EMD ≈ 3–5 bp → 2 × cost ≈ 3–9 bp per leg → PASS the economics screen on the published number even after a 0.5 haircut; the live question is whether a 09:31 fill reproduces a daily-data "open".

### C-5-09 Cross-country intraday lead–lag: the US first half-hour return (09:30–10:00 ET) predicts the European last half-hour (17:00–17:30 CET) — GER40 / FRA40 / EU50 / UK100 CFDs
- mechanism: Li, Sakkas and Urquhart test the US first half-hour return as a predictor of each other market's last half-hour return after controlling for the local first half-hour: the slope is positive in 12 of 15 countries and significant in 8 (Germany β_US = 2.99**, t 2.05; France 4.64***, t 3.09; Netherlands 3.84**, t 2.43; UK 2.58**, t 2.17; Sweden 4.51***; Norway 13.58***; Ireland 6.77***; Austria 4.70**; not Japan, Australia, Canada, whose sessions close before the US open). A global portfolio built on the US signal ("Type 3 GITSM") earns 5.17 %/yr equally weighted (SD 2.93 %, Sharpe 1.77, t 5.47) and 5.71 % value-weighted (Sharpe 1.63), against country Sharpe ratios of −0.40 to 1.12 — gross, cash indices, no transaction costs, 2005-10-04 → 2017-12-29. The mechanism is late-informed domestic trading and infrequent rebalancing into the European close (the same late-day hedging/rebalancing channel as ITSM), and the predictor is known a full hour before the trade window, so it is a genuine lead–lag rather than a contemporaneous correlation. Daily precedent: Becker–Finnerty–Gupta (1990) US open-to-close → next-day Japan.
- evidence: working paper, full text read (2020 WP of the JFM 2022 article), http://wp.lancs.ac.uk/fofi2020/files/2020/04/FoFI-2020-092-Zeming-Li.pdf (fetched 2026-11-03), Table 7 Panel B and Table 8; journal version: Journal of Financial Markets 57:100619 (2022) (not fetched: reading-9.eprints-hosting.org unresolvable, centaur.reading.ac.uk bot-blocked). Post-2015 OOS: sample ends 2017-12; the 2010–2017 expanding-window results in Table 8 (IV/MD/MV/MinV columns) are pseudo-OOS; no post-2018 number anywhere.
- instruments: FTMO GER40, FRA40, EU50, NL25 (if offered), UK100 CFDs (last half-hour of Xetra/Euronext 17:00–17:30 CET; LSE 16:00–16:30 London); no CME product with liquidity → FTMO only. Signal from ES/SPY 1-minute (in hand).
- holding period and expected trades per year: 30 min per market per day (sign = sign of the US 09:30–10:00 ET return, optionally only when it agrees with the local first half-hour); ≈ 250/yr per market, 4–5 markets.
- data needed: ES or SPY 1-min RTH (in hand); DAX/ESTX50 1-min 2010–2018 HistData copy (in hand per the D4 record; licence check) and FTMO MT5 history or another free source for 2019–2026 (Dukascopy datafeed blocked); $0 inside the Databento plan.
- expected gross edge per trade and cost sensitivity: EW portfolio 5.17 %/yr ÷ 252 ≈ 2 bp per market-day gross; CFD cost 0.5–1.5 bp per side → round trip 1–3 bp → threshold 2–6 bp → FAIL to marginal; registered because (i) the trade count is ≈ 1,000/yr across markets, (ii) gating on |US signal| > 1 σ and on agreement with the local signal plausibly doubles the per-trade edge, and (iii) it is the only lead–lag in this sweep with a published multi-market, pseudo-OOS Sharpe above the bar. The closest tested relative (D4-01, local-signal last-half-hour on 19 CFDs) was gross +0.002 and net t −16 — the same cost problem.
- firm structure fit: FTMO intraday CFD (window is 17:00–17:30 CE(S)T, inside the daily-loss day; no swap); not available at the US futures firms; FTMO Swing not needed. News: no Tier-1 US release at 11:00 ET; ECB days (14:15/14:45 CET) excluded for EU50.
- nearest closed relative: D4-01 (Gao last-half-hour across a CFD basket incl. GRXEUR/ETXEUR — closed; local signal), H0006/H0019 (S&P last-half-hour — closed); this uses a foreign signal one hour ahead, a materially different construction with its own published evidence.
- decay haircut: 0.5 (published 2020/2022). Split dates: 2017-12 (sample end), 2018-01 (MiFID II / Eurex extended hours), 2021-01.
- plausibility of net Sharpe ≥ 1.5: low — gross Sharpe 1.77 on frictionless cash indices against a cost that equals the whole per-trade edge; only a gated, high-|signal| version has a route past 2 × cost.
