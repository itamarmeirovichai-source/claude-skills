### C-6-02 Decision-clock tranching: stagger each sleeve's 30-minute decision times across three offsets (rebalance-timing-luck removal as a construction layer)
- mechanism: Newfound shows that strategies differing only in rebalance schedule disperse by 100–440 bp/yr (momentum the worst, 4.4 %/yr; two semi-annual momentum tranches once sat at −50.3 % vs −13.8 % drawdown) and that tranching ("a little but frequently") removes most of it and adds a mild trend-following payoff. Intraday sleeves at a 30-minute cadence have the same exposure: a 09:30-anchored clock and a 09:40-anchored clock trade different bars. Twist (ours): run every intraday sleeve as three tranches with decision clocks offset by 0, 10 and 20 minutes, one third of risk each; the published result is a variance reduction with no mean change, so the candidate is a layer that lowers the noise in every Gate-2 statistic and the chance that a firm's daily-loss clause is hit by one unlucky clock. Not an alpha.
- evidence: blog with code-backed figures (Newfound/Return Stacked research library): "The Dumb (Timing) Luck of Smart Beta" (2019-11-18), https://blog.thinknewfound.com/2019/11/the-dumb-timing-luck-of-smart-beta/ and "Tranching, Trend, and Mean Reversion" (2020-04-27), https://blog.thinknewfound.com/2020/04/tranching-trend-and-mean-reversion/ (both reached via the tag page https://blog.thinknewfound.com/tag/rebalance-timing-luck/, fetched 2026-11-03; numbers quoted from the tag-page summaries, code not published); the formal paper is Hoffstein, Sibears, Faber, "Rebalance Timing Luck: The Difference between Hired and Fired" (Journal of Index Investing 2019; not fetched). Post-2015 OOS: not applicable (a construction identity, not a return).
- evidence window: US equity factor portfolios 2000–2019, monthly/semi-annual rebalances; the intraday transfer has no evidence at all.
- instruments: any sleeve that survives Gate 1; ES/MES first.
- holding period and expected trades per year: as the underlying sleeve; trades per year rise by up to 3× in count but not in notional (each tranche is one third size), so dollar cost is unchanged while micro-contract rounding (one MES per tranche) sets a floor on account size: three tranches × 20 roots × 1 micro each ≈ $100k notional per root-set.
- data needed: none beyond the sleeve's own data; $0.
- expected gross edge per trade and cost sensitivity: zero expected mean change; variance of the sleeve's daily P&L falls by ≈ (1 − ρ̄)/3 where ρ̄ is the correlation between clock-offset variants (unknown; Newfound's monthly figures imply ρ̄ ≈ 0.7–0.9 for slow strategies). Cost per unit notional unchanged if micro contracts exist; where they do not (ZC, NG), tranching is impossible at $100k and the layer is skipped.
- firm structure fit: all four structures; it tightens the daily-loss (FTMO 5 %, Topstep daily loss limit) breach probability for a given vol target, which is the clause this layer touches. Minimum-hold clauses (Lucid 5 s, Tradeify 10 s) unaffected.
- nearest closed relative: none; inherited stack work (`S/stack_backtest.py vol_scale`) and F1 ensemble are risk layers of a different kind.
- decay haircut: not applicable (no mean). Split dates: none; test is in-sample by construction (variance of clock-offset variants on the same data).
- plausibility of net Sharpe ≥ 1.5: low — adds no return; it is registered as a construction layer whose only claim (lower P&L variance for identical mean) is measured, never as a candidate edge.
- original: yes (timing-luck tranching applied to intraday decision clocks; Newfound's result is for monthly/semi-annual portfolios)

### C-7-01 Inactivity rules for intraday breakout/trend sleeves: no trades on FOMC decision days and roll weeks, decided from the calendar before the open
- mechanism: the adversary's calendar. On FOMC decision days dealers and macro funds hold risk back until 14:00 ET and the first intraday break is reversed by the post-decision repricing; in roll weeks (equity indices: the Thursday before the third Friday of Mar/Jun/Sep/Dec; other roots per CME) volume migrates between contracts and continuous-contract bars carry spurious range. The inherited E2 family showed that trading the events themselves has gross ≈ 0 in ES/NQ, so the only remaining question is whether REMOVING those days from a breakout/trend sleeve raises its Sharpe and lowers its breach count. Twist (ours): a pre-registered calendar inactivity rule (FOMC decision day; CPI/NFP day until 10:00 ET; the root's roll week) applied to every breakout/trend sleeve as a fixed layer, evaluated on breach counts and Sharpe change, with the share of days lost reported (≈ 8 FOMC + ≈ 24 CPI/NFP + ≈ 20 roll days ≈ 20 % of the year).
- evidence: page-backed, no code, and the main practitioner number is RETRACTED by its author: Krueger Algorithms, "Trend days need no catalyst", https://kruegeralgorithms.com/en/research/trend-days-need-no-catalyst (fetched 2026-11-03): DAX/FTSE/NQ/Dow CFDs 2015-01-05→2026-06-05, 66,511 trades on 2,949 days, six intraday breakout/fade setups on M15, net of spread and slippage, in-sample, no walk-forward; book R/day QUIET (2,092 days) +3.03, NFP (131) +4.56 (t +2.0), CPI (134) +2.01 (t −1.6), PPI (136) +2.05 (t −1.5), FOMC (90) +1.18 (t −2.8), P(trend day ≥ +5 R) 36.5 % quiet vs 25.6 % FOMC. The page carries two later notes (2026-09-29, 2026-10-05): the backtest chain books positive R on a random market and a re-run found none of 25 breakout rule × market combinations beats a random market (mean −0.08 R per trade after costs), so "positive R levels and comparisons in this study are therefore not proven". What survives is the ranking of day classes (FOMC worst, NFP best), not the levels. Inherited E2-01..12 (`~/quant-edge-inherited/pr4/skills/prop-fleet/references/w2-events.md`): best FOMC fade +16.0 bp/event-day, t 1.09, opposite sign in-sample. Post-2015 OOS: none.
- evidence window: index CFDs 2015–2026 (Krueger, in-sample, retracted levels); SPY/NQ 2019–2025 (E2).
- instruments: any intraday breakout/trend sleeve; evaluated first on the C-6-01 universe excluding ES/NQ.
- holding period and expected trades per year: as the sleeve, minus ≈ 20 % of days.
- data needed: FOMC/CPI/NFP calendar (free: FRED release calendar, BLS), CME roll calendar (free: CME product pages); sleeve data as registered; $0 extra.
- expected gross edge per trade and cost sensitivity: no per-trade edge; the claim is a Sharpe increase from dropping lower-expectation days; even on Krueger's retracted levels FOMC days contributed +1.18 R/day, i.e. the rule removes a weaker day, not a losing one. Cost falls in proportion to days removed.
- firm structure fit: intraday futures and FTMO intraday CFD; directly satisfies MFFU Rapid/Pro and Lucid Daily news-window bans (±2 min incl. resting orders) by never holding across the release; the inactivity clauses (MFFU 7 days, FTMO 30 days) are never triggered by a 20 % day-drop.

- decay haircut: 0.5 (published statistic). Split dates: 2022-01 (CPI becomes the dominant release), 2020-03.
- plausibility of net Sharpe ≥ 1.5: low — a filter cannot create an edge, its only practitioner evidence is retracted at the level, and the ranking it keeps says FOMC days are weaker, not negative; registered only as a layer whose effect on breach counts and Sharpe is measured once per surviving sleeve, mainly because it is what the MFFU/Lucid news clauses force anyway.
- original: yes (as a combined pre-registered inactivity calendar; the day-class ranking is Krueger's)

### C-7-02 Implied-range sizing overlay: scale intraday sleeve risk by the VIX term structure / VVIX / dealer-gamma range forecast (predicts width, not direction)
- mechanism: Krueger finds that VIX term structure, VVIX and dealer gamma predict the day's realized range 0.12–0.35 typical daily ranges wider (t 2.6 to 5.4) but not direction. A vol-targeted sleeve that sizes on trailing realized vol reacts after the fact; sizing on a pre-open implied-range forecast reduces the chance that a wide day breaches the 5 % FTMO daily loss or a Topstep daily loss limit at the same expected return. Twist (ours): position = target vol / forecast range, where the forecast is a fixed pre-registered linear map of (VIX − VIX3M spread, VVIX level, GEX sign) fitted on 2010–2019 and frozen; compared against the trailing-realized-vol scaler (A-C30) on breach counts, not Sharpe. Secondary, lane-7 proper: a breakout sleeve active only in the top tercile of forecast range and a fade sleeve only in the bottom tercile (P-C11's regime split with an ex-ante implied input instead of the 5-day realized range).
- evidence: page-backed, no code, with a real holdout: Krueger Algorithms, "VIX curve, VVIX and dealer gamma predict range, not direction", https://kruegeralgorithms.com/en/research/vix-term-structure-vvix-gamma-predict-range-not-direction (fetched 2026-11-03): SPX/NQ/Dow (daily SPY/QQQ/DIA, Yahoo) and DAX; search to end-2018 (series start 2000–2011), single holdout 2019→2026-10 (1,949 days, 4 filters, costs 2 bp per round trip); gamma leg Dukascopy minute CFDs, search 2015–2022, holdout 2023→2026-06-05. ### C-6-03 Carver dynamic optimisation (greedy minimum-tracking-error with buffering) to run 20–45 micro-futures sleeves on one $100k funded slot
- mechanism: a $100k slot cannot hold one contract of every root at a 10–20 % vol target — MES at ≈ $32k notional needs ≈ 2 % vol per contract, ZN ≈ $110k, CL (no micro at MFFU/Topstep for some plans) ≈ $65k — so the breadth that C-6-01 depends on is destroyed by integer rounding. Carver's production method (pysystemtrade `dynamic_optimisation`): each day pick the integer-contract portfolio that minimises the tracking-error variance to the unrounded optimal weights plus a shadow trading cost, with a portfolio-level buffer (≈ 10 % of target risk) so trades happen only when the tracking error exceeds the buffer. Twist (ours): run it at the intraday sleeve level inside the prop clauses — max-contract limits (Topstep 50K: 5 minis/50 micros), the firm's scaling plan, and the daily-loss limit as hard constraints — so a 20-root book is held as 6–12 rounded positions that track it. Not an alpha; a construction layer that decides whether breadth is purchasable at all.
- evidence: blog with code and results (Rob Carver, pysystemtrade, GPL): "Mr Greedy and the Tale of the Minimum Tracking Error Variance" 2021-10-01, https://qoppac.blogspot.com/2021/10/mr-greedy-and-tale-of-minimum-tracking.html (fetched 2026-11-03): $100k, 45 instruments, three EWMAC + carry rules, Sharpe unrounded 1.17 / rounded 1.06 / optimised 1.19, costs ≈ 0.13 SR for all three, tracking error 0.486 % vs 0.494 %, t 3.7 optimised vs rounded; $50k: optimised ≈ +0.1 SR over rounded, ≈ −0.2 vs unrounded — SUPERSEDED by the author's 2021-10-22 note (sparse-position cost error). Production version, "Mr Greedy..." revisited, November 2021, https://qoppac.blogspot.com/2021/11/mr-greedy-and-tale-of-minimum-tracking.html (fetched 2026-11-03): ≈ $500k, 114 instruments, shadow cost 250, buffer ≈ 1.25 %, correlation shrinkage; Sharpe net dynamic 1.287 vs static 34-instrument benchmark 1.275 vs unrounded 1.359; return correlation to unrounded 0.986; costs ≈ 5 bp/yr in SR units; turnover 51.1 vs 37.5 per year. Earlier failure at $25k / 20 instruments (2021-06-25, https://qoppac.blogspot.com/2021/06/optimising-portfolios-for-small.html, fetched 2026-11-03): Sharpe original > 1.0, rounded 0.72, optimised 0.56 — the grid-search variant "doesn't seem to add any value". Code: pysystemtrade (moved to https://github.com/pst-group/pysystemtrade in January 2026; README fetched 2026-11-03, no results in README). Post-2015 OOS: the posts are full-sample 1970s–2021 backtests, no holdout.
- evidence window: 20–114 daily futures, ≈ 1980–2021 (Carver); nothing intraday.
- instruments: the C-6-01 universe in micros where they exist (MES, MNQ, M2K, MYM, MGC, SIL, MHG, MCL, M6E, M6B, M6A, MBT) plus minis where not (ZN, ZF, ZB, NG, ZC, ZS, ZW, 6J, 6C).
- holding period and expected trades per year: as the sleeves; the buffer cuts position changes by ≈ 30–50 % (Carver: turnover 51 vs 37 per year at daily cadence; intraday numbers unknown).
- data needed: none beyond the sleeves' bars; the covariance matrix comes from the same 1h/1m bars; $0 extra.
- expected gross edge per trade and cost sensitivity: no edge; the measurable claims are (i) correlation of the rounded book to the unrounded book (Carver 0.986 at $500k; at $100k with intraday vol it will be far lower — that number is the result) and (ii) cost in SR units. At $100k the June-2021 failure ($25k, 20 instruments: 0.56 vs 1.0) is the relevant warning: below some capital per root the layer cannot save breadth.
- firm structure fit: intraday futures (the layer exists because of the contract limits and daily-loss clauses); FTMO Swing/intraday CFD in principle (CFD lot sizes are divisible, so rounding is a smaller problem and the layer is nearly moot there).
- nearest closed relative: none; inherited D1/F2 economics models size one strategy per slot (`D/w1-prop/prop_model.py`), not a rounded multi-root book.
- decay haircut: not applicable (no mean). Split dates: none; evaluated by simulation on the sleeves' own series.
- plausibility of net Sharpe ≥ 1.5: low — construction only; it can at best preserve the Sharpe of C-6-01, which is itself rated low.

### C-6-04 Cross-root signed-volume breadth (fraction of roots in an asset class with positive first-30-minute BVC flow) as a gate on each root's intraday trend sleeve
- mechanism: order flow is correlated across roots in an asset class (rates: ZF/ZN/ZB/UB; energies: CL/HO/RB; metals: GC/SI/HG; grains: ZC/ZS/ZW) because the same macro and CTA programs trade them as blocks; a root whose own early flow agrees with its class breadth is more likely to carry a class-wide, slow-to-execute flow (VWAP/TWAP slices through the day) than idiosyncratic noise. Twist (ours): compute per root the first-30-minute bulk-volume-classified signed volume (BVC on 1-minute OHLCV, free with the bars), form the class breadth B = share of roots with positive signed volume, and let a root's C-6-01 sleeve trade only in the direction of B when |B − 0.5| ≥ a pre-fixed threshold (e.g. ≥ 3 of 4 rates roots agree); inactive otherwise. CFDs could not do this (no real volume), which is why D4 never saw it.
- evidence: own idea by analogy; the BVC proxy is code-backed as a measurement only: screamer microstructure notebook, https://screamer.readthedocs.io/en/stable/notebooks/11-microstructure-order-flow.html (fetched 2026-11-03): on 4,093 aggressor-signed Deribit BTC-perp trades (2023-08-04) the tick rule agrees with the venue sign on 79.3 % of trades, BVC(20) buy-share correlates 0.50 with the true buy share, and 1-minute OFI correlates 0.45 with the same bar's return (contemporaneous, 319 bars, not predictive); Easley, López de Prado, O'Hara, "Bulk Classification of Trading Activity" (academia.edu listing, search snippet only, fetched 2026-11-03): BVC classifies ≈ 91 % of E-mini S&P volume correctly. Cross-root flow breadth itself: no published test found. Related negatives: Krueger "Volume gate phantom edges" (+0.90 R with a same-day volume filter, −0.11 R without — a lookahead artefact; https://kruegeralgorithms.com/en/research/volume-gate-phantom-edges, index summary), so the gate must use only the first 30 minutes' bars; pre-sweep A-C38 (BVC imbalance as a conditioner, single root) and A-C36 (cross-sectional 1-minute lead-lag LASSO). Post-2015 OOS: none.
- evidence window: none for the construction; proxy validation on crypto 2023 and ES (Easley et al., ≈ 2008–2010).
- instruments: the non-equity half of the C-6-01 universe grouped by class; ES/NQ excluded (closed base).
- holding period and expected trades per year: 10:00 ET entry (30 min after the class's main open: 08:20 CT rates/energies/metals → 08:50; grains 08:30 → 09:00), flat by the class settlement or the firm deadline; gated days ≈ 30–50 % → 70–120 trades per root-year.
- data needed: ohlcv-1m for 12–16 non-equity roots (≈ $6–7 each RTH → $80–110, over the ceiling) or ohlcv-1h as a first pass (BVC on hourly bars, ≈ $15 for 15 roots, inside the ceiling); a 3-month `trades` sample for one root to validate the BVC proxy against true aggressor signs (ES trades full history ≈ $154 per `databento-cost-check-2026-11-03.md` → ≈ $2.5 for three months).
- expected gross edge per trade and cost sensitivity: unknown; the base family's open→close continuation is 2–6 bp on index futures and unmeasured elsewhere; thresholds as in C-6-01 (metals/FX 1–2 bp RT, rates 3 bp, energies 4 bp, grains 11 bp) → grains FAIL, the rest need ≥ 2–6 bp gross, which is plausible only if the class-flow story is true on roughly half the days.
- firm structure fit: intraday futures (flat by deadline); not available on CFDs (no volume) → FTMO legs excluded. Clauses: 10:00 ET entries sit after the 08:30 releases and before 14:00 FOMC; MFFU/Lucid news windows respected by construction except 10:00 ET ISM/consumer-sentiment days (skip them).
- nearest closed relative: D4-02 (open move → rest of session across a CFD basket, price-based, net t ≤ −4.6) and H0005/H0011 (noise-area momentum ES/SPY); the signed-volume breadth is a new input, not a new mechanism for the base.
- decay haircut: 0.7 (original). Split dates: 2015-01 (CME Globex volume share and micro launches 2019-05), 2020-03.

### C-6-05 Cross-sectional overnight→intraday (CO-OC) reversal across COMMODITY and RATES futures, formed at the open, flat at the close (the Della Corte–Kosowski–Wang futures extension that A-C4-01 left out)
- mechanism: Della Corte, Kosowski, Wang ("Market Closure and Short-Term Reversal", Nov 2015 WP): assets with above-average close-to-open returns earn below-average open-to-close returns because overnight price pressure from illiquid-hours trading is corrected when the day session's liquidity arrives; the effect grows with the overnight change in VIX and is "especially pronounced for futures". The academic scout registered the equity-index version (C-4-01, medium); this row is the commodity class (CL, HO, RB, NG, GC, SI, HG, ZC, ZS, ZW) and the rates class (ZF, ZN, ZB, UB) as two separate breadth books, dollar-neutral within class, sized by the deviation of each root's overnight return from the class mean, with the VIX-change gate as a pre-registered variant.
- evidence: working paper, figures only for futures, gross: CXO Advisory summary, https://www.cxoadvisory.com/technical-trading/overnightintraday-return-reversal-trading/ (fetched 2026-11-03): futures sample July 1982–December 2014 across stock-index, interest-rate, commodity and currency futures; CO-OC results "generally consistent, though much weaker" than in stocks; "the larger the change in VIX from the prior close to the open, the larger the CO-OC reversal effect ... especially pronounced for futures"; all statistics gross, turnover ≈ 100 % per day, delaying formation by one minute "sharply reduces returns". Quantpedia lists it as strategy #301 "Overnight-Intraday Daily Reversal in Commodities" and #303 "...Weekly Reversal in Interest Rate Futures" (futures/CFDs, intraday rebalancing; Quantpedia update 2016-03-31, https://vvv.quantpedia.com/?p=666 — HTTP 503 at fetch, search snippet only; the strategy page slug was not found). The academic scout read the 2015 conference PDF in full for C-4-01 (equity indices). Post-2015 OOS: none published.
- evidence window: futures 1982–2014 (all four classes, gross). A registered row on 2015–2026 commodity/rates futures is untouched on dates; the equity-index class is C-4-01's.
- instruments: commodity book CL, HO, RB, NG, GC, SI, HG, ZC, ZS, ZW; rates book ZF, ZN, ZB, UB (micros where they exist for sizing).
- holding period and expected trades per year: open → close of each root's day session (commodities: pit-hour opens 08:20/08:30/09:00 ET to 13:30/14:30 ET settlements; rates 08:20 → 15:00 ET); every day, ≈ 250 × roots-per-book position changes → 2,500 (commodities) and 1,000 (rates) round trips per year.
