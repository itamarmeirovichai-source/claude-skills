### C-5-12 ES–SPY (and NQ–QQQ) one-minute basis dislocation as a liquidity-stress gate for existing ES/NQ sleeves — never as an arbitrage
- mechanism: the futures–ETF basis is held by arbitrageurs in milliseconds (Budish–Cramton–Shim-type races), so a basis that stays dislocated for a whole minute on 1-minute closes is a symptom, not an opportunity: arbitrage capital has stepped back (stress, halts, LULD pauses, roll days, dividend dates). The tradable use at retail latency is an **inactivity rule**: when the 1-minute |basis − rolling median| exceeds k × its rolling MAD, suspend new entries in the ES/NQ sleeves for the next n minutes, because realized spread and slippage are then far above the common-brief floors. Who is forced: nobody; the gate protects the bot from paying the stress spread.
- evidence: own idea built on documented facts: futures lead ETFs at 1 second (NQ → QQQ asymmetry +0.077 [0.071, 0.084], ES → SPY +0.042 [0.038, 0.047]; repo as in C-5-11, fetched 2026-11-03) and the lead is ≈ 2 s; the information share of E-mini vs ETF rises in high volatility (cited inside arXiv 2408.05659, abstract via search 2026-11-03, abstract only). No study of 1-minute basis persistence after 2020 was found (search 2026-11-03). Post-2015 OOS: none for the gate.
- evidence window: NQ/ES/QQQ/SPY 2016–2026 (lead measurement only); no evidence window for the gate itself.
- instruments: signal from SPY/QQQ 1-minute (one year in hand) vs ES/NQ 1-minute; applied to ES/MES, NQ/MNQ sleeves. FTMO: US500/US100 CFD spreads already widen in stress, so the gate is redundant there.
- holding period and expected trades per year: overlay; suppresses perhaps 2–5% of minutes.
- data needed: SPY/QQQ 1-minute (in hand, one year; Alpaca free key pending for more), ES/NQ ohlcv-1m ($6–7 per root RTH); dividend and rate adjustment of the basis from free FRED/Yahoo series. $0–14.
- observability: **1m-only** (basis from two 1-minute closes; time-alignment to the second matters — the repo found vendor timestamps one hour late for 90% of history, so the first task is a timestamp audit).
- expected gross edge per trade: none; the value is avoided cost. Economics screen: n/a (overlay); registered only as a conditioner of a sleeve that has its own row.
- firm structure fit: intraday futures and overnight-session futures (SPY/QQQ exist only 04:00–20:00 ET, so the gate is RTH + extended hours only); touches no clause.
- nearest closed relative: none closed; sibling of the realized-vol gates in lane 7; C-5-11.
- decay haircut: 0.7 (original); split dates 2020-03 (COVID basis dislocations), 2022 (0DTE era).

### C-5-13 Bar-derived depth proxy: minute-level Kyle lambda / realized Amihud (|Δp| per contract traded, range per contract traded) as a depth-regime conditioner — depth without a book feed
- mechanism: top-of-book depth is what makes a 1-minute signal executable at the quoted spread; retail bars carry no depth, but price impact per unit volume is observable from bars: λ̂_t = |close_t − close_{t−1}| / volume_t (and range_t / volume_t) averaged over the last 15–30 minutes, normalised by the time-of-day profile, is the inverse of effective depth. When λ̂ is in its lowest tercile the book is thick (liquidity providers present, moves are flow-driven and revert); when it is in its top tercile the book is thin (moves are information-driven and continue, and slippage is multiples of the floor). The conditioner says which sleeve (reversal vs continuation) may trade and when to stay out; it is the bar-only stand-in for the book depth the common brief says we cannot see. Who is forced: liquidity providers who widen or withdraw at predictable times (open, settlement, news), leaving the same aggressive flow to move price further.
- evidence: own construction; measurement background peer-reviewed: "Realized Illiquidity" (AFA session paper, https://afajof.org/management/viewp.php?n=31640, snippet via search 2026-11-03, abstract only): realized Amihud from 5-minute returns measures price impact accurately and noise becomes negligible at the 1-minute frequency; Lou & Shu (2016/2017, RFS) show that the daily Amihud return premium is mostly a volume effect, not price impact (https://www.lehigh.edu/~jms408/Lu_2014.pdf, snippet, abstract only) — a warning that λ̂ may be a volume proxy. Depth and spread move predictably across the day in ES (Takahashi 2025, arXiv 2508.06788, abstract, fetched 2026-11-03). Post-2015 OOS: none for the conditioner.
- evidence window: none for the construction; Amihud literature is daily US equities 1964–2015.
- instruments: ES/MES, NQ/MNQ, CL/MCL, GC/MGC (any contract with reliable bar volume; CFD volume at FTMO is broker-internal, so futures only).
- holding period and expected trades per year: overlay on sleeves; the terciles change state a few times a day.
- data needed: ohlcv-1m for each root (Databento $6–7 per root RTH, $20 full session); validation of λ̂ against true top-of-book depth needs one week of `bbo-1m` or `tbbo` for ES (small; `get_cost` first). Total inside the ceiling.
- observability: **1m-only** for λ̂ itself; **1s-pilot** sharpens the estimate (λ̂ from 1-second changes); calibration of λ̂ against depth needs `bbo-1m` (cheap) — the only paid step.
- expected gross edge per trade: none standalone; as a conditioner it must raise a sleeve's gross edge in the admitted state by ≥ 1 ES tick to matter. Economics screen: n/a (overlay).
- firm structure fit: all four; inactivity rules must still satisfy consistency/inactivity clauses (stated per sleeve).
- nearest closed relative: none closed; lane 7 realized-vol regimes are the price-only sibling; pre-sweep P-C19 (book skew, needs MBP-1) is the book-feed version.
- decay haircut: 0.7 (original); split dates 2019-05 (micros split depth across two books), 2022 (0DTE era), 2020-09 (ES settlement window moved).
- plausibility of net Sharpe ≥ 1.5: low — it is a measurement device for executability and regime, not an edge; its test is whether it beats a plain realized-vol tercile, which the Lou–Shu result suggests it may not.
- original: yes

### C-5-14 Minute-sampled top-of-book imbalance from Databento `bbo-1m` (bid size vs ask size at the minute boundary) as a one-minute-ahead conditioner — the cheapest honest look at depth
- mechanism: the queue imbalance at the best quotes predicts the sign of the next mid move (Cont–Kukanov–Stoikov OFI; Databento's own skew example in pre-sweep P-C19) because the thin side is the one that will be depleted first; at the millisecond horizon the effect is strong and fully arbitraged, at a 1-minute sampling it is a weak conditioner: a bar that closes with a lopsided book is more likely to open the next bar on the thin side. For a retail bot the only admissible use is passive: place the resting order of an existing sleeve on the thick side when the imbalance agrees, or skip the entry when it disagrees. Who is forced: market makers rebalancing lopsided queues.
- evidence: vendor documentation: Databento, "BBO on interval (bbo-1s, bbo-1m)", https://databento.com/docs/schemas-and-data-formats/bbo (fetched 2026-11-03): last best bid/offer with sizes and order counts plus last sale with aggressor side at each 1-s or 1-m boundary, a subset of MBP-1; forward-filled when nothing changed; dataset coverage not stated on the page (GLBX.MDP3 to be confirmed with `get_cost`). Predictability background: Lucchese–Pakkanen–Veraart 2024 and Kolm–Turiel–Westray 2023 (pre-sweep A-C35: predictability ubiquitous at HF, no net profit at retail latency); Cont–Kukanov–Stoikov 2014 (A-C38). Treasury null at 100 ms with full book: Microstructure Lab (C-5-11). Post-2015 OOS for a 1-minute-sampled book imbalance: none found (search 2026-11-03).
- evidence window: equities and index futures tick data 2010–2022 for the HF literature; none at 1-minute sampling.
- instruments: ES/MES, NQ/MNQ (sizes in contracts; micro books are separate and thinner — use the E-mini book as the signal for micro execution).
- holding period and expected trades per year: 1-minute conditioner on sleeves with minute-scale entries (lanes 3, 4, 9).
- data needed: `bbo-1m` ES and NQ 2021–2026 — a 1-minute sampled L1 schema should be far smaller than `tbbo`; price unknown until `get_cost` (no paid call is allowed until the Databento credit is confirmed; board 2026-11-04). The aggressor-side last sale in the same record is a free by-product that lets us calibrate every bar-only proxy in lane 9 at minute resolution.
- observability: **needs `bbo-1m`** (not ohlcv); the same purchase calibrates C-9-01/05/06/07; **impossible-retail** for any active crossing of the spread on this signal.
- expected gross edge per trade: as a passive-side conditioner perhaps 0.1–0.3 tick of improved fill per trade; fails the economics screen standalone, registered only as an overlay.
- firm structure fit: intraday and overnight-session futures; minimum-hold clauses (Lucid 5 s, Tradeify 10 s) are not touched by a passive entry conditioner.
- nearest closed relative: negative-evidence item "order-book deep learning at retail latency" (this is a one-feature linear conditioner, not a book model); P-C19, A-C35.
- decay haircut: 0.7 (original use of a vendor schema); split dates 2019-05 (micros), 2020-09 (settlement move), 2022 (0DTE).
- plausibility of net Sharpe ≥ 1.5: low — the literature says the book's information is consumed within seconds; whatever survives to the minute boundary is a fill-quality improvement, not a strategy.

### C-5-15 Cross-asset flow divergence: BVC-signed minute flow in the leader (ES) not yet matched in the follower (NQ, RTY, YM) — flow-based lead–lag instead of price-based
- mechanism: price-based lead–lag at 1 minute is dead (C-5-11) because arbitrage equalises prices; signed flow is not arbitraged. If institutional index flow hits ES first (futures overlays, risk-parity, vol-control desks rebalance through ES) and sector-specific flow hits NQ/RTY later or not at all, then a minute in which ES shows a large same-sign BVC imbalance while NQ/RTY flow is flat should be followed by follower flow — and by follower price movement in excess of what the price co-movement already delivered. Who is forced: multi-asset rebalancers executing through the most liquid proxy first and then completing in the sector contracts.
- evidence: own idea; components: BVC from bars (Easley–López de Prado–O'Hara 2016, JFE 120(2):269–285, pre-sweep A-C38; RePEc listing https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jfinec/v120y2016i2p269-285.html fetched 2026-11-03, abstract only) and the liquidity-share result that the price lead follows relative dollar volume (repo in C-5-11). Negative prior: Andersen–Bondarenko 2015 (Review of Finance 19(1):1–54, https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:oup:revfin:v:19:y:2015:i:1:p:1-54., abstract via search 2026-11-03): BVC is inferior to a tick rule on E-mini and its errors correlate with volume and volatility — so a "flow divergence" built from BVC may be a volatility divergence in disguise. Post-2015 OOS: none.
- evidence window: none for the construction; BVC validation literature ES 2008–2012.
- instruments: leader ES; followers NQ/MNQ, RTY/M2K, YM/MYM.
- holding period and expected trades per year: 5–15 minutes; a few hundred signals per year at a 2-σ divergence threshold.
- data needed: ohlcv-1m ES, NQ, RTY, YM 2018–2026 (≈ $28 RTH at $7 per root; the ES/NQ copies in hand serve the cross-check only); one week of `bbo-1m`/`tbbo` to replace BVC by true aggressor flow in the calibration (C-5-14).
- observability: **1m-only** for the BVC version; **1s-pilot** replaces BVC with a 1-second tick rule (C-9-05) if the 1m version shows gross t > 3; the true-flow version needs `tbbo` (cheap for a week, not for years).
- expected gross edge per trade: unknown; must clear ≈ 2.3 bp in RTY (RTY round trip ≈ 0.2 pt + $5 ≈ 1.1 bp) to pass the economics screen; 2 × cost run required.
- firm structure fit: intraday futures; also overnight-session futures (flow is thinner, BVC noisier).
- nearest closed relative: negative-evidence item "1-minute lead-lag without a mechanism" (this names a flow mechanism and uses a non-arbitraged variable); A-C22/A-C38; C-5-11.

- plausibility of net Sharpe ≥ 1.5: low — the mechanism is coherent but the measuring instrument (BVC on 1-minute bars) is documented to be noisy exactly when volume is high, which is when the signal fires.

### C-9-01 BVC minute imbalance → 5-minute reversal/continuation in ES, NQ, CL, GC, calibrated first against a one-week aggressor-flag sample (extends pre-sweep A-C38 with the Andersen–Bondarenko critique as the pre-registered failure mode)
- mechanism: signed order flow moves price through inventory and information; bulk volume classification assigns each bar's volume to buys and sells by the standardised price change (V·Φ(Δp/σ)), giving signed flow without ticks. Lagged flow imbalance predicts short-horizon reversal when flow is uninformed (inventory pressure) and continuation when informed. Who is forced: liquidity providers unwinding inventory after one-sided minutes. The new twist versus A-C38: the pre-registration names the failure mode in advance — Andersen–Bondarenko show BVC's classification errors correlate with volume and volatility, so the test includes a volume-and-volatility-matched null and a go/no-go calibration step (BVC vs true aggressor side on one week of `tbbo`/`bbo-1m`, pass only if agreement ≥ 75% at 1 minute).
- evidence: peer-reviewed (abstract only, SSRN blocked): Easley, López de Prado, O'Hara (2016), "Discerning information from trade data", JFE 120(2):269–285, https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jfinec/v120y2016i2p269-285.html (fetched 2026-11-03): BVC and tick rules both classify the aggressor side reasonably; BVC relates better to information proxies. ### C-9-02 VPIN from bars (volume-bucket imbalance) as an inactivity/regime gate — pre-registered with the expectation that it adds nothing beyond volume and realized volatility
- mechanism: VPIN (volume-synchronised probability of informed trading) is the rolling mean of |buy − sell| / bucket volume over n volume buckets; high VPIN is said to precede liquidity withdrawals and volatility (the "toxicity" story around the 2010 flash crash). A bot can only use it as a gate: no new entries when VPIN is above its 90th percentile, because that is when spreads widen and reversals fail. Who is forced: market makers withdrawing when flow turns one-sided. Honest prior: the gate is probably a noisier version of a realized-vol gate.
- evidence: peer-reviewed: Easley, López de Prado, O'Hara (2012), "Flow Toxicity and Liquidity in a High-frequency World", RFS 25(5):1457–1493 (not fetched; cited in A-C38); the "volume clock" companion: Easley, López de Prado, O'Hara (2012), "The Volume Clock: Insights into the High-Frequency Paradigm", Journal of Portfolio Management 39(1):19–29, https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2034858 (SSRN blocked; publisher listing via search 2026-11-03, abstract only). Peer-reviewed refutation: Andersen & Bondarenko (2015) as in C-9-01: controlling for trading intensity and volatility, BVC-VPIN has no incremental predictive power for future volatility; VPIN unsuitable as an early-warning signal. Practitioner implementation from 1-minute bars with code but no predictive statistics: MQL5, "Market Microstructure in MQL5 (Part 6): Order Flow", https://www.mql5.com/en/articles/22939 (fetched 2026-11-03): NQ M1 Jan 2024–Jun 2026, close-location VPIN_OHLC rises ≈ 6% in two stress regimes, r = 0.33 with the author's noise ratio; author states it is not the ELO VPIN. Post-2015 OOS: none as a trading gate.
- evidence window: ES 2008–2012 (both sides of the debate); NQ 2024–2026 descriptive only.
- instruments: ES/MES, NQ/MNQ, CL/MCL (where bar volume is exchange volume; not CFDs).
- holding period and expected trades per year: overlay; active ≈ 10% of the time by construction.
- data needed: ohlcv-1m (as C-9-01); $0 incremental if C-9-01 data is bought.
- observability: **1m-only** (volume buckets assembled from minute volume; bucket size = 1/50 of average daily volume); **1s-pilot** gives finer buckets but the critique is about classification, not resolution.
- expected gross edge per trade: none; test statistic is the difference in a sleeve's net Sharpe with the VPIN gate vs a matched realized-vol gate at equal time-out-of-market. Economics screen: n/a (overlay).
- firm structure fit: all futures structures; the gate interacts with inactivity rules (state the maximum consecutive gated days).
- nearest closed relative: G1 (VRP/VIX — not a product here); lane-7 vol terciles; A-C38.
- decay haircut: 0.5 (published); split 2010-05 (flash crash), 2019-05, 2022.
- plausibility of net Sharpe ≥ 1.5: low — the strongest independent test says the measure is volume × volatility with extra noise; it is registered so that the comparison is on record, not because it is expected to win.
- original: no

### C-9-03 Volume-clock bars assembled from 1-minute volume: constant-volume buckets as the sampling grid for existing breakout/mean-reversion rules (time-bar rules re-run in volume time)
- mechanism: markets process information in volume time, not clock time; returns sampled by volume are closer to Gaussian and less heteroskedastic (ELO 2012 volume clock; AFML ch. 2 dollar bars). A rule that fires after "n bars" in clock time mixes dead lunch minutes with news minutes; in volume time each bar carries comparable information, so breakout thresholds and reversal z-scores are better calibrated and fewer trades happen in dead periods (fewer cost events). Who is forced: nobody — this is a sampling change, so any edge belongs to the underlying rule; the hypothesis is that the same rule nets more per cost unit in volume time.
- evidence: peer-reviewed: ELO (2012) JPM (abstract only, as C-9-02). Working paper with code-level detail: Fayyaz, Jabbar, Qureshi, Jalil (2026), "A Frequency-Controlled Comparison of Tick- and Minute-Based Information Bars for Cryptocurrency Markets", arXiv 2608.26158 (abstract, fetched 2026-11-03): BTCUSDT 2020–2025; dollar/volume/volatility/range/Renko bars built from 1-minute OHLCV retain most of the statistical benefit of tick-built bars for dollar bars, less for bars that depend on intra-minute activity; tick volatility bars cut serial dependence 69% vs minute baseline (|VR(4)−1| 0.028 vs 0.089); no forecasting test. Hudson & Thames (2019), "Does Meta-Labeling Add to Signal Efficacy?", https://hudsonthames.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/04/Does-Meta-Labeling-Add-to-Signal-Efficacy.pdf (PDF read 2026-11-03): dollar bars had the smallest Jarque–Bera statistic among tick/volume/dollar/time bars on ES. ### C-9-07 Wick-asymmetry absorption proxy: volume-weighted (upper wick − lower wick)/range over the last k minutes → short-horizon reversal against the wick
- mechanism: a long upper wick on a high-volume minute means aggressive buying met resting offers that did not move (depth absorbed the flow); the buyers are now long at worse prices than the close and the resting seller is still there, so the next minutes lean down. The proxy is W_t = Σ_{k} v_i · [(H_i − max(O_i,C_i)) − (min(O_i,C_i) − L_i)] / (H_i − L_i) over the last k = 3–5 minutes, normalised by the volume profile; the rule fades W (sell when W is large positive) with a 5–10-minute horizon and a 1-tick-stress exit. Distinct from C-9-06: CLV measures where the close sits; W measures rejection symmetric about the body, which is the absorption footprint. Who is forced: aggressive takers who were absorbed by passive depth and now hold losing inventory.
- evidence: own idea. Nearest literature: Nagel-type liquidity provision after large minute moves (pre-sweep, C-4-04/C-4-06 in the academic file) and the ES finding that depth and spreads vary predictably through the day (Takahashi 2025, abstract). No study of wick asymmetry as an order-flow proxy with validation was found (search 2026-11-03); the construction is folklore in candlestick trading, which lowers the decay haircut's effectiveness rather than raising plausibility.
- evidence window: none.
- instruments: ES/MES, NQ/MNQ, GC/MGC (gold's tick/price ratio makes 1-tick stress survivable, C-4-06), CL/MCL.
- holding period and expected trades per year: 5–10 minutes; 200–500 per root per year at a 2-σ threshold on W.
- data needed: ohlcv-1m (as above; $0 incremental); calibration against aggressor-signed volume per C-9-05 to check that W is not a volatility proxy.
- observability: **1m-only** (O/H/L/C/V suffice); **1s-pilot** could locate the wick inside the minute (early vs late rejection), only after gross t > 3.
- expected gross edge per trade: must reach ≈ 5.6 ES ticks at 2 σ to pass; a reversal of one third of the wick on a 4-tick wick is ≈ 1.3 ticks — the screen is failed unless the rule is restricted to the top decile of wick × volume, where 10-tick wicks occur (news minutes, which the MFFU/Lucid news clauses forbid).
- firm structure fit: intraday futures; the news-window exclusion removes a large part of the signal mass — stated as a clause cost in the row.
- nearest closed relative: H0013/H0014 (IBS reversal, closed — IBS is a daily close-location measure; this is a minute-level wick measure with volume weighting, a materially different construction, but the lineage is declared); C-4-04/C-4-06 (price-only burst fades).
- decay haircut: 0.7 (original); split dates 2019-05, 2022 (0DTE), 2020-09.
- plausibility of net Sharpe ≥ 1.5: low — the arithmetic above says the typical wick is smaller than two round trips; only the news-minute tail is large enough and the firms forbid trading it.
- original: yes

### C-9-08 Volume-surprise persistence as the footprint of VWAP/TWAP order splitting: abnormal minute volume with same-sign flow for k minutes → continuation to the end of the parent order's clock

### C-10-03 Labelling as a pre-registered design choice: triple-barrier vs trend-scanning vs fixed-horizon labels on one existing sleeve, same features, same CPCV — decided before any model is fit
- mechanism: the label defines what the model learns; fixed-horizon labels ignore the path (a trade stopped out at −1 R but up at the horizon is labelled a win), triple-barrier labels encode the stop and target that the firm rules impose anyway (daily loss, max loss), and trend-scanning labels (sign and t-stat of the best-fitting linear trend over look-forward windows) suit continuation sleeves. For a bot whose exits are rule-based, the label must match the exit — otherwise the model optimises a trade it will never place. Who is forced: nobody; this is methodology registered as a row so the choice cannot be made after seeing results.

### C-10-04 ML on the cost side, not the signal side: a pre-registered model of next-minute executability (realized-spread/slippage proxy) from bar features, used to time the entries of existing sleeves

Target variable: a bar-level executability proxy — next-minute range / tick, next-minute volume vs profile, Corwin–Schultz high–low spread estimator at 1 minute, bar-Amihud (C-5-13) — calibrated once against true spread and depth from one week of `bbo-1m`/`tbbo`. Who is forced: nobody; the bot avoids paying liquidity providers at their most expensive hours.
