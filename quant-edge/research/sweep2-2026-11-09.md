### SW2-01 — Famous-firm post-close earnings-cluster eve: ES/NQ long 18:00 ET (T−1) → 15:55 ET (T)
- mechanism: on the few days when several high-attention firms report after the close, investors (retail and attention-driven
  institutions) buy the announcers and the index ahead of the event; dealers/market makers demand a premium to carry index
  inventory into a known, concentrated variance event (announcement-risk premium at the market level). The effect is earned
  before the release and is uncorrelated with the announcement outcome (no leakage), so it is a priced-risk/attention premium,
  not information. Persistence argument: the premium is a compensation for bearing scheduled jump risk; it is small in aggregate
  (12 days/yr) and requires holding beta into a jump, which deters arbitrage.
- evidence: Chen, Cohen, Wang, "Famous Firms, Earnings Clusters, and the Stock Market" (SSRN 3685452, WP 2020/2021). FETCHED the
  CXO Advisory summary https://www.cxoadvisory.com/calendar-effects/three-high-attention-earnings-announcement-clusters-drive-market/
  (2026-11-09): E-mini S&P 1999–2018, three most prominent PM clusters per earnings month (Jan/Apr/Jul/Oct) = 12 days/yr;
  close-to-close +0.34% vs +0.02% typical day; split ≈ 0.11% overnight (evening before) + 0.23% day session; only PM clusters, not AM;
  prominence = prior-year Dow Jones news count; weaker but significant with count or market-cap definitions; "annualized Sharpe 4.5
  on these days" (per-day). FETCHED https://wallstreethorizon.com/news/famours-firms-earnings-clusters (abstract only: "17x the norm").
  Related, SNIPPET only: AQR "The Earnings Announcement Premium and Trading Volume" (premium strong in large caps); Ogneva–Xia–Ye
  (index futures move on large-firm releases; PDF 403).
- evidence window: 1999–2018 (in-sample); 2019–2024 is post-publication (6 y) inside our OOS window.
- rule sketch (all fixed ex ante): famous-firm set = the 10 largest S&P 500 members by market cap at the prior 31 Dec plus a frozen
  attention list {AAPL, AMZN, MSFT, GOOGL, META, NFLX, TSLA, NVDA, INTC, IBM} (union, ≤ 20 names). An after-close (AMC) release date is a
  cluster day if ≥ 3 set members report AMC that date. In each Jan/Apr/Jul/Oct take the up-to-3 cluster days with the most set members
  (ties → earlier date). Trade: long 1 ES (variant pre-registered as a second arm: NQ) at the 18:00 ET Globex open of the evening before
  (T−1), exit 15:55 ET on T (before the releases and before every firm deadline). No stop. Size at 1% daily vol target from 20-day ES
  realized vol at T−1.
- data in hand: PARTLY. ES/NQ 1h 2010-06..2026 (Databento) in hand; earnings dates/timing NOT in hand — free from SEC EDGAR 8-K item 2.02
  acceptance timestamps (after 16:00 ET = AMC) for ≤ 20 tickers (reachable, $0, labour ≈ 1 day; acceptance time is point-in-time).
- gross per trade vs cost: in-sample +34 bp close-to-close; haircut 0.5 → ≈ 17 bp vs ES RT 1.2 bp (14×); passes the screen easily.
- trades/yr: 12.
- realistic decayed S: per-trade Sharpe in-sample ≈ 0.34/1.2 ≈ 0.28 → annual 0.28·√12 ≈ 0.98 gross; × 0.5 haircut → ≈ 0.48 net
  (costs negligible). As a standalone sleeve it is mostly flat cash; S is per-year of the sleeve.
- OOS years: 10 (2015–2024); 6 post-publication (2019–2024).
- criterion-4 power: Φ(0.48·√10 − 3.6807) = 0.015 (6 y: 0.006). Even at the unhaircut S ≈ 0.98: Φ(3.10 − 3.68) = 0.28.
- honest prior: 0.15 (clear mechanism, futures-level evidence by the authors, cheap to implement; but 12 events/yr and the 2019–24
  era of mega-cap concentration could amplify or arbitrage it).
- closed-family check: none. Not H0023 (turn-of-month), not E2 (macro releases), not the bare OPEX/day-of-week rows; IG C-12-24
  (buyback dip buying) is stock-flow based and different. Not in candidates.md.
- firm-structure fit: overnight-session futures (18:00 → 15:55 is inside one trading day; flat before Topstep 16:10 ET) — IF/ONF;
  also own capital; FTMO intraday CFD on US500/US100 is possible only for the 09:30 → 15:55 day leg (≈ 2/3 of the published return),
  because the 18:00 ET entry sits at the FTMO day boundary (00:00 CE(S)T) — treat FI as a weaker second arm.
- power×prior: 0.015 × 0.15 = 0.002. Value is as a stackable sleeve, not as a standalone pass.

### SW2-02 — Bond-risk-day UST → SPX intraday lead (Hu–Jin–Pan) — DECLINED (economics + negative-evidence row)
- mechanism: on days when the intraday stock–bond correlation is high ("bond risky days": rates/inflation/dealer-capacity shocks),
  Treasury futures lead equity futures at 5-minute horizon; on other days equities lead bonds negatively.
- evidence: Hu, Jin, Pan, "The Stock-Bond Correlation: A Tale of Two Days in the U.S. Treasury Market" (AFA 2025). FETCHED slides
  https://en.saif.sjtu.edu.cn/junpan/slides_Corr_AFA.pdf (2026-11-09; read with pdftotext): correlation from 5-min ES and ZN futures
  returns of day t; risky days = top 20%; regression SPX(i+1) on UST(i): coefficient 0.15 (t 2.65), R² 0.39%, 70,147 five-minute obs.
  QuantSeeker recap (FETCHED https://www.quantseeker.com/p/weekly-research-recap-0e7, post 2025-09-30) summarises the same paper.
- why declined: (1) the regime is measured with day-t data (same-day correlation) → not tradable as stated; an ex-ante version must use
  the running correlation up to bar i or day t−1, which dilutes it; (2) predicted ES move ≈ 0.15 × ZN 5-min σ (≈ 1.5 bp) ≈ 0.2 bp per
  5-min bar vs ES RT 1.2 bp → fails max(2 ticks, 2×RT) by ≈ 10×; (3) inside the negative-evidence row "1-minute lead-lag without a
  mechanism" in spirit (this one has a mechanism, but the size is the documented sub-cost ratio). Power ≈ 0. Useful only as a REGIME
  variable (see SW2-03), not as a signal.

### SW2-03 — Flight-to-safety overshoot unwind: short GC and ZN 18:00 ET → 14:55 ET after a safety day
- mechanism: on flight-to-safety days (equities down hard, bonds/gold bid, stock–bond correlation strongly negative), forced de-risking
  and safety demand push Treasury convenience yield and gold above fundamental levels; the demand is temporary (Baur–Lucey: gold is a
  haven for ≈ 15 trading days after the shock, then loses value as stocks recover). Who is paid: the liquidity provider who sells the
  haven asset to forced buyers. Persistence: de-risking is constraint-driven (VaR, margin, vol targets), not information-driven.
- evidence: Baur & Lucey, "Is gold a hedge or a safe haven?", Financial Review 45(2) 2010 — SNIPPET (abstract via search; CXO summary
  https://www.cxoadvisory.com/gold/gold-as-hedge-and-safe-haven/ seen as snippet): haven only ≈ 15 trading days after extreme shocks,
  returns turn negative as stocks recover; no quantified next-day reversal. Hu–Jin–Pan slides (FETCHED, above): on safety days the
  aggregate market averages −36 bp, convenience yield widens, term premium shrinks; no reversal horizon reported.
- evidence window: 1979–2009 (Baur–Lucey daily); 2000s–2024 (Hu–Jin–Pan).
- rule sketch: day t is a safety day if ES 09:30→16:00 ET return ≤ −1.25% AND the hourly ES–ZN return correlation over t's 23 Globex
  hours is ≤ −0.5 (1h bars in hand; ex-ante at 16:00 because all bars are closed). Trade: short 1 GC-equivalent and short ZN at
  equal 1% daily vol each, 18:00 ET (t) → 14:55 ET (t+1). No stop. No re-entry on consecutive safety days (one trade per cluster).
- data in hand: YES — ES/ZN 1h 2010-06..2026 (Databento), GC 1m 2010-06..2026 (Databento).
- gross vs cost: unknown; plausible 10–25 bp per leg per event if the haven premium decays over ≈ 15 days (≈ 1/15 of a 2–4% haven move
  per day) vs GC RT 1.3 bp, ZN RT ≈ 3 bp → passes the screen IF it exists.
- trades/yr: ≈ 10–15 events.
- realistic decayed S: per-event SR ≈ 0.10 (10 bp on ≈ 100 bp overnight-session σ), × √12 ≈ 0.35, two partly correlated legs → ≈ 0.4.
- OOS years: 10 (2015–2024).
- criterion-4 power: Φ(0.4·√10 − 3.68) = 0.008.
- honest prior: 0.05 (mechanism plausible; no source measures a next-day reversal; gold's safe-haven behaviour has drifted since 2022).
- closed-family check: not G1 (VRP), not G2 (dip buying is the opposite asset), not E2. Not in candidates.md (C-12-04 margin-hike
  liquidation in GC is about margin events, not haven demand).
- firm fit: overnight-session futures (18:00 → 14:55 inside one trading day) — ONF; own capital.
- power×prior: 0.0004.

### SW2-04 — Oil-shock → equity underreaction (Driesprong–Jacobsen–Maat), weekly ES tilt — LOW
- mechanism: investors underreact to oil-price information for equity cash flows; a rise in oil predicts lower equity returns over the
  following weeks/months.
- evidence: Driesprong, Jacobsen, Maat, "Striking oil: Another puzzle?", JFE 89(2) 2008 — SNIPPET (abstract via search, econpapers):
  monthly data ≈ 30 y, 12 of 18 countries; authors caution results are in-sample; a 2011 Univ. of Chile WP (dt-406, SNIPPET) claims
  out-of-sample predictive power. No 2015+ evidence found.
- rule sketch: every Friday 15:50 ET, x = WTI (FRED DCOILWTICO) 4-week log change; if x ≥ +10% short ES, if x ≤ −10% long ES, hold
  to next Friday 15:50 ET (own capital) — 2–6 signals/yr in normal years.
- data in hand: YES (FRED WTI daily; ES 1h).
- gross vs cost: monthly-scale effects ≈ 50–100 bp per signal in-sample, cost negligible.
- trades/yr: ≈ 4; S ≈ 0.15 after haircut. OOS 10 y → power ≈ 0.0005.
- prior 0.03 (old, weekly-horizon, beta-like; oil–equity correlation flipped sign after 2008).
- closed-family check: not D2 (FX/CFD trend+carry) nor H0007 (own-asset trend) — cross-asset. Firm fit: own capital only (multi-day
  futures hold); FTMO Swing US500 short earns swap but long pays ≈ −8%/yr. power×prior ≈ 0.

### SW2-05 — Semi-monthly payday / 401(k) contribution flow (mid-month) — DECLINED (H0023-adjacent, faded)
- mechanism: payroll-deducted retirement contributions are invested with a settlement lag; uninformed buying pressure around the 15th
  and month-end.
- evidence: AEA 2022 preliminary paper (redirect not followed; seen as SNIPPET only): higher mid-month returns for stocks heavily held
  by retirement funds; Ma & Pratt (2017) "Payday anomaly" (cited only, not seen). FETCHED https://payday-effect.pages.dev/payday-effect/
  (2026-11-09; personal site, no OOS): +0.055%/day peak, p = 0.019, 7–8 trading-day lag, concentrated on Fridays, "fading in the 2010s",

- why declined: the month-end leg is the closed H0023 (turn-of-month, net t 1.56, beta); the mid-month leg is ≈ 5 bp/day with a
  daily σ ≈ 100 bp → per-day SR 0.05, ≈ 24 days/yr → S ≈ 0.25 before haircut → power ≈ 0.001; evidence is a single personal page.

### SW2-06 — Mortgage-convexity hedging continuation in UB/ZN, gated by refinancing-sensitivity regime
- mechanism: when long rates move sharply, MBS durations extend (sell-offs) or shorten (rallies); servicers and MBS hedgers trade
  Treasury futures/swaps in the SAME direction with a lag of hours to days (the "convexity event" feedback loop). Who is forced:
  negatively-convex MBS holders/hedgers. State dependence: the effect is large only when a big share of mortgages is near the money
  (refinancing incentive near zero), and smaller when the Fed holds a large share of MBS (2009–2022 QE) — a regime variable fixes when
  to trade.
- evidence: Reuters (via Kitco) 2026-10-12, SNIPPET: "US yield surge triggers mortgage hedging, adds to bond market pressure" — analysts
  say impact is smaller than in past sell-offs and fades as more mortgages lock in; NY Fed 2013 analysis (SNIPPET via search) of the
  2013 taper sell-off; RBS estimate ≈ $150 bn 10y-equivalents per 25 bp (SNIPPET). Peer-reviewed background (not fetched this session):
  Hanson, "Mortgage convexity", JFE 2014 (daily/monthly excess-return predictability from MBS duration). Evidence is mostly journalistic.
- evidence window: 1990s–2013 (Hanson); anecdotes 2013, 2023, 2026.
- rule sketch: refinancing-sensitivity regime ON if the FRED 30-year mortgage rate (MORTGAGE30US, weekly, last print before t) is
  within 0.75 pp of its trailing 3-year minimum (many loans near the money → strongly negative convexity). On regime-ON days, if the UB
  day-session return 08:20→15:00 ET is ≤ −1.5 σ (20-day σ of that window) → short UB 18:00 ET → 14:55 ET next day; if ≥ +1.5 σ → long.
  ZN as the second arm. Regime-OFF days: no trade (control arm reported, not counted).
- data in hand: YES — UB/ZN 1h 2010-06..2026 (Databento), FRED MORTGAGE30US.
- gross vs cost: hedging flow ≈ 10–20 bp of UB price per event if continuation exists vs UB RT ≈ 5–6 bp → borderline 2×RT.
- trades/yr: ≈ 12–20 (regime ON roughly 2010–13, 2016, 2019–21; OFF most of 2022–24 because rates far above 3-y minima) → OOS events
  concentrated in 2015–2021.
- realistic decayed S: ≈ 0.3.
- OOS years: 10 nominal, ≈ 5 effective (regime-ON years).
- criterion-4 power: Φ(0.3·√10 − 3.68) = 0.003.
- honest prior: 0.04 (mechanism real, but Fed MBS holdings 2010–2022 muted it, and continuation over a day is easily front-run).
- closed-family check: not H0007/IG C-11-04 (no trend lookback; a one-day flow continuation conditioned on a state); not E2 (no release).
- firm fit: overnight-session futures (18:00 → 14:55 inside one trading day) — ONF; own capital.
- power×prior: 0.0001.

### SW2-07 — Hour-of-day return periodicity (Heston–Korajczyk–Sadka) transferred to 10 CME roots — DECLINED (economics)
- mechanism: recurring institutional order flow at the same time each day (VWAP slices at the open, MOC at the close) creates
  same-slot return persistence for weeks.
- evidence: HKS, JF 2010, stocks (SNIPPET: arXiv 1005.3535 abstract); Haendler–Heston–Korajczyk–Sadka, "The Intra-Day Stock Return
  Periodicity Puzzle", SSRN 5749704, Nov 2025 (SNIPPET): open periodicity driven by VWAP trading, close periodicity by MOC trading.
  No futures evidence found; a TradingView script asserts it for NQ (not evidence).
- why declined: the close-slot version is inside H0006/H0019 (last half hour); any 1-hour slot trade costs ES 1.2 bp / ZN 3 bp / NG
  ≈ 8 bp RT against a stock-level slot premium of ≈ 1–2 bp, and the futures crowd has no VWAP/MOC clock outside the cash open/close.
  Fails max(2 ticks, 2×RT). Power ≈ 0.
