# PART E — FOR THE OWNER (English)

## The diagnosis — 5 brutal bullets
**Verify each one before saying it out loud** — these are the five failures a WordPress + Elementor restaurant site of this kind almost always has, each confirmable in ten minutes.

1. **It loads like a brochure.** Run PageSpeed Insights on the Boca page, mobile. Elementor plus lazy-loaded images usually lands LCP over 4s. Every second past 2.5s costs orders.
2. **The menu is decoration, not data.** Is every dish name and price selectable text? If it sits in an image or a PDF, Google can't read it, screen readers can't, and nothing can filter or search it.
3. **The hours lie.** Look at the site on a Friday and a Saturday afternoon. Static text cannot express "closed Friday, opens an hour after Shabbat" — the thing customers most need, and the thing no competitor has to solve.
4. **The brand is louder than the website.** Kong, the red, the yellow, "EVERYBODY'S A WINNER" live in the restaurant and on the packaging. The site is a template with the logo dropped on top.
5. **Nothing proves the burger.** The strongest asset you own is what the Loaded Smashed looks like inside, and the site never shows it.

## The pitch
**What's wrong now.** You're the first Glatt kosher smashed burger restaurant in America. Your site says "a burger place." It's slow on the phone every customer uses, the menu is invisible to search, and the hardest question you get — "are you open?" — has no honest answer on the page.

**What this changes.** One idea: *everything gets smashed, and comes back better.* A cinematic scroll takes the Loaded Smashed off the griddle — tossed, frozen mid-air, exploded into its nine named layers, slammed back together, then cut open. Built from a scan of a real burger from your Boca kitchen and rendered frame by frame, so it looks like food, not a cartoon. Around it: a neon sign lit only when you're actually open, a Motzei Shabbat countdown from real sunset times, Kong reacting to the cursor, and the whole site going quiet on Shabbat.

**What it does for the business.** More online orders — every path ends on your Toast link and it opens instantly. More app downloads and loyalty signups. Catering leads with the guest count prefilled. Hours nobody has to phone about, every Friday and every holiday, forever. And a site people send each other, which is the cheapest marketing a restaurant will ever get.
