## 1. The core finding

After studying all the cases, nearly every legendary ad reduces to:

> **ONE human truth (insight) + ONE unexpected move (twist) + ONE ownable brand role, executed with total commitment.**

The twist almost always comes from a small set of repeatable mechanisms. Those mechanisms are the 12 "lenses" of the Idea Engine (section 4):

| # | Mechanism | Canonical example |
|---|---|---|
| M1 | **Reversal / Flip the weakness** | VW "Lemon"/"Think Small", Avis "We try harder", Burger King Moldy Whopper, KFC FCK |
| M2 | **Exaggeration to absurdity** | Old Spice, Snickers "You're not you", Kalshi AI ad |
| M3 | **Demonstration as spectacle** | Volvo Epic Split, Shot on iPhone, Hyundai "Night Fishing" |
| M4 | **Category-convention hijack** | Tide "It's a Tide Ad", Geico "Unskippable", Claude "A Time and a Place", DoorDash "All the Ads" |
| M5 | **Mashup / bisociation** | Liquid Death (water × heavy metal), Dumb Ways to Die (death × cute song), Heinz Draw Ketchup |
| M6 | **Participation / personalization at scale** | Share a Coke, Spotify Wrapped, Nutella Unica, Cadbury My-Ad |
| M7 | **Act, don't ad (do a real thing)** | Fearless Girl, Burger King Whopper Detour, AXA "Three Words", Too Good "Paid Sick Leave for Cows" |
| M8 | **Hijack a news moment / turn a crisis around** | KFC FCK, KitKat Heist, Burger King "Burn That Ad" |
| M9 | **Social truth / tension spoken out loud** | Dove Real Beauty Sketches, Always #LikeAGirl, Heineken Worlds Apart, Nike Dream Crazy |
| M10 | **Enemy / David vs. Goliath** | Apple 1984, Liquid Death "Death to Plastic", Claude vs. ads-in-AI |
| M11 | **Proof via the audience's own mind** | Heinz Draw Ketchup, Heinz A.I. Ketchup, "Unskippable" |
| M12 | **Distinctive character / asset** | Old Spice Guy, Dumb Ways beans, Hathaway eyepatch, Liquid Death skull |

## 2. The case library: 36 campaigns

Format per entry: **Brand, Agency, Year, Award/Result, Link**, then the **Mechanism**, then **How to apply to an AI video ad**, with a concept and prompt.
Prompt syntax is model-agnostic (Seedance 2.5 / Kling 4.0 / Higgsfield). Shots are written as `[SHOT n | dur | camera] description`.

### A. The foundations (pre-2010 classics that every CD quotes)

**1. Volkswagen "Think Small" / "Lemon"**: DDB (Bill Bernbach; copy Julian Koenig, art Helmut Krone), 1959–60. Ad Age's #1 campaign of the 20th century. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Think_Small)
- *Mechanism (M1 Reversal):* The American car market worshipped big. DDB made "small" the virtue and huge white space the visual. "Lemon" called a perfect car a lemon because an inspector rejected it for a blemished chrome strip, which turned a quality-control obsession into a story. **Admitting a flaw buys belief for the claim.**
- *AI application:* Open on the product looking "wrong": tiny, ugly, imperfect. Hold the shot uncomfortably long, then reveal why the flaw is the point.
- *Concept (local bakery):* "Ugly." Extreme macro of a cracked, misshapen sourdough loaf. VO: "Ugly." Pull back to a baker who tore it open by hand at 4am. Super: "Machines make pretty bread."
- *Prompt:* `[SHOT 1 | 4s | static macro, 100mm] a cracked, lopsided sourdough loaf on bare white seamless background, huge negative space, loaf occupies 10% of frame lower-left, soft top light, flour dust hanging in air, minimalist 1960s print-ad composition, no text`

**2. Avis "We Try Harder"**: DDB, 1962. Avis reportedly moved from losing money to profit within about a year.
- *Mechanism (M1):* "We're #2" was turned into the brand promise. Underdog honesty creates sympathy and a reason to believe in the service.
- *AI application:* For any local #2 business, dramatize the effort gap: the #2 does absurdly more.
- *Concept:* The "second-best smoothie in Boca" proudly lists the 11 extra things it does because it's #2.

**3. Apple "1984"**: Chiat/Day (Steve Hayden, Brent Thomas, Lee Clow), dir. Ridley Scott. Aired nationally once, Super Bowl XVIII. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1984_(advertisement))
- *Mechanism (M10 Enemy):* A cinematic dystopia casts IBM as Big Brother without naming it. The product barely appears. **The ad sells a side, not a spec.** Airing it once became an event and created scarcity.
- *AI application:* AI excels at cinematic world-building at near-zero cost. Build a grey "world without the brand" and one colorful character who breaks it.
- *Prompt:* `[SHOT 1 | 5s | slow dolly forward] rows of identical grey office workers staring at a giant screen in a brutalist hall, desaturated blue-grey grade, haze, anamorphic; [SHOT 2 | 3s | tracking] a woman in a bright mango-orange tracksuit sprints through the aisle carrying a blender jar, the only saturated color in frame`

**4. Hathaway "The man in the Hathaway shirt"**: Ogilvy & Mather, 1951. [Ogilvy bio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Ogilvy_(businessman))
- *Mechanism (M12 "story appeal"):* One odd detail, an eyepatch on an aristocrat, makes readers ask "why?" Ogilvy called it story appeal. A single **unexplained incongruity** creates curiosity and memory.
- *AI application:* Give every recurring brand character one strange, consistent prop. AI character consistency (reference images) makes the prop ownable across 50 videos.
- *Concept:* The smoothie bar's spokesperson always wears one swim fin, never explained.

**5. Rolls-Royce "At 60 mph the loudest noise comes from the electric clock"**: Ogilvy, 1958.
- *Mechanism (M3, specific fact as drama):* One hyper-specific, verifiable fact beats adjectives.
- *AI application:* Make the fact visible. Use a sound-design-driven ad where the only audible thing is a tiny detail.
- *Prompt:* `interior of a luxury car at speed, motion blur outside windows, total silence except a close-up analog clock ticking, ultra-shallow focus on clock hands, cinematic, sound: only tick`

**6. Guinness "Surfer"**: AMV BBDO, 1999. Repeatedly voted the best UK ad ever.
- *Mechanism (reframe the weakness):* Guinness takes ages to pour. The brand line "Good things come to those who wait" turned waiting into reward. The white horses in the waves are a mythic, epic metaphor.
- *AI application:* Turn any slow or inconvenient product trait (cold-pressed, made to order) into an epic wait metaphor. AI can render the mythic imagery.

**7. Cadbury "Gorilla"**: Fallon London, 2007. Film Grand Prix, Cannes 2008.
- *Mechanism (pure emotion, zero product claim):* A gorilla drums to Phil Collins. It's absurd, joyful, and unexplained. Brand-as-feeling ("a glass and a half full of joy").
- *AI application:* A single-take absurd performance by an unexpected creature is AI-native. Keep one long continuous shot and use a slow build.
- *Prompt:* `[ONE TAKE | 10s | slow push-in] a realistic gorilla sitting at a drum kit in a dark studio, eyes closed, breathing, neck rolling, warm purple key light, anticipation, then explodes into a powerful drum fill on beat, photoreal fur, 24fps film grain`

### B. Super Bowl and broadcast legends (2010–2026)

**8. Old Spice "The Man Your Man Could Smell Like"**: Wieden+Kennedy Portland (Craig Allen, Eric Kallman), dir. Tom Kuntz, Feb 2010. Film Grand Prix, Cannes 2010. Response campaign: about 186 personalized videos in roughly 2 days. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Man_Your_Man_Could_Smell_Like) · D&AD 2011 "Old Spice Response" case study
- *Insight:* Women bought most men's body wash. *Twist:* Talk to her, and make the male fantasy so exaggerated it becomes comedy (M2).
- *Craft mechanism:* A **"seamless impossible one-take"**: bathroom to boat to horse, with no cuts and deadpan, fast delivery. The Response phase (M6) answered real tweets with videos within hours.
- *AI application:* This is the best template for AI ads. AI makes "impossible transitions in one continuous move" cheap (morphing environments). Also copy the personalized-response mechanic: make 20 quick replies to real comments with the same character.
- *Concept (smoothie bar):* "Look at your smoothie. Now back at me." The host stands at the counter. The camera never cuts while the counter becomes a Boca beach, then a yacht, then back. He ends holding the cup.
- *Prompt:* `[CONTINUOUS SHOT | 12s | slow lateral dolly left] confident shirtless man in a towel standing behind a pastel smoothie bar counter speaking directly to camera; as camera moves, the wall behind him slides away revealing a Boca Raton beach at golden hour, then the set rotates into the deck of a white yacht on turquoise water, he never breaks eye contact, he raises a pink smoothie cup and diamonds spill out of the straw; bright commercial lighting, deadpan comedic tone, single take, no cuts`

**9. Snickers "You're Not You When You're Hungry"**: BBDO NY, Super Bowl 2010 (Betty White).
- *Mechanism (M2 + character swap):* Hunger literally turns you into someone else. Casting the "wrong" person for the role is a visual metaphor. It became a 10+ year platform.
- *AI application:* AI face/character swapping is a perfect fit: a customer turns into a grumpy old man or a toddler until they sip. (Use fictional characters, never real celebrities without license.)
- *Prompt:* `a rugged construction worker in a group on a Florida job site, mid-sentence, suddenly visibly replaced by a tiny grumpy 90-year-old woman in the same hi-vis vest and hard hat, coworkers react deadpan, natural daylight, documentary handheld`

**10. Tide "It's a Tide Ad"**: Saatchi & Saatchi NY, Super Bowl 2018 (David Harbour). Film Grand Prix and Film Craft recognition, Cannes 2018.
- *Mechanism (M4 convention hijack + M11):* Every Super Bowl ad features clean clothes, so every ad *is* a Tide ad. Retroactively, it hijacked competitors' ads all night and turned the format itself into the gag.
- *AI application:* AI can cheaply parody every genre: perfume ad, car ad, pharma ad. Build a series: "Every [genre] ad is secretly a [brand] ad."
- *Concept (smoothie bar):* "Is this a car commercial? No, it's a SmoothieBar ad. Look how fresh that guy's mango is."

**11. Geico "Unskippable"**: The Martin Agency, 2015. Film Grand Prix, Cannes 2015.
- *Mechanism (M4 + M11):* YouTube pre-roll lets you skip after 5 seconds. The ad finishes in 5 seconds ("You can't skip this Geico ad because it's already over"), then the actors freeze while a dog eats their dinner. **Design for the platform's mechanics.**
- *AI application:* For Reels/TikTok, design for the swipe. Deliver the whole ad in 2 seconds, then reward the people who stayed with absurd continuation.
- *Prompt:* `a perfect family at a dinner table frozen motionless like a still photograph, staring at camera, while a large golden retriever climbs onto the table and slowly eats all the food, realistic, static locked-off camera, 10 seconds, only the dog moves`

**12. Volvo Trucks "The Epic Split" (Live Test series)**: Forsman & Bodenfors, Nov 2013 (Jean-Claude Van Damme, Enya). Film Grand Prix, Cannes 2014; 100M+ views. Fast Company, "How Volvo Trucks Pulled Off An Epic Split" (18 Jun 2014) · [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volvo_Trucks)
- *Mechanism (M3 Demonstration as spectacle):* An engineering claim (precise dynamic steering) proven by a stunt so absurd it becomes entertainment. Calm music fights the extreme action.
- *AI application:* Turn the boring product spec into an impossible demo. **Warning:** AI demos are not proof, because viewers know it's AI. Use AI for obviously-fantastical demos, and real footage for literal claims (FTC truth-in-advertising).
- *Concept (blender-free smoothie bar using real fruit):* A chef balances between two moving pineapple carts at sunset. Super: "Precision. Only real fruit."

**13. Apple "Shot on iPhone"**: TBWA\Media Arts Lab, 2015. Outdoor Grand Prix, Cannes 2015.
- *Mechanism (M6 + M3):* Users' photos became the billboards. The work is the proof, and the customer is the hero.
- *AI application (adapted honestly):* For local businesses, combine real customer UGC with AI-generated "frames" around it. Never pass AI images off as customer photos.

**14. Nike "Dream Crazy"**: Wieden+Kennedy Portland, Sept 2018 (Colin Kaepernick). Outdoor Grand Prix, Cannes 2019; Emmy for Outstanding Commercial. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dream_Crazy)
- *Mechanism (M9 + M10):* A polarizing stance chosen deliberately because it matched the core young audience. The film is a montage of "crazy" dreams that came true, with a line people quote: "Believe in something. Even if it means sacrificing everything."
- *AI application:* Montage of "impossible" local underdog stories is very AI-friendly. Keep real people real; use AI only for stylized, symbolic interstitials.
- *Rule:* Polarization works only when the brand already has the right to that stance. Local businesses rarely do, so use mild, warm tension instead.

**15. Heineken "Worlds Apart" (#OpenYourWorld)**: Publicis London, 2017. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heineken_Worlds_Apart)
- *Mechanism (M9 social experiment):* Strangers with opposite views build furniture together, then watch each other's views on video and choose: leave or talk over a beer. **The product is the bridge.**
- *AI application:* Social experiments need real people, so AI is not suitable for the core. Use AI for a stylized "two worlds" intro or an animated metaphor version.

**16. Burger King "Whopper Detour"**: FCB New York, Dec 2018. Titanium Grand Prix and Direct Grand Prix, Cannes 2019.
- *Mechanism (M7 + M10):* The BK app unlocked a 1¢ Whopper only when the user was within 600 ft of a McDonald's. The competitor's real estate became BK's distribution.
- *AI application:* The story of an activation is told in a case film. AI can visualize a geofence-hijack idea for a pitch deck cheaply before it is built.
- *Local adaptation:* "Show us a receipt from [chain] and get $2 off." The AI ad shows the receipt flying across town like a paper airplane.

**17. Burger King "The Moldy Whopper"**: INGO Stockholm, David Miami, and Publicis Bucharest, Feb 2020. Multiple Grand Prix at Cannes 2021, including Outdoor and Print & Publishing (verify categories). [BK advertising, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burger_King_advertising)
- *Mechanism (M1 extreme reversal):* Food ads always show perfect food. BK showed its burger rotting over 34 days in a time-lapse to prove "no artificial preservatives." **The ugliest image in food history became a beauty claim.**
- *AI application:* AI time-lapse is native (morph over time). It is ideal for "what happens over time" proofs, but if the claim is literal, film it for real and use AI only for the stylization.
- *Prompt:* `[TIME-LAPSE | 8s | locked-off 45° hero angle] a fresh strawberry-banana smoothie in a clear cup on a white pedestal, over days it separates, browns and grows fuzzy mold, dramatic studio light, museum-like presentation; final frame title card space` (Super: "Real fruit does this. That's how you know." Only use if the business really uses no preservatives.)

**18. KFC "FCK"**: Mother London, Feb 2018, UK chicken shortage. Gold Lions, Cannes 2018.
- *Mechanism (M8 + M1):* Hundreds of stores closed because there was no chicken. A full-page apology ran with an empty bucket reading "FCK." The crisis was turned into charm via self-aware wit with **a single rearranged asset.**
- *AI application:* Keep "crisis-response" templates ready. When the store has an outage (blender broken, supply issue), ship a same-day AI apology that rearranges the logo.
- *Concept:* "SMOOTH_E. We're out of I's... and ice. Back tomorrow." The AI shot shows a melting letter on a tray.

**19. Claude "A Time and a Place"** (incl. "Can I Get a Six Pack Quickly"): Mother London for Anthropic, Super Bowl LX, Feb 2026. **Film Grand Prix, Cannes Lions 2026.** Four ads; two aired in the game. [Wikipedia: Anthropic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropic) (cites Reuters and Adweek, Feb 2026)
- *Mechanism (M4 + M10 + M2):* Each spot depicts an AI assistant that, mid-conversation about something personal (abs, communicating with your mom), suddenly pivots into an awkward sponsored product pitch. It dramatizes the *enemy* behavior (ads inside AI chats, which a rival had announced) through **exaggerated role-play** instead of naming the rival. The brand promise, "no ads," is proven by showing the absurd alternative.
- *AI application:* The "show the bad alternative absurdly" format: act out what life is like with the competitor's annoying trait, pushed to comedy.
- *Concept (smoothie bar):* A customer at a chain smoothie shop asks for "just a smoothie." The cashier launches into an upsell monologue that never ends as the day turns to night and seasons change. Cut to the local bar: "Just a smoothie? Here."
- *Prompt:* `[SHOT 1 | 6s | static medium] a cashier at a sterile corporate smoothie chain enthusiastically talking to a tired customer, behind them the windows show day turning to night to day rapidly, the cashier's name tag grows longer, fluorescent light, comedic deadpan; [SHOT 2 | 3s | handheld] a sunny local smoothie bar, a barista silently slides a cup across the counter, warm natural light`

**20. DoorDash "All the Ads"**: Wieden+Kennedy Portland, Super Bowl 2024. **Titanium Grand Prix, Cannes 2024.**
- *Mechanism (M4 + M6):* A contest gave one winner every product advertised in the Super Bowl, delivered by DoorDash. It turned all the other brands' ads into DoorDash's ad and a puzzle (a promo code assembled from clues in other ads).
- *AI application:* For a local strip mall or business association, offer "Everything on Federal Highway": one winner gets one item from every participating shop. The AI ad is a single tracking shot of a delivery cart piling up absurdly.

**21. GoDaddy "Act Like You Know" (Jeff Goldblum)**: Super Bowl 2025. Cannes Lions 2025 Grand Prix (verify category).
- *Mechanism:* A celebrity persona plus a product demo of its AI tool ("Airo") that makes you look like an expert. The humor comes from fake-it-until-you-make-it confidence.
- *AI application:* Show the product as a "confidence costume": the customer drinks and instantly gains absurd expertise.

**22. Kalshi "NBA Finals" AI ad**: PJ Accetturo (PJ Ace), June 2025. Made with Google Veo 3 for roughly $2K and a few days of work; aired on national TV during the NBA Finals. [CNBC](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7T2q_h2Cp0I) · [PJ Ace: "Disney approved our insane AI Kalshi ad"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-QMftwmyW-A) (352K views)
- *Mechanism (M2 + AI-native absurdity):* A rapid-fire montage of unhinged, GTA-style Florida characters (one in a pool of eggs, old men betting) each yelling a one-line bet. **The weirdness hides AI flaws and becomes the point.** It also made news in its own right ("first AI ad on the NBA Finals"), adding PR value.
- *AI application (the house style for local businesses):* "Florida Man energy" montages. Each shot is 1–2 seconds, one absurd character, and one line of on-camera dialogue (Veo/Seedance native audio). Cut hard on the beat.
- *Prompt (per shot):* `[1.5s | handheld selfie-style] sunburned retiree in a pink flamingo pool float in a Boca Raton backyard pool filled with smoothie instead of water, shouting to camera "I'm SWIMMING in it, Linda!", hyperreal saturated colors, harsh midday sun, smartphone aesthetic`

**23. Coca-Cola "Holidays Are Coming" AI remake**: Secret Level, Silverside, and Wild Card, Nov 2024 (repeated 2025). Heavy creator backlash ("EVERYONE HATES Coca-Cola's AI Christmas ad", 446K views).
- *Mechanism (negative lesson):* It used AI to imitate a beloved human-crafted warmth ritual. Viewers noticed uncanny details (wheels, faces) and read it as "cheap" and "soulless." **Using AI to replace craft in a nostalgic ritual signals low effort, a costly-signal failure (Sutherland).**
- *Rule:* Don't use AI to fake sincerity or nostalgia. Use it where its strangeness is *the joke* or *the impossibility* is the point. If you must do warm work, add visible human craft (real voice, real hands, a real founder).
- Related: **Toys"R"Us "Origin" brand film**, Native Foreign with OpenAI Sora, June 2024 ([teaser](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=F_WfIzYGlg4), 295K views): first Sora brand film, widely mocked for its uncanny child face. Same lesson.

### C. Purpose, truth, and "act don't ad"

**24. Dove "Campaign for Real Beauty" / "Evolution" / "Real Beauty Sketches"**: Ogilvy (Toronto "Evolution" 2006; Ogilvy Brazil "Sketches" 2013). "Evolution" won 2 Grand Prix (2007); "Sketches" won the Titanium Grand Prix (2013) and was the most-viewed online ad of its time. Ad Age ranked Real Beauty #1 of the 21st century; Ad Age reported soap sales growing from $2B to $4B in three years. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dove_Campaign_for_Real_Beauty)
- *Mechanism (M9 + experiment):* In "Sketches," an FBI-trained sketch artist draws women from their own description, then from a stranger's. The gap between the two drawings *is* the insight, made visible: "You are more beautiful than you think."
- *2024–25 AI extension:* "The Code" (2024) and "Real Beauty Redefined for the AI Era" (Cannes Lions 2025 Grand Prix case, verify) showed AI image generators producing narrow beauty standards and published prompt guidelines for more real representation. **The brand used AI as the villain/subject, not as the medium.**
- *AI application:* "Two versions" split-screen. How the customer sees themself vs. how others see them, rendered as two AI portraits. Use with consent and real people only.

**25. Always "#LikeAGirl"**: Leo Burnett, 2014. Cannes 2015 PR Grand Prix (and other honors); Super Bowl 2015 cut.
- *Mechanism (M9 + M11):* Ask adults to "run like a girl," and they flail. Ask 10-year-old girls, and they run hard. The audience's own bias is exposed, and an insult is reclaimed.
- *AI application:* A "what does X look like?" prompt-reveal format. Ask an AI image model to "show a Boca Raton smoothie customer" and reveal its cliché, then show real customers (see #33 Heinz A.I. Ketchup).

**26. Fearless Girl**: McCann New York for State Street Global Advisors, March 2017. **4 Grand Prix at Cannes 2017** (Titanium, Outdoor, PR, Glass), one of only two campaigns ever to do so. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fearless_Girl)
- *Mechanism (M7 + M8 + mashup with an existing icon):* A small statue of a girl faces the Charging Bull. **Meaning is created by juxtaposition with an existing cultural symbol.** It was physical, photographable, and tied to a real policy (gender-diverse boards).
- *AI application:* Place a small brand symbol in confrontation with a giant local icon (AI-render it as a "what if" concept; label as concept if it doesn't really exist).
- *Prompt:* `cinematic wide shot of downtown Boca Raton's Mizner Park at dawn, a giant 20-foot pastel smoothie cup sculpture faces a row of grey corporate coffee-chain storefronts, a small girl in flip-flops stands beside the cup mimicking its pose, long shadows, photoreal, editorial photography` (Note: don't depict real third-party trademarks.)

**27. Dumb Ways to Die**: McCann Melbourne for Metro Trains, Nov 2012. **5 Grand Prix**, Cannes 2013, the most for any campaign at the time; reported 20%+ reduction in near-misses. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dumb_Ways_to_Die)
- *Mechanism (M5 mashup + M12):* Grim safety message × adorable characters × an earworm song. The safety message is withheld until the end ("...the dumbest way to die: standing on the edge of a platform"). Cuteness disarms the "don't lecture me" defense. The characters became a game franchise.
- *AI application:* AI is excellent at making a cast of consistent cute characters plus an AI music track (Suno/Udio-class). Write a 30-second jingle listing "dumb things," with the brand point at the end.
- *Concept (smoothie bar):* "Dumb Ways to Spend Your Morning" (skipping breakfast, gas-station donut, 3rd coffee, ...) and the ending "...or drink a smoothie in 90 seconds."
- *Prompt:* `cute rounded jellybean-shaped character, pastel orange, big eyes, 2D flat animation style with soft shadows, eats a stale gas-station donut and slowly turns grey and droops, upbeat ukulele, simple white background, 3s loop`

**28. AXA "Three Words"**: Publicis Conseil, Paris, 2024–25. **Titanium Grand Prix, Cannes Lions 2025.** [case film](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pY-03zuV7mE)
- *Mechanism (M7 "the product change IS the ad"):* AXA added three words to its home-insurance contracts so the policy covers victims of domestic violence (emergency rehousing and support). The tiny edit to a boring document became the creative. **The most powerful idea can be a product/policy change; the ad just reveals it.**
- *AI application:* For local businesses, find the "three words" you can change in your offer (e.g., "free refill if it rains"). The AI ad zooms into the fine print as it rewrites itself.
- *Prompt:* `extreme macro of a printed menu's fine print, ink letters physically crawl and rearrange themselves to form a new line "free refill when it rains", paper texture, shallow depth of field, soft window light, rain on window reflected`

**29. Too Good "Paid Sick Leave for Cows"**: The Partnership agency, 2025–26. **Grand Prix, Sustainable Development Goals, Cannes Lions 2026.** Snippet from the case film: "When dairy cows are treated with antibiotics, their milk..." (verify full details).

**30. KitKat "The KitKat Heist"**: VML/Nestlé (verify agency), March–April 2026. **PR Grand Prix, Cannes Lions 2026.** Real event: about 12 tonnes (413,793 bars) stolen in transit between Italy and Poland before Easter.

**31. SKF "The Faroe Islands Space Program"**: SKF Group with Visit Faroe Islands, 2025–26. **Grand Prix, Cannes Lions 2026.** [Film](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ExF39ZIYXRI) (3.4M views). ## 3. How the greats think: methods, distilled

### 3.1 Bill Bernbach (DDB): persuasion is an art; truth plus execution
- Execution matters as much as the message ("how you say it"). DDB invented the copywriter + art director **two-person team** ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Bernbach)).
- Simplicity, wit, and honesty: admit the flaw (Lemon, Avis).
- His famous memo warned against "technicians" who know all the rules. Rules produce sameness, and sameness is invisible.
- **Apply:** Claude must always produce both a *verbal* idea (line) and a *visual* idea (image) and check that they multiply rather than repeat each other ("Think Small" + tiny car in white space).

### 3.2 David Ogilvy: research, the Big Idea, and respect for the consumer
- Trained at Gallup. Research before creating. "The consumer isn't a moron; she is your wife."
- **Big Idea test** (from *Ogilvy on Advertising*): Did it make me gasp when I first saw it? Do I wish I had thought of it myself? Is it unique? Does it fit the strategy to perfection? Could it be used for 30 years?
- Story appeal (the Hathaway eyepatch); specific facts beat adjectives (Rolls-Royce clock).
- **Apply:** Run the 5-question Big Idea test as a hard filter in the Idea Engine (Stage 7).

### 3.3 James Webb Young: *A Technique for Producing Ideas* (1940)
An idea is "nothing more or less than a new combination of old elements." There are five stages:
1. **Gather** raw material: specific (product, customer) and general (life, culture).
2. **Digest:** look at facts from different angles and find relationships.
3. **Incubate:** drop it and let the unconscious work.
4. **Eureka:** the idea arrives.
5. **Shape and test** it against reality.
- **Apply:** Claude can't sleep on it, but it can simulate incubation by *deliberately switching domains*: forced random-input combinations (de Bono) after the gather phase.

### 3.4 Dan Wieden / Wieden+Kennedy
- "Walk in stupid every morning": beginner's mind. Office motto: "Fail harder." Coined "Just Do It" (1988), inspired by Gary Gilmore's last words. W+K brought humanity and humor to sport, and later the Old Spice absurdity.
- **Apply:** Always generate at least 3 ideas that feel "too stupid." Old Spice was one of them.

### 3.5 David Droga (Droga5 → Accenture Song)
- Ideas that are bigger than advertising: the UNICEF Tap Project (pay $1 for tap water in restaurants) was named among the 21st century's best. Behavior and product ideas, not just films ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Droga)).
- **Apply:** At least one idea per brief must be a *thing the business does* (offer, ritual, event), with the AI video documenting or dramatizing it.

### 3.6 Rory Sutherland (Ogilvy UK), *Alchemy*
- "The opposite of a good idea can also be a good idea." Logic is not psycho-logic.
- The Eurostar example: instead of £6bn to shave 40 minutes off the trip, make the journey more enjoyable (hire supermodels to hand out free champagne) and people would ask for it to be slower. **Change perception, not reality.**
- Costly signalling: effort and expense signal confidence (why a lavish ad works, and why "cheap AI" can backfire, as in the Coca-Cola AI holiday ad).
- Test counterintuitive ideas because competitors won't.
- **Apply:** The "Perception Lens": for every problem, ask how to change how it *feels* instead of what it *is*.

### 3.7 Dave Trott: *Predatory Thinking*, *Creative Mischief*
- "Get upstream of the problem." Reframe the question before answering it. Most ads are invisible (he cites about 4% remembered positively, about 7% negatively, and 89% not noticed at all), so impact comes first. Write the way people talk.
- **Apply:** Stage 2 of the Engine reframes the brief into 5 different problem statements before generating ideas.

### 3.8 John Hegarty (BBH): "When the world zigs, zag"
- Levi's black sheep (1982), when everyone else went white. Zag against category conventions.
- **Apply:** The "Category Conventions" list (Stage 3). List the 10 clichés of the category, then break the one that matters most.

### 3.9 Lateral thinking tools (Edward de Bono) and bisociation (Arthur Koestler)
- **Provocation ("Po"):** State an absurd premise ("Po: the smoothie drinks the customer") and mine it for movement.
- **Random entry:** Pick a random word or object and force a connection.
- **Bisociation** (Koestler, *The Act of Creation*, 1964): Creativity, humor, and discovery all happen when two unrelated "matrices" collide. This explains Liquid Death, Dumb Ways to Die, and Too Good.
- **SCAMPER:** Substitute, Combine, Adapt, Modify/Magnify, Put to other use, Eliminate, Reverse.

### 3.10 Effectiveness science: making ideas actually sell
- **Binet & Field (IPA, *The Long and the Short of It*):** Emotional, fame-driving brand campaigns produce bigger long-term effects. A rule of thumb is about 60% brand / 40% activation spend (varies by category). "Fame" campaigns (people talk about the ad) are the most effective.
- **Ehrenberg-Bass (Byron Sharp, *How Brands Grow*):** Growth comes from mental and physical availability. Use **distinctive brand assets** (colors, characters, sounds, shapes) consistently and brand early and often. Don't rely on "differentiation" claims.
- **System1 (Orlando Wood, *Lemon*):** Right-brain features drive long-term effects: characters, a sense of place, a story with progression, dialogue, humor, melody, and implicit not explicit messaging. Left-brain flat, abstract, "product on a white background with a voiceover list" underperforms.
- **Apply (non-negotiables for every AI ad):** (1) Brand cue within the first 2–3 seconds, ideally a distinctive asset rather than just the logo. (2) A character or creature. (3) A specific place. (4) A story with an arc, even in 8 seconds. (5) Humor or feeling over claims. (6) Consistent assets across all videos.

### 3.11 Idea volume: how top shops actually work
- Linus Pauling: "The best way to have a good idea is to have lots of ideas." Agencies routinely write dozens to 100+ headlines/lines per brief (the tissue session) and kill 95%.
- Ideas are judged on "tissue" (a scamp: one line + one image) before production. Production polish hides weak ideas.
- First ideas are the category's clichés, so the good ones usually appear after the first 15–20.
- **Apply:** Claude generates **at least 40 raw ideas**, deliberately discards the first 10 as "the obvious set" (keeping one only if it's executionally brilliant), and scores the rest.

### 3.12 "One insight, one twist": the formula
- **Insight** = an unspoken human truth with tension: "I want X, but Y gets in the way / I secretly feel Z."
  - Good insight test: the audience would say "that's so true" and *it implies the brand's role*.
  - Template: `[Audience] believes/does [behavior] because [hidden motivation], but [tension].`
- **Twist** = the single mechanism (M1–M12) that makes the insight surprising.
- **Brand role** = why only this brand can say it.
- **Idea sentence** (one line, a 10-year-old understands it): "It's an ad where ______, which shows that ______."

## 4. THE IDEA ENGINE: the procedure Claude runs on any brief

Run all stages in order. Show the work compactly to the user. Output: 3 finished concepts plus a backlog.

### Stage 0: Intake (ask or assume, then state your assumptions)
Business/product · audience (who, where, when they decide) · the ONE action wanted (visit, DM, order, remember) · offer · budget and platform (Reels/TikTok 9:16, 8–30s) · brand assets (colors, mascot, sound, founder) · tone limits · legal constraints (claims, likenesses, alcohol/health rules) · deadline.

### Stage 1: Gather (Webb Young step 1) — 10 facts each
- **Product truths:** odd, specific, verifiable facts (ingredients, process, origin, numbers, the founder's weird habit).
- **Customer truths:** rituals, frustrations, guilty pleasures, the exact moment of need, the words they use.
- **Culture/place truths:** local slang, landmarks, weather, seasonal events, memes, what's in the news now.
- **Category conventions** (the clichés to break): list 10 (e.g., smoothies are slow pours, fruit splashes, yoga girl, "healthy & delicious").

### Stage 2: Reframe the problem (Trott: upstream) — 5 problem statements
Rewrite the brief 5 ways: as a perception problem (Sutherland), a habit problem, an enemy problem, a fame problem, and an occasion problem.
Example: "Sell more smoothies" becomes "Make 7am feel like vacation," "Beat the drive-thru coffee habit," "Be the thing Boca talks about," and so on.

### Stage 3: Insight mining — 5 candidate insights, then choose 1–2
Use the tension template. Score each insight 1–5 on: *true* (people nod), *tension* (there's conflict), *brand-implied* (the brand solves it), and *fresh* (not the category cliché). Keep the top two.

### Stage 4: Divergence — 12 lenses × at least 3 ideas each (at least 40 raw ideas)
Run every lens against the chosen insight. One line per idea, in the format `[Lens] Title: one sentence`.

| Lens | Prompt-question to self |
|---|---|
| L1 Reversal | What is the opposite of what the category always shows? Can the weakness be the hero? |
| L2 Exaggeration | Take the benefit to 1000x. What literally happens? |
| L3 Demonstration spectacle | What impossible stunt proves the product truth? |
| L4 Convention hijack | Which ad format, platform mechanic, or genre can I parody or break? (pre-roll, unboxing, GRWM, pharma ad, nature doc, true crime) |
| L5 Mashup / bisociation | Pair the product with a random, opposite world (heavy metal, NASA, opera, HR, courtroom, telenovela). |
| L6 Personalization / participation | How can each viewer or customer be in it or get their own version? |
| L7 Act don't ad | What real thing could the business DO that the video then documents? |
| L8 Newsjack / moment | What's happening this week/season locally that we can ride? |
| L9 Social truth | What does everyone feel but nobody says? |
| L10 Enemy | Who or what is the villain (the habit, the chain, the heat, Mondays)? |
| L11 Audience's own mind | Can the viewer prove our point by guessing, drawing, or imagining? |
| L12 Character / asset | What creature/person/object could be our ownable recurring asset? |

Plus two AI-specific forcing questions: **(a)** What would be *impossible or absurdly expensive* without AI? **(b)** Where does AI weirdness become the joke instead of a flaw?

### Stage 5: Provocation round — 5 "too stupid" ideas
Write 5 ideas that would get you fired (Wieden: "walk in stupid"). Use de Bono "Po" plus a random word. Then ask of each: "What's the sane version of this?"

### Stage 6: Kill the obvious
Mark the first 10 ideas generated and any idea a competitor could run by swapping the logo. Kill them unless the execution is outstanding.

### Stage 7: Score (convergence)
Score the survivors 1–5 on 8 criteria (max 40). Advance the top 3 plus one wildcard.

| Criterion | Question |
|---|---|
| Stopping power | Would it stop a thumb in 1.5 seconds? (visual hook in frame 1) |
| Simplicity | Can I say it in one sentence a 10-year-old gets? |
| Insight truth | Does the audience nod? |
| Brand-linked | Is the brand indispensable to the joke/story (fails the logo-swap test)? |
| Distinctiveness | Does it build a repeatable asset/platform (Ogilvy: 30 years)? |
| Shareability/fame | Would someone send it to a friend or would the local news cover it? |
| AI-feasibility | Can current models render it convincingly in 4–10s shots with consistent characters? |
| Safety/legal | Any claim, likeness, trademark, taste, or cultural risk? (Score 1 = blocker.) |

Then apply **Ogilvy's 5-question gasp test** to the top 3.

### Stage 8: Develop — for each of the 3 finalists
1. **Idea sentence** + **line/tagline** + **visual key frame** (Bernbach pair).
2. **Platform potential:** 3 sequels (if it can't do 3, it's an execution, not a campaign idea).
3. **Shot list:** 4–8 shots, hook in shot 1 (≤1.5s), brand cue by 2–3s, payoff, end card with CTA.
4. **Prompts** per shot (model-agnostic), plus native-audio lines, music/SFX notes, and on-screen text.
5. **Risk notes** (claims to substantiate, AI disclosure where platforms require it, no real celebrities or trademarks).

### Stage 9: Present
Present like a CD: insight → idea → how it looks (key frame) → why it works (mechanism + effectiveness principle) → how it scales. Present the boldest first, the safest last. Never present more than 3.

## 5. Worked example A: a Boca Raton smoothie bar

**Brief (assumed):** Independent smoothie and acai bar near Mizner Park, Boca Raton. Audience: busy 25–55 locals (gym-goers, FAU students, moms after school drop-off, retirees). Goal: morning foot traffic and Instagram follows. Budget: small, AI video Reels. Assets: tropical pastel palette (mango orange + flamingo pink), a fruit-forward menu, an owner who is a former lifeguard (assumed for the example).

**Stage 1 (selected gather):**
- Product: made to order in 90 seconds; real frozen fruit, no syrups; the owner was a lifeguard.
- Customer: Boca mornings are 85°F+ and humid; people "earn" breakfast after the gym; drive-thru coffee is the default habit; retirees treat outings as social events; "Boca" stereotypes (country clubs, Boca Mom, golf carts, luxury SUVs, iguanas falling from trees on cold nights).
- Category clichés: fruit splash slo-mo, yoga girl in white, "healthy & delicious," a straw sip with a smile.

**Stage 2 (reframes):** (1) Make 7am feel like vacation. (2) Beat the coffee drive-thru habit. (3) Be the thing Boca talks about. (4) Turn "healthy" from a chore into a treat. (5) Own the heat.

**Stage 3 (insights):**
- I1: "In Boca the heat starts before your day does. By 8am you're already melting and your coffee makes it worse." (true, tension, brand-implied) ✅
- I2: "Everyone in Boca acts like they're on vacation, except on weekday mornings." ✅
- I3: "Healthy food feels like punishment." (true but category cliché) ✗

**Stage 4 (40-idea excerpt; 3 shown per lens for 6 lenses):**
- L1 Reversal: "Slowest smoothie in Boca" (90s feels long, so we make the wait a show); "Unhealthiest-looking healthy thing" (it looks like a decadent milkshake); "We're closed at noon. Mornings only." (scarcity)
- L2 Exaggeration: the sip drops body temperature so much that a person frosts over in a Boca parking lot; the first sip turns a Monday into a Saturday (the world transforms); the smoothie so cold that iguanas fall from the trees (Florida-specific).
- L4 Convention hijack: a **nature-documentary** narrator observing "the Boca Mom in her natural habitat"; a pharma-ad parody ("Ask your barista if Mango Tango is right for you. Side effects include: smiling at strangers"); a true-crime "who stole the morning?"
- L5 Mashup: smoothie × NASA launch countdown (the 90-second blend as a launch); smoothie × telenovela drama; smoothie × weather channel ("Today's forecast: 100% chance of mango").
- L10 Enemy: "The Drive-Thru Line" as a monster; "the heat" as a villain in a superhero spoof; the "sad desk breakfast."
- L12 Character: a lifeguard owner who "rescues" overheated locals (whistle, red float); a pastel flamingo who rates smoothies; a talking frozen iguana.

**Stage 5 (too stupid):** "Po: the smoothie drinks the customer" becomes a giant cup with a straw slurping people out of the heat into the shop (sane version: the shop as an AC rescue). "Po: smoothies are illegal in Boca" becomes a speakeasy "password" morning menu.

**Stage 7 (scores, max 40):** Lifeguard Rescue 35 · Frozen Iguana Forecast 34 · Boca Nature Doc 33 · Pharma parody 29 (claim risk) · NASA blend 30.

### Finalist 1: "Smoothie Lifeguard" (L12 + L2 + Old Spice one-take)
- **Insight:** By 8am Boca is already trying to kill you with heat.
- **Idea:** The former-lifeguard owner "rescues" overheated locals all over Boca and drags them to the counter. Every episode features a new victim.
- **Line:** "Heat stroke? We've got a smoothie for that." / End card: "Rescue station open 7am."
- **Platform sequels:** rescuing a golfer melting on the 9th hole; an FAU student at an 8am exam; a Boca Mom whose SUV AC broke; a retiree mid-pickleball.
- **Shots (15s, 9:16):**
  1. `[1.5s | handheld telephoto, heat-shimmer] a Boca Raton office worker in a suit literally melting like wax at a bus stop on a palm-lined boulevard, sweat streaming, sun flare, hyperreal comedic` (hook)
  2. `[2s | whip pan] a tanned lifeguard in red shorts and a whistle sprints in slow motion across a parking lot holding a red rescue float, Baywatch homage, golden morning light, pastel storefronts` (native audio: whistle blast)
  3. `[2s | dynamic tracking] he tosses the rescue float, it lands around the melting man, he is pulled across the asphalt toward a pastel mango-orange and flamingo-pink smoothie bar`
  4. `[3s | macro] a thick mango smoothie poured into a cup, condensation beads, frost forming on the cup, crushed-ice sound`
  5. `[3s | medium] the rescued man sips, his melted face snaps back to normal, a cool blue breeze ruffles his hair, he exhales; lifeguard nods deadpan` (dialogue: "You're safe now, sir.")
  6. `[3s | end card] shop exterior, logo in mango-orange, text: "Rescue Station • Open 7am • [address]"`
- **Why it works:** a distinctive character asset (Ehrenberg-Bass), exaggeration (M2), place specificity (System1), and an infinitely sequel-able format. AI renders "melting people" impossibly cheaply, and the weirdness is the joke.
- **Risk:** don't imply medical treatment of heat stroke. Super: "Not an actual medical service. Do stay hydrated."

### Finalist 2: "Boca Forecast: Frozen Iguana Warning" (L5 weather-channel mashup + L8 local newsjack)
- **Insight:** Every Floridian knows the "falling iguana" cold-weather alert, a beloved local meme.
- **Idea:** A deadpan TV meteorologist issues a "frozen iguana advisory" because a new Mango Freeze smoothie is so cold. Iguanas stiffen in the trees near the shop only.
- **Shots:** weather map with a giant snowflake over one block of Boca → field reporter in a hurricane jacket in 90°F sun ("It's... freezing here, Jim") → an iguana falls stiffly from a palm onto a café table → a customer sips, frost on his eyebrows → end card "Mango Freeze. Advisory in effect 7–11am."
- **Prompt (shot 3):** `[2s | static medium wide, slight high angle] a green iguana frozen stiff with tiny frost crystals slowly tips off a palm frond and lands with a thud on a pastel café table next to a smoothie cup, sunny Boca Raton sidewalk, people at the next table don't react, photoreal, comedic timing`
- **Ride the moment:** post it the first morning the real cold-iguana alert trends (January), as a L8 newsjack.

### Finalist 3 (wildcard): "Boca Nature Documentary" (L4 genre hijack + L9 social truth)

## 7. Quick rules (Claude's checklist when ideating AI ads)

1. **One insight, one twist, one brand role.** If you can't write the idea in one sentence, it isn't an idea yet.
2. **Generate at least 40 ideas, kill the first 10, score the rest.** Present 3 and lead with the bravest.
3. **The first frame is a question.** A weird image in ≤1.5s (melting man, raisin person, frozen iguana) earns the next 8 seconds.
4. **Brand early and distinctively.** Use a color, character, or sound by second 3. Build assets you can reuse for years.
5. **Use AI where it's the point, not a shortcut.** The best uses are impossible transformations, absurd exaggeration, genre pastiche, personalization at scale, and AI-as-witness (Heinz). Never use AI to fake warmth, nostalgia, real testimonials, or real proof (Coca-Cola 2024, Toys"R"Us lesson).
6. **Weirdness hides AI artifacts.** Stylized and absurd beats photoreal-sincere (Kalshi).
7. **Prefer a platform over an execution.** Every idea needs 3 sequels.
8. **Act, don't just ad.** Pair at least one concept with a real offer, ritual, or stunt the business does.
9. **Be specific to place.** Boca, Florida heat, iguanas, golf carts, and Mizner Park beat generic "beach."
10. **Humor and character beat claims** (System1/IPA). Claims must be true and substantiated, and any literal demo must be real.
11. **No real celebrities, trademarks, or competitor logos** without rights. Parody the *behavior*, not the brand (the Claude Super Bowl approach).
12. **Ride the moment.** Keep a newsjack and crisis template ready (KFC FCK, KitKat Heist) and ship within 24 hours.
