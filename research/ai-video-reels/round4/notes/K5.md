## 0. The core model: an ad is ONE turn

Every short ad that works, from a 6s bumper to a 60s brand film, does the same thing: it sets up an expectation and then **turns** it. Randy Olson's ABT ("And, But, Therefore") — itself derived from the *South Park* writers' "Rule of Replacing" (replace "and then" with "but" or "therefore") — is the cleanest statement of this: momentum, conflict, resolution ([SESYNC ABT toolkit](https://www.sesync.org/resources/communications-toolkit-and-therefore-statement)). In 15 seconds you have room for exactly **one** "But".

**Rules Claude follows:**
- **One ad = one turn = one message.** If the brief has three benefits, pick one and leave the others for other cuts.
- **The turn must be visible.** If you can only explain it with words, it is not a video idea yet.
- **The brand must be inside the turn, not just after it.** The product causes, solves, or *is* the twist. A twist the brand only watches from the end card is a "vampire" twist: people remember the joke and forget who paid for it. YouTube's ABCD research recommends the brand within the first 5 seconds and in natural use, which lifts recall ([Think with Google ABCD](https://www.thinkwithgoogle.com/intl/en-gb/marketing-strategies/video/viewer-attention-for-youtube-ads); [YouTube ABCDs](https://www.youtube.com/intl/en_ae/ads/abcds-of-effective-video-ads)). Google reports ads following ABCD showed +30% short-term sales likelihood and +17% long-term brand perception.
- **Distinctive assets beat logos.** Ehrenberg-Bass/Byron Sharp: mental availability is built by consistent distinctive assets (colour, shape, character, sound, line) ([Branding Strategy Insider](https://brandingstrategyinsider.com/how-mental-and-physical-availability-drives-brand-growth/)). In scripts, give every brand a repeatable asset: a sound (can crack, a bell), a colour, a gesture, a recurring character, a fixed end line.

**How to apply to an AI video ad:** Before writing any shot, write the "turn sentence": *"We think we're watching ___, but actually ___, because of [product]."* If you can't fill it, don't prompt anything yet.

## 1. Twelve story structures for 6–60s ads (with templates)

Each template lists: beats → timing for 15s/30s → when to use → a famous example → AI application.

### 1.1 ABT (And, But, Therefore)
- **Template:** `[Situation] AND [normal detail]. BUT [problem/tension]. THEREFORE [product → new state].`
- **15s:** And 0–4s · But 4–9s · Therefore 9–13s · End card 13–15s.
- **Use for:** almost everything; the default when unsure. Especially service businesses.
- **Example logic:** "You love your boat AND your weekends. BUT you spend them cleaning it. THEREFORE charter ours."
- **AI application:** Each of A, B, T becomes 1–2 shots. The "But" shot should be the most visually striking frame (it's the hook if you reorder — see "cold open" below).

### 1.2 Pixar Story Spine (Kenn Adams)
- **Template (8 lines):** Once upon a time… / Every day… / But one day… / Because of that… / Because of that… / Until finally… / And ever since then… ([NPR, 2026 interview with Kenn Adams](https://www.npr.org/transcripts/nx-s1-5750619)).
- **Compression for ads:** 30s = Every day (routine, 2 shots) → But one day (break) → Because of that ×1–2 (escalation) → Until finally (payoff with product) → Ever since then (new routine = brand promise).
- **Use for:** 30–60s brand films, emotional local businesses (family restaurant, bakery), "origin" stories.
- **AI application:** The "Every day" beat is perfect for AI: repeat the same framing with small changes (match-cut montage). Lock the character with a reference image and keep the camera position identical across routine shots — consistency becomes the rhythm.

### 1.3 The 15-second Hero's Journey
- **Template:** Ordinary world (1 shot) → Call/obstacle (1 shot) → Mentor/tool = product (1 shot) → Ordeal overcome (1–2 shots) → Return changed (1 shot) → brand.
- **Use for:** sports, energy, fitness, cars — "the product is the magic sword".
- **Example:** Nike's many "athlete overcomes" spots; the product is the enabler, the person is the hero. Never make the brand the hero; make it the mentor (StoryBrand principle, Donald Miller).
- **AI application:** Use a single location with a changing state (dark → lit, rain → sun) rather than many locations; fewer environments = better consistency.

### 1.4 PAS (Problem – Agitate – Solve)
- **Template:** Show the pain (literal) → Make it worse/funnier (exaggerate) → Product resolves instantly.
- **15s:** P 0–3 · A 3–8 · S 8–13 · CTA 13–15.
- **Use for:** direct response, local services, med spa, cleaning, pest control, apps.
- **Danger:** Pure PAS is "left-brain" salesmanship. System1/Orlando Wood show long-term effectiveness drops with close-ups, on-screen commands and no characters ([System1 "The Serious Case for Humor"](https://system1group.com/blog/adage-look-out)). Fix: agitate with humor and a character, not with ugly stock-style misery.
- **AI application:** Agitation = exaggeration, which AI is great at (the pile of laundry grows to the ceiling; the inbox counter spins like a slot machine).

### 1.5 Reveal / Twist (the misdirect)
- **Template:** Frame A that implies meaning X → camera move/cut reveals context → meaning becomes Y (product-related).
- **Example:** Old Spice "The Man Your Man Could Smell Like" (Wieden+Kennedy, 2010; dir. Tom Kuntz; Craig Allen & Eric Kallman) — one continuous take where every reveal re-frames the space (bathroom → boat → horse). Sales reportedly doubled within months ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Man_Your_Man_Could_Smell_Like); [Creative Review oral history](https://www.creativereview.co.uk/an-oral-history-of-the-old-spice-ads/)).
- **AI application:** Reveals are native to AI video: "camera pulls back to reveal…", "rack focus from foreground to background", "whip pan". Write the reveal as the ONLY camera move in that shot.

### 1.6 Mini-sitcom (two characters, one situation, one button)
- **Template:** Status quo between 2 characters → one wants something → product creates complication → button line or look.
- **Example:** Snickers "You're not you when you're hungry" (BBDO NY, 2010, Betty White Super Bowl spot): the character is literally replaced by another person when hungry — sitcom logic with a product reset.
- **AI application:** Keep dialogue to ≤2 short exchanges, mostly reactions. The "button" should be a look or an action, not a line (looks are easier to generate than perfect lip sync).

### 1.7 Mockumentary / fake-serious
- **Template:** Documentary grammar (talking head, lower third, handheld B-roll) applied to something trivial.
- **Example:** Dollar Shave Club "Our Blades Are F***ing Great" (2012, founder Michael Dubin, dir. Lucia Aniello) — deadpan founder monologue walking through a warehouse; the "corporate video" format is the joke.
- **AI application:** Talking heads are the hardest AI shot (lip sync). Do it as: VO over B-roll + **one** 2–3s talking-head line. Lower thirds ("DENNIS, 54 — HAS OPINIONS ABOUT FOAM") do the comedy in text = sound-off proof.

### 1.8 One-joke escalation (rule of three, then break)
- **Template:** Situation → bigger → biggest → absurd peak → product.
- **Example:** Allstate "Mayhem" (Leo Burnett, 2010) — each spot is an escalation of one absurd premise; Old Spice also escalates.
- **AI application:** Generate the same shot 3–4 times with one escalating variable (size, number, speed). Easy, consistent, cheap.

### 1.9 Listicle ("3 reasons…", "things only Boca people know")
- **Template:** Promise a number in the hook → deliver items fast (2–3s each) → strongest item last → CTA.
- **Use for:** restaurants, realtors, local services; native to TikTok/Reels.
- **AI application:** Each item = one self-contained shot. Numbers as big supers. The list is a shot list.

### 1.10 Day-in-the-life (DITL)
- **Template:** Time stamps (6:00 AM, 9:14 AM…) → routine with product woven in → small surprise near the end.
- **AI application:** Use on-screen clock supers as structure; the same character across 4–6 scenes requires a strong reference image and identical wardrobe description.

### 1.11 POV ("POV: you just…")
- **Template:** Hook super "POV: [relatable situation]" → first-person or over-the-shoulder shots → payoff.
- **Use for:** hospitality (yacht, restaurant, hotel), experiences; platform-native.
- **AI application:** First-person camera ("POV shot, hands visible in frame") avoids face consistency problems entirely. Strongly recommended for AI.

### 1.12 Before/After & Transformation (with care)
- **Template:** State A → transition device (snap, door, wipe, match cut) → State B.
- **Caution:** For med spa / health, before-after images carry regulatory risk (FTC truthful-claims rules; "results vary"; no fabricated results). Use **emotional** before/after (tired → rested, stressed → calm) rather than fake clinical results — and never AI-generate fake "real patient" results.
- **AI application:** Match cuts (same framing, different state) are where AI shines; prompt the "A" and "B" frames as start/end images.

### Picking a structure (decision table)

| Brief says… | Use |
|---|---|
| "We need sales / bookings now" | PAS, ABT, Listicle |
| "Make people love us" | Story Spine, Mini-sitcom, Hero |
| "Go viral / be talked about" | One-joke escalation, Reveal, Mockumentary |
| "Show the experience" | POV, Day-in-the-life |
| "Launch a product" | Reveal, ABT |
| Luxury | Reveal (slow), Story Spine (quiet), POV |
| 6s bumper | Single reveal or single visual pun + logo |

## 2. The first 3 seconds

**Facts that drive the rules:**
- Meta: ~47% of a video ad's value is delivered in the first 3 seconds and ~74% by 10 seconds ([cited via ADM/Benly summaries of Meta research](https://www.accelerateddigitalmedia.com/insights/designing-facebook-and-instagram-video-ads-for-sound-off/)).
- TikTok: introduce the proposition in the first 3s for recall; hook in the first 6s; text at roughly 5–10 words/sec max is readable ([TikTok creative best practices](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/creative-best-practices)).
- YouTube: attract + brand in first 5s; 95% of YouTube ads are watched with sound on (vs. feeds, which are mostly silent) ([Think with Google](https://www.thinkwithgoogle.com/intl/en-gb/marketing-strategies/video/viewer-attention-for-youtube-ads)).

**So:** platform decides the hook mix. Feed (Reels/TikTok/FB) = visual + super first, sound is a bonus. YouTube/CTV = VO and music carry more.

### 2.1 Three hook channels
| Channel | What it is | Strength | Rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Visual** | a strange, specific, moving image in frame 1 | works sound-off, universal | movement in first 30 frames; no logo-only openings; no slow fade-in |
| **On-screen text (super)** | 3–7 words, top-third or center | carries meaning sound-off | must be readable in 1.5s; contrast box; never cover the face/product |
| **Verbal** | first line of VO or dialogue | strongest with sound on (YouTube) | first word is a hook word, not "Hi" / "Are you…" filler |

Best practice: **stack two channels** — a visual oddity + a super that frames it. Never three competing messages.

### 2.2 Visual hook patterns (AI-friendly)
1. **Impossible physics** — a cup that pours upward, a car parked on the ceiling.
2. **Wrong scale** — a giant croissant blocking the road; a tiny person on a plate.
3. **Cold open on the climax** — start at the most dramatic frame (the "But"), then rewind ("2 hours earlier").
4. **Extreme close-up of a texture** — sizzle, foam, condensation, then pull back.
5. **A face mid-reaction** — shock, laughter, disbelief (the cause is withheld).
6. **Pattern break** — 4 identical things, one is wrong.
7. **Direct address + prop** — someone looks into lens holding something odd.
8. **Speed contrast** — world in slow-mo, subject at normal speed (or reverse).
9. **Satisfying process** — pouring, slicing, unwrapping, perfect fit.
10. **Mid-action start** — never start before the action; start inside it.

### 2.3 Fifty proven hook lines (fill-in templates)
Use as VO first line or as super. `[ ]` = swap in specifics. Specific numbers and local names beat generic adjectives.

**Curiosity / open loop**
1. "Nobody in [Boca] knows about this [place] — yet."
2. "This is the reason [my neighbors] stopped [cooking on Fridays]."
3. "I wasn't supposed to show you this."
4. "Watch the [glass] — not the [guy]."
5. "Wait for the [last plate]."
6. "There's one thing wrong in this [kitchen]. Find it."
7. "This took [11] seconds. It used to take [an hour]."
8. "What [$40] gets you on [Palmetto Park Road]."
9. "The [owner] of this [boat] has one rule."
10. "Here's what [real estate agents] won't say on camera."

**Contradiction / pattern break**
11. "Stop [drinking coffee] in the morning."
12. "The worst [espresso] in Florida. (Ours, in 2019.)"
13. "Don't book this [charter] if you like [checking email]."
14. "[Energy drinks] are boring. This one is mostly boring too. Except…"
15. "We're the [slowest] [kitchen] in town. On purpose."
16. "This [house] has no [TV room]. Here's why that's genius."
17. "Most [med spas] want you to look different. We don't."
18. "You don't need [more sleep]. You need [this]."

**Relatable POV / identity**
19. "POV: it's [3:07 PM] and your brain left the building."
20. "POV: your [mother-in-law] is coming and you have [20 minutes]."
21. "If you've ever [pretended to like your partner's coffee] — this is for you."
22. "Things only people who live [east of Federal Highway] understand."
23. "Every [Boca] [mom] at [10 AM] on a Tuesday:"
24. "Tell me you're [from New York] without telling me…"
25. "When the [group chat] says 'dinner?' and nobody picks:"
26. "Me explaining to my [Israeli] parents what I do for work:"

**Numbers / specificity**
27. "[3] things I'd never [buy] in [Boca] — and [1] I would."
28. "[47] unread emails. [0] of them matter today."
29. "[$0] down. [1] signature. [12] minutes."
30. "We made [14,000] [pizzas] last year. [This] is the one people [film]."
31. "[92] degrees outside. [38] degrees in this glass."
32. "One [6-ounce] [cup] changed my [mornings]."

**Challenge / test**
33. "Bet you can't [watch this] without [getting hungry]."
34. "Guess the price of this [view]."
35. "Pause on the [third frame]."
36. "Name a better [Sunday] than this. I'll wait."
37. "Sound on for this one."  *(only when the sound IS the payoff)*

**Story-in-a-line**
38. "[My grandmother] has made this [sauce] every Sunday since [1974]."
39. "He's been [pouring] it wrong for [30] years. Today he finds out."
40. "She asked for '[a little refresh].' Here's what that means."
41. "This [house] was built for [one] reason."
42. "Day [1] of [quitting caffeine]." *(then cut to chaos)*

**Direct benefit (use sparingly, best for retargeting)**
43. "[Botox] that doesn't look like [Botox]."
44. "Your [boat day], without the [boat]."
45. "[Lunch] in [12] minutes. [Real] [pasta]."
46. "[Zero] [sugar]. [Zero] [crash]. [All] [afternoon]."
47. "The [view] sells it. We just [open the door]."

**Deadpan / meta**
48. "This is an ad. It's [15] seconds. Here's the [drink]."
49. "Our lawyer said we can't say [it's the best]. So here's [a dog] instead."
50. "We had a big budget for this ad. We spent it on [the food]."

**How to apply to an AI video ad:** Pick the hook line first, then design frame 1 as its *visual proof*. E.g. hook #31 → prompt: *"macro close-up, iced coffee glass sweating heavy condensation, a thermometer sticker on the glass reading 38°F, harsh Florida sunlight behind, droplet slides down in slow motion."* The super carries the numbers; the image carries the feeling.

## 3. Sound-off writing (supers)

**Facts:** ~85% of Facebook video views were muted (Digiday, 2016) ([Digiday](https://digiday.com/media/silent-world-facebook-video/)); captions increase view time on average ~12%, top videos have captions of 4–6 words ([ADM](https://www.accelerateddigitalmedia.com/insights/designing-facebook-and-instagram-video-ads-for-sound-off/); [Rev](https://www.rev.com/blog/resources/how-to-add-captions-subtitles-to-facebook-video-ads)). Exact percentages have shifted since 2016 (Reels/TikTok are more sound-on), but the creative rule holds: **design to work muted, reward people who unmute.**

### 3.1 Super rules
- **3–7 words per card**, one idea per card. Max ~2 lines.
- **On screen ≥ word count × 0.3s, minimum 1.5s** (reading speed on mobile ≈ 3–4 words/sec for ad text; TikTok says up to 5–10 words/sec, but that's an upper bound, not a target).
- **Position:** top third or center; stay inside the 9:16 safe zone (avoid bottom ~20% and right ~15% where UI covers it).
- **Contrast box or heavy shadow,** never thin type over busy footage.
- **Supers ≠ transcript.** Supers say the *idea*; VO says the *emotion*. If both say the same words, cut one.
- **Write supers as punchlines, not labels.** "Monday" (label) vs. "Monday called. Let it ring." (punchline).
- **Burn in brand + CTA as a super** at the end even if VO says it.
- **Caption every spoken line** (open captions styled on-brand) for feed placements.

### 3.2 The Mute Test (Claude runs this on every script)
Read only the VIDEO column and the SUPER lines. Answer:
1. What happened? (the turn)
2. Who is the brand? (seen, not just heard, by ≥ the midpoint)
3. What do I do now? (CTA visible ≥1.5s)
If any answer requires the VO, rewrite a shot or add a super.

**How to apply to an AI video ad:** Don't ask the AI model to render supers inside the video (text in generated video is still unreliable and locks you in). Generate clean plates; add supers in the edit (CapCut/Premiere/After Effects). In prompts add "no text, no captions, no logos" unless a sign is the gag.

## 4. Voiceover: words per second

| Read style | Words/sec (EN) | 6s | 15s | 20s | 30s | 60s |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Luxury, slow, intimate | 1.6–2.0 | 8–10 | 22–28 | 30–38 | 45–55 | 95–110 |
| Natural conversational | 2.3–2.5 | 12–14 | 30–35 | 42–48 | 65–75 | 130–145 |
| Energetic / promo | 2.8–3.0 | 15 | 38–42 | 52–56 | 80–85 | 160+ |

Industry consensus: ~2.5 wps, so 15s ≈ 35 words, 30s ≈ 70–80, 60s ≈ 140; write ~10% under to leave room for pauses and the end card ([VoiceBros](https://voicebros.com/en/blog/how-to-write-a-commercial-voice-over-script); [Killerspots script timer](https://killerspots.com/scriptcounter)).

**Claude's rules:**
- Budget the **end card** (last 2–3s) with no VO or only the tagline.
- Budget a **cold open** of 1–2s with no VO (let the image hook land).
- So a 15s spot realistically carries **20–28 VO words**; a 30s carries **50–65**.
- Numbers count as their spoken length ("$1,250,000" = "one-point-two-five million" = 4 words).
- **Hebrew:** words carry prefixes (ו/ה/ב/ל/ש), so fewer, longer words. Plan **~2.0 Hebrew words/sec** for a natural read and time by reading aloud — count syllables (~4–5 syllables/sec) rather than words. *(Heuristic from production practice; verify by read-aloud.)*
- **AI TTS** (ElevenLabs etc.) reads ~5–10% faster than a human by default; set stability/speed and insert punctuation pauses ("…", line breaks) to control rhythm.

## 5. Natural dialogue (and when to avoid it)

**Writing natural lines:**
- People don't say product names or full sentences. "Is that the new Ember?" ❌ → "Wait. What is this?" ✅
- Use interruptions, half-sentences, reactions ("Huh." "No." "Okay, fine.").
- Subtext > text: the character wants something they don't say.
- **One line per character per shot**, ≤6 words. Comedy lives in the pause after.
- Give the best line to the reaction, not the setup.

**AI reality (Oct 2026):** Kling (3.x+) and Veo 3.1 can do native lip sync and even multi-speaker dialogue; best practice is still to segment into ~10s chunks, keep lips unobstructed, use clean audio, and avoid music under the dialogue during sync ([Atlas Cloud lipsync guide](https://www.atlascloud.ai/blog/guides/video-lipsync-wan-kling-veo); [BuildFastWithAI model comparison](https://www.buildfastwithai.com/blogs/seedance-2-5-vs-veo-3-1-vs-kling-3-0-best-ai-video-2026)). Long sync still drifts, and close-up "uncanny" mouths kill trust in luxury and medical categories.

**Dialogue hierarchy for AI ads (prefer top):**
1. **No dialogue** — action + reactions + supers + music/SFX.
2. **VO narrator** over visuals (no mouths to sync).
3. **Off-screen line** (character's back to camera, phone call, voice from another room).
4. **One short on-camera line** (≤6 words), medium shot, not extreme close-up.
5. **Two-line exchange** in separate shots (A says, cut, B reacts).
6. ❌ Long on-camera monologue — only with a real human filmed, or an avatar tool built for it.

## 6. Humor writing toolkit

System1's work (Orlando Wood, *Lemon*, *Look Out*) links amusement and characters to stronger long-term effects; ads with fluent devices/recurring characters were reported as 30% more likely to increase profit gains, yet only ~4% of US ads use them ([System1](https://system1group.com/blog/adage-look-out); [MarTech Series](https://martechseries.com/analytics/behavioral-marketing/memorable-characters-ads-boost-chances-profit-30-just-4-us-brands-using-says-system1/)). Humor works through "juxtapositions, satisfying repetitions and unexpected reversals".

| Device | Mechanism | Template | AI application |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Rule of three** | 2 items set a pattern, 3rd breaks it | "Fresh pasta. Fresh basil. Fresh gossip." | 3 quick shots, same framing; 3rd shot is the odd one |
| **Misdirection** | lead to assumption A, reveal B | open on what looks like a crime scene → it's a toddler with sauce | reveal via pull-back or rack focus |
| **Specificity** | precise details are funnier and more believable | not "a lot of emails" → "47 unread, 3 from Gary" | specific props in prompt: "sticky note reading 'GARY AGAIN'" (add in edit if text) |
| **Escalation** | each beat bigger than last | plant wilts → plant on life support → plant writes a will | same shot, escalating state variable |
| **Deadpan** | the character treats the absurd as normal | waiter calmly serving a lasagna the size of a door | prompt "neutral expression, matter-of-fact, unbothered" |
| **Callback** | reuse an early element as the ending | the plant from shot 2 returns healthy at end | keep the prop in the reference image |
| **Literalization** | make a figure of speech literal | "hit a wall" → a literal brick wall at 3PM | AI is ideal: visual metaphors cost nothing |
| **Status reversal** | the low-status thing wins | the dog runs the restaurant | character reference image |
| **Understatement in super** | super undersells a huge image | giant yacht → super: "We have a boat." | super added in edit |

**Rules:** humor must serve the product truth (the joke should *be* the benefit). Never punch down at customers, ethnicities, or bodies — especially med spa (no fat/age shaming). Deadpan + specificity is the safest high-performing combo for AI because it needs minimal acting.

- **Short + imperative + attitude** (1, 2, 27): for energy/sport/youth.
- **Admit a flaw → virtue** (4, 20, 3): perfect for small local businesses ("We're slow. That's the sauce.").

## 8. CTAs

**Principles:** one action, verb-first, specific, low friction, matched to funnel stage and platform button.

| Stage | CTA lines | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Cold / awareness | "Find us at Mizner Park." · "Look for the orange can." | No hard sell; brand cue |
| Consideration | "See the menu." · "Take the 30-second quiz." · "Watch the full tour." | Reduce effort |
| Conversion | "Book your complimentary consult." · "Reserve Friday's table." · "Text BOAT to 561-…" | Concrete next step + reason now |
| Local urgency | "Only 4 sunset charters left in May." · "Happy hour ends at 6." | Only if TRUE (FTC; trust) |
| Retargeting | "Still thinking about it? Your table's still here." | Callback to earlier ad |

- Show the CTA as a super **and** say it in VO (if VO exists). Hold ≥2s.
- Local: phone numbers with local area code (561) build trust; show the neighborhood name.
- Never three CTAs. "Visit, call, or follow" = none.

## 10. Bilingual notes (Hebrew ↔ English, Israeli in Boca)

1. **Transcreate, don't translate.** Rebuild the joke/line in Hebrew with its own wordplay. "Un-slow your day" ≠ literal Hebrew; find a native idiom ("תוריד הילוך" is the opposite; "תחזור לחיים" works).
2. **Register:** Israeli audiences respond to *dugri* (direct) copy and self-aware humor; American copy is more polished/aspirational. Israeli copy that sounds like an American ad feels fake; American copy that sounds Israeli can feel pushy. For Boca's Israeli-American audience, a mixed register ("Hebrew-ish English" or English with one Hebrew word) can be a strong identity signal — e.g., super "Sababa." on a yacht shot.
3. **RTL supers:** check punctuation placement, numbers and Latin brand names inside Hebrew lines (bidi issues), use fonts with proper Hebrew support (Heebo, Assistant, Rubik, Frank Ruhl Libre for luxury). Never render Hebrew text inside AI video generation — it will garble; add in edit.
4. **Timing:** Hebrew supers are often shorter in characters but need the same on-screen time. Re-time the edit per language; don't reuse the English cut blindly.
5. **VO:** Hebrew AI TTS quality varies; test pronunciation of brand names and stress (מִלְרַע/מִלְעֵיל). Native-speaker review mandatory.
6. **Bilingual packaging for clients:** offer EN master + HE version + ES/PT caption version (Boca's Latin/Brazilian community). Write the script with a "translation-safe" track: avoid English-only puns in the *visual* layer so the picture works in all languages; keep puns in the super/VO layer which gets transcreated.
7. **Cultural cues:** Friday-night dinners, Shabbat, holidays (Rosh Hashanah, Hanukkah) are strong local moments in Boca for restaurants/bakeries; also Thanksgiving/season openers. Avoid religious imagery as a gimmick.

## 11. Translating scripts into AI-friendly shots

**The 10 rules**
1. **One shot = one action = one camera move.** "She opens the can" — not "she opens the can, drinks, smiles and walks away".
2. **2–5 seconds per generated shot** for 15–30s ads; generate 5–10s and trim.
3. **Favor visual storytelling:** objects, reactions, environment changes, match cuts, scale gags, speed ramps.
4. **Avoid long lip-sync**; follow the dialogue hierarchy (§5).
5. **Lock identity:** one reference image per character/product; repeat the same descriptor string every shot (wardrobe, hair, age, lighting).
6. **Fewer locations.** 1–2 environments per 15s spot.
7. **Hands and products:** AI still mangles text on labels and complex hand–object interactions; show product hero shots as image-to-video from a real product photo; keep hands simple (hold, lift, pour).
8. **No text in generation;** add supers/logos in post.
9. **Write the sound separately:** music, SFX, VO are assembled in edit; brand sound (can crack, bell) is a designed asset.
10. **Prompt the end frame first** (the tagline image), then the hook frame, then fill the middle.

**Script → shot → prompt translation template**
```
SHOT [#] | [tc in–out] | [shot size] | [subject + single action] | [camera move] | [light/mood] | [ref: char/product image] | [SFX] 
Prompt: "[shot size], [subject descriptor string], [single action], [environment], [camera move], [lighting], [lens/look], [mood], no text, no logos"
```

## 14. Quick checklist (Claude, before delivering any script)

- [ ] Turn sentence written; one message only
- [ ] Structure named; beats timed
- [ ] Hook = visual oddity + ≤7-word super in 0–2s
- [ ] Brand cue (asset, product, sound) by ~5s for performance; name by end at latest
- [ ] VO words ≤ budget (−10%); end card has silence/tagline only
- [ ] Mute test passes
- [ ] Dialogue ≤6 words per on-camera line; prefer VO/reaction
- [ ] Each shot: one action, one camera move, 2–5s
- [ ] Real product/food/property from real footage or image-to-video refs
- [ ] Tagline scored ≥20/25; CTA single and specific
- [ ] Compliance lines for category
- [ ] 6s cut + alt hook + Hebrew transcreation offered
