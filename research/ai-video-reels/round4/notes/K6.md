## 0. How to use this file (for Claude)

When a user brings a brief ("make an ad for X"), do **not** jump to prompts. Run the operating system:

1. **Normalize the brief** (§4, step 0). Ask at most 2 questions; otherwise assume and state the assumptions.
2. **Mine insights** (§4, step 1). Product truth, audience truth, category convention, cultural tension.
3. **Generate 30 raw ideas through 6 mechanisms** (§4, step 2). Use the metaphor bank (§1.6) and the AI-strengths map (§2) as fuel.
4. **Score** with the rubric and kill gates (§4, step 3). Pick the top 3.
5. **Translate** each of the top 3 with the pipeline (§3) into full packages.
6. **Self-critique** with the v2 checklist (§5.6) before showing anything.

The full prompt that does all of this is in **§4.2 (v2, final)**. §5 shows it run on four briefs.

## 1. Visual rhetoric and metaphor: the theory that generates ideas

### 1.1 Why metaphor (and not "show the product nicely")

A reel has about 0.5–2 seconds to make a claim the viewer *feels* before they decide to scroll (chapter 03 §1.3). A literal product shot makes no claim. A metaphor makes a claim **without words**: "this soda is lighter" becomes *gravity switches off*. Three research streams explain why this works, and each one gives a tool:

| Stream | Core finding | Tool it gives us |
|---|---|---|
| **Visual rhetoric typology** (Phillips & McQuarrie, *Marketing Theory* 2004) | Visual figures differ in *structure* (juxtaposition < fusion < replacement, rising in complexity) and in *meaning operation* (connection "A is associated with B", comparison "A is like / unlike B"). More complex structures invite more elaboration, up to a point. [verified: [JFAVA summary](https://jfava.ut.ac.ir/article_86686_en.html), [Utrecht/Tilburg replication](https://research-portal.uu.nl/en/publications/refining-replacements-validating-a-revised-typology-of-visual-met/)] | A **structure dial**: choose how hard the viewer has to work. On a reel, use fusion or transformation for the hook (instantly decodable) and save replacement for the payoff. |
| **Creativity templates** (Goldenberg, Mazursky & Solomon, *Marketing Science* 18(3), 1999) | Most award-winning ads in their sample (89% of 200, per the secondary summary) fit a small set of templates. Ads built from templates were rated more creative than ads from free association. [verified for the citation and the 89% figure via [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/inm/ormksc/v18y1999i3p333-351.html) and [Human Factors summary](https://www.humanfactors.com/Newsletters/six_ways_to_think_out_of_the_box.html); the template names below are from my reading of the paper and should be checked against [the PDF](https://business.columbia.edu/sites/default/files-efs/pubfiles/3384/fundamental.pdf) before quoting] | A **mechanism menu** (§1.3) that turns ideation from waiting for inspiration into filling slots. |
| **Brand linkage / distinctive assets** (Romaniuk, Ehrenberg-Bass) | Distinctive assets (colours, shapes, sounds, characters) work through **fame** (share of category buyers who link the asset to the brand) and **uniqueness** (it triggers only this brand). Getting attention first and branding later "is like using darts to tackle a swarm of wasps." [verified: [Marketing Architects](https://www.marketingarchitects.com/blog/v41-n3-your-brand-assets-may-be-working-against-you), [WARC](https://www.warc.com/newsandopinion/news/brands_need_distinctive_assets/40501), [Contagious](https://contagious.com/io/article/jenni-romaniuk-on-distinctive-assets)] | The **brand-swap test** and the rule that the *product or brand asset must cause the transformation*, not watch it happen. |
| **Right-brain features** (Orlando Wood, *Lemon*, IPA/System1) | Ads with more right-brain features (people, narrative, dialogue, a strong sense of place) do better on long-term effectiveness. UK ads now average 3.9 left-brain vs 2.3 right-brain features. [verified: [LBB on Lemon](https://lbbonline.com/news/ipa-publishes-lemon-a-repair-manual-to-reverse-the-crisis-in-creative-effectiveness), [MarTech Series](https://martechseries.com/analytics/behavioral-marketing/make-ads-right-brain-long-term-growth/)] | A **humanity check**: AI spectacle tends toward left-brain (abstract, product-only, voiceless). Add a person, a place, a small story. |

### 1.2 The four visual structures (video edition)

Phillips & McQuarrie wrote about print. In video, time adds a fourth structure, and it is the one AI models are best at.

| Structure | Definition | Print classic | Video / AI form | Reel position |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Juxtaposition** | A and B side by side, same size | Split before/after | Split screen, Reality-Swap (chapter 03 §1.2), match-cut A→B | Hook (decoded in <0.5s) |
| **Fusion** | A and B merged into one hybrid object | A lemon with a zipper | Hybrid object that *moves* (a can whose bubbles are tiny balloons) | Hook or key visual |
| **Replacement** | B appears where A should be; context implies A | A tyre where the sun should be | Object substitution inside a familiar scene (the moon is an ice cube in the glass) | Payoff / "aha" |
| **Transformation** *(video-only)* | A becomes B over time | n/a | Morph, reverse time-lapse, material change (the pour turns into silk) | Turn / proof beat |

**How to apply to an AI video ad:** pick the structure *before* the metaphor. For a 15s reel, the strongest default is **fusion hook → transformation turn → replacement payoff**. Example: (hook) a soda can on an office desk with bubbles rising *out of the closed can* like a lava lamp (fusion) → (turn) the can is opened and every object on the desk starts to float (transformation) → (payoff) the clock on the wall reads 3:00 and its hands float off too: the 3pm slump is gone (replacement of "slump" with "lift").

From Goldenberg et al. (1999). Sub-versions from my reading of the paper; check against the PDF before quoting. Each row ends with how to apply it in AI video.

| Template | Sub-versions | Mechanism | Real ad that runs on it | How to apply to an AI video ad |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Pictorial analogy** | Replace a product attribute with a symbol that carries it | Borrow a property from a familiar thing | **Mercedes-Benz "Chicken" (Magic Body Control, Jung von Matt, 2013)**: a chicken's head stays still while its body is moved. Stability shown with no car on screen until the end [not verified: agency/year from memory] | Find the animal, material or natural process that *is* the benefit, then let AI animate it photoreal. "Stable ride" → a hummingbird hovering perfectly still in a storm. |
| **Extreme situation** | Absurd alternative · extreme attribute · extreme worth | Push the benefit to an absurd degree | **Volvo Trucks "The Epic Split" (Forsman & Bodenfors, 2013)**: Van Damme does the splits between two reversing trucks to show steering precision [not verified] | AI makes the absurd cheap. "Long-lasting lipstick" → a kiss print on a glass survives the dishwasher, a hurricane and a coral reef. |
| **Consequences** | Extreme consequence · inverted consequence (what happens without it) | Show the ripple effect, not the product | **Snickers "You're not you when you're hungry" (BBDO, 2010)**: the inverted consequence of *not* eating [not verified] | Chain reaction: one sip, then the city's traffic lights all turn green. Or the inverted version: a world without the product, which is grey, slow and heavy. |
| **Competition** | Attribute in competition · worth in competition · uncommon use | Put the product against something unexpected | **Heinz "A.I. | **Interactive experiment** | The viewer participates or tests something | Make the viewer do or notice something | **"Dumb Ways to Die" (Metro Trains Melbourne, McCann, 2012)**, a song and game rather than a warning [not verified] | Reel-native: "Pause at 0:04 and look at the reflection." Scale (product as a building), time (one shot across decades), multiplication (1,000 cans as a murmuration). |

### 1.4 The metaphor-construction algorithm (use this every time)

```
1. BENEFIT        What the customer gets, in their words. ("I stop feeling sluggish at 3pm.")
2. ABSTRACT       The underlying quality, a single word. ("lightness", "lift")
3. SOURCE DOMAINS 10 concrete things that *are* that quality and that everyone knows in under 0.5s.
                  (helium balloon, astronaut, feather, bubbles, zero gravity, hot-air balloon, kite,
                   a weight being dropped, a sunrise, a cork popping)
4. FILTER         Keep sources that (a) can be linked to the product's form, colour or asset,
                  (b) are an AI strength (§2), (c) are not cliché for the category,
                  (d) do not imply a claim you cannot substantiate.
5. STRUCTURE      Choose juxtaposition / fusion / replacement / transformation (§1.2).
6. AGENCY         Make the PRODUCT the cause of the change (opening the can switches gravity off).
                  If the product is a bystander, linkage fails.
7. HUMAN          Add one person reacting and one real place (Orlando Wood's right-brain features).
8. SHOT           Write one 5-slot sentence (chapter 07 MASTER-D) to check it is filmable.
```

**Worked example (beauty, "hydration"):** benefit "my skin looks plump, not dry" → abstract "rehydration" → sources: raisin→grape, dry sponge, cracked riverbed after rain, wilted plant reviving, deflated balloon, dried flower in water → filter: raisin→grape (instant, macro = AI strength, not a cliché in skincare) → structure: transformation → agency: a single drop of serum lands on the raisin and it swells into a dewy grape → human: cut to a woman's cheek catching window light, a 1-second hold with no claimed "result", just mood → shot: `Macro, 100mm: a single clear drop falls onto a wrinkled raisin on white marble; on impact the raisin swells smoothly into a glossy green grape, beads of moisture on its skin. Soft window light from camera left. Locked-off. Audio: one soft drip, a low bloom whoosh, no music.`

### 1.5 Ten rules for metaphors that sell (each with "how to apply")

1. **Source must decode in under 0.5s.** If you have to explain the metaphor, it is a riddle, not an ad. *Apply:* test the key visual frame on someone for one second and ask "what is this saying?" In prompts, choose universally known objects (balloon, ice, pearl) over clever ones (a tardigrade).
2. **One shared property only.** "Silk" says smooth. Do not also want it to say "luxury, natural and long-lasting." *Apply:* one metaphor per beat. A 15s reel holds at most two (hook and payoff).
3. **The product is the agent.** *Apply:* write the trigger into the prompt, e.g. `as the can's tab snaps open, ...`, `the moment the drop touches ...`.
4. **Moderate incongruity beats wild.** Replacement is the most complex structure. In a 0.5s hook, fusion or transformation works better. *Apply:* hook = fusion or transformation, payoff = replacement.
5. **Embed a distinctive asset inside the metaphor.** Brand colour as the light that causes the change, the bottle silhouette as the shape the clouds form, the sonic logo as the "pop". *Apply:* list the brand's assets in the brief and require every idea to use one *inside* the visual (Romaniuk).
6. **Pass the brand-swap test.** Replace the logo with the category leader's. If the ad still works just as well, linkage is weak. *Apply:* make the metaphor depend on something only this brand has: a shape, colour, name pun, origin or founder.
7. **Metaphor ≠ claim in regulated categories.** In med spa, supplements and functional drinks, a metaphor can still *imply* a result (an "un-wilting face" implies anti-aging results). *Apply:* metaphors about *experience and feeling* (lightness, calm, confidence) are safer than metaphors about *outcomes* (younger face, cured gut). See §5 legal notes.
8. **Design for an AI strength.** Transformations, scale, physics, macro and liquids are strong. Hands, text and contact choreography are weak (§2). *Apply:* if the metaphor needs a hand doing something fine, re-stage it so the object does it by itself.
9. **Add a human and a place.** *Apply:* at least one shot with a real person's reaction (filmed for V2V, or AI with a face lock), and name a real kind of place (Mizner Park arcade, Atlantic Avenue at dusk) in the prompt.
10. **Kill clichés by name.** Overused in 2025–26 AI ads: things floating for no reason, golden-hour drone reveals, slow-motion splash with fruit, liquid "morphing into the logo", cosmic/galaxy backgrounds, "a tear rolls down the cheek". *Apply:* keep a kill list in the meta-prompt (§4.2) and only allow a cliché if there is a twist that makes it the brand's.

### 2.3 Five design patterns that turn weaknesses into strengths

1. **The object acts by itself.** Instead of a hand pouring, the bottle tips by itself in zero gravity and the liquid floats into the glass. *Prompt fragment:* `the bottle tilts on its own, no hands in frame, liquid ribbons drift upward and fold into the glass`.
2. **Real human, AI world (hybrid).** Film the person (performance, hands, dialogue) on a phone, then use Genjutsu Motion Transfer for the world. This was the 2026 pattern that got praised: Google's Creative Lab used Veo for storyboards and hired directors for the finals, and hybrid AI + human craft beat fully generated work [verified as a pattern summary: [shhots.ai](https://shhots.ai/blog/best-ai-commercials/)]. *Prompt:* MASTER-B (chapter 07 §1.2).
3. **Stylise to forgive.** If the idea needs many people or contact, move it into miniature, claymation or felt. Audiences grade stylised worlds on charm, not physics.
4. **Cut on contact.** Show the hand reaching (shot A), hard cut to the result (shot B). The model never renders the contact frame. *Timeline:* `0:03-0:04 Shot 3: fingertip approaches the can's tab, macro, shallow focus. Hard cut on contact. 0:04-0:06 Shot 4: tab already open, a burst of fine mist ...`
5. **Text lives in post, and in the metaphor.** If the idea needs a word, make the word physical (cut it from paper, build it from objects in a real shot) or add it in the edit. Never ask the model for brand text.

### 2.4 "AI-native" vs "AI-replacement" ideas: the strategic rule

The 2024–25 backlash cases (Coca-Cola's AI holiday remakes in 2024 and 2025, criticised as "soulless" and "AI slop" [verified: [TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/ai-platforms-assistants/coca-cola-faces-backlash-again-for-missing-the-spirit-of-the-holidays-with-another-ai-slop-ad), [RetailBoss](https://retailboss.co/coca-colas-ai-generated-holiday-campaign-and-the-backlash-that-went-viral)]) used AI to **replace** what a camera does (warm human moments, a remake of a filmed classic). The celebrated cases used AI for what a camera **cannot** do, or made the AI itself the joke:

- **Heinz "A.I. Ketchup"**: the AI *is* the idea (competition template).
- **Kalshi** (PJ Accetturo, 2025): absurdist, chaotic vignettes ("The world's gone mad. Trade it."), where AI's surreal edge fits the message [verified: tagline per [Inside](https://www.inside.com.tw/tag/3531-NBA)].
- **Liquid Death spec ad** (Noam Sharon, Tal Rosenthal, Amir Ariely, Veo 3, about $800) [verified as reported by [shhots.ai](https://shhots.ai/blog/best-ai-commercials/)]: an over-the-top brand fits AI's excess.

**Rule for Claude:** score "AI-feasibility" *and* "AI-nativeness". An idea that would be better shot on a real camera should be shot on a real camera (or done hybrid). The best AI ideas could not exist without AI, or are honest about using it. Relevant context: the Cannes Lions 2026 Film Grand Prix went to Mother London's work for Claude, which is human-crafted comedy [verified: [Contagious](https://www.contagious.com/en/article/cannes-lions-2026-film-winners-628c9e7c8dd74a04ba20d2ad80f8ed96), [Adweek](https://www.adweek.com/creativity/watch-all-the-grand-prix-winners-of-cannes-lions-2026/); sources give slightly different spot titles]. Craft and wit still win the top prizes. AI is a production method, not an idea.

## 3. The translation pipeline: idea → key visual → beats → shot list → keyframes → video prompts → edit & sound

Chapter 03 §0 describes the *production* pipeline. This is the *creative translation* layer that feeds it. Every stage has a template. A worked example runs through all stages (VOLTA "Afternoon Gravity", the winning idea of Test 1 in §5.1).

### Stage 0: Brief normalization
```
BRAND: name · category · price tier · 3 distinctive assets (colour/shape/sound/character/tagline)
PRODUCT: the one SKU/service to sell · 3 verifiable facts (on label / licensable)
AUDIENCE: who (demo + mindset) · what they do now instead · their words for the problem
GOAL: awareness / consideration / conversion / retention · the ONE action wanted
PLATFORM & SPECS: IG Reels / TikTok / Shorts / Meta ads / CTV · 9:16 default · lengths (6/15/30s)
BUDGET: production $ (AI credits + human hours) · media $ if known
MANDATORIES: logo, legal lines, offer, CTA, URL, location
NO-GO: claims, competitors, tone, people, places
JURISDICTION: where it runs (FL? NY? national?) and which category rules apply (§5 legal notes)
```

### Stage 1: Insight statement
Template: **"[Audience] [does / feels X] because [Y], but [tension]. So [brand] [role]."**
VOLTA: *"Young office workers grab a soda at 3pm because they need a lift and a treat, but they feel they're trading their afternoon for a sugar crash. So VOLTA gives the lift without the trade-off."*

### Stage 2: Idea (one line) + key visual (the poster test)
- **Idea line (≤15 words):** "At 3pm, gravity gets heavy. VOLTA switches it off."
- **Key visual:** *one frame* that sells the idea with the sound off and no copy. If you can't describe a single frame, there is no idea yet.
  VOLTA: *An open-plan office at 3pm; every person slumped; above one desk, a VOLTA can with its tab just opened, and around it pens, sticky notes and a coffee cup floating upward in a column of bubbles.*
- **Key-visual prompt (image model, 9:16):** written in Stage 5 format below.

### Stage 3: Story beats (the reel spine)

## 4. The CREATIVE DIRECTOR META-PROMPT

### 4.1 Design decisions (why the prompt is built this way)
- **Diverge before converging.** 30 raw ideas through 6 *different* mechanisms is enough to stop the model's first idea (usually the category cliché) from winning by default. Goldenberg et al. found template-based ideation beat free association; the mechanisms are the templates.
- **A quota per mechanism (5 each)** forces breadth. Without it, v1 over-produced physics-break spectacle (see §5.6 critique).
- **Score with weights and gates, not vibes.** Legal risk and AI-feasibility are *gates*: an idea that cannot be made, or cannot legally run, dies whatever its originality.
- **Forced score spread** (v2 change): at most 5 ideas may score ≥4.0 overall, otherwise LLM scoring inflates.
- **Packages end in prompts that obey chapters 03/07** (MASTER-A/B/D, locks, negatives), so the output goes straight into production.
- **Self-critique pass** before output, with a fixed checklist (from the §5.6 failures).

### 4.2 The meta-prompt (v2, final; copy as a system prompt / Claude Project instruction)

```
You are CREATIVE DIRECTOR — a world-class advertising creative director (Cannes-level ideas, direct-response discipline) who also produces AI video. You turn a business brief into bold, on-brand, AI-feasible, legally safe ad ideas and then into production-ready packages for Seedance 2.5, Kling 4.0, Higgsfield Genjutsu and image models. The operator is a creator in Boca Raton, FL selling AI video ads to brands and local businesses (US market).

=== STEP 0 · BRIEF NORMALIZATION ===
Parse the brief into: BRAND (category, price tier, 3 distinctive assets: colour/shape/sound/character/tagline) · PRODUCT (one SKU/service, 3 verifiable facts) · AUDIENCE (demo, mindset, what they do instead, their words) · GOAL (one action) · PLATFORM & LENGTHS (default IG Reels/TikTok 9:16, 15s master + 6s + 30s) · BUDGET (production $; assume a 20–25x generation-to-keeper ratio for hero shots) · MANDATORIES · NO-GO · JURISDICTION (where it runs).
If a critical input is missing, ask at most 2 questions; otherwise assume, and list assumptions in one line each, marked [ASSUMED].
If the brand has no distinctive assets, propose 2 (a colour + a sound, or a character) and use them.

=== STEP 1 · INSIGHT MINING ===
Write 3 candidate insights in each lens (12 total), each ≤25 words:
 a) PRODUCT TRUTH — what is genuinely different or true about the product (only verifiable facts).
 b) AUDIENCE TRUTH — a feeling/behaviour the audience recognises but rarely says out loud.
 c) CATEGORY CONVENTION — what every competitor's ad does (to break it).
 d) CULTURAL / LOCAL TENSION — something in culture or in the local scene (e.g. Boca/Delray) the brand can take a side on.
Then choose the ONE sharpest and write it as:
"[Audience] [does/feels X] because [Y], but [tension]. So [brand] [role]."
Also write the CATEGORY CLICHÉ LIST: 5 things ads in this category always show (these are banned unless twisted).

=== STEP 2 · 30 RAW IDEAS THROUGH 6 MECHANISMS (exactly 5 each) ===
Each idea = a title (≤5 words) + one line (≤25 words) + its key visual in ≤15 words.
 M1 VISUAL METAPHOR — benefit → abstract quality → known source domain → structure (juxtaposition / fusion / replacement / transformation). The product must CAUSE the change.
 M2 CREATIVITY TEMPLATES — use a different template for each: pictorial analogy · extreme situation (absurd alternative / extreme attribute / extreme worth) · consequences (extreme or inverted) · competition (attribute / worth / uncommon use) · interactive experiment · dimensionality alteration (scale / multiplication / time leap).
 M3 INSIGHT INVERSION — say the unsaid, flip the category convention, name the enemy, confess a flaw, take a local side.
 M4 AI-NATIVE SPECTACLE — ideas that could not be filmed: scale play, physics breaking, transformation, impossible camera path, macro worlds. Max one law broken per idea.
 M5 FORMAT / CULTURE HIJACK — borrow a format the audience already loves: nature documentary, heist, ASMR, true crime, FOOH, prompt-reveal, "POV:", mock-luxury, sports broadcast, weather report, video-game UI (UI added in post), local news.
 M6 CHARACTER & RECURRING ASSET — a character, mascot, world or series that can run 10+ episodes and becomes a distinctive asset; include people, place and dialogue (right-brain features).
Rules: no two ideas with the same key visual; at least 6 ideas must feature a real human; at least 4 must be producible mostly with filmed footage + V2V (hybrid); at most 3 may use "objects floating" or "morphing into the logo".

=== STEP 3 · SCORING ===
Score every idea 1–5 on:
 ORIGINALITY (20%) — 1 = category cliché, 5 = never seen in this category, still instantly clear.
 BRAND LINKAGE (20%) — 1 = fails the brand-swap test (works equally with a competitor's logo); 5 = depends on a distinctive asset, name or product truth.
 EMOTIONAL PUNCH (20%) — 1 = information; 5 = laughter, awe, recognition or tension in the first 2 seconds.
 AI-FEASIBILITY (20%) — 1 = needs hands doing fine tasks, contact choreography, readable text or long dialogue; 5 = built on AI strengths (scale, physics break, transformation, impossible camera, macro, liquids, stylised worlds) or on a hybrid with filmed performance.
 COST (10%) — 5 = ≤4 generations of ≤10s plus a simple edit; 1 = many shots, characters, iterations or a live shoot with crew.
 LEGAL SAFETY (10%) — 5 = no claims beyond verifiable facts, no real people, no IP; 1 = implies unsubstantiated results, uses likeness/IP, or breaks category rules.
GATES (automatic kill regardless of total): LEGAL SAFETY ≤2 · AI-FEASIBILITY ≤2 with no hybrid fix · BRAND LINKAGE ≤2.
CALIBRATION: at most 5 ideas may total ≥4.0. Show a table of all 30 (scores + weighted total + gate status). Bold the top 3. The top 3 must come from at least 2 different mechanisms, must include at least one idea with a real human, and must include one "safe-but-sharp" option (legal 5, cost ≥4). If two finalists are the same idea at different levels (a claim and its execution, a ritual and its spectacle), MERGE them into one package and promote the next idea.

=== STEP 4 · FULL PACKAGES FOR THE TOP 3 ===
For each:
 4.1 IDEA LINE (≤15 words) + WHY IT WORKS (mechanism, insight, the psychology in 2 lines).
 4.2 KEY VISUAL — one frame description + an image-model prompt (9:16, no text).
 4.3 SCRIPT — beats (Hook / Setup / Turn / Proof / Payoff+Brand / CTA) with VO or dialogue (English; ~2.5 words per second; or "no VO") and on-screen supers (added in post).
 4.4 TIMECODED SHOT LIST — table: # | time | beat | shot | size/angle/lens | light | ONE camera move → end state | transition | audio | METHOD (T2V / I2V / KF-seq / V2V / LIVE / POST) | AI-RISK A–D + mitigation.
 4.5 KEYFRAME PROMPTS — for every I2V shot.
 4.6 VIDEO PROMPTS — (a) Seedance 2.5 full-ad prompt in MASTER-A structure: ASSET BINDING / GLOBAL STYLE / SCENE / TIMELINE / PHYSICS / AUDIO / CONSISTENCY LOCKS / NEGATIVE; (b) a Kling 4.0 keyframe version of the hardest shot; (c) a Genjutsu CHANGE/PRESERVE prompt if any shot is V2V.
    Prompt rules: verbs over adjectives; one camera move per shot with an end state; named light source and direction; no "stunning/epic/8K"; no on-screen text; no hands in contact shots (cut on contact); product shots get PRODUCT LOCK; ≤1 location per shot; any human in beauty/medical/wellness gets "natural skin texture, visible fine lines, no smoothing".
 4.7 EDIT & SOUND NOTES — pace per shot, the cut points, speed ramps, supers, colour arc, sound layers, sonic asset, music rights, cutdowns (6s / 15s / 30s).
 4.8 HOOK VARIANTS — 5 alternative first 2 seconds (anomaly, text-tension, sound-led, POV, payoff-first).
 4.9 PRODUCTION PLAN — what to film for real (if anything), the generation count estimate (keepers × 20–25 for hero shots, × 5–8 for simple ones), hours, AI cost range, and a suggested client price range.
 4.10 RISKS — creative (what could make it fall flat), technical (the 2 shots most likely to fail + fallback), legal (claims, disclosures, platform policies, jurisdiction), brand-safety, AI-backlash risk (would this be better on a real camera?).

=== STEP 5 · SELF-CRITIQUE (do silently, then fix before output) ===
Check and fix: (1) Does each top-3 idea pass the brand-swap test? (2) Is any banned category cliché or AI cliché used without a twist? (3) Does any shot require fine-motor hands, text, contact or >2 lines of dialogue? (4) Is any claim, before/after, testimonial, price/offer or result implied that the client cannot substantiate? Add the required disclosures for the category and state. (5) Is there a human and a place? (6) Is the hook visible with sound off in frame 1? (7) Do the timelines cover the full duration without gaps? (8) Are cost estimates realistic (generation ratio applied)?
Then output a one-line "CD NOTE" per idea: what you would fight for in the client meeting.

=== OUTPUT FORMAT ===
Markdown. Order: Assumptions → Insight work → Cliché list → 30 ideas (grouped by mechanism) → Scoring table → Top 3 packages → CD notes. Be specific and visual. Write prompts in English. If the operator writes in Hebrew, write the explanations in Hebrew but keep every prompt in English.

=== CATEGORY LEGAL QUICK RULES (US/FL; flag, don't give legal advice) ===
- All: FTC — claims need substantiation; endorsements/testimonials must be real and typical, with material connections disclosed (2023 Endorsement Guides). Platform AI labels (Meta "AI info"; undisclosed AI can be rejected). NY synthetic-performer disclosure if a photoreal AI human appears in an ad that may run in New York (from 9 Jun 2026). No real-person likeness without written consent.
- Food/bev/functional: no health/structure-function claims beyond what's substantiated and on the label (e.g. "gut healthy" → class action risk; Poppi settled for $8.9M).
- Alcohol: target 21+ (Meta requirement in the US); no excessive or rapid drinking, no drinking with driving/boating/swimming, no therapeutic or mood-fix claims; talent should look 25+ (industry code); include responsible-drinking cues; check state rules before advertising drink prices/specials (FL: Division of Alcoholic Beverages and Tobacco).
- Med spa / medical (FL): no false/misleading claims or "unjustified expectations of beneficial assistance" (FL Admin Code 64B8-11.001); no AI-generated before/after or results; real results only, typical, with "results vary"; free/discounted service ads need the FL §456.062 all-caps 72-hour right-to-refuse statement; accurate credential/board-certification claims; injectable brand names follow manufacturer rules.
- Real estate (FL): brokerage licensed name in every ad (FREC 61J2-10.025); never alter the property's real features with AI; label digitally staged/enhanced visuals and "transitions digitally created"; Fair Housing — describe the property, not the ideal buyer; housing ads run in Meta's Special Ad Category (limited targeting).
```

  - *Legal / platform:* Meta requires 21+ targeting for alcohol ads in the US [verified: [Meta Transparency Center](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/restricted-goods-services/alcohol/)]; no excessive or rapid consumption (one drink on screen, no shots, no "rounds"); no drinking + driving/boating/swimming (the "tide" is metaphorical and indoors; never show patrons walking into the sea); talent looks 25+ (industry self-regulatory codes [not verified for the exact code text]); responsible-drinking line. TTB rules mainly bind producers/importers/wholesalers, but their social-media guidance on misleading or curative claims is a good standard [verified: [Sprout Social summary](https://sproutsocial.com/insights/social-media-alcohol-marketing/)]: **[v2 fix]** v1 VO had "the cure for your week", a therapeutic claim, so it was cut. Florida-specific rules on advertising drink prices or specials: **[not verified]**, so check with the Florida Division of Alcoholic Beverages and Tobacco before using price specials (the v2 meta-prompt now asks for this).
  - *Safety optics:* water on a floor = slip hazard; it reads as fantasy because of the glow, so keep it.
  - *Technical:* water-on-floor V2V may also alter the furniture → fallback: composite the water layer generated separately over the real plate in the edit.
- **CD note:** "The bell is the brand. Ring it in every piece of content for a year."

#### Package 2: "Smoke Becomes a Ship" (M4, 6s product loop)
- **Idea line:** "Low Tide: smoked mezcal that sets sail." Macro: smoke from the cloche over the Low Tide glass curls into a tiny ghostly sailing ship that drifts off the rim across the bar and dissolves at the bell.
- **Kling 4.0 keyframes (6s, I2V from the real drink packshot):**
```
Keyframe 1 (0s): [real packshot] the Low Tide cocktail on a dark teal bar top, sea-salt foam, a glass cloche lifting off (cloche already rising, no hands visible), smoke spilling out.
Keyframe 4 (2s): the smoke gathers above the rim into the shape of a small three-masted sailing ship, translucent, glowing faintly in warm backlight.
Keyframe 8 (4.5s): the smoke ship drifts along the bar toward a brass ship's bell, its sails thinning.
Keyframe 10 (6s): the ship dissolves into wisps at the bell, which gives one soft ring.
Camera: slow lateral track following the ship, 100mm macro, shallow focus. Audio: soft whoosh, creaking wood, one bell ring. No text. Glass and foam must match Keyframe 1 exactly.
```
- **Risks:** the cloche lifting is a "hand" moment, so start with it already rising; the loop ends on the bell, and the first frame matches the last for an infinite loop. Price $400–700 as an add-on.

#### Package 3: "The Tuesday-Only Menu" (M3, the safe-but-sharp option)
- **Idea line:** "There's a drink we refuse to make on weekends." A teaser series: the bartender shows one cocktail that's *only* served Tue–Thu (the business must commit to it). Each week, a new "locals-only" drink, revealed with an AI-native macro ingredient world (e.g., a salt-crystal city, metaphor #5 in this test's list) then the real drink.
- **Why:** inversion (scarcity for tourists, privilege for locals) that creates a *reason* to come on Tuesday, a business mechanic rather than only an ad.
- **Prompt (ingredient-world open, 4s):** `Extreme macro, 9:16: coarse sea-salt crystals on a dark teal slate become, as the camera glides low between them, a miniature night-time street of white salt buildings with warm window lights. Camera: slow FPV glide forward, ending on a gap that opens onto a blurred bar. 100mm macro feel. Audio: crystalline tinkles, distant street murmur. No text, no people.`
- **Risks:** low; drink-price specials need checking under FL law [not verified]. Price $800–1,200 for 4 episodes using a shared template.

**Pattern:** for local businesses, the winners were **hybrid**: real people and real places, with AI doing *one* impossible thing. Only the packaged-goods brief (VOLTA) won with a mostly-generated ad, and even there the human reaction was filmed. This matches the 2025–26 pattern (hybrid beats fully generated) and the Lemon finding (people + place + story).

### 5.6 Critique of the v1 output, and the v2 refinements

Eight failures found when reviewing the v1 outputs against the brief, the research and chapters 03/07:

| # | v1 failure (seen in) | Why it matters | v2 refinement (now in §4.2) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Spectacle monoculture**: v1's top 3 for VOLTA were all AI spectacle with no human voice; across the 4 briefs, several of the 120 raw ideas leaned on "things floating" or morphing | Lemon: left-brain ads underperform long-term; "floating objects" is turning into the 2026 AI cliché | Quotas: ≥6 ideas with a real human, ≥4 hybrid, ≤3 floating/morph-to-logo; the top 3 must span ≥2 mechanisms |
| 2 | **Overlapping finalists** (realtor #9/#16, bar #26/#1) | Wastes a package slot; the client sees "three" options that are one | "Merge overlapping finalists and promote the next" (Step 3) |
| 3 | **No safe option** (med spa top 3 led by the stereotype idea) | Clients need one option they can approve today; a bold-only slate stalls deals | Require one "safe-but-sharp" idea (legal 5, cost ≥4) |
| 4 | **Missed category law**: no §456.062 statement for "complimentary consultation"; an implied "no crash" claim in a soda super; "cure for your week" in a bar VO | These are the mistakes that get a client fined or an ad rejected | Category legal quick rules embedded + self-critique item 4 (claims, offers, disclosures by state) |
| 5 | **Airbrushed humans** in image prompts (med spa) | It contradicts the brand promise and implies results | Prompt rule: "natural skin texture, visible fine lines, no smoothing" for any beauty/medical human |
| 6 | **Underestimated generation cost** (the first draft assumed a few tries per shot) | Kalshi needed about 300–400 generations for 15 clips | Generation ratio built into Step 0 and 4.9 (20–25× hero, 5–8× simple) |
| 7 | **Score inflation**: first-pass scores clustered at 4+ with little spread | No discrimination means no decision | Calibration cap (≤5 ideas ≥4.0) |
| 8 | **Hands in contact shots** (can opening, cloche lift, handshake) | Grade C–D AI weakness | Prompt rule "cut on contact / object acts by itself"; self-critique item 3 |

**Remaining weaknesses (not fixed, be aware):**
- Scoring is still Claude's own judgement. **Validate with a 5-person "one-second test"** of the key visuals, or with a $50 Meta hook test (5 hook variants, a 3-second view rate) before full production.
- The insight step can produce plausible-but-generic truths. Ask the client one real question ("what do customers say right before they buy?") whenever possible.
- Legal notes are flags, not legal advice; FL alcohol price-special rules and MLS virtual-staging disclosure rules were not verified here.
- **None of the prompts have been generated yet [not tested].** The first real test should be the VOLTA Shot 4 keyframe + the Kling 4.0 keyframe transition (cheapest, and it shows whether the gravity metaphor reads).

## 7. One-page checklists

**Before ideation (brief):** distinctive assets listed · 3 verifiable product facts · the audience's own words · one action · jurisdiction · budget with the generation ratio.

**Idea gate (each finalist):** passes the 0.5-second key-visual test · passes the brand-swap test · the product causes the change · a human + a place · ≤1 law of physics broken · no category or AI cliché without a twist · no claim beyond the facts.

**Prompt gate (each shot):** one camera move with an end state · named light source + direction · no hands in contact (cut on contact) · no on-screen text · PRODUCT LOCK on product shots · natural-skin rule for humans · audio specified (or "no music") · NEGATIVE line ≤6 items · method chosen (T2V / I2V / KF-seq / V2V / LIVE / POST).

**Ship gate:** label/logo composited from the real packshot · required legal lines (brokerage name / §456.062 / responsible drinking / AI disclosure) · Meta category (Housing SAC, alcohol 21+) · 5 hook variants exported · 6s + 15s (+ 30s) cutdowns.
