## 1. Definitions and a short timeline

**FOOH (fake / faux out-of-home):** a short vertical video in which CGI or generative AI places an impossible advertising stunt into footage of a real, recognizable public place, styled to look like a passer-by filmed it. Nothing was installed. The video exists to be shared on social media; the "OOH" exists only in the frame.

**Neighbors (do not confuse them):**
- **Anamorphic 3D billboards** (real screens with a forced-perspective illusion). They are physically real, but as OOH Insider points out, they only work from one spot on the sidewalk, so "anyone who's actually done it will tell you they did it for the social media content". Source: OOH Insider, "The Great Debate: Real vs Fake OOH", YouTube rKsyeH364HI (transcript).
- **AI impossible-product stunts**: fully generated (no real plate). Examples are giant products in invented cities, or "room explosion" reveals.
- **Hybrid AI production** (Kaufland 2026): real actors with AI-generated worlds. This is not FOOH, but it is the same lesson about which parts must stay real.

## 2. Case files (frame-grid analyzed)

Each case gives what is on screen (from the grid), the mechanism behind it, the numbers, and the lesson for us.

### 2.1 Jacquemus "Bambino bags on wheels" (Paris, April 2023, Ian Padgham/Origiful)
- **Grid (Independent TV cut, mnDNAv9HBSM, and HuffPost FR fact-check, x3MWAM_DmcA):** low, handheld street-level phone shots on Rue Auber and Place de l'Opéra. The Haussmann façades are instantly French. Three bags (pink, chocolate, orange) drive toward the camera, and one passes so close it fills the frame (a pink wall with a little windshield). The grain and exposure are those of a normal phone, and the sky is flat. In the HuffPost cut, the earlier Jacquemus storefront FOOH (a washing machine tumbling bags, with "red" bags spilling out) and Angèle's pink rollercoaster through Paris (Pompidou) both appear, which shows Padgham's series.
- **Padgham's own reveal:** "this was the original video… it was just a small car going down the street that I filmed that I thought looked cute." The plate came first, the idea came second (ADC talk).
- **Mechanism:** a **scale inversion of the brand's own signature.** Jacquemus was famous for absurdly *tiny* bags (Le Chiquito), so making them bus-sized is a brand joke ("goes the opposite direction", Hypebeast). It also borrows the authority of the most famous place in the city, and keeps the plausibility window wide open with phone footage and a real car's motion path.
- **Lesson:** the strongest FOOH twists something the brand is already known for. Find the brand's existing "signature fact" and invert its scale or context.

### 2.2 Maybelline Sky High "Tube lashes" (London, July 2023, Padgham)
- **Grid (case-study film, nYBnQw72bsU):** the opening frames are the strategic insight, typed on screen: "MASCARA. FACES. CLAIMS. INFLUENCERS. IT ALL LOOKS THE SAME… AS THE NO.1 [brand we] NEEDED TO CHANGE IT UP… **NO PEOPLE. NO BEFORE AND AFTER.**" Then a red Tube train with lashes, a station platform, a building with a giant wand, and a wall of TikTok reaction videos. The results cards read **116,639,328 views · 6,263,287 engagements · 825M+ PR circulation · +118% sales uplift · "biggest peak sales during week of virality"**, plus other percentage cards that are not fully legible at storyboard resolution. The voice-over says: "because this was so absurd it led to something called **speculation engagement** where you have troves of people speculating whether this was real… driving that video to millions and millions more people."
- **Brand side:** Lauren Chapman (UK brand manager) found Padgham on Instagram and wanted "is it real, is it fake type content". Maybelline US VP Fernando Febres said: "a way to honestly disrupt the environment and get people talking… 'Is it real or is it not?'". He also said, "If we had done the same activation in traditional out-of-home, we probably wouldn't have gotten the eyeballs", and "if it becomes too expected, we'll probably switch to something else." (Modern Retail/Digiday, 11 Aug 2023)
- **Mechanism:** a **category-convention break.** Every mascara ad shows a face, a claim and a before/after, so this one shows none of them. The product *demonstrates its action* (lengthening and sweeping lashes) on a city object that is anthropomorphized (a train "face" with lashes). The benefit becomes the spectacle.
- **Lesson:** write down the visual clichés of the client's category, then ban them. Let the product perform its function on the city.

### 2.3 Barbie (Dubai, July–Aug 2023, Eye Studio)
- **Grid (jPpuGwUrqsY):** a wide, locked, symmetrical shot of the Burj Khalifa boulevard. A giant pink Barbie box stands in the plaza, and the doll steps out and walks. The rest of the compilation shows real Barbie movie OOH and retail. People couldn't tell which parts were real, and fact-check channels ran "video does NOT show a giant Barbie".
- **Mechanism:** **cultural moment plus the most famous skyline on earth.** The film had huge pre-existing anticipation, so the FOOH rode a wave rather than creating one.
- **Lesson:** time FOOH to something people are already talking about locally (Art Basel Miami Beach in December, the Fort Lauderdale boat show in late October/November, spring break, hurricane season *(handle with care, see §7)*, the World Cup 2026 legacy).

### 2.4 Santam "umbrella" (South Africa, insurer, CGI)
- **Grid (yK6BBjgC_aA):** a sunset rooftop dinner with people at tables. A giant yellow umbrella rises behind the Johannesburg skyline and opens over the city. Night shots show the umbrella glowing over the lit city, and a daytime crowd on a rooftop films it on their phones. The finale is over Cape Town and Table Mountain with the Santam logo, and many small yellow umbrellas spread across the city below.
- **Mechanism:** a **visual metaphor for an intangible product** (insurance = cover = umbrella). The **in-frame audience** (people filming) tells viewers how to react, and acts as social proof inside the shot. *Agency and date not verified.*
- **Lesson:** services with no physical product (insurance, law firms, dental, roofing) can still do FOOH by turning the *promise* into an object.

### 2.5 "Beyond Reality" CGI compilation (fbKvWpFFNfY) and FOOH compilation (-5yyjNNkC18)
- **Grid:** a Paris street with giant sneakers and soft-sculpture shoes dropping into traffic; an empty downtown avenue (closed-street look) with a herd of elephants walking toward camera; vertical clips of Paris landmarks with pink objects.
- **Lesson:** this is the generic tier (objects floating or walking in a city with no product logic). It is exactly what Padgham calls diminishing returns, and what the FOOH Awards jury says no longer wins: "Making something impossible happen on camera is no longer enough."

### 2.6 Toys"R"Us "The Origin of Toys"R"Us" (Native Foreign, Sora, Cannes Lions June 2024)
- **Grid (ah4kzfuc3wo):** a 1930s bike shop exterior, a boy (young Charles Lazarus) with glasses, a giraffe toy on the counter, the boy asleep, a dream with Geoffrey the Giraffe, a cosmic toy store, a toy galaxy, and a Polaroid of Geoffrey with Charles. The end card reads "Toys"R"Us @ Macy's". **The boy's face visibly drifts between shots** (jaw, hair and glasses shape change), and the shots are short with dreamy blur and particles that hide detail.
- **Producer's admission:** "Everything you see was created with text, but some shots came together quicker than others… The blocking, the way the character looks, what they're wearing, the emotion, the background — it has to be a perfect dance." (Nik Kleverov to CNN, via Hollywood Reporter.) Wikipedia records the backlash for "creepiness", and Ars Technica noted "unnatural movement, strange visual artifacts, and the irregular shape of eyeglasses".
- **Lesson:** a **human face carrying an emotional story** is the hardest thing for gen-video and the first thing audiences judge. In FOOH, keep people small, background or real. Use the dream framing (which they did) to license strangeness.

### 2.7 Coca-Cola "Holidays Are Coming", AI versions 2024 and 2025
- **Grid, 2024 "Secret Santa" variant (IQWUKWM2JrQ):** night forest, a squirrel, polar bears, a red truck convoy, a snowy village, and **many close-ups of human faces** smiling at the trucks, plus a Coke bottle in hand. Dark grading hides texture.
- **Grid, 2025 (Yy6fByUmPuE):** a **Santa's hand placing a toy truck** into a miniature snow village (a toy-scale opener that hides the physics), then polar bears, penguins, hedgehogs, a panda, a sloth, seals, bunnies watching a Coke billboard, puppies at a window, squirrels, and a squirrel placing the star on a tree. **No human faces in close-up.** Humans appear only as tiny crowds or Santa's hand and legs.
- **Silverside making-of (downloaded from their case page; transcript + grid):** the style was tested across "2D Animation" and "Realistic" looks, and they landed on "super expressive hyper-realism". A visible prompt card reads *"A hyper realistic animation of a panda trying to see something at a distance in the back of the bamboo woods. Closeup. Winter, snow, night shot. Realistic textures and fur, beautiful light."* The toolbox slide shows **ComfyUI, Flux, Veo 3, Kling, Runway, Higgsfield, Krea**. The claims: "five specialists… over 70,000 video clips… in just 30 days". About 2024: "wheels looked like they were sliding instead of rolling. Physics needed correction. Logos had to be adjusted and repositioned." The 2025 thesis: **"Post-production is the new pre-production."**
- **The backlash, in critics' words (oZGYU0CcfbY, 446K views):** "there's almost no sense of continuity… no long continuous shots… no recurring characters other than the trucks, which also seems to change in appearance every single shot… all of these shots transition after like 2 or 3 seconds… the longer you let some of these shots play out, the higher the chances of them looking absolutely uncanny." Also: "the plot… was limited by AI… forced to constantly change animals and locations." Plus safety-looking errors (crowds too close to moving trucks). Top YouTube comment: "The most profitable commercial in Pepsi's history" (Hyperight). Coca-Cola and WPP said it tested well with consumers (Stash Media). Pepsi reportedly used a polar bear in its Super Bowl 2026 spot to mock this (YouTube and LinkedIn commentary, not independently verified).
- **Lesson:** (1) **The 2–3-second cut tell:** fast cutting to hide artifacts is now recognized by audiences as an AI signature. (2) **Anchor continuity:** one recurring hero object that stays identical (in FOOH, the product) does more than a parade of new subjects. (3) Heritage brands remaking a beloved human-crafted classic with AI invite comparison and anger. Local clients with nostalgic assets should be warned.

### 2.8 Real footage + Seedance VFX ("I Added Insane AI VFX to Real Footage in 4K", Yte-UGhYkPQ, 376K views)
- **Grid and transcript:** the creator films himself (walking, driving, monkey bars, a staircase), drops each clip into Claude with a "skill", and Claude writes a Seedance 2.0 prompt that **"writes down everything that has to stay exactly as I shot it. My face, my walk, the rings on my hand, every gesture, and the exact handheld camera move, and locks it. It also writes the exact seconds to change the background."** Results: a desert swap on a finger-snap, neon/lava/sky worlds while driving (Seedance relights the face with the new environment light), a head of hair on fire, a hand morph timed to a spoken line, **big lizards climbing a building behind him** (using a GPT-Image reference of the lizard: "when you already know exactly what you want, it's better to show the AI than to describe it"), dinosaurs, and a kraken.
- **Key claims:** handheld chaos is the hardest case, and "shoot each clip already knowing what I want to see in it."
- **Lesson:** this is **Pipeline B** (§4) and it is the FOOH engine for 2026. A real phone plate keeps plausibility (real people, real shake, real light), and the model adds only the impossible object. The "lock list" is the craft.

### 2.9 C4D untextured render → Seedance retexture (GqyohMi8Dvg)
- **Transcript:** a gray Cinema 4D scene (Sketchfab car plus a cube, lit, with an animated camera, **no textures**) goes into Seedance "universal reference" with the render video and **a reference image made by putting frame 1 into Nano Banana** ("make a beautiful render… do not change the camera angle"). The prompt: *"Change the style of every frame of @video to match the art style of a cinematic movie with real textures like in the reference @image. A gray Porsche covered in ice stands on an ice floe across the ocean."* Seedance also handled a **start + end frame** version (the car drives, the ice cracks, the car falls in), which Runway, Luma and Kling could not do at the time. Seedance followed the source motion over the reference (it removed dust that implied motion the source didn't have).
- **Lesson:** this is **Pipeline C**. It confirms the chapter 14 approach for products and scale: geometry and camera come from 3D, and look comes from the reference frame.

### 2.10 Award-winning FOOH 2026 (FOOH Awards jury)
The jury drew three lessons, which are now the bar:
1. **"Bigger doesn't automatically mean better":** scale needs a purpose through movement, humor or interaction (Primark's bag walks and leaves a light trail; Lidl's halva box won't fit in the car).
2. **"The product needs a reason to be there":** InterMed's sun-care bottle pops out of its billboard and drops "sun shields" over pedestrians; BMW's iX3 writes "Happy Season" with its tire tracks in snow.
3. **Simple ideas still work, and the brand stays part of the idea:** golf balls flood a NYC subway platform (Ryder Cup), and an airport lounge becomes a water slide (Discover Airlines: "Your vacation starts now").

## 3. The mechanism: why FOOH works (and when it stops working)

Each principle ends with how to apply it to an AI video ad.

### 3.1 Scale shock, with exactly one anomaly
Our brains run a constant "expected size" check against known references (cars, doors, people). A violated size prior grabs attention before conscious thought does. That violation has to be **legible in under a second**, so it needs reference objects in frame. Two anomalies split attention and read as "fantasy", which shuts down the "is it real?" loop.
- **How to apply:** one impossible object per shot. Always include at least two true-scale anchors (a car, a person, a bus, a palm tree, a door). Name them in the prompt.
- *Prompt fragment:* `…the giant [product] is about 12 meters tall, roughly the height of the three-storey building beside it; real cars and pedestrians at true scale pass in front of it for reference…`

### 3.2 The plausibility window ("speculation engagement")
Doubt holds attention better than spectacle (Kalex Studio; Maybelline case film). The ideal FOOH sits in a band where the viewer *can't decide for two or three seconds*. If it is too fake, they scroll; if it is too real, nobody comments. The comment-section argument ("I was there yesterday", "it's CGI") is free distribution.
- **How to apply:** make the object physically *possible to build* (a wrapped bus, an inflatable, a projection, a crane lift) and its *behavior* slightly impossible (it moves, breathes or interacts). Start with the most plausible frame and end on the impossible beat.
- Also disclose (see §7). Disclosure in the caption rarely kills the debate. Kalex: "the 'how did they do this' conversation survives it."

### 3.3 Location recognizability (borrowed authority and identity)
A famous place makes a small brand look like it bought a big-budget stunt, and gives locals tribal pride ("that's MY Las Olas"). Locals then share it and tag it. The Opéra, the Tube and the Burj each did half the work.
- **How to apply:** choose landmarks that locals recognize from the silhouette alone, and frame them so the landmark is readable in frame 1. In South Florida that means the Fort Lauderdale drawbridges, the A1A wave wall, Miami Beach lifeguard towers, Ocean Drive Art Deco, the Brickell skyline from the Rickenbacker, Mizner Park arches, and the Boca Inlet. Avoid trademarked buildings (§7.4).

### 3.4 Witness aesthetics (it must look like nobody planned it)
"A tripod shot looks like an ad, a wobbly one looks like a witness" (Kalex). Phone exposure, slight overexposure, compression, a finger near the lens, someone saying "yo, what is that?" and street noise all sell the scene. Polished cinematic grading moves it into "commercial" territory and drops the doubt.
- **How to apply:** write "handheld smartphone footage, slight natural shake, auto-exposure, a little highlight clipping, 1x lens, eye level from the sidewalk" in every FOOH prompt. Add real ambient audio and one off-screen human reaction in post. Never add music in the first 2 seconds.

### 3.5 The product is the transformation (benefit made visible)
The FOOH Awards jury put it this way: "the CGI supports the message instead of becoming the entire message." Maybelline's wand *does what mascara does*; Santam's umbrella *does what insurance does*; InterMed's bottle *does what SPF does*.
- **How to apply:** before choosing any visual, write one sentence: "The product does [its job] to [a city element that resembles the problem]." Example: "The hair dryer gives the frizzy A1A palms a blowout."

### 3.6 Loops and a short arc
FOOH clips run 6–15 s (Jacquemus was 8 s per Paper). A 3-beat arc works: **normal → anomaly arrives → interaction or reveal.** Then either a seamless loop (the last frame matches the first) or an abrupt cut that makes people rewatch ("did I see that?"). Rewatches and loops push completion above 100% on Reels and TikTok.
- **How to apply:** in Seedance or Kling, set the **end frame = start frame** (or the same plate frame) so the cycle closes. Alternatively, end on the object exiting the frame while the street returns to normal.

### 3.7 Diminishing returns and the "boy who cried wolf"
Padgham's own three warnings (ADC 2024), which every pitch should respect:
1. **Diminishing returns:** "every video I see is just people taking large objects for a brand and having them float around a city… the joke has been told." Fast Company likewise says "a best-before date".
2. **Gullibility and harm:** his *Arc-en-ciel de Triomphe* rainbow (a Pride video) was reposted as if real by right-wing media, and he got death threats. Viewers still DM him asking where his clearly fake *Valley of the Gods* is on Google Maps.
3. **Trust:** "if you are intentionally doing stuff to trick the people following you it will have a negative effect… the idea of using AI or using 3D CGI to trick people is just bad business."
4. His positive rule: **"It's not the technology… it's the fact that something like this is just a fun idea."** Context licenses fakery: April Fools (his Eiffel Tower bedroom got about 20M views, with "calls from Saudi princes and Russian oligarchs"), or a known artist persona.
- **How to apply:** pitch FOOH as a *joke the city is in on*, not a hoax. Build a **series** (FOOH.com's 2026 forecast calls serialized storytelling the trend) so the format becomes the brand's recognizable signature rather than a one-off trick.

### 3.8 Performance benchmarks to set expectations
- Only **19.05%** of 4,570 FOOH videos reached High Performer or Elite (FOOH.com 2026). Expect 1 in 5 to break out; budget for a series of 3–5.
- The categories with the best success rate in 2026 are travel, automotive and sports, ahead of fashion and beauty, which are over-saturated (FOOH.com). For South Florida this points to hotels, boat charters, car dealers, sports bars and pickleball clubs.
- Post on quiet windows, not the busiest days (FOOH.com report summary).

## 4. Production workflows: choose one per concept

| | **A. Real plate + 3D CGI** | **B. Real plate + AI video edit** | **C. Blender blockout → AI render** | **D. Fully AI (T2V/I2V)** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| What it is | Film a phone plate → camera-track (Blender/SynthEyes) → model and animate the product → light match → composite | Film a phone plate → Seedance 2.5 reference-video edit (or Genjutsu/Kling edit) with a lock list → add the impossible object | Build the street and product as colored blocks in Blender (Claude writes bpy) → animate camera and object → render gray/semantic → Seedance with ref images | Prompt or keyframe → Seedance/Kling/Veo video |
| Plausibility | Highest (exact product geometry) | Very high (real people, real light) | Medium-high (location invented, so it needs a strong reference) | Lowest (generic places, AI tells) |
| Product/logo accuracy | Exact | Medium; fix the logo in post with a tracked packshot | Medium-high (geometry exact, label in post) | Low |
| Landmark accuracy | Exact (it's real) | Exact (it's real) | Approximate | Approximate, often wrong details locals will notice |
| Skill and time | Days–weeks, VFX skill | 1–2 days | 1–2 days (chapter 14 pipeline) | Hours |
| Cost to client (market) | Mid thousands to tens of thousands of USD | Low thousands | Low thousands | Hundreds (Kalex ranges) |
| Use for | Hero brand work, exact product | **Default for local South Florida FOOH** | Places you can't film (rooftops, aerial impossible moves, pre-construction) | Concepts, pitches, mood tests, background extras |

**Recommendation:** default to **B**. Use **C** when the camera move is impossible to film or the location is private or unbuilt. Use **D** only for pitch animatics and storyboards. Use **A** when the client has a 3D model of the product (many CPG brands do: ask for it).

### 4.1 Pipeline B, step by step (real plate + Seedance edit)
1. **Scout and shoot the plate.** Use a phone at 4K 30fps, 1x lens, eye level, handheld, with a slow, *simple* move (a 3–6 s pan or walk). Leave **empty space** where the object will appear, keep the sky or road visible, and shoot 3 takes. Film in the same light as the hero time (golden hour = 1 hour before sunset). Record 30 s of clean ambient audio. Avoid faces close to camera (privacy, and the NY synthetic performer issue if the AI alters them).
2. **Make the object reference** with GPT Image / Nano Banana. Show the product at the right scale in a frame from the plate ("in this exact frame, add…"). This is the "show, don't describe" rule (§2.8). Make 2–3 options.
3. **Write the lock list prompt** (template below). Claude reads the plate as frames and lists everything that must not change.
4. **Generate in Seedance 2.5:** reference video = plate, reference image = the composited frame, 480p test → 720p/1080p. Generate 4–6 variants.
5. **Fix the logo:** track a real packshot or PNG of the label onto the object in CapCut/After Effects/Blender. Never trust AI text.
6. **Witness grade:** add a little noise, mild compression, a slight highlight clip, phone-style sharpening and a tiny handheld jitter if the AI over-stabilized.
7. **Sound:** real ambient + a Foley "whoosh/creak/thud" at the scale beat + one off-screen voice reaction ("wait… what?"). Real recorded voices are safer than synthetic ones (NY law).
8. **Loop and cut:** 7–12 s total, last frame ≈ first frame.
9. **Disclose** (caption + platform AI toggle). See §7.5.

### 4.2 Pipeline C, step by step (Blender blockout → Seedance)
This follows chapter 14 (`skills/reel-studio/scripts/blockout.py`), adapted for FOOH:
- **FOOH colors:** white = buildings, light gray = road and sidewalk, cyan = water, green spheres = palms, orange capsules = pedestrians, dark blocks = cars, **YELLOW = the giant product** (bright and unique), magenta = the label face (so the label can later be tracked).
- **Camera:** simulate a phone. Use a 26 mm equivalent lens at 1.6 m height, with a slow walk path plus small noise (add procedural noise to the camera location, about 1–2 cm, at roughly 2 Hz).
- **Scale anchors:** always include block cars (4.5 m) and capsule people (1.75 m) near the product block so the model learns the scale.
- Render a 6 s 720×1280 gray/semantic animation → Seedance with 2–3 reference images (location look + product).

### 4.3 Pipeline D (fully AI): when it is acceptable
Use it for pitch animatics, mood tests, or deliberately stylized "dream" pieces where obvious fakeness is the point. To reduce the Coca-Cola failure modes:
- Hold shots for 4–8 s instead of cutting every 2 s, and use one recurring hero object (identical reference every shot).
- No close-up human faces, no hands manipulating the product, no readable text.
- Prefer one continuous shot over a montage.

## 6. Fully-AI ads vs FOOH: what to tell clients in 2026

| Risk in all-AI ads | Seen in | Mitigation in our concepts |
|---|---|---|
| Faces drift between shots | Toys"R"Us (Sora), Coke 2024 crowds | Real people from the plate; AI only for objects and worlds |
| Fast cuts to hide artifacts read as "slop" | Coke 2024/25 critique | 1–2 long shots per FOOH; continuous takes |
| Physics (sliding wheels, wrong scale) | Coke 2024 (Silverside's own admission) | Blockout (C) or real plate (B) supplies the physics |
| Logo errors | Coke 2024 ("logos had to be adjusted") | Label tracked in post from the client's vector file |
| "AI is the story" instead of the brand | Kaufland 2025 vs 2026 (FOOH.com) | The idea must work without mentioning AI |
| Fans of a legacy ad feel robbed | Coke AI remakes | Don't remake a beloved human-made local classic with AI without a reason |

**Hybrid playbook (Kaufland "Ice Ice Baby", EASYdoesit, 2026):** 12 real actors, 3 physical tracksuits (the rest AI-fitted), 23 AI worlds built as stills first and **displayed live on the set monitor so the gaffer could match the light**, and AI artists working beside compositors. "Shoot the humans. Generate the worlds. Composite the truth." (fooh.com/blog/kaufland-ice-ice-baby-hybrid-ai-campaign)

**Spec-ad note (Nike × Wembanyama by USC student Asher Hyde, 2026):** a fan-made AI concept went viral because it tied together a *real cultural moment* (nuns courtside at Spurs games). It shows that cultural timing is the advantage, not polish. Spec work must be labeled "spec / not affiliated" (FOOH.com newsletter, 29 May 2026).

## 7. Legal and ethical notes (USA/Florida first; this is not legal advice)

### 7.1 Deception law (the core test is the "net impression")
- **FTC Act §5 (federal)** prohibits deceptive acts in commerce. Regulators judge ads by the *overall impression* left on a reasonable consumer, not by literal truth (also Kalex: "advertising regulators such as the FTC in the US, the ASA in the UK… judge ads by the impression they leave"). A FOOH clip is fine as *obvious spectacle*. It becomes deceptive when it implies a **false material fact**: a product capability ("the sunscreen created a shield": avoid efficacy visuals), an event ("we're opening at Las Olas Saturday"), an amenity ("this condo has a rooftop pool"), a partnership ("official sponsor of X"), or a price. The FTC's AI pages (ftc.gov/industry/technology/artificial-intelligence) focus on deceptive AI claims and fake AI reviews and testimonials. **Never fake reviews or customer reactions as testimonials** (the FTC fake-reviews rule, 2024).
- **Florida Deceptive and Unfair Trade Practices Act (FDUTPA, Fla. Stat. 501.201 et seq.)** is the state-level analogue. Our clients are Florida businesses, so this is the most likely lever for a competitor or consumer complaint.
- **Florida political ads:** since 2024, AI-generated political ads must carry a disclaimer (HB 919). Do not take political FOOH work without counsel.

### 7.2 Synthetic people
- **New York GBL §396-b (effective 9 Jun 2026):** anyone who produces an ad must "conspicuously disclose" when it includes a **synthetic performer** (a digitally created asset intended to look like a human performance of someone not identifiable), where they have actual knowledge. Penalties are **$1,000 for the first violation and $5,000 for each subsequent one.** Audio-only ads and translation-only uses are exempt. Applies if the ad reaches New York audiences, which matters for social campaigns (Governor's press release; JD Supra). **For FOOH:** if the AI generates or alters *people* (pedestrians reacting, a "driver"), add the disclosure. Pipeline B with real, unaltered passers-by avoids most of this.
- **Right of publicity:** Florida Stat. §540.08 bars commercial use of a person's name or likeness without consent. Real passers-by recognizable in a plate used commercially need a release or blurring. Never generate a recognizable local celebrity, athlete or politician.

### 7.3 Platform rules (check at posting time; they change)
- **TikTok:** requires the AIGC label (organic) or AI disclosure (ads) for realistic AI-generated or significantly altered scenes, faces, voices and backgrounds. Undisclosed AI ads can be rejected, and accounts face escalating penalties (secondary summaries: auditsocials.com, ugcvids.ai, 2026; verify in TikTok's Ads Policy).
- **Meta:** "AI info" labels, auto-applied from C2PA/IPTC metadata or self-applied. **YouTube:** the "altered or synthetic content" disclosure in Studio for realistic content.
- **EU AI Act Art. 50 (applies from 2 Aug 2026):** deployers must disclose deepfakes (AI content resembling real persons, places or events that would falsely appear authentic). There is a lighter regime for evidently artistic, satirical or fictional work. Relevant if a client's ad targets EU tourists or customers.

### 7.4 Places, buildings and other people's IP (South Florida specifics)
- **Buildings visible from public places** can generally be photographed and depicted (US copyright, 17 U.S.C. §120(a), the architectural works exception). But **trademarked building designs and names** are a separate issue. Avoid using the Seminole Hard Rock **Guitar Hotel** (Hollywood, FL), **The Boca Raton** resort's pink tower and name, the **Fontainebleau** name and logo, or **Brightline** trains and livery as the canvas for *another* brand's stunt. That implies affiliation (Lanham Act false endorsement). Use public infrastructure (bridges, beaches, streets, generic skylines) instead, or partner with the owner.
- **Murals (Wynwood Walls and others)** are pictorial works, *not* covered by the architectural exception. Muralists have repeatedly sued brands for using their walls in ads. Keep murals out of frame or license them.
- **Public property and uniforms:** don't put brands on police, fire, lifeguard or ambulance vehicles or uniforms in FOOH. That suggests government endorsement and can confuse people in an emergency. Miami Beach lifeguard towers can be depicted as landmarks, but don't imply city sponsorship.
- **Filming the plate:** casual handheld phone filming in public spaces is usually fine. Tripods, crews, closed lanes or commercial shoots in Miami-Dade, Broward, Palm Beach County and on the beaches may need a **film permit** from the county or city film office. **Drone plates need FAA Part 107** certification and must respect airspace rules (most of coastal Miami and Fort Lauderdale is near MIA, FLL, or Boca Raton Airport BCT controlled airspace).

### 7.5 Ethical red lines and the disclosure kit
**Never FOOH-fake** in South Florida: hurricanes or storm damage, flooding, beach closures, shark sightings, sargassum, emergency scenes, police activity, real store openings or events that aren't happening, or anything people might **travel to see** (the Bordeaux "wine tram" tourists problem). And never deny it was CGI once asked: "the brand insists it was real" is the worst backfire (Kalex).

**Disclosure caption templates** (combine with the platform AI toggle):
- `CGI/AI art — not a real installation (yet 👀). Made by @[creator] for @[brand].`
- `Spec ad · AI-generated · not affiliated with [brand]` (portfolio pieces).
- `This didn't happen. But [real offer] does — [CTA].` (This turns the disclosure into the hook.)
- First comment pinned: `How we made it 👉 [BTS link]` (the BTS "blocks → reality" reveal from chapter 14 is a second post that earns more reach).

### C1. "The 7 A.M. Bridge Pour": coffee roaster or café (Fort Lauderdale)
- **Landmark:** a Fort Lauderdale bascule drawbridge (e.g. the Andrews Ave or 3rd Ave bridge over the New River) as it opens.
- **The image:** the bridge leaves rise, and the gap between them is filled by a giant stream of espresso pouring from a cup-shaped bridge leaf into the river, which turns crema-brown, while a boat passes underneath.
- **Why:** locals all know (and hate) waiting for the bridge. The insight "the bridge opens, so do we" pairs a daily ritual with a daily ritual. It also loops naturally (the bridge closes).
- **Shots:** S1 (0–2 s) handheld from the sidewalk at the bridge gates, bells ringing and leaves starting to rise. S2 (2–7 s) the leaves at full height; the far leaf is a giant white ceramic cup rim pouring espresso into the river, with crema foam spreading around a passing sailboat. S3 (7–10 s) the leaves lower, the river returns to blue, and the gate lifts (loop). S4 end card (real product, PNG): "Open at 7. Unlike the bridge."
- **Pipeline:** B (film a real opening; they happen often).
- **Prompt (Seedance 2.5):**
```
@Video1 is real handheld smartphone footage of a Fort Lauderdale bascule drawbridge over the New River opening, filmed from behind the pedestrian gate. Keep everything exactly as filmed: camera shake, framing, timing, the rising bridge leaves, cars waiting, palms, sky, light.
Add ONE element: as the far bridge leaf reaches full height (2.0s), its top edge is the rim of a giant white ceramic espresso cup (matching @Image1), and a thick stream of dark espresso with golden crema pours from it into the river below, as wide as a car. 2.0–7.0s: the river surface around the stream turns crema-brown with realistic foam spreading in circles; a white sailboat (already in @Video1) passes through the foam, leaving a wake. 7.0–10.0s: the pour stops, the foam dissipates downstream, the leaves lower exactly as filmed.
Correct morning light, steam rising from the stream, reflections on the water. Smartphone look, no text, no logos.
```
- **Sound:** real bridge bells + a deep liquid pour + a passer-by: "of course it's coffee."
- **Caption:** "Bridge up = coffee break. ☕ (CGI, obviously.) Open 7am on [street]."
- **Risks:** don't show fake traffic incidents or anyone in danger near the moving leaf. Film from the public sidewalk only.

### C2. "Palm Blowout on A1A": hair salon or blowout bar (Fort Lauderdale or Boca)
- **Landmark:** the palm line along A1A at Fort Lauderdale Beach (the white wave wall in foreground).
- **The image:** a giant salon hair dryer and round brush float behind the palms and give the frizzy, wind-whipped fronds a glossy, bouncy blowout, one tree at a time.
- **Why:** humidity frizz is *the* South Florida hair pain point. The palms are anthropomorphized hair (Maybelline logic). It works as a satisfying before/after on the city, not on a face.
- **Shots:** S1 (0–2 s) a slow pan along messy palms in wind. S2 (2–7 s) a giant matte-black dryer (about 8 m) and a round brush sweep across three palms; the fronds become sleek and voluminous and shine catches the sun. S3 (7–9 s) a jogger stops and looks back. End card: "Humidity: 0. Blowout: 1."
- **Pipeline:** B.
- **Prompt:**
```
@Video1: real handheld phone footage panning along a row of tall palm trees on A1A, Fort Lauderdale beach, white wavy beach wall in foreground, windy morning. Keep camera, people, cars, wall, ocean and sky exactly as filmed.
Add ONE element: a giant matte-black professional hair dryer and a giant round brush (design from @Image1), each about 8 meters long, hovering at the height of the palm crowns. 2–7s: they move along the row together like a stylist's hands; where they pass, the frizzy, tangled fronds become smooth, glossy and bouncy, curling neatly at the tips, catching sunlight like healthy hair; leaves and sand blow away from the dryer's airflow.
Shadows of the dryer fall on the sand consistent with the sun. Smartphone look, no text.
```

- **Sound:** a real salon dryer whoosh pitched down, a wind gust, a laugh.
- **Caption:** "Even the palms booked us. CGI, real results in-salon 💁‍♀️"

## 9. Quick rules (for Claude as creative director)
1. One impossible thing per shot, with two true-scale anchors in frame.
2. The product must *do its job* to the city; if you remove the product, the idea should die.
3. Use a landmark a local recognizes from its silhouette, and never someone else's trademark as the canvas.
4. Witness look: handheld phone, real ambient sound, a human reaction. No cinematic grade, no music in the first 2 s.
5. Real plate + AI edit (B) by default; blockout (C) for impossible cameras; full AI (D) only for pitches.
6. Real people stay real; AI makes objects and worlds (the Kaufland rule). Disclose any synthetic people (NY §396-b).
7. Hold shots long, and loop the end to the start. Avoid 2-second AI montages (the Coca-Cola tell).
8. Never fake events, weather, emergencies, openings or claims; always disclose CGI/AI; never deny it.
9. Timing beats polish: tie the stunt to a live local moment (boat show, Art Basel, spring training, 3:05 cafecito).
10. Expect 1 in 5 to break out, so pitch series.

## Appendix A: getting YouTube frames when downloads are blocked (2026-10)
- Plain `yt-dlp` returns "Sign in to confirm you're not a bot". The `tv`/`ios`/`android_vr` clients fail. The `web_embedded` client works for metadata, but video URLs return **403** (a PO token is needed).
- **What works:** `yt-dlp --js-runtimes "node:/opt/node22/bin/node" --extractor-args "youtube:player_client=web_embedded" -f sb0 -o "sb_%(id)s.%(ext)s" <url>`. Node must be given explicitly, because the default `node` on PATH is v20, which yt-dlp marks "unsupported". This produces an `.mhtml` of storyboard sheets (5x5 frames at 160x90 each, evenly spaced over the video). Extract the JPGs with Python `email.message_from_bytes` and view them, or `vstack` them with ffmpeg.
- Subtitles work with the same flags: `--skip-download --write-auto-subs --sub-langs "en-orig,en"`. Expect intermittent 429s; sleep 30–60 s.
- For search, `curl https://r.jina.ai/https://html.duckduckgo.com/html/?q=...` returns results and links (a search substitute while WebSearch quota is exhausted).
