## 1. Why they are hot (one sentence)
A Louisville, founder-built personalised-pet-gift brand running ~174 active US Meta ads (60 % of the top 30 cards are stills, mostly catalogue and dynamic cards), which published a new holiday line this week (Custom Pet Face Pajamas in holiday prints on 10/7, Pet Face Wrapping Paper and two-pet mugs on 10/9), with its gifting season about to peak.

## 2. The business
- **What they sell:** made-from-your-photo pet products: the flagship Original Plush Cuddle Clone ($247.99), Huggable and Warmable plush, plush ornaments ($99), bobbleheads, slippers, Custom Pet Face Pajamas (kids $29.25, adults $33.75 after a 25 %-off offer), wrapping paper ($29.25–36.75), mugs, prints. 623 products in the store.
- **Channels:** DTC on Shopify; Target Takeoff accelerator alumna (2021) [c]; Inc. "fastest-growing private company in Kentucky" 2019 (UofL) [c].
- **Current creative:** two tracks. **Memorial** ("Goodbyes are hard", customer quotes such as "It looks EXACTLY like him") and **gifting / pajamas** ("Upload your favorite photo of your pet and wear their adorable face everywhere", "Save 25% Off Pajamas with your pet's face"). 12 of the top 30 cards are video, mostly product turntables and UGC. **Gap:** the pajama and wrapping-paper side, the funny half of the brand, has no story film.
- **Ad lifespan:** pet creatives live 40–74 days (doc 52 N3), so a holiday film bought now can run from Dec 1 into Valentine's.

## 3. The person
- **Who decides:** **Jennifer Williams**, founder (UofL Business School: "founder and Chief Cloning Officer"; Target Takeoff interview 2021: "We hope that Cuddle Clones can become a household name and that pet parents everywhere can get something special from us."). 2016 TV coverage names her Jennifer Graham (likely a former surname) [c]. Her current title in 2026 is [unverified].
- **Origin:** the idea came after her Great Dane, Rufus, died (multiple outlets).
- **What she cares about:** accuracy to the real pet (their whole promise: "We don't do generic"), emotional truth, growth.
- **What she'd fear from an AI studio:** (1) an AI pet that doesn't look like a real pet, which undercuts the brand's accuracy promise; (2) tone: anything flippant near the memorial products; (3) AI faces in a family scene.

## 4. Fit and red flags
- **Fit:** a constant drop calendar (177 products created since 8/10), the photo-to-product promise that maps onto our "from your real photo" method, and a funny product line (pet faces on pajamas and paper) that its current ads present as catalogue cards.
- **Budget proxy:** ~174 live ads, $29–248 tickets → Premiere $2,500 is comfortable; Season plausible [inf].
- **Red flags:** (a) **never use the memorial products for comedy**; the film sells pajamas and wrapping paper only. (b) Their ads quote customers; we never invent a testimonial (FTC 16 CFR 465). (c) The printed pet face on pajamas must come from the client's real product photo; AI never draws the print. (d) Custom items have lead times (Dogster: plush "typically eight weeks"), so the pajama and paper line, not the plush, carries a Dec 1–20 film. (e) One shop tag reads `redirect` on the wrapping paper and mug, but both pages render publicly (HTTP 200, own title); they are named only as public products. (f) Claims scan: one customer quote says "I sleep with it every night" (an emotional testimonial, not a health claim); no fear, toxic or rival claims.

## 5. The buying path
**The ONE film: "Main Character" (15 s, 9:16 + 4:5, cut to 8 s).**
Logic: once you've ordered the pet-face pajamas and paper, the dog's face is on every gift. The product causes the punchline: the dog is the most-represented member of the family, and knows it.

| t (s) | Shot (real rig) | Action / sound |
|---|---|---|
| 0.0–1.0 | Overhead tripod on the floor by the tree | **Frame 0 in motion, sound first:** hands already tearing wrapping paper printed with a dog's face (rip on the measured frame). Super: "Christmas morning." |
| 1.0–3.0 | Same overhead | The next gift: same face on the paper. Then the next. |
| 3.0–5.5 | Handheld, chest height | Two people pull on the pet-face pajamas, framed chest-down; the dog's face prints line up across both shirts. |
| 5.5–8.0 | Insert, macro on a slider | **Proof slot:** the client's real product photo or footage of the printed face on the fabric. No generated print. |
| 8.0–12.0 | Tripod, medium on the sofa | The real dog (Kling i2v from a customer photo used with permission, ≤5 s, one animal) sits between the two pajama torsos, perfectly still, looking at camera. Super: "Main character." |
| 12.0–15.0 | End card | Pajamas + paper flat lay. **VO (2 lines, locked voice, no lips):** "Put their face on everything. Cuddle Clones." Logo + swappable offer slot. |

- **Rigs:** overhead floor tripod, handheld at chest height, slider insert, sofa lock-off. **People:** hands and torsos only, no AI faces. **Animal:** one dog, from a real photo, sitting only. **Sound:** paper rips on measured frames; −14 LUFS / ≤ −1 dBTP.

**Proof they need:** 5 stills built from their real pajama and paper product photos (the print unchanged), plus one frame from a customer pet photo they choose.
**Re-buy path:** Valentine's (pet-face gifts), Mother's Day, National Pet Day (April), Halloween; every new print → re-shoot the "Main Character" skeleton (Season plan).
**Suggested tier:** Premiere $2,500 for a Dec 1–20 refresh, cut-downs for Valentine's.
