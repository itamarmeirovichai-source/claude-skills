## 2. The business
- **What they sell:** fine jewellery in recycled gold, with lab-grown diamonds and gems. Price-anchored messaging such as "10 carats under $500" and "22 carats under $1k" (Motion summary).
- **Hero products and prices:**
  - Tennis necklaces and bracelets: $3,500–4,900 at the top end.
  - Median catalogue price: $438.
- **Size and channels:**
  - DTC plus NYC stores [unverified current count].
  - Raised **$24.95M over 6 rounds**, the latest a Series B of $7.4M on 2023-01-25 (CB Insights); a Series A of $13M in 2019 (Preqin).
  - Revenue is private; possibly **above** the $20M band [inf]. ⚑ Budget-band flag.
- **Where they advertise:** Meta; a 21-ad snapshot, ~4 new a week.
- **Current creative:**
  - **What works:** price-anchored offers.
  - **What's weak:** ~90 % stills (2 of the top 30 cards are video), the same offer-banner format (25 % off in September, 40 % off since 10/6) repeated across rings, bracelets and necklaces, no motion and no story.
  - The tennis line is a *sparkle* product, and stills can't show sparkle.

## 3. The person
- **Who decides:** the co-founders **Sophie Kahn** and **Bouchra Ezzahraoui (Darwazah)** (brand copy, Preqin, Forbes Middle East 2021). At their funding stage, a CMO or performance lead likely runs Meta [inf; not verified].
- **Their public voice:** thin in this run. Forbes ME (2021): Bouchra came from a long finance career, and AUrate pieces have been worn by Julia Roberts, Charlize Theron and others. Brand copy: they founded it out of frustration with the lack of affordably priced fine jewellery.
- **What they care about [inf]:** honest pricing, sustainable gold, accessible luxury, conversion on offers.
- **What they'd fear from an AI studio:**
  1. **Stone counts, prongs and settings rendered wrong.** A wrong number of stones on a "9 ct" necklace is a misrepresentation.
  2. AI models on a fine-jewellery brand (doc 48: generated people are the biggest backlash risk).
  3. **FTC Jewelry Guides:** "lab-grown" must be clearly qualified every time a diamond is shown.

## 4. Fit and red flags
- **Fit:**
  - A big new line with zero motion.
  - High AOV, so one converting film pays for itself fast.
  - A giant-product, hands-only format avoids AI people entirely.
- **Red flags:**
  - (a) Probably above the band and VC-backed, so a slower buying path through a marketing team.
  - (b) The founders' public voice wasn't verified in 2025–26.
  - (c) The only public contacts are generic (hello@, care@).
  - (d) Their creative is offer-led, so they may want discount films. Keep a swappable offer card.
  - (e) Every frame needs a "lab-grown" super. The stone count and setting come from a real-photo still (Kling i2v), never generated (LESSONS: dial text and stone counts from real photos).

## 5. The buying path
**The ONE film that would make them buy now: "Net Worth" (15 s, 9:16 + 4:5) for the 9 ct lab-grown tennis necklace.**
A giant product with a real hand for scale (the Jacquemus format, LESSONS). The punchline is *caused* by the product: the necklace is so brilliant that it becomes the net.

| t (s) | Shot (real rig) | Action / sound |
|---|---|---|
| 0.0–1.0 | Low tripod at baseline height on a Manhattan rooftop tennis court, dusk | **Frame 0 in motion plus sound-first:** a tennis ball is already mid-serve, with the pock of the racket. Super: "Lab-grown. 9 ct." |
| 1.0–3.0 | Side-on, locked-off at net height | Reveal: the net is a **giant AUrate tennis necklace** strung post to post. Stones and settings come from a Kling i2v of the real product photo; the stone count matches the real piece. |
| 3.0–5.0 | High-speed insert, locked-off | The ball clips the necklace. It **rings like a bell** (sound on the measured contact frame), and the ball rolls along the stones like a tightrope. |
| 5.0–7.0 | Same lock-off | The ball drops over onto the far side. The umpire, heard and **never seen**, says: "Let." |
| 7.0–10.0 | Macro, slider | **Proof slot:** real product footage or a real-photo i2v of the clasp and the stone line. Super: "Lab-grown diamonds · 14k recycled gold" (client-verified spec only). |
| 10.0–12.5 | Close-up, a real hand (licensed hand model or the client's footage) | The real-size necklace is lifted off a tennis-ball can lid. No face. |
| 12.5–15.0 | End card | **VO (2 lines, locked voice):** "Serious sparkle. Lab-grown honesty." AUrate logo and a swappable offer slot. |

- **Physics:**
  - The ball doesn't bounce off a chain like a racket would; it clips, rolls and drops.
  - The net posts are real hardware.
  - Wind is noted once (from the river side).
- **Camera:** every position is a real rig; no flying through the net.

**The proof they need:**
- 5 free frames: the giant necklace-net built from **their** product photo, with the stone count verified against the PDP.
- A "lab-grown" disclosure plan (FTC Jewelry Guides).
- A hands-only, no-AI-people promise.

**Re-buy path:** the holiday → Valentine's → Mother's Day → graduation and bridal jewellery calendar. New lab-grown drops; offer-card swaps per sale. Season plan once proven.

**Suggested tier:** Premiere $2,500.
