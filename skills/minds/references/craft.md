# Craft & Anti-AI Reference — write and build like a master human, never like a machine

Load this whenever you produce writing, copy, design, code you'll show a client, or a plan. Two jobs: (1) **the Iron Rule** — nothing you output may read as machine-written; (2) **master craft** — the specific techniques that separate expert work from competent-generic work. This is about being genuinely better, not about deceiving anyone or gaming a detector — the research is clear that only real quality (variance, specificity, stance) survives; cosmetic tricks get caught.

---

## PART 1 — THE IRON RULE: kill every AI tell

No single item is fatal; the giveaway is **density** (several tells in one paragraph) and **uniformity** (everything the same length and shape). Ctrl-F your draft against this and cut.

### Banned words (replace with plain ones)
delve, leverage, utilize, harness, streamline, underscore, foster, cultivate, elevate, unlock, unleash, empower, showcase, spearhead, embark, garner, boast/boasts (meaning "has"), navigate (abstract), realm, landscape (abstract), tapestry, testament, synergy, paradigm, cornerstone, beacon, plethora, myriad, robust, seamless, vibrant, dynamic, intricate, meticulous, comprehensive, holistic, multifaceted, nuanced, pivotal, crucial, vital, paramount, invaluable, unparalleled, unprecedented, groundbreaking, cutting-edge, state-of-the-art, transformative, profound, bustling, tailored, bespoke, burgeoning, ubiquitous, unwavering. Transitions: moreover, furthermore, additionally, consequently, thus, hence, notably, importantly, whilst, nevertheless (as sentence-openers). Replace "serves as / stands as / represents / boasts / features" with plain **is/has**.

### Banned phrases & frames
"it's not just X, it's Y" · "not only X but also Y" · "in today's fast-paced world" · "in the ever-evolving landscape of" · "in the realm of" · "when it comes to" · "at its core" · "dive/delve into" · "let's explore" · "unlock the power/potential of" · "harness the power of" · "navigate the complexities of" · "plays a crucial/pivotal/vital role" · "a testament to" · "stand the test of time" · "pave the way for" · "push the boundaries" · "take it to the next level" · "the perfect blend of" · "more than just" · "whether you're X or Y" · "the world of X" · "it's worth noting that" · "it's important to note" · "that being said" · "on one hand… on the other" · "in conclusion / in summary / at the end of the day / ultimately" · "I hope this helps" · "great question" · ending with "Would you like me to…" or "Let me know if…".

### Banned typography & structure
- **Em dashes** used as default punctuation — prune them; use commas, periods, parentheses. (Contemporary human prose uses far fewer.)
- **Rule of three everywhere** — triple adjectives and triple examples in every claim. Use two, or four, or one.
- **Bold-everything** and **emoji as bullets** and **Title Case Headers** — stop.
- **Uniform rhythm** — every sentence 15–25 words. This is the #1 structural tell.
- **-ing significance tails** — "…underscoring its importance," "…highlighting its role," "…reflecting broader trends." Delete them.
- **Symmetric hedged conclusions** — every point given an equal-and-opposite counterpoint so nothing commits.
- **Closing on a rhetorical question or an offer to help.**

### Banned tone
Relentless positivity. Corporate-neutral / press-release voice. Over-explaining the obvious. Stacked qualifiers ("truly," "incredibly," "arguably," "in many ways"). No real, falsifiable opinion. "Various factors" / "numerous studies" with nothing named.

## PART 2 — WHAT TO DO INSTEAD (structural humanization)

Attack the cause, not the symptom. Detectors and readers both key on **uniform sentence architecture + generic claims + no stance** — word-swapping and fake typos don't fix that and get caught.

1. **Burstiness — vary length hard.** Never let three sentences in a row land in the same length band. Follow a long, clause-heavy sentence with a very short one that lands the point. Short sentences carry weight. (Gary Provost's rule: "I vary the sentence length, and I create music.")
2. **Specificity over abstraction — the single highest-leverage move.** "A recent study" → name it. "Many people" → the number. "A popular tool" → the brand. "A lot of nerve endings" → "72,000 nerve endings" (Sugarman). Specific = read as verified; vague = read as invented. Real numbers, names, dates, places.
3. **Take a stance; cut hedges.** State the position — "Hamlet is a coward," not "Hamlet can be read many ways." An imperfect committed argument reads more human than a flawless hedged one.
4. **Uneven effort.** Humans get tighter and more specific where they actually care, looser elsewhere. Uniform polish start-to-finish is itself a tell.
5. **One voice, not a brand.** Contractions, first/second person, idiom, a recurring turn of phrase. Draft like you're explaining it to one specific person, then tighten.
6. **Genuine imperfection lives at the argument level** — an aside that doesn't fully resolve, a caveat that's there because it's true — not at the typo level.

### Self-checks before sending
- **Sentence-length count**: five consecutive sentences all within ~10 words of each other → rewrite two to be much shorter/longer.
- **"So what?"** each paragraph until it lands on a concrete outcome, or cut it.
- **Specificity audit**: grep "many / several / various / recent / popular / significant" → replace each with a real thing.
- **Hedge audit**: grep "it's worth noting / it could be argued / in many ways" → delete or make a direct claim.
- **Stance test**: can you point to one sentence a reasonable person could disagree with? If not, it has no point of view.
- **Read aloud**: if a paragraph runs in one flat breath-pattern, break it.

## PART 3 — MASTER CRAFT BY DOMAIN

### Conversion copywriting (Schwartz, Sugarman, Hormozi, Wiebe, Ogilvy)
- **Diagnose before writing.** Schwartz's **5 Awareness stages** (Unaware → Problem → Solution → Product → Most Aware) — each needs a different opening; don't sell price to the Unaware or teach the problem to the Most Aware. **5 Sophistication levels** — in a claim-numb market you win on **mechanism**, then on **identification** (who it's for) or radical simplicity, not a louder claim.
- **Specificity is the whole game** (Sugarman): "92% of new dentists" beats "dentists everywhere." Odd exact numbers read as measured.
- **Slippery slide**: every sentence's only job is to get the next one read. Short opener, short paragraphs.
- **Hormozi Value = (Dream Outcome × Perceived Likelihood) / (Time Delay × Effort).** Amateurs inflate the top (bigger claims). Masters attack the bottom — engineer and narrate a fast, frictionless first win.
- **Voice of customer** (Wiebe): lift the exact phrases customers use (reviews, tickets, calls) into headlines. Test: does it sound like the customer talking, or the company talking about the customer.
- **"So what?"** every feature until it terminates in status/money/time/pain-removed. Ogilvy: state facts, not superlatives.

### Design that reads bespoke, not "AI slop" (Wathan/Schoger, Anthropic frontend cookbook)
- **Name the disease:** the generic AI look = Inter/Roboto font, purple/indigo gradient, three-icon cards in a row, timid low-opacity everything, rounded corners on all, centered symmetric hero. Avoid all of it explicitly.
- **Hierarchy through size/weight/placement before color.** Don't fear oversized elements — scale signals importance; timidity reads as default.
- **Extreme contrast, not safe pairing:** pair a 100/200 weight against an 800/900; jump sizes 3×+; pair a serif with a geometric sans or a display face with mono. Similarity reads as default; contrast reads as designed.
- **Reserve color** for the one thing that matters on a screen; avoid pure black; systematic spacing scale.
- **Asymmetric / editorial layout** over centered-symmetric SaaS grid — full-bleed image, off-grid type, intentional tension. Real content (voice-of-customer copy), never lorem or "Feature One / Feature Two."
- **Method:** reference 3–5 real designs and say *why* they work; give explicit negative constraints (fonts/clichés to avoid); ask for 3 distinct directions, not one (the first output is the boring statistical mean).

### Decision & strategy mastery (Tetlock, Munger, Klein, Duke, Kahneman)
- **Outside view / base rates** (Tetlock, Kahneman): "how often do things of this sort happen in situations of this sort?" Estimate from how similar cases actually resolved, not from your project's inside story (planning fallacy).
- **List the disconfirmer in advance** (Tetlock): for your position, name the specific signs that would flip you to the other side — before you look.
- **Inversion** (Munger): ask "what would guarantee failure here?" and avoid those, instead of only planning success.
- **Pre-mortem** (Klein): "It's a year later and this failed. Write the history of what went wrong." Surfaces ~30% more risks than a normal review, and dodges the social resistance a devil's-advocate hits.
- **Don't confuse decision quality with outcome** (Duke's "resulting"): a good decision can lose. Judge the process. Assign real probabilities to outcomes before acting; say "I'm not sure" more; when asking others to review a decision, don't reveal the outcome first.

### Prose voice (Orwell, Zinsser, Strunk & White)
- Orwell: never use a figure of speech you've seen in print; short word over long; **if you can cut a word, cut it**; active over passive; plain English over jargon; break any rule before writing something barbarous.
- Zinsser: "Clutter is the disease." Strip every qualifier, hedge, redundant pair, and throat-clearing preamble. Find the sentence where the piece **actually begins** and delete everything before it.
- Strunk & White: omit needless words; use definite, specific, concrete language ("it rained every day for a week," not "unfavorable weather"); positive form; put the emphatic word at the sentence's end.
- **Cross-domain primitive:** specificity-over-abstraction shows up independently in copywriting AND prose craft. It's one rule doing double duty — concrete detail reads as verified, abstraction reads as invented. Encode it once, apply everywhere.
