## 0. The ten things to know before pitching a beauty brand

1. **The winners scale short.** The 9 paid-scaled beauty ads I measured (≥ 7M views at a like-rate ≤ 0.5 %, i.e. spend behind them) run **4–25 s, median 11 s** [m]. Four of the nine are under 8 s. A 30 s cut is the exception (e.l.f. "Unglammy Valley", 30 s, 1.4 s average shot).
2. **Product or product texture is on screen at 0.0 s in 15 of 18** measured ads [m]. The exceptions are character comedies (e.l.f.), and even those land the product by 19 s.
3. **A text hook sits on screen from frame 1 in at least 10 of 18** [m]: a price ("glowy skin for less than $20"), a reframe ("dot cake but it's our vitamin C powder"), an instruction ("your reminder to put that lash and brow serum on EVERY. DAY."), or a newness line ("OUR FIRST EVER EYE CREAM IS HEREEE").
4. **Texture is the hero, and texture is what AI does best.** CeraVe's most-scaled ad (56.4M views) is pure product CG: bottle trio, a milky ribbon poured over the bottle, the bottle dragged through a pool of serum, one finger-on-cheek application, end on the trio [m]. Every shot except the 2 s cheek shot is reproducible with Kling i2v + Cinema Studio today.
5. **Food-framing of a product is the strongest organic mechanic in the corpus.** The Ordinary "dot cake" (vitamin C powder jar dressed as sprinkles, 7 s, 16.3M) and Starface "where my clear star came from" (a giant hydrocolloid star set in a cake tin and cut with a knife, 22 s, 6.4M) [m]. Glow Recipe cuts its hydrogel puck in half with a knife on a plate to prove "not dripping with serum" (17 s, 1.6M, 4.3 % likes, 140× the account median) [m].
6. **Comedy works when the product causes the punchline and the joke is a category truth.** CeraVe's 5 s "Jenga" (22.9M): a tower of moisturizer boxes; a hand pulls out the AM and the PM box and the tower collapses; caption "the pieces holding your routine together" [m]. That is the VXO formula in 5 seconds.
7. **Beauty clicks, but post-click it underperforms.** Health & Beauty has the best CTR of 14 categories (2.36 %) and the 3rd-best hook rate (28 %), but ROAS of 1.82 vs 2.17 cross-industry, 11th of 14 (Billo, 88,329 Meta video ads, H1 2026) [v]. A beauty buyer will judge a film on CPA and ROAS, not views.
8. **No AI face may ever "prove" a result.** 52 % of women are "not OK" with AI-generated models (Attest 2025 via eMarketer) [v]. Guess's AI models in Vogue (Aug 2025) drew boycott calls. Dove has pledged never to use AI to represent real women (Apr 2024). FTC mock-up doctrine and NAD (SolaWave, Jan 2025: before/after photos dropped) make a fake demo a legal risk. **VXO beauty films show product, texture, hands, objects and metaphor. People appear only as comedy characters, never as a skin result.**
9. **Every film needs a "proof slot" the client fills with real footage.** The AI film carries attention; a 1.5–3 s insert of the client's own real swatch, real wear test or real clinical stat carries belief. This hybrid answers the buyer's biggest fear (§5) and the compliance problem (§4) at once [inf].
10. **Policy words to fear:** "treats/cures/heals acne", "repairs", "regrows", "removes wrinkles", "waterproof", "sunblock", "clinically proven" without a study, "miracle", and anything that says the viewer's face is inferior. SPF is an OTC drug in the US, with its own label rules (§4).

### 1.1 Cross-ad patterns (what to copy)

| Pattern | Evidence [m] | How VXO uses it |
|---|---|---|
| **Texture full-frame in second 1** | rhode smear, Ordinary eye cream, CeraVe ribbon, Glow Recipe jar | Open on a macro of the product's own material with motion already happening (a ribbon falling, a spatula wiping) |
| **One super, one idea, on screen from 0.0 s** | ≥10/18 ads | Native text super at 0.0–0.5 s; claim, price, reframe or newness (45 Q3) |
| **Food or kitchen framing** | dot cake, star-in-a-cake-tin, hydrogel cut on a plate, factory watermelons | The "dessert counter" family of concepts (§6, C2) |
| **Cross-section or knife proof** | Glow Recipe, Starface | A knife through the product (balm, stick, hydrogel) is a physical truth AI renders well when the cut face is written (§3) |
| **Category truth as the gag** | CeraVe Jenga, e.l.f. Unglammy Valley | The product causes the punchline (44 §8.5) |
| **Swatch parade with tiny shade names** | rhode (14 shades/12 s), e.l.f. rainbow (9 shades) | Shade walls are the easiest high-retention format; colour accuracy is the risk (§3) |
| **ASMR quiet track** | CeraVe ASMR at −55 dB RMS | Keep native foley; no music bed; normalise to −14 LUFS only at the end (46 §2.4) |
| **Short pacing** | ASL 1.0–2.1 s on every multi-shot paid winner | 15 s hero = 7–9 shots; a 5–7 s one-shot loop variant |
| **Diegetic camera** | Bigfoot found-footage, ring-light selfie | Use for the comedy concepts; it excuses AI artefacts |

**What this means for VXO [inf]:**
1. **Formats that sell in beauty:** texture macro + claim super (CeraVe), swatch parade (rhode, e.l.f.), objection-handling with a physical proof (Glow Recipe), food reframing (Ordinary, Starface), category-pain comedy (e.l.f., CeraVe Jenga), and creator demos (everyone). VXO can make the first five. The sixth (a creator on camera) stays with the brand's creators; we supply the cinematic "hero + hook variants" that sit beside them.
2. **Claims that sell and survive review:** ingredient + % ("3 Essential Ceramides"), sensory ("invisible finish", "melts to an oil"), usage ("AM + PM", "reapply every 2 hours"), process ("set hydrogel, not soaked"), price ("under $20"), newness. Efficacy numbers ("stronger barrier in 1 hour") only when the client owns the study and footnotes it.
3. **Proof:** a real swatch on real skin, a cross-section, a real lab or filling-line clip, a footnoted consumer-perception stat. AI supplies the world around the proof, never the proof.
4. **Length:** a 15 s hero, a 5–7 s one-shot loop (Jenga/dot-cake class), and a 6 s swatch or texture bumper. Make 30 s only for character comedy.
5. **Placements:** TikTok In-Feed + Spark (native supers, trending-safe audio), Meta Reels (supers burned in, hero at 15 s), TikTok Shop video (must show the product in hand and price), PDP silent loop (the texture shot).
6. **Ship variants, not one film.** A buyer refreshing every 2–3 weeks needs 3 hooks × 2 lengths per film (45 Q2 "Hero + DR stack"). Price the Season tier ($3,500/mo) around that cadence.

## 3. AI realism pitfalls in beauty and the fixes

Routing follows 43 §1: **Kling 3.0 Pro i2v** for single-move label and texture inserts (holds text from the input image), **Cinema Studio 4.0** for the one-take hero, **Seedance 2.5 r2v** for multi-shot comedy, **Wan 3.0 480p** for blocking previs. Stills come first (11 §Recipe); every motion shot starts from an approved still.

| Pitfall | What goes wrong | Fix (prompt / reference / post) |
|---|---|---|
| **Skin** | Waxy, poreless, "AI glow"; front light flattens it | Avoid faces as results. When skin appears (hands, a cheek dot): side or raking key, "visible pores, vellus hair, slight redness at knuckles, natural oil sheen on high points, no smoothing, no beauty retouch" ([Claid](https://claid.ai/blog/article/fix-ai-skin-texture), [Morphic](https://morphic.com/resources/how-to/fix-ai-generated-skin-realistic)). Use a real-photo hand ref. Grain in post (43 §5 "plastic skin") |
| **Cream, balm, serum** | CG frosting; uniform peaks; serum behaves like water or like jelly | **Write viscosity as behaviour with numbers**: "the ribbon holds for about 1 s, then folds over itself in 3 coils", "the drop hangs 0.5 s from the pipette tip, stretches, releases", "a few trapped air bubbles, matte edges where it thins". Name a real reference ("like cold Greek yoghurt", "like warm honey") |
| **Liquids on glass** | Liquid passes through the bottle; no meniscus; level never changes | Script the object's fate (43 rule 12): "the level in the bottle drops by 2 mm after the draw". Keep the bottle rigid: "rigid glass, never deforms" |
| **Frosted / clear glass** | Refraction wrong, label floats, the cap merges with the glass | Generate the packshot still with the product ref first; Kling i2v with `last_image_url` = the same packshot to land the end frame; "one vertical specular moves 2 cm, nothing else changes" |
| **Labels, INCI, fine print** | Text redrawn, softened, misspelled | Text comes **only** from the reference image. Close, stable framing. "The label text '[EXACT]' is the only readable text; all other text unreadable." Kling over Seedance for label inserts [B-ADH]. Claims and footnotes go on as supers in post, never generated |
| **Lipstick / tint shade** | Shade drifts between shots; skin tones homogenise | Give the hex and Pantone in the prompt; generate each shade swatch as its own still; **check ΔE ≤ 3 against the client's real swatch photo on the hero frame** (11 makeup edit rule); grade-protect the swatch. Generate each skin tone separately |
| **Swatch strokes** | The applicator stroke morphs; the line paints itself | Show the **press and the lift**, not the stroke; or cut on the stroke (start still → end still, Kling first/last frame). Spatula wipes (rhode) are simpler than doe-foot strokes |
| **Powder, pressed pans** | Powder pans regrow after a swipe; clouds look like smoke | "The swipe leaves a clean groove that stays"; powder cloud "fine, settles within 1 s, falls downward" |
| **Pumps, droppers, twist-ups** | Mechanisms morph, fingers merge with the pump | Press off-frame or one finger on the pump with named contact ("index finger pad on the actuator, thumb behind the neck"); "exactly five fingers per hand" (43 §5) |
| **Nails** | Nail shape and colour change across cuts | Put the hand in the character sheet with the nail colour named; one hand ref per hand model |
| **Hair** | Fused strands, helmet hair, strands clip through fingers | Seedance 720p for swing; backlit silhouettes; 1–2 comb strokes; mannequin heads or back-of-head only (11 haircare) |
| **Mist / spray (setting spray, SPF mist)** | Mist looks like smoke or a CG cone | "Fine aerosol cone, 25 cm long, backlit, droplets visible at the edge, dissipates within 1 s" |
| **SPF white cast** | Models invent or erase the white cast randomly | Never show cast (or its absence) on AI skin; make "invisible" a metaphor (§6, C4) or a real insert |
| **Foam and lather** | Bubbles too uniform | "Irregular bubble sizes, some popping, foam collapses slowly" |
| **Reflections** | Mirrors show the wrong room; the crew is missing | Avoid mirrors in AI shots, or write "the mirror shows only the tiled wall behind" |
| **Food stand-ins (C2)** | Product reads as real food (confusion risk) | Label always visible in the frame; a closing super "not food" (also a real safety point) |
| **Miniatures (C8)** | Scale flicker; the tiny figure grows | Write the scale ("figure 3 cm tall next to a 9 cm bottle"); tilt-shift macro; one figure per shot |

**Style header delta for this doc (add to 11's skincare/makeup headers):** "Product-film still, 100 mm macro, [hard window light through blinds / raking side key], one colour world matched to the product, real surfaces (white tile, travertine, mint paper), label text '[EXACT]' as the only readable text, viscous texture with trapped air bubbles and matte thin edges, no CG sheen, no beauty retouch, no faces unless written, exactly five fingers, natural nails [colour]."

## 4. Policy and legal (US first; mark the client's own counsel as the final word)

### 4.1 Cosmetic vs drug: the line every script must stay behind
- **FDA (FD&C Act):** a product that claims to treat or prevent disease or affect the body's structure or function is a drug. Acne treatment, dandruff, hair regrowth, eyelash growth, "removes wrinkles", "repairs barrier damage" (as a treatment) are drug claims ([FDA](https://www.fda.gov/cosmetics/laws-regulations/cosmetics-us-law); 11). Safe verbs: "improves the look of", "visibly", "appearance of", "feels", "hydrates", "smooths the look of".
- **Watch the implied claim.** The Ordinary avoids the lash-growth claim by framing its lash serum as a daily reminder ("trust the process") [m]. NAD told NuOrganic to discontinue "naturally grown long lashes" TikTok and Instagram claims ([CosmeticsDesign](https://www.cosmeticsdesign.com/Article/2025/01/21/nad-decisions-roundup-recent-advertising-claims-challenged-in-beauty-and-personal-care-industry)).
- **MoCRA (2022):** brands must hold safety substantiation and keep adverse-event records (6 years; 3 for small businesses); serious events reported in 15 days ([CosmeticsDesign/Mintz](https://www.cosmeticsdesign.com/Article/2023/01/11/Modernization-of-Cosmetics-Regulation-Act-MoCRA-changes-for-beauty-industry-full-analysis-from-Mintz)). It does not regulate ad copy directly, but plaintiffs can discover the files [inf].

### 4.2 Sunscreen (SPF) is an OTC drug
From the FDA labeling rule (2011 final rule, as summarised by [AAD](https://www.aad.org/sun-protection/understand-sunscreen-labels) and [NBC](https://www.nbcnews.com/news/amp/wbna55366320)):
- **Never say** "waterproof", "sweatproof" or "sunblock".
- **"Water resistant (40 minutes)" or "(80 minutes)"** only, and only if tested.
- **"Broad Spectrum"** only if the product passed the broad-spectrum test. Only Broad Spectrum **SPF 15+** may claim to reduce the risk of skin cancer and early skin aging, "if used as directed with other sun protection measures".
- No "all-day protection" or "protects for more than 2 hours" and no "instant protection" without FDA approval. **"Reapply at least every 2 hours"** is the direction; make it a feature (§6, C4).
- A VXO SPF film never shows sun "bouncing off" skin, never shows a burn vs no-burn comparison, and never puts an SPF number on screen that the client hasn't given us from its Drug Facts.

### 4.3 FTC and NAD
- **Substantiation:** objective claims ("hydrates for 72 hours", "clinically proven") need competent and reliable scientific evidence before the ad runs. NAD's 2025 SolaWave decision rejected an in-home use test with no washout period and **recommended dropping before/after photos** that implied measurable improvement ([Kilpatrick Townsend alert](https://ktslaw.com/en/insights/alert/2025/2/{AE57C1E6-9FB2-416E-B236-D7A76ADE1320}?pdf=1); [NAD Digest 2025 Cosmetics](https://bbbprograms.org/getmedia/bcd5d6ca-7f1d-4f6c-854e-07da60a0ea7c/NADDigest_2025_Cosmetics.pdf)).
- **Demonstrations and mock-ups:** a staged demo shown as real proof is deceptive (FTC v. Colgate-Palmolive, 1965, the "sandpaper shave" case). **An AI shot that shows the product working is a mock-up.** Rule: AI only for (a) obvious metaphor or exaggeration no viewer reads literally, or (b) depiction with a "Dramatization" super, and the claim itself proven by the client's real insert [inf].
- **Fake reviews rule (Oct 2024):** no AI "customer" praising the product; no invented review cards (45 §5.3).
- **Endorsement guides:** creator spark-ads need #ad/paid-partnership disclosure; NuOrganic was also flagged on influencer disclosure.

### 4.4 Platform rules
- **Meta** ([Health & Wellness ad standard](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/restricted-goods-services/health-wellness)): no "statements of inferiority about physical appearance" (no "Tired of your dull skin?" said to the viewer; use "for skin that feels tight by noon"). Before/after of cosmetic products is allowed when targeting adults; non-permanent cosmetics (creams, makeup, hair products) are **exempt from the 18+ requirement**; procedures need 18+; no skin-whitening/bleaching products that permanently change skin colour.
- **TikTok** ([Healthcare & Pharmaceuticals policy](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/tiktok-ads-policy-healthcare-pharmaceuticals?lang=en)): "Do not make medical claims"; no "exaggerated performance promises"; no "claims to remove or cure acne, remove wrinkles, or treat hair loss"; no "miracle" or "holds the secret" claims; no claims of results similar to prohibited cosmetic or surgical treatments ("Botox in a bottle" is out). Many markets restrict aesthetic-procedure ads to 18+.
- **Teens [inf]:** Starface, Bubble and similar brands sell to teens. Don't build anti-aging messages into a film likely to reach under-18s, and keep teen-coded characters out of anti-aging concepts.
- **AI disclosure:** Meta "AI info" label for photoreal AI humans; TikTok AIGC toggle (reported mandatory for ads from 21 Jul 2026); **New York synthetic-performer disclosure (in force 9 Jun 2026)** for any AI human in an ad shown in NY (45 §5.3). VXO default: "Features AI-generated imagery" end-card line on any film with an AI person; hands-only shots are likely outside the NY rule [inf].
- **Retailer brand standards [inf]:** brands sold at Sephora, Ulta or Target often have to clear retailer co-op creative; the buyer will ask whether AI footage is allowed there. Ask early.

### 4.5 Pre-flight checklist for a beauty film (add to 45 §5.4 and 46 §7)
1. Every claim on screen is on the client's approved claims list, with the footnote text supplied by the client.
2. No AI face or skin shows a result (texture change, wrinkle, pore, acne, lash length, hair growth, SPF cast).
3. Shades: ΔE ≤ 3 vs the client's real swatch on the hero frame.
4. Label: legible, correct, unmirrored in every product shot (frame strip at 2 fps).
5. Words banned: treat, cure, heal, repair (as treatment), regrow, remove wrinkles, waterproof, sweatproof, sunblock, miracle, "like Botox", "clinically proven" (unless the client's study is cited).
6. No negative-appearance address to the viewer.
7. "Dramatization" super on any depicted effect; AI disclosure line when an AI person appears.
8. Proof slot filled with the client's real footage, or the film runs as a brand/awareness asset only.
