# 38 — Hooks, Retention and the Path to Purchase

Research brief, 2026-10-08. Scope: the first 0–3 s, holding attention through a 30 s **single continuous take** (no cuts), and turning attention into a purchase. Sister docs: 35 (punchlines), 36 (emotion/virality), 37 (AI action execution). Read with 00-master-playbook ("one big-idea shot is the hook in 0–3 s") and 15 (concept engine and pretesting).

Rules used here: every number has a source URL; if a figure is contested, that is stated. `[inf]` = our inference, not a published finding. Benchmarks from vendor blogs are marked as directional.

---

## 0. TL;DR — the ten things that matter

1. **Recall starts in under a second.** Facebook-cited Fors Marsh research: people recall mobile feed content at a statistically significant rate after **0.25 s**. A Facebook/Nielsen analysis of 173 Brand Effect studies found lift in ad recall (+47%), awareness (+32%) and purchase intent (+44%) among people who watched **≤3 s**. Frame 1 is the ad.
2. **Brand + message early, then again.** Meta x Toluna Reels study (100 ads x 100 viewers, 2024): brand and main message in the first 5 s → **1.7x** more likely to be top-20% for purchase intent. Brand shown more than once → **1.8x**.
3. **For direct response, show the product with a reason.** Same study: product shown more than once → **2.7x**. Product plus context (USPs, features, details) → **5.3x**. Brand presence for **≤25% of the duration** → **4.8x**. A CTA, visual and/or spoken → **1.9x**.
4. **The hook is visual + audio together.** Meta x Toluna: hooks that use visual *and* audio cues → **1.5x** purchase intent. Meta says **80%+ of Reels are played with sound on**. Speech + music → **2.0x** brand interest.
5. **The four parts of a hook must say the same thing.** Kallaway: a hook = spoken + visual + text + audio, and performance comes from "max alignment" between them. Viewers go **visual → audio → back to the visual** to check, so the **key visual** comes first.
6. **Motion *onset* grabs the eye. Constant motion does not.** Abrams & Christ (2003): objects that have just started moving capture attention, while steady motion does not. Looming motion (something coming toward the camera) also captures attention (Franconeri & Simons 2003). In a one-take, every re-hook should be a *new* motion start, not just more motion.
7. **A face looking at the product sends the viewer's eyes to the product.** In eye-tracking, an averted gaze toward the product got more attention on the product and better brand memory than a direct gaze. Purchase intent was also higher (Palcu et al., 2017). Otto should look at the product, not the lens, except for deliberate 4th-wall beats.
8. **Hook-rate benchmarks** (3 s views ÷ impressions). Motion's glossary calls **20–40%** solid. A 2026 cross-industry Meta average was **~25%**, and Motion-relayed 2026 medians were ~28% on Meta and ~33% on TikTok. All are directional. Your own account is the baseline.
9. **Google ABCD** (Attention, Branding, Connection, Direction): ads built on it were associated with **+30% short-term sales likelihood** and **+17% long-term brand contribution** (Google/Kantar). It is a correlation model, not a guarantee.
10. **Purchase happens on the page.** The median ecommerce landing page converts at **4.2%** (Unbounce 2024). The average Shopify store converts at **~1.4%**, and the top 20% at **>3.2%** (Littledata, via secondary sources). The film's last frame must match the first screen of the landing page (same product, same colour, same offer).

### 1.3 Attention science

- **Motion onset** captures attention. Motion as such does not (Abrams & Christ 2003, *Psychological Science*): https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12930472/. **Looming/translating** stimuli also capture attention, and receding ones are disputed (Franconeri & Simons 2003): https://www.kellogg.northwestern.edu/faculty/research/detail/2003/moving-and-looming-stimuli-capture-attention.
  → *Implication for a one-take `[inf]`:* the camera move alone will not hold the eye, because the brain gets used to it. Each beat needs a new start of motion: a drop falls, a flame catches, a cap twists, Otto's hand enters. Things moving **toward** the lens beat things moving away.
- **Faces and gaze cueing** (eye-tracking, Palcu, Sudkamp & Florack 2017, *Frontiers in Psychology*): https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2017.00881/full. Animation captures attention. A face gazing **toward the product** increases attention to the product and memory of the brand and message, compared with a face gazing at the viewer. It also raised purchase intent. Earlier print studies found the same. One cosmetics-print thesis did not replicate it, so treat it as likely, not certain.
- **Kallaway's comprehension sandwich** (video "the 6 hook formats", https://youtu.be/xnOe8aA9Pmw, transcript reviewed 2026-10-08): "our eyes process 10 to 100 times more information per second than our ears". The viewer sees first, then hears, then looks back at the visual for confirmation. Misalignment between the four hook layers causes "comprehension loss". He says you have "5 seconds to make it stick". Hooks work by building a **curiosity loop through contrast**: the viewer believes A, and you show B.
- **MrBeast production memo** (leaked 2024, authenticity confirmed by two ex-producers to Passionfruit; summaries: https://www.creatorhandbook.net/leaked-document-allegedly-reveals-mrbeasts-secrets-to-youtube-success-the-key-takeaways/, https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20240917-mrbeast-youtube-production). The opening is where most viewers leave, so "frontload" the best moments. The thumbnail/title promise must be delivered (for us: the frame-1 promise must be paid off). He uses **re-engagement** beats at fixed points in long videos. The tracked metrics are CTR, AVD and AVP. *Scaled to 30 s `[inf]`:* front-load the money image in second 0–1 and re-engage every 3–5 s.
- **Dara Denney / creative strategists**: film **3–5 hooks per ad**, because the hook is the biggest variable (secondary summary: https://hawky.ai/blog/how-to-create-effective-ugc-ads). Brian Moncada: we found no published hook taxonomy, only YouTube-ads interviews (https://hustleandflowchart.libsyn.com/brian-moncada-a-complete-framework-for-successful-youtube-ads). Do not attribute a framework to him.

### 1.4 What this means for frame 1 of a VXO film `[inf]`

- **Frame 0 is a poster.** It must make sense as a still, because the 0.25 s recall finding and autoplay-before-sound both point that way. No black frames, no fade-ins, and no logo cards. Start on full-contrast, high-saliency image.
- **Something starts moving by 0.3–0.5 s** (motion onset). The camera can already be moving, but an *object* event is what wins attention.
- **Product identifiable by 2 s and brand/message by 5 s** (Meta x Toluna). The brand can be the label on the product; it does not need to be a logo card.
- **The sound layer hooks too:** a crisp diegetic transient (match strike, can crack, glass clink, spritz hiss) within the first 0.5 s. It works for the 80% who listen with sound and costs nothing for the silent viewers.
- **The text hook is on screen at 0.0–0.5 s**, ≤7 words, inside the safe zone, and it says the same thing as the visual.

## 3. Retention mechanics through 30 s

### 3.1 The loop structure
- **Open loop at 0–3 s, payoff withheld to 22–27 s.** Frame 1 asks a visual question ("why is the bottle dry on the sea?"). The answer (the reveal or the transformation) arrives late, followed by the packshot/CTA. This fits the MrBeast rule that the promise must be paid off.
- **Nested loops.** Open a smaller loop before the current one closes (for example, the flame catches at 9 s, but the wax still hasn't reached the mould), so there is never a moment when everything is resolved before the end `[inf]`.
- **Re-hook every 3–5 s.** This applies MrBeast's "re-engagement" at 30 s scale, plus TikTok's "Hold attention with stimulation". In a one-take, a re-hook is one of:
  1. a **new motion start** (object event), which is the strongest per Abrams & Christ;
  2. a **camera-speed change** (ease-in → whip → slow), so the speed changes without a cut;
  3. a **brightness/colour/contrast change** (light sweep, sun flare, colour flood, lights-out);
  4. a **scale/focus change** (macro → wide reveal, rack focus);
  5. a **sound event** (transient, music drop, VO line);
  6. a **new text line** (one at a time, ≤7 words);
  7. a **character beat** (Otto's glance, gesture or double-take).
- **Avoid flat stretches longer than 4 s `[inf]`.** A slow orbit with nothing happening is where viewers leave. Our notebook already showed that a constant orbit with no event reads as "good, not enough" (00-master-playbook).

### 3.2 Camera speed in a one-take `[inf]`
- 0–3 s: the camera is **already moving** at frame 0 (never start from a standstill) or an object event happens on frame 0.
- Vary speed in the pattern **fast → slow → fast → hold**. Steady speed causes habituation.
- Keep the **packshot hold ≥2 s and still** (end), so it can be read and screenshotted, and so it loops.
- Push-ins (looming) suit hooks. Pull-backs suit reveals and scale. Orbits suit material/beauty. Choose one move family per film and change *speed*, not direction, to keep AI generation stable (see 37).

### 3.3 Where the eye goes (saliency hierarchy for directing)
Approximate priority in a vertical frame, combining the research above with standard saliency findings `[inf]`:
1. **Faces and eyes**, and then wherever those eyes look (gaze cueing).
2. **Motion onset / looming** objects.
3. **Hands** acting on objects (a reach, pour, twist or spritz).
4. **Highest local contrast / brightest point** (flame, specular glint, gold label).
5. **Text**, especially new text appearing.
6. Saturated colour against a muted background.
→ *Rule:* at any moment, those cues should **point to one place**. Otto looks at the can, his hand reaches for the can, the can carries the glint, and the text sits beside the can. Competing saliency splits attention.

### 3.4 Loopable endings (rewatch = watch time)
- The last frame's composition, light and colour should match frame 0 so the autoplay loop is invisible `[inf]`. Rewatches count toward watch time, which Mosseri names as a ranking signal along with likes and sends (via https://www.tubefilter.com/2025/01/23/instagram-trial-reels-growth-hacking-creator-economy/).
- For ads, the CTA/packshot has to come before the loop point. The loop is a bonus for organic Otto & Vee reels.

## 4. From attention to purchase

### 4.1 Persuasion stack (in the order the viewer needs it)
1. **Clear product identification** by ~2 s, with brand/message by 5 s (Meta x Toluna 1.7x).
2. **Product repeated** (2+ appearances, 2.7x), **with context/USP** (5.3x): what it is, what it smells or tastes like, what it's made of, the size.
3. **Sensory benefit made visible.** In the scroll, the viewer can't smell, taste or touch the product, so translate the sense into image + sound: scent becomes a visible atmosphere; fizz becomes macro bubbles plus a crack; the creamy texture of skincare becomes a slow smear; for jewelry, a glint plus a chime `[inf]`.
4. **Desire and identity.** A slice-of-life scene with people gives 1.5x (brand). Show the *moment of use*, not the product in a void.
5. **Honest social-proof cues.** Use only real items: a real review count, real press, "restocked" only if true. FTC endorsement rules apply to US ads (https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/advertising-marketing/endorsements-influencers-reviews). Never fabricate reviews or UGC.
6. **Offer.** Price or a starter offer *in text*, for DR cuts.
7. **Urgency** only when it is real (a limited batch or a seasonal drop). False scarcity breaks trust and FTC rules.
8. **CTA, visual + audio** (1.9x). Use short imperative wording matched to the landing page: "Shop SOL", "Get the 4-pack", "Find your scent", "Try the trio". Avoid vague "Learn more" on DR `[inf]`.
9. **Message match / landing-page continuity.** The landing page's first screen should repeat the film's last frame (same product angle, colour, headline and offer). Unbounce: product pages often "don't match your ads' exact premise", and it recommends pre-cart landing pages for on-point messaging (https://unbounce.com/industry/ecommerce/). There is no published lift number, so treat it as a best practice.

### 4.2 How much product, and when to show the logo and price
- **Product on screen ≥60% of the 30 s, the logo/brand card ≤25%** `[inf]`. This combines the 2.7x/5.3x product findings with the ≤25% brand-presence finding (4.8x). The product label *is* branding, so the logo card can stay short.
- **Logo timing:** on the product at 0–5 s (label legible), again mid-film (dynamic branding, 1.8x), and as a lock-up at 26–30 s.
- **Price timing:** on DR variants, a price or offer line at ~20–24 s (after desire, before the CTA). On brand variants, no price, and the CTA points to the page `[inf]`.
- **CTA timing:** a soft verbal or text CTA at ~22–24 s and a hard CTA card at 27–30 s. The earlier CTA reaches viewers who drop off before the end (as the vendor guides note, only finishers see an end-only CTA).

### 5(b) 30-second retention timeline template (one continuous take)

| Sec | Beat | Job | Visual / motion | Sound | Text (safe box) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.0 | **Poster frame** | Stop the thumb | Highest-saliency frame; product or key visual already on screen; camera already moving | Transient at 0.1–0.3 (crack, strike, hiss) | Hook line, ≤7 words |
| 0.3–1.0 | **Motion onset** | Win attention | An object event starts (pour, flame, drop, Otto's gesture) | Music bed enters under the foley | — |
| 1–3 | **Open loop** | Create the question | Impossible / contrast element is fully visible | VO line 1 or a sound cue | Text holds |
| 3–5 | **Brand + message** | Meta 5 s rule | Label legible; one-line promise | VO: promise | Swap to promise text (or none) |
| 5–8 | **Re-hook 1** | New motion start | Speed change + second object event | Hit / riser | — |
| 8–12 | **Sensory proof** | Desire | Macro texture: bubbles, wax, mist, glint | ASMR foley up | USP 1 (≤5 words) |
| 12–15 | **Re-hook 2** | Light/colour shift | Light sweep or colour flood; 2nd product appearance | Music lift | — |
| 15–19 | **Context / use** | Product + context (5.3x) | Moment of use with hand or person; gaze to product | VO: benefit | USP 2 |
| 19–22 | **Re-hook 3 / escalation** | Peak spectacle | Biggest visual event; loop nearly resolved | Build | — |
| 22–25 | **Payoff + soft CTA** | Close the loop | Reveal answers frame-1 question; 3rd product appearance | VO: "Get yours at …" | Offer/price (DR cut) |
| 25–28 | **Packshot** | Purchase memory | Camera settles; product hero, still, legible | Sonic logo | Brand + CTA verb |
| 28–30 | **Hold / loop** | Screenshot + rewatch | Static hold that matches frame 0 composition | Tail out to silence or loop | CTA stays |

Ratios to check: product visible ≥18 of 30 s; logo lock-up ≤25% of runtime (≤7.5 s); ≥5 re-hook events; no gap without a new event longer than 4 s `[inf]`.

### 5(c) Pre-production hook/retention checklist (score 0 = no, 1 = partly, 2 = yes)

| # | Item | 0–2 |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Frame 0 works as a still poster (no black or fade, high contrast, product or key visual present) | |
| 2 | An object motion starts by 0.5 s (not only a camera move) | |
| 3 | The hook is **one** idea: visual, text, VO and sound all say the same thing (Kallaway alignment) | |
| 4 | It works muted (text carries it) **and** rewards sound (transient within 0.5 s) | |
| 5 | Product identifiable by 2 s; brand + main message by 5 s | |
| 6 | Frame 1 opens a question that is answered only at 20–27 s | |
| 7 | ≥5 re-hook events, and no flat stretch over 4 s | |
| 8 | Camera speed varies (not constant); the move stays within one move family (generation stability) | |
| 9 | Saliency points to one place each beat (gaze, hand, glint and text converge on the product) | |
| 10 | Product on screen ≥60%, shown 3+ times, with at least 2 concrete USPs/context | |
| 11 | Sensory benefit translated into visible + audible cues (bubbles, mist, wax, glint) | |
| 12 | Logo/brand card ≤25% of runtime and never in the bottom third | |
| 13 | All text and the hero product are inside the safe box (y 270–1248, x 65–940) | |
| 14 | CTA appears twice (soft ~22 s, hard 27–30 s), verb-first, and matches the landing page wording | |
| 15 | Last frame matches the landing page's first screen (product angle, colour, offer) and loops to frame 0 | |

**Pass threshold:** **≥26/32**, and items **1, 3, 5, 10, 14 and 16 must each score 2**. A film scoring 20–25 gets one revision round on the weakest items. Below 20, re-concept the hook before spending on generation `[inf]`.

### 5(d) A/B test plan: test 3–5 hooks cheaply before full films

**Principle:** the hook is the biggest variable (Denney: film 3–5 hooks per ad). Test **only the first ~3–5 s**, with the body held constant.

**How to do it in a one-take (no cut allowed) `[inf]`:**
1. Generate the **body** (5–30 s) once.
2. Extract its **first frame** (the "handoff frame").
3. Generate 3–5 alternative **openers (3–5 s)** using end-frame control, each *ending exactly on the handoff frame*. The join is continuous camera motion, so the result still plays as one take. Match the motion direction and speed at the seam (see 37 for execution).
4. Cost ≈ 3–5 short clips instead of 3–5 full films.

**Cheapest pre-test (no media spend):**
- **Hook-only stills test:** post the 3–5 frame-0 posters + text as a poll in Stories, or ask 10 target buyers "which would you stop for?" This is a sanity filter only.
- **Instagram Trial Reels** (launched Dec 2024; shown to non-followers first, so the main feed isn't spammed): https://www.tubefilter.com/2025/01/23/instagram-trial-reels-growth-hacking-creator-economy/. Post each Otto & Vee hook variant as a trial reel and compare 3 s retention / average watch time in Insights after 48–72 h `[inf]`.

**Paid test (Meta), per spec film:**
- Setup: one campaign, objective Traffic or Sales; **one ad set**, broad US targeting, Advantage+ placements limited to Reels/Stories (or a manual Reels placement); 3–5 ads that are identical except for the opener; same copy, same CTA, same landing page. Alternatively, use Meta's built-in A/B test tool so each variant gets a fair share of delivery.
- Budget `[inf]`: about **$20–40 per variant per day for 3–4 days**, targeting **≥3,000–5,000 impressions per variant** before any decision. Hook rate stabilises at a few thousand impressions; CTR needs more.
- Primary metric: **hook rate** (3 s plays ÷ impressions). Secondary metrics: **hold** (ThruPlays ÷ 3 s plays), outbound CTR, and cost per landing-page view. Decide on purchase only if volume allows (it usually won't at this budget).
- Decision rule `[inf]`: the winner beats the median variant by **≥20% relative hook rate** *and* does not lose on hold or CTR (a "clickbait" hook that spikes hook rate but kills hold is a loss). Run a two-proportion z-test on 3 s plays/impressions and require p < 0.1 at this budget, since this is directional.
- Then: build the full film around the winning hook. Re-test the top 2 with the soft-CTA variants. Keep a **hook log** (hook type, text, score on the 5(c) checklist, hook %, hold %, CTR) so the bank learns which types win per category.
- For client pitches: present the hook-rate result *as our own test, with sample size*, never as a guarantee.
