# 32 · Studio positioning, offer, name, voice and 30-day go-to-market

Research brief, 2026-10-08. It turns the studio's facts into a market position and a sellable offer. The studio is one faceless founder in Boca Raton, FL. It makes premium 15–25 s AI product films with no talking heads, for US founder-led DTC brands in fragrance, beauty, drinks, candles/home, jewelry and gourmet food. It has 50 researched prospects, **no paid clients**, and three spec films for invented brands (AURUM, VESPER, SOL).

This file builds on `28` (cold email, objections, proposal, contract, ethics), `25` (studio mind), `23` (what sells) and `14`/`20` §1 (Rourke Heath's pricing and sales). It does not repeat them.

**Evidence.** Books are cited from summaries and author interviews; the books themselves were not read. Three YouTube transcripts were fetched with `yt-dlp` at 45–120 s spacing: Dunford on [Lenny's Podcast](https://youtu.be/hdjlCLb9Hl8), Enns on [The Futur](https://youtu.be/KtL02T5MonA), and Chris Do's ["3 Pricing Strategies"](https://youtu.be/b5OtiUpNvqs). A fourth, Do's ["How to Charge for Design"](https://youtu.be/ivKnj9ffcmE), returned no subtitles. Market prices come from vendor blogs and live listings, so read them as directional. Domain checks are DNS/RDAP snapshots from 2026-10-08, not legal clearance. `[inf]` marks our inference.

## 1. Positioning

### 1.1 The method (Dunford)
April Dunford's order is: **competitive alternatives → unique attributes → value → best-fit customers → market category**, plus a trend if one fits. She starts with alternatives because the first question is "what do I have to beat to win a deal?": the status quo plus the shortlist. She chooses the category last, because it is "the context I position this thing in that makes my value kind of obvious" ([transcript](https://youtu.be/hdjlCLb9Hl8); [summary](https://readingraphics.com/book-summary-obviously-awesome/)). The category also tells the buyer what price to expect ([summary](https://readingraphics.com/book-summary-obviously-awesome/)). For us that is the whole game:
- Call us "AI video" and the buyer compares us with Fiverr gigs that are mostly ≤$50–200 ([Fiverr listings](https://fiverr.com/sam051/cinematic-ai-video-ads-for-your-product)).
- Call us a "product film / tabletop commercial" studio and the buyer compares us with $3–10k polished product videos ([DesignRush](https://www.designrush.com/agency/video-production/trends/cost-of-video-production), [Advids](https://advids.co/pricing/how-much-creative-product-video-creation-cost)) `[inf]`.

### 1.2 Competitive alternatives (what a founder does instead of hiring us)

| Alternative | Typical cost | What it's good at | Where it fails for our buyer | Our wedge `[inf]` |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **UGC platforms / creator farms** (Billo, Insense, freelance creators) | ~$150–200 per video on average; $99–500 on platforms; paid usage +30–50 % ([Billo](https://billo.app/blog/ugc-rates/), [DesignRevision](https://designrevision.com/blog/ugc-creator-pricing.md)) | Volume, faces, native feel. Beauty's best long runners are people talking (`23` §4) | Off-brand for premium. Creator ads ran shorter in our sample (21–37 days, `23` §4). The founder still directs every brief | We don't replace UGC. We make the **shots UGC can't**: the macro pour, the scent becoming a room. These run next to UGC as brand/hero variants |
| **Traditional production** (tabletop studio, freelance DP) | $500–3k simple; $3–10k polished; studio day $500–2k ([DesignRush](https://www.designrush.com/agency/video-production/trends/cost-of-video-production)) | Real product, real light, no AI label | Weeks of lead time, one look per shoot, every new variant costs a reshoot | **The tabletop look without the shoot**, in days, with variants built in |
| **In-house** (founder + phone, a content hire) | Salary/time | Brand knowledge, speed for native posts | Can't do impossible or cinematic shots, and runs out of new hero creative | We plug in as the "hero shelf" behind their native output |
| **Fiverr / cheap AI gigs** | Mostly ≤$50–200, 1–13 days ([listings](https://fiverr.com/dil_vortex/create-high-quality-cinematic-ai-video-ads-for-your-product)) | Price | Generic prompts, melting labels, no brief or brand thinking | Product fidelity, art direction, a brief (`25` §4), and frames approved before anything moves |
| **Do nothing** (keep running statics) | $0 | Statics hit well (Motion: text-only 11.6 %, `23` §6.3) | Fragrance ads fatigue in ~4 weeks (`23` §3). No motion asset for Reels or TikTok | One hero film becomes 6 s, 15 s and 20 s cuts plus stills, so it feeds the statics too |

### 1.3 Unique attributes → value themes
Attributes (true today): AI-native pipeline; product-only scenes, where AI is strongest (`28` §6); a premium "clean aesthetic" house style; variants as standard (3 hooks × 3 lengths × 2 ratios, `23` §6); frames first, so the client approves the look before motion; one person from brief to final, with no middlemen (Mother's lesson, `25` §2).

Value themes (Dunford's "so what"):
1. **Premium look without a production budget or a shoot.**
2. **Enough fresh variants to keep paid social from fatiguing.**
3. **You see it before you pay for it.** Frames come first, then a deposit, then the balance on approval.

### 1.4 Best-fit customer
A US founder-led DTC brand with roughly $500k–$20M revenue that already runs Meta/TikTok ads (it shows in the Ad Library). Its product **sells through the senses**: scent, texture, pour, sparkle, steam. Its packaging and site already look premium, but its ads are mostly static or old. Extra triggers: a launch, Q4 or seasonal drops, or a job post for a content role. Weakest fit: makeup and actives skincare with efficacy claims, where faces and claims win (`23` §5). Florida brands are a bonus for meetings, not a requirement.

### 1.5 Three positioning options

| | A · "Product film studio" | B · "Creative testing studio" | C · "AI ad studio" |
|---|---|---|---|
| Category | Product film / tabletop commercial studio, made with AI | Performance creative: hero film plus a monthly variant supply | AI video-ad studio |
| Dunford strategy | Big fish, small pond: an existing category, narrowed to sensory DTC goods | Head-to-head with UGC platforms and performance agencies | A category in formation |
| Price anchor | Shoots ($3–10k) | Retainers and UGC bundles | Fiverr AI gigs and AI tools |
| Statement | "The tabletop commercial without the shoot, for products you have to smell, taste or touch." | "Fresh premium variants every month, so your ads never go stale." | "Premium AI ads for DTC brands." |
| Risk | Must look as good as a shoot. Spec work has to carry it | Promises a performance outcome we can't prove yet. No data | Commodity, races to the bottom; "AI" is the buyer's top objection |

**Recommendation: A, with B as the upsell** `[inf]`. A puts us next to a price the buyer already respects. It names the outcome (a film), not the tool. AI becomes the *how*, which is disclosed in every message (`28` §9), rather than the *what*. It also matches what our spec reel actually proves: craft, not ROAS. B becomes the retainer story once a client's numbers exist. **Trend layer:** 2026 video models (Kling, Seedance, Veo) now hold labels and liquids well enough for product-only scenes, which they couldn't do a year ago (`14`, `20` §1.3).

**Positioning statement:**
> For founder-led DTC brands whose product sells through the senses, [Studio] makes premium 15–25 s product films: the tabletop commercial without the shoot. Frames in days, a finished film with test-ready cuts in about a week, and you approve the look before you pay the balance.

## 2. Offer design

### 2.1 Principles
- **Hormozi's value equation:** value = (dream outcome × perceived likelihood) ÷ (time delay × effort). We raise likelihood (frames first, balance on approval), cut the delay (days, not weeks) and cut effort (no shoot, no product shipping). "Fast beats free" ([notes](https://www.maxmednik.com/blog/notes-on-100m-offers-by-alex-hormozi)).
- **Price on output, not hours** (Chris Do, model 2). Fixed fees with fixed deliverables, capped revisions ("revisions constitute tweaking, not starting over"), and a delay clause that closes a stalled project ([transcript](https://youtu.be/b5OtiUpNvqs)).
- **Usage is a price lever.** Do's lesson from photographers is to ask "what is the usage?" (same transcript). Paid social, organic and the site are included. TV, out-of-home and retail screens are priced separately.
- **Three options, most expensive first.** Enns: "three options and lead with the most expensive one. Your average settled price will go up" ([Impact Pricing](https://impactpricing.com/podcast/ep27-blair-enns-pricing-creativity-applied-to-products/)). Every tier must be a real offer (`28` §8).
- **Rourke's floor:** $2,500 is "a great starting block… that now becomes your floor" (`20` §1.1). We set the Launch pack at $2,500 and treat the Starter as a deliberate on-ramp for first clients, not the price the studio is built on `[inf]`.

### 2.2 Three productised tiers (names fit the shortlisted brand; swap them if the name changes)

| | **Season** (monthly) | **Premiere** (recommended) | **Short** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Who it's for | Brands running paid social every week | A launch, drop or Q4 push | A first test of us |
| Deliverables | 1 new concept a month: a 20 s hero, an 8 s loop and 8–12 variants (3 hooks, 6/15/20 s, 9:16 + 4:5), plus 6 stills. Refreshing hooks every ~4 weeks matches fragrance's ad life (`23` §3) | 5 storyboard frames, a 20 s hero, an 8 s loop, 6 variants (3 hooks × 2 lengths), 9:16 + 4:5 (+1:1 on request), 3 stills, a captions file | 5 frames, a 15–20 s hero and 3 cuts (6 s, 15 s, a hook swap) in 9:16 + 4:5 |
| Turnaround | Frames by day 5 of the month; films by day 10; a variant drop each week | Frames day 2 → first cut day 5 → final day 7–8 | Final in 5 business days |
| Revisions | 2 rounds per concept | 2 rounds per film | 1 round (+$150 per extra round) |
| Usage | Perpetual, worldwide, all paid and organic digital channels and the site. TV, OOH and retail screens quoted separately | Same | Same |
| Price | **$3,500/mo**, 3-month minimum | **$2,500** | **$1,200** |
| Payment | Monthly in advance | 50 % to start, 50 % on approval | 50 / 50 |

Add-ons, priced on output: rush delivery under 5 days (+25 %); an extra SKU or scent in the same world (+$400); a 30 s faceless VO-mechanism cut (+$500; `23` recommends it for beauty, drinks and food); localised text (+$150 per language). These are consistent with `28` §6–8 `[inf]`.

### 2.3 An honest risk reversal
Hormozi lists unconditional, conditional, "anti", implied and performance guarantees ([notes](https://www.maxmednik.com/blog/notes-on-100m-offers-by-alex-hormozi)). A performance guarantee is ruled out because we never promise ROAS (`28` §9). Our version is a **conditional, staged guarantee** we can afford:
1. **See before you buy.** The 5 frames are free. If they don't feel like your brand, we stop and you owe nothing.
2. **Approve before you pay the balance** (Premiere and Short). A 50 % deposit starts the film; the 50 % balance is due only when you approve the final cut. If two revision rounds still don't get there, you can walk away. You owe no balance and you don't receive the final files. The deposit covers the work done.
3. **On-time promise:** if the first cut is late through our fault, the balance drops 10 %.

Say it exactly like that, with the conditions visible. Don't call it "100 % risk-free": the deposit is at risk. Offer it only while you can absorb a walk-away (`28` §6).

**Founding-client terms (first 5 brands, then removed; the cap is real).** In Enns's own words to a new agency: "the mistake is not in discounting or even doing it for free; the mistake is in not having that open and direct conversation with the client about what your intentions are" ([transcript](https://youtu.be/KtL02T5MonA)). So we say it openly: "You'd be one of our first five clients. In exchange for permission to show the work once it's live, plus a 15-minute debrief, you get one extra variant." **Never trade value for a review or testimonial:** the FTC's 2024 rule covers bought and fake reviews (`28` §9).

### 2.4 The lead magnet: 5 free frames → paid deal
- **Scope:** 5 watermarked "AI concept" stills, 1 concept, the client's hero product, 1 light tweak round, delivered in 3–5 days. Cap production at 5 frame sets a week so it can't eat the studio `[inf]`.
- **The tension with Enns:** Win Without Pitching says don't give creative away before the problem is diagnosed ([Digital Pigeon](https://www.digitalpigeon.com/improve-your-workflow/winning-work-without-pitching-4-thoughts-from-blair-enns/)). Our answer `[inf]`: with no clients, the frames are **proof of craft, not free strategy**. Keep the diagnosis (the `28` §7 questions) for the call, and say plainly why the frames are free: "no clients yet, so judge me on your product."
- **Conversion path:** reply → frames made → **delivered live on a 15-minute call**, never as attachments (`28` §8) → "What jumps out?" → a short diagnosis → "Frame 3 is your storyboard" → two-yes close: "Premiere at $2,500 with the 6 variants, or the Short at $1,200 to test first?" → one-page proposal the same day → deposit invoice.
- **If they say no:** offer one re-direction, never a second free round. Log the reason. A frame made for a prospect stays private unless they give written permission (`28` §5).
- **Funnel targets** `[inf, from 28's benchmarks]`: 50 sends → 5–8 replies → 4–6 frame sets → 3–4 calls → 1–2 paid.

### 2.5 Retainer upsell (Premiere → Season)
- **When:** at final approval of a Premiere, while the client is happiest, and again at day 21, when the first variants begin to fatigue `[inf]`.
- **Script** (the two-yes form, `28` §4): "The hero is built, so new hooks are cheap now. Shall we do one more Premiere for spring, or set up the Season plan so a new variant drops every week?"
- **Retainer sweeteners** (bonuses, not discounts, per Hormozi): the Premiere's project files become the Season's base, a monthly 1-page "what ran / what's next" note built on *their* numbers, and first claim on our calendar. The calendar claim is honest only while capacity really is limited.
- **Cancel terms:** a 3-month minimum, then 30 days' notice. No auto-renew surprises.

### 2.6 Pricing-page layout and anchoring
1. **Hero line:** "Product films without the shoot. From $1,200." Show the low entry, then anchor upward.
2. **Context strip (an honest anchor):** "A polished product video from a production company typically runs $3–10k ([DesignRush](https://www.designrush.com/agency/video-production/trends/cost-of-video-production)). A single UGC video is about $150–200, and you still direct it." Sourced, and never presented as our "list price".
3. **Three cards, left to right: Season → Premiere → Short** (most expensive first, per Enns). Premiere is visually raised with a "Recommended for launches" ribbon. **Never** "Most popular": we have no data for it.
4. **Per-asset maths under each card:** Premiere = 1 hero + 1 loop + 6 variants + 3 stills, about $230 per asset. Season works out to roughly $250–330 per asset `[inf]`.
5. **The risk-reversal box**, worded exactly as §2.3, with its conditions.
6. **The "free 5 frames" CTA**, with a two-product picker ("which product?"). This is the main conversion; the page sells the frames, and the call sells the tier.
7. **FAQ:** Is it AI? (Yes; we flag every generated element.) Will Meta label it? (It may; `28` §6.) Who owns it? (A licence plus assignment; the AI copyright caveat from `28` §8.) Do you have clients? ("Not yet. These are spec films for invented brands.") Rush? Usage beyond social?
8. **Mascot cameo** in the FAQ only, never beside the prices. Prices stay calm `[inf]`.

## 3. Studio name

### 3.1 Criteria
Naming guides say: settle the positioning before the name, choose a style (coined, metaphor, mash-up or founder), avoid keyword-descriptive names, check the domain *while* ideating, test spelling aloud, and run a trademark search ([NameClub](https://www.nameclub.com/blog/creative-marketing-agency-names), [CoSchedule](https://coschedule.com/agency-marketing/digital-marketing-agency-name-ideas)). Our filters:
- premium and cinematic;
- a wink of play, so a weird mascot can live inside it;
- no "AI" in the name, which dates fast and drags the price anchor down;
- no clash with brands in our six niches `[inf]`.

### 3.4 Top 3 (recommendation order)
1. **Still Life Club.** *Still life* is the art and photography term for exactly what we make (objects, light, composition), so it reads premium and cinematic to a founder. *Club* adds play and membership, and it gives the mascot a home: the club's strange host or doorman.    **Tagline:** *"Objects, beautifully misbehaving."*
2. **Slow Gloss.** Two words that *are* the house style: slow motion and glossy macro surfaces. Short, easy to say, clearest search results. Weaker for the mascot, which becomes the "Gloss" persona.
   **Tagline:** *"Product films you can almost touch."*
3. **Object Fever.** The most playful of the three: the products are the stars and the studio is obsessed with them. It hosts a weird mascot well (a "fever dream" host). The neighbours (Object & Animal, Fever-Tree) need a quick check.

### 3.5 The mascot (guardrails)
- A weird-human AI host is a strong faceless device, and it's distinctive (`25` §5: an ownable brand code repeated everywhere).
- **Rules** `[inf; FTC fake-review rule via 28 §9]`:
  - Always disclosed as AI.
  - Never presented as a real person.
  - Never a "customer" endorsing a client product.
  - Never in a client's ad unless the client asks for it.
- **Its job** is our own content: introducing films, reacting to products in surreal ways, and hosting "how it was made" breakdowns.
- **Keep it out of the work.** Keep it off the pricing cards, and never in client deliverables by default. The work has to read premium; the mascot reads playful.

## 4. Brand voice guide (site, video, email)

**Voice in three words:** *calm, exact, sensory.* The mascot adds a fourth, *deadpan-weird*, in social content only. Abbott's rule applies: words are "the servants of the argument", plain and familiar (`25` §2).

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Short sentences, concrete nouns (glass, steam, wax, pour, clasp, label) | Abstract hype ("next-level visuals") |
| Name the product and the moment | Talk about "content" in general |
| State limits plainly ("no clients yet", "AI-made", "Meta may label it") | Hedge, or hide the AI |
| Prices and terms in plain numbers | "Contact us for pricing" |
| One wink per piece (via the mascot or a twist line) | Jokes on the pricing page or in proposals |
| "You/your" ≥ 2× "I/we" (`28` §2) | Opening with "We are a…" |

**Words we use:** film, frame, still, cut, loop, hero, variant, hook, light, texture, pour, macro, approve, made with AI, spec (for invented brands), "you see it first".

**Words we never use:** elevate, unlock, game-changer, seamless, cutting-edge, revolutionary, "AI magic", viral, guaranteed (results/ROAS), "trusted by", "our clients" (until true), "shot/filmed" (for AI footage), "100 % risk-free", "I hope this finds you well", "quick question", "just following up" (`28` §1).

**Five example lines:**
1. Site hero: "Product films without the shoot. Made with AI, directed by taste."
2. Spec label: "VESPER is a fragrance we invented, so we could show you what we'd make for yours."
3. Email: "Your candle's whole story is the first strike of the match. I'd open on that, in macro, and let the room warm around it."
4. Guarantee: "You see five frames before you spend a dollar, and you pay the balance only when you approve the cut."
5. Mascot (social): "I don't have a sense of smell. I made this one by staring at the bottle for six hours."

## 5. Thirty-day go-to-market

**Rule zero** (Rourke): "find the clients first and then learn how to fulfil what they want" (`20` §1.1). Outreach is the main job; content supports it.

### 5.1 Outreach cadence for the 50 (the `28` §1.1 ramp, from a personal Gmail)

| Days | First touches | Follow-ups (same thread, `28` §5) | Other |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1–3 | Finish the name decision, the one-page site and the pricing page. Grade all 50 emails to 16+/20 | — | 3 frame demos for the site |
| 4–8 (Tue–Thu weighted) | 8–10 a day, fit 8+ prospects first → ~40 | Day-3 notes start | Make frames within 3–5 days of each "yes" |
| 9–12 | The last ~10 | Day 3 and day 7 (one finished frame as a link) | LinkedIn: connect with the founders first (Rourke: LinkedIn DMs get read, `20` §1.1) |
| 13–20 | — | Day-14 close-the-loop, ≤25 sends a day in total | Frame calls; proposals the same day |
| 21–30 | Start a second list of 25, built from what replied | Respect every "not now" date | Retainer pitch to anyone who approved a Premiere |

IG/TikTok DMs only where the brief lists them as the public channel. Every email carries the CAN-SPAM footer (`28` §1.2).

### 5.2 Content: faceless and mascot-led

| Channel | Cadence | Formats | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| **LinkedIn** (founder page, faceless avatar = studio mark) | 3 a week | "How it was made" breakdowns: brief → frame → film, with prompts partly shown (Rourke: "every post is a job interview", BTS often beats the spec, `20` §1.1) | Credibility with founders; the most direct buyer channel |
| **TikTok + IG Reels** | 4–5 a week | (1) An 8–15 s spec film; (2) the mascot reacting to or "testing" spec products; (3) a before/after: phone snapshot → film; (4) a narrative-loop BTS with a comment keyword → DM a free "frames checklist" (`14` #14) | Reach + proof; the content funnel into "5 free frames" |
| **Site** | Once, then weekly portfolio adds | The 3 spec films + new niche specs, each labelled "spec, invented brand" | Where every message points |

Don't publicly post spec work for *real* brands, and don't tag them, without written permission (`28` §5). This is a deliberate departure from Rourke's "jab, jab, hook" `[inf]`. Make spec films for invented brands only.

### 5.3 Portfolio priorities (fill the niche gaps)
Today we have juice (AURUM), fragrance (VESPER) and candles (SOL). In order of the prospect mix `[inf]`:
1. **Jewelry:** a wrist/hand macro with a single-shot render, our strongest faceless evidence (`23` §3).
2. **Skincare texture:** a dropper or cream macro plus a proof line on screen.
3. **Gourmet food:** steam, crack or drizzle ASMR plus a one-line mechanism.
4. **A DR cut for one existing spec:** a native layer (iMessage or review card) and a 30 s VO version, to show performance range.

Each new spec ships as a hero, an 8 s loop and a 4:5 crop, the deliverable spec from `23` §6.5. Target: 2 new specs by day 20.

### 5.4 Metrics (review every Friday)

| Metric | 30-day target `[inf]` | Benchmark |
|---|---|---|
| First touches sent | 50 (+25 new) | `28` ramp |
| Reply rate (any) | 8–15 % | Instantly top quartile is 5.5 %+, top decile 10.7 %+ (`28` §1) |
| Frame requests | 4–6 | — |
| Frames → call | ≥60 % | — |
| Call → proposal → paid | 1–2 paid projects, ≥$2,500 booked | — |
| Spam complaints / bounces | 0 / <2 % | Google <0.1 % spam rate (`28` §1.1) |

**Kill/adjust rules** `[inf]`:
- Under 5 % replies after 30 sends: rewrite line 1 and the idea line.
- Frames requested but not converting: fix the call (`28` §7), not the price.
- Every lost deal says "AI looks fake": go to `06` anti-AI realism before any more outreach.

## 6. Honesty checklist
- No client logos, testimonials, invented numbers or "trusted by". The spec films are always labelled as spec.
- AI is disclosed in message 1, on the site and in the proposal.
- The mascot is always labelled AI and never endorses anything.
- Scarcity only when true: the five founding slots and real capacity.
- No ROAS promises. The guarantee is about approval, not results.
