# 26 — Art Direction: see like a commercial AD, director and photographer

Researched 2026-10-08. This file covers the layer above the shot: **one idea, one world, one colour arc, one composition plan for the whole ad.** It does not repeat the light/lens vocabulary (05), the AI ban list and film chain (06), the hero recipes and still rubric (`image-direction.md`) or the stills factory and judge (17). Read those for execution.
Inferences are marked **[inf]**. Source IDs are listed at the end. Real brand treatments are confidential pitch documents, so very few are public. §1 is built from directors explaining their own treatments (one with a treatment shown next to the finished spot [T2]) and from industry guides. I did not find a public Behance archive of real brand treatments; Behance mostly lists treatment-design *services*.

## 0. The ten rules

1. **One thesis per ad, one idea per frame.** A director turns the agency script into one visual metaphor. In [T2] that was "clean air is volume and space", which gave "no paintings, no rugs, no nothing, just empty space". Every choice afterwards is a consequence of it.
2. **Write the world before the shots.** Palette, shape, texture, light logic and a signature device go into a *world bible* (§2). Shots are then samples of that world, not 8 separate "nice images".
3. **Constraints make style.** Lubezki: "Art is made of constraints. When you don't have any, you go crazy, because everything is possible" [L5]. Write rules and a "never" list for each ad.
4. **Colour is a script, not a swatch.** Plan how colour *changes* across the 8 shots (§3), the way Pixar colour scripts map "the emotional beats of a story" [A2].
5. **Compose with contrast and affinity.** Block's principle: more visual contrast means more intensity, more affinity means less [A1]. Ramp contrast towards the reveal and resolve to affinity on the end card (§4).
6. **Every light needs a source you could point to.** Deakins dislikes "two shadows from one sun", or "a multitude of colour lights with no justification" [L1].
7. **Pretty is not the goal.** "People confuse pretty with good cinematography." Too many accents turn a frame into "some piece of confectionery instead of something that's really simple and direct" [L1].
8. **Heroes carry the world, inserts carry one property.** Never ask one frame to do both (§5).
9. **Moodboard images are evidence, not inputs.** Each reference gets one job, which is then translated into words. Never paste a mood frame in as a style reference without a stated job (17 rule 3).
10. **The generic AI look is the average.** Models drift to "shiny, bright, waxy skin, and over-use of bokeh" because averaging tastes is "almost adversarial" to aesthetics [M2]. An opinionated world bible is the counterweight.

## 1. How commercial directors write treatments (and what we steal)

A treatment is a 5–12 page pitch (5–15 slides, about 3–4 days of work [T6]). It shows the agency your version of their script. Its real question is "why you" [T3][T4][T5]. It is a sales tool: "Visuals should back the director's idea, not the other way around" [T6].

**Section order, consolidated from [T1][T3][T4][T5][T6][T2]:**

| # | Section | What pros put in it | Our AI equivalent |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Intro / hook** | A one-liner or quote that captures the idea. A callback to the briefing call [T1] | **Thesis line** (≤15 words) |
| 2 | **Brief echo** | Restate the client's objective, for the "people who don't read the brief", such as the production designer and DP [T2] | Objective, audience, single-minded proposition (SMP), mandatories |
| 3 | **Approach / why me** | Personal connection. Honest reasons [T1] | Skip, or replace with "why this concept beats the default" |
| 4 | **Story / beats** | Plot summary in beats. Storyboard only if the client "isn't very visual" [T1][T2] | 8-shot beat sheet (§7) |
| 5 | **Casting / characters** | Archetypes, "bare minimum" backstory, voice type if VO [T1][T2] | Character card: age, look, energy, one behaviour |
| 6 | **Visual style / camera** | Lighting mood, colour, camera movement *and why* (static vs handheld) [T1][T5] | World bible §2 and camera philosophy |
| 7 | **Art direction / sets** | Where it happens, and what the place says about the character. "Show not tell" detail [T1] | Set plus a signature device |
| 8 | **Wardrobe** | Often the brief repeated plus the director's preferences and exclusions [T2] | Palette-locked wardrobe line |
| 9 | **Sound / music** | Considered from the start. Example: "starts without any music", so it "doesn't immediately scream commercial" [T2] | 13-elevenlabs plan |
| 10 | **Edit / post** | Quick cuts, match cuts, grade. Often a single linked reference for pacing instead of prose [T1][T2] | Rhythm line and graphic matches (§4.4) |
| 11 | **References** | Film stills and commercial frames, which must "feel cinematic, yet ultimately feel like you" [T4]. Links "so the client understands where I'm drawing from and how we differ" [T2] | Moodboard with jobs (§6) |
| 12 | **Gear / budget / outro** | Format, lens package, how the gear makes the look [T3] | Model router choice (`model-router.md`) |

**Craft lessons from the real treatment vs finished spot (Samsung AC, [T2]):**
- **Camera follows thesis.** Minimalism meant "no sliders, dollies or gimbals ... static locked-off shots". The director's reasoning: "directing is honestly just that: taking conscious decisions and knowing what effect that decision will have". *For us:* AI motion still needs micro-breath (06 §Motion). Locked-off means *compositionally* still, not dead pixels [inf].
- **Light carries the story when the camera doesn't.** Temperature is coded into the light: "when it's super hot the lighting is all bright and orange; when it's nice and cool it's all soft and dim and airy". This is a two-state colour script.
- **The signature device came from the product.** The set walls were "perforated to echo the AC units with 23,000 micro dots". The client "really loved" it. **This is the single most transferable move: lift one physical feature of the product and make it the world's texture.**
- **Say what you *won't* do.** The client wanted neon. The director kept "super saturated neon colours but with soft lighting and **no neon tubes in the frame**." This is a constraint as a negotiation tool.
- **Tone is shown, not named.** The guides agree that tone words must be backed by matching references and on-page writing in that tone [T4][T5][T7]. Use **3 tone words plus 3 anti-words** ("tactile, sun-struck, wry. NOT glossy, epic, cute") [inf].
- **Write in active voice and commit to bold choices** [T7]. Clients want "visual, bold, and unique" plus proof you will "stay in control" [T3].

## 2. Building a visual world: the world bible

Art direction is *subtraction plus repetition*. Pick few elements, then repeat them until they become a language [inf, consistent with A3, A4, P1].

**2.1 Palette discipline.**
- Use **3 colours plus 1 accent**: dominant (≈60% of frame area), secondary (≈30%), neutral/dark (≈10%). Then one **accent reserved for meaning**. The accent is usually the product or the payoff, and it appears nowhere else [inf, the 60-30-10 interior-design heuristic].
- Production designers police this hard. On *The Day the Earth Stood Still* even one red tomato in a stadium was questioned: "We don't do red!" On an earlier film the rule was "no brown!" The palette was settled over "ten or fifteen rounds" before it became law [A3].
- On *The Last Jedi*, red was "used very sparingly ... a very specific application" and saved for the Crait battlefield [A3].
- A USC production designer describes working with few colours "so that when a new or more vibrant colour appears, it amplifies a certain emotional beat" [A3]. **That is the accent rule.**
- Write the palette as *named physical materials*, not hex alone: "oxblood lacquer, bone-white plaster, smoked oak". Models render materials more reliably than colour codes. Keep hex for the grade and the end card [inf].

**2.2 Shape language.** Pick one dominant basic shape and one opposing shape for conflict.
- Block uses circle/square/triangle as the basic shapes [A1]. The common reading: **circle** is soft, friendly, whole. **Square/rectangle** is stable, ordered, institutional. **Triangle/diagonal** is dynamic, tense, aggressive [A1 summaries, verify specifics; inf].
- Derive the shape from the product silhouette: a round can means circles, a tall bottle means verticals, a sneaker means wedges/diagonals. Echo it in props, set openings, shadows (gobos) and the type lockup [inf].
- The Incredibles colour script used "abstract geometric shapes" in pop-art colour [A2]. Shape *is* part of the colour script.

**2.3 Texture.**
- Choose **two textures in contrast**, for example matte plaster vs wet glass, or brushed steel vs raw linen. Name them in every prompt.
- Texture is the cheapest anti-AI cue, because the AI default is "overly soft textures" [M2].
- Ideas from current fashion/beauty art direction [A4]: textured backlit plexiglass ("instant depth and texture"), intentional controlled spill ("texture and tension"), scanner-flattened objects, printed on-location backdrops, **incomplete backdrops** that show the set edge ("showing the artifice instead of hiding it"), colour blocking, overhead "voyeuristic" views, platform builds.

**2.4 Light logic.**
- Pick **one world light**, for example "low winter sun through a single west window", and a time-of-day arc.
- Lubezki: "different times of the day express different emotions". Light is "the broth that contains the soup ... it determines the mood and the atmosphere of a scene more than anything" [L4].
- Every frame inherits this logic. Inserts may add a *motivated* kicker, never a new sun [L1].

**2.5 Lens family and camera philosophy.**
- Choose one lens family and one movement rule.
- Lubezki/Malick dogma, as a template for writing our own rules [L5]:
  - "shoot in backlight for continuity and depth"
  - "negative fill to avoid light sandwiches"
  - "never front light"
  - "avoid white and primary colours in frame"
  - "short-focal-length, hard lenses"
  - "Z-axis moves instead of pans or tilts"
  - "no zooming"
  - "do some static tripod shots in the midst of our haste"
  - plus an "Article E" exception
- Deakins on lens psychology: a close-up on a 21–27 mm close to the face feels like intimacy. A 100 mm from afar feels observational. "It's the same as your relationship in real life" [L1].

**2.6 Signature device.** This is one recurring visual idea that makes the ad ownable. Test: could a frame with the logo cropped out still be recognised as this brand's ad? [inf]
Sources for a device:
- (a) **A product feature as world texture.** Example: AC micro-perforation as the wall pattern [T2].
- (b) **A brand shape as recurring frame**, such as a can-top circle repeated as sun, porthole, spotlight or puddle.
- (c) **One colour accent** that only ever touches the product.
- (d) **One recurring action**, such as a hand gesture or a pour angle.

The device must appear in **≥3 of 8 shots**, and in the end card.

**2.7 Casting and wardrobe.**
- Casting by archetype with "bare minimum" backstory [T2].
- Wardrobe is locked to the palette: no off-palette garments, no logos, one texture from §2.3.
- Name *one behaviour* per character: "chews the straw", "checks reflection". In AI, behaviour sells realism more than an adjective does [inf].

## 3. Colour script (Pixar method → 8-shot ad)

**What it is.** A row of small, abstract thumbnails made early. Their purpose is "mostly the emotional tone of each scene", shown with "pure abstract shapes and colour". Lou Romano compares reading one to reading sheet music [A2, MoMA audio guide].
- Ralph Eggleston made the first one for *Toy Story*, in chalk pastel on long strips of black paper. Lasseter: the colours "intensified with the emotions and turned muted during somber moments". Pixar has made one for every film since [A2].
- Toy Story's soft pastels go "bleak and muddy when something sinister occurs". Toy Story 3 goes to fiery oranges and reds for peril. *Up*'s opening runs "beat by emotional beat" with colour carrying the story without dialogue [A2].

**How to make one for an ad [inf, adapted]:**
1. Write the **emotional beat** of each of the 8 shots in one word (for example: friction, friction, curiosity, contact, release, delight, pride, sign-off).
2. Give each beat **four values**:
   - **dominant hue**
   - **value key**: low, mid or high (overall brightness)
   - **saturation**: 1–5
   - **temperature**: warm or cool
3. Apply three rules:
   - **The turn gets the colour shift.** The problem→solution cut is where hue or temperature flips. In [T2] this is the hot orange → cool airy flip.
   - **Saturation peaks at the payoff**, usually shot 6 or 7. The accent colour is first seen at full strength on the product reveal.
   - **The end card returns to the brand palette at mid key.** The resolution is affinity (§4).
4. Make a **thumbnail strip**: 8 flat colour blocks with the main shape silhouette. Use a cheap model (Z-Image Turbo, 17 §1) with the prompt "flat abstract colour study, no detail, [shape] silhouette, [hue/key]", or draw it with code. Approve the strip *before* any hero generation.
5. **Grayscale check:** desaturate the strip. Value alone should still show the arc. Pixar scripts read as light/dark rhythm as much as hue [inf].

**Template row:** `S3 | curiosity | teal-grey dominant, amber accent 5% | mid-low key | sat 2 | cool | circle top-right`.

## 4. Composition with Bruce Block's *The Visual Story*

**Framework** [A1, 3rd ed. 2020; publisher listing, author's USC syllabus, student analyses]:
- Seven basic visual components: **space, line, shape, tone, colour, movement, rhythm**.
- Each can be pushed toward **contrast** (difference, which raises visual intensity) or **affinity** (similarity, which lowers it).
- Controlling them is how the picture-maker "stirs an audience's emotions, creates a visual style and gives structure and unity".
- The core practice is to **graph story intensity, then graph visual intensity to match it**. Contrast rises to the climax, and affinity sits at exposition and resolution.

**4.1 The seven components as ad decisions [Block's categories; ad mappings are inf]:**

| Component | Contrast (raise intensity) | Affinity (calm, unify) | Ad use |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Space** | Cut between deep and flat. Push depth cues (overlap, size change, aerial haze, focus falloff) | Keep one space type. Block's four types: **deep, flat, limited, ambiguous** | Problem shots in *ambiguous/limited* space. Hero reveal in *deep* space. End card in *flat* space (it is a graphic) |
| **Line** | Diagonals vs verticals, or real lines crossing implied lines | All horizontals (calm) or all verticals (formal) | Product verticals vs a diagonal action line at the turn |
| **Shape** | Circle against triangle | One shape family echoed | §2.2. The shape match is the graphic match (4.4) |
| **Tone** | Wide brightness range, hard falloff | Narrow range (all mid-grey, or all dark) | Low-key problem, high-key payoff, or the inverse for luxury |
| **Colour** | Complementary hues, saturated vs desaturated, warm vs cool | Analogous hues, matched saturation | Follows the colour script (§3) |
| **Movement** | Opposing directions between cuts, still vs fast | Same screen direction and speed | Keep the screen direction consistent until the turn, then reverse it [inf] |
| **Rhythm** | Irregular cut lengths, accents | Even tempo | Even 1.5 s cuts in the setup, staccato at the turn, then a held final beat |

- **Point of attention (eye trace).** Know where the eye lands at the end of shot N. Then start shot N+1's subject in the same screen zone for a smooth cut (affinity), or in the opposite zone for a jolt (contrast) [A1 concept; inf application].
- In 9:16 this matters more, because the eye can only travel vertically [inf].

**4.2 Intensity graph for a 15–20 s, 8-shot ad [inf]:** `S1 4 · S2 5 · S3 6 · S4 7 · S5 9 (turn) · S6 10 (reveal) · S7 6 · S8 3 (end card)`.
- Write each shot's number next to its Block choices.
- A shot scored 9–10 should be contrasty on **at least three** components.
- A shot scored ≤4 should be affinity on most components.
- Hook exception: S1 must still stop the scroll. Give it **one** strong contrast (usually tone or scale) inside an otherwise calm frame (03 hook rules).

**4.3 Negative space for type.**
- Decide the type zone *before* composing. Overprinting a headline on a busy photo makes it compete with the image, so leave clear space [M4].
- Reserve the zone as a physical thing in the prompt. Write "the upper third is an empty, evenly lit [colour] plaster wall with nothing on it". Don't write "copy space". Models ignore abstract layout words but obey described emptiness [inf, consistent with 17 §4.5].
- Keep the product's eye-trace pointing *towards* the type zone (gaze, a diagonal, a pour direction).
- Respect platform safe zones (03).
- The end card is flat space: frontal plane, no perspective lines, one tone.

**4.4 Graphic matches.**
- A graphic match is a cut where "shapes, colours, or overall movement" line up across two shots. The classics are the *2001* bone → satellite cut and the *Citizen Kane* opening [M3].
- For AI ads this is structural, not decorative. Clips are generated separately, so "cuts feel assembled". A repeated shape or colour lets viewers read continuity across changes [M3, vendor claim, plausible].
- Plan **1–2 per ad** using the signature device: can top → sun disc, ice-cube edge → building edge, swirl in a glass → vinyl record.
- **Execution:** generate S(N) first. Then make S(N+1)'s *start frame* by keeping S(N)'s last-frame silhouette position, scale and tone (a layout map image, 17 §4.5) and changing everything else.

## 5. Light and the one idea: how masters decide

**Deakins** [L1][L2][L3]:
- Lighting comes *after* feeling. "You don't ever want to go on a location scout thinking first primarily 'where's the light coming from'... that's death." Ask what the audience should feel, then light.
- Minimalism: "My lighting's really minimal." He would give up lighting before framing [L2]. He wants images lit "as though somebody sitting in a room by a lamp is being lit by that lamp" [L3].
- Justify on screen: "within the scene somewhere ... see that practical or see the window to know the justification for the light" [L1].
- **One idea:** the best stills "strip away all the things ... on the periphery and concentrate on that one little image that says more than... a big wide shot" [L1].
- Don't make shots to be loved: "you shouldn't love a shot... it should all be part of a whole."

**Lubezki** [L4][L5][L6]:
- Light is dramaturgy. Locations "make you feel sad... lonely... joyful", so scout them at different hours. He uses wide lenses even in close-ups so "you still feel the light changes... the wind and the cold".
- Backlight with heavy negative fill, never even two-sided light. "We want the blacks; we don't like milky images."
- On rewriting rather than adding lights: "instead of bringing in lights, we would rewrite the scene and reassemble it outside" [L6]. *For us:* when a prompt fights its light, change the staging (move the subject to the window) rather than adding more light words [inf].

**Still-life masters:**
- **Carl Kleiner** builds one graphic idea in camera. Rigging lets him "only show what I want to show". He uses mirrors "two centimetres" wide to place a single highlight, and does "as much as possible in camera". Retouching only "perfects things I've done by hand" [P1]. *For us:* one placed highlight beats global "studio lighting" [inf].
- **Jonathan Mauloubier** starts by understanding "what needs to be depicted". He tests light "to reveal the essence of the subjects". On what makes a still life: "it is definitely the stage that does it" [P2]. Set first.
- **Henry Leutwyler:** still life "doesn't run away... so you better know what you're doing". He took Helmut Newton's seven-minute rule to heart and shoots fast [P3]. Plan fully, then don't over-iterate.
- **Jonathan Knowles** turns motion into still life by "slicing and isolating the subject matter" [P4]. This is the logic of the insert.
- **Working product shooters on TikTok** (via TikTok discover-page summaries; individual posts not opened) [P5]:
  - two lights (product plus backdrop) cover most e-commerce work
  - the backdrop gets its own light, so its tone is set independently
  - black Duvetyne makes a bottle "pop"
  - "light the product, not the room"

**5.1 The "one idea" test (per frame).**
- Write the frame's idea as **subject + one property + one feeling**: "the bottle's *frost* feels *dangerous-cold*".
- If you need "and" twice, split it into two frames [inf].
- The idea decides what is sharpest, what is brightest, and where the accent colour sits. All three must coincide on the idea (Block: tone and colour contrast pull the eye first; saturated yellow "will always attract a viewer's eye first" [A1]).

**5.2 Hero vs insert logic [inf, from production practice in 05 plus the sources above]:**

| | **Hero** | **Insert** |
|---|---|---|
| Job | Establish world plus product identity | Prove one property (texture, mechanism, cold, crunch) |
| Space | Deep, with world-light visible (window, sun, practical) | Limited/flat. World light implied by its colour only |
| Composition | Product ~30–55% frame height, type zone reserved, signature device in frame | Fill frame with the property. The edge of the product may be cropped |
| Light | The world light plus one motivated accent | One placed highlight or backlight that reveals the property |
| Block intensity | Peak or resolution | Rising action. Contrast on 1–2 components |
| Count per 8 shots | 2 (reveal plus end) | 4–5 |
| Duration | 1.5–3 s | 0.4–1.2 s |
| Model | Product-exact path (17) | Plate/texture path. A product text needn't be legible |

## 6. Moodboards and lookbooks → prompt blocks

**Where references come from:**
- **ShotDeck**, built by DP Lawrence Sher after years of making reference decks "mostly from stills of other movies". It is a tagged library (1.4M+ shots), filterable by frame size, composition, lighting, colour and lens [M1].
- **FrameSet** (commercials and music videos, searchable by colour and lighting) [T1].
- **Pinterest** [T2], Behance/muz.li for trends [T6], **FilmGrab** [T4], and campaign photography from art-direction newsletters [A4].
- **Rule:** use real photographs and film frames only. **Never moodboard with AI images**, because that feeds the average back in [inf, M2].

**From board to prompt: deconstruct, don't paste.**
1. **12–20 frames maximum, each tagged with ONE job:** light, palette, composition, texture, casting, set or device. A frame with no job is cut.
2. **For each frame, write one sentence of physical fact:**
   - "low sun from frame-left at ~10°, hard, warm; shadows long; face in shade, rim on hair"
   - "70% of the frame is a flat sage wall; subject small in lower right"
   - "condensation on brushed steel, beads 1–2 mm, one drip track"
3. **Merge the sentences per job** into the world bible (§2) and drop the duplicates. This *is* the lookbook.
4. **Pass images to the model only for identity** (product, character), and only with stated jobs and "DISCARD its lighting/background" (17 rules 3–4). Mood frames stay human-facing.
5. **Name the anti-references:** 2–3 frames of the look we refuse (often the category cliché, such as a perfume bottle on black with gold sparkles). Their traits go into the **anti-words** and the negative list.

**Avoiding the generic AI aesthetic** (adds to 06's ban list):
- The default look is "shiny, bright, waxy-skin, over-use of bokeh". It comes from aesthetic scorers "biased towards ... blurry backgrounds, overly soft textures, and bright images" [M2]. BFL markets FLUX.1 Krea against "the oversaturated 'AI look'" [M2].
- Counter-moves [inf, from sources]:
  - **Commit to a key:** low-key or high-key, never "balanced bright".
  - **Deep focus somewhere.** Lubezki's dogma: "compose in depth" [L5]. Use bokeh only when the one-idea test needs isolation.
  - **Asymmetry and cropping.** Props cut by the frame edge (image-direction §1), and the subject off-centre via a layout map.
  - **One accent, not harmony everywhere.** AI loves evenly harmonised palettes. Real art direction has *one* discord.
  - **Remove the stock adjectives:** "cinematic, 8K, hyper-realistic, stunning, epic". They pull to the average. Replace each with a physical fact.
  - **Show artifice on purpose** when the brand allows it, through an incomplete backdrop or a visible rig edge [A4]. Intentional constructedness reads as human authorship [inf].

## 7. The Art Director Protocol (run for every ad)

**Step 0: Inputs.** Brief, product reference sheet (17 step 1), platform/format (03), concept (15).

**Step 1: Mini-treatment, one page:**
```
THESIS: <≤15 words, the visual metaphor>
SMP: <one benefit>   AUDIENCE: <who, mood they're in when scrolling>
TONE: <3 words>   NOT: <3 anti-words>
CAMERA PHILOSOPHY: <one rule + why, e.g. "locked frames, life moves inside them: product calm vs world chaos">
EDIT RHYTHM: <e.g. "even 1.5s setup, staccato turn (0.4–0.6s), held 2.5s end">
SOUND IDEA: <e.g. "no music until the reveal; ambience only">
REFERENCES: <3 frames with jobs + 1 anti-reference>
```

**Step 2: World bible.** Fill in all of it, then freeze it.
```
PALETTE: dominant <material+colour> 60 | secondary <..> 30 | dark <..> 10 | ACCENT <colour> = product/payoff only
BANNED COLOURS/ELEMENTS: <e.g. no pure white, no neon tubes in frame, no gold glitter>
SHAPE: dominant <circle/square/triangle family, source in product> vs opposing <shape for the turn>
TEXTURES: <A> vs <B>
WORLD LIGHT: <one source, direction, time of day; arc across the ad>
LENS/CAMERA: <family, e.g. 24–35mm close for people, 90–100mm macro inserts; movement rule>
SIGNATURE DEVICE: <what, which shots (≥3), how it appears on the end card>
CASTING/WARDROBE: <archetype, one behaviour, palette-locked wardrobe>
NEVER LIST: <5 items>
```

**Step 3: Colour script.** 8 rows (§3 template). Make a thumbnail strip. Run the grayscale check. Approve.

**Step 4: Composition plan.** One row per shot:

| Shot | Beat | Hero/insert | Intensity | Space | Line | Shape | Tone key | Colour (from script) | Movement and eye-trace in → out | Type zone | Device? | Graphic match? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Rules: the contrast count must match intensity (§4.2). Eye-trace out of S(N) is planned against eye-trace in of S(N+1). 1–2 graphic matches. Device in ≥3 rows.

**Step 5: Style block.** Paste it verbatim into every image prompt, after the shot-specific content and before the preserve block (17).

```
WORLD: <dominant material+colour> world with <secondary>; <dark> shadows; the only <ACCENT> in frame is on the product.
SHAPE: <shape> forms echoed in <props/set/shadows>.
TEXTURE: <A> against <B>, visible at full resolution.
LIGHT: single <world light source, direction, quality, colour temp>, motivated and visible as <window/practical/sun edge>; negative fill on the shadow side; one placed highlight on <property>. No second sun, no unmotivated coloured light.
LENS: <focal length, distance, depth (deep focus / shallow on <x>)>.
GRADE: <key: low/high>, <saturation level>, true blacks, no milky shadows, fine grain.
AVOID: <anti-words>, <never list>, waxy skin, generic bokeh, symmetrical stock composition.
```
Keep it at ≤90 words so it doesn't drown the shot content (17 §4.1). Translate it per model with the format rules in `image-direction.md` §4 [inf].

**Step 6: Generate.** Follow the stills factory (17 §2): heroes first, then inserts, then the end card.

**Step 7: Review.** The checklist below comes first, then the 17 §5 judge.

## 8. Review checklist (sequence first, then frames)

**Contact-sheet tests.** Lay all 8 frames side by side at thumbnail size.
- [ ] **Squint test:** the value/colour rhythm matches the approved colour script. The turn is visible as a shift.
- [ ] **Grayscale test:** desaturated, the intensity arc still reads, and the product is the brightest or most contrasted point in its hero frames.
- [ ] **Palette police:** no off-palette colour anywhere. The accent appears *only* on the product/payoff.
- [ ] **Signature device** is visible in ≥3 frames and on the end card. With the logo cropped out, the ad is still identifiable [inf].
- [ ] **Shape language:** the dominant shape repeats, and the opposing shape appears only at the turn.
- [ ] **Light logic:** every frame passes "point to the source". No double shadows, no unmotivated rims or gels [L1].
- [ ] **Intensity curve:** the contrast count per frame follows §4.2. No flat middle, and no two consecutive frames at the same intensity, except deliberate holds.
- [ ] **Eye-trace:** play the cuts at speed. The eye lands where the next frame expects it, except at planned jolts.
- [ ] **Graphic match** aligns on position, scale and tone (overlay at 50% opacity).
- [ ] **Hero/insert balance:** 2 heroes, 4–5 inserts, 1 end card. No insert tries to be a hero.

**Per-frame tests:**
- [ ] **One-idea sentence** is written, and the sharpest, brightest and accent areas all coincide on it.
- [ ] **Type zone** is clean (no texture, no highlights, no props) and the product points towards it.
- [ ] **Anti-AI:** no waxy skin, no default bokeh soup, no harmonised-everything palette, no centred stock pose. There is a real texture at 100% zoom. 06 ban list respected.
- [ ] **Deakins test:** does any element exist only because it's "pretty"? Remove it [L1].
- [ ] **Material truth:** the two named textures read correctly (17 §0 rule 7).
- Then hand over to the 17 §5 hard gates and the pairwise judge.

**Fix order when a frame fails [inf]:** idea → staging/composition → light logic → palette → texture → finish. Never fix a composition failure with grade words.

## 9. Worked mini-example (fictional "KOLD" canned cold brew, 9:16, 15 s)

- **Thesis:** "Inside the can it's always 6 a.m. in winter."
- **Tone:** crisp, hushed, wry. **NOT:** glossy, energetic, cosy.
- **Palette:** frost-blue painted plaster 60, raw concrete grey 30, espresso-black 10. **Accent:** copper, only the can's ring-pull and logo.
- **Shape:** circles (can top, ring-pull) vs one diagonal (the pour) at the turn.
- **Textures:** hoar-frost crystals vs matte concrete.
- **Light:** low blue dawn through one high window. The arc goes from grey-blue to a copper sunrise edge at the reveal.
- **Device:** the ring-pull circle reappears as the window shape, a frost ring on the counter, and the end-card logo holder.
- **Script:** S1 hook, a frost-bloom macro (insert, intensity 4, ambiguous space, cool, sat 1) → … → S5 turn, the ring-pull crack with a diagonal spray (insert, 9, contrast on line/tone/movement) → S6 hero reveal: the can in deep space, the window circle behind it, a first copper rim light (10, sat 4) → graphic match from the can top to the window circle → S8 end card: flat frost-blue plane, copper logo, type zone in the upper third (3).
