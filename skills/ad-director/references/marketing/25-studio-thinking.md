# 25 · Studio thinking: how the best agencies think, and the "studio mind" protocol

Research brief, 2026-10-08. Read it **before** `15-concept-engine` Stage 1. It covers the thinking that comes *before* volume ideation: diagnosis, the insight, the brief, judging ideas and presenting them. It does not repeat the four laws and gates (`../creative-brain.md`), the awareness levels, CEPs/JTBD and 15 gates (`01`), the hook/persona grids and tournament (`15`), or the twist patterns and storyline card (`19`).

**Evidence levels.** Sources are cited inline. Most agency material comes from interviews, trade press and book summaries rather than internal documents: no top agency publishes its real brief form. One primary transcript was used (Dave Trott, TEDx, fetched with `yt-dlp`). `[inf]` marks our own inference or synthesis. Public TikTok and Instagram creative-director content turned up nothing citable, so none is used.

## 1. The real pipeline at top shops (and what each stage is for)

| Stage | Who | Output | What it's for | Our AI analog `[inf]` |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Diagnosis** | Planner + client | Answers to "Where are we? Why are we here? Where could we be? How could we get there? Are we getting there?" (Stephen King's planning cycle, JWT, [Murrell](https://www.alexmurrell.co.uk/summaries/stephen-king-a-masterclass-in-brand-planning)) | Agree the *business* problem before anyone writes an ad | Intake Q&A + product/review/category research |
| **Strategy** | Planner | Objective as "To…", strategy as "By…", the way the ad is meant to work, current vs. wanted buyer (Alan Cooper, [Murrell](https://www.alexmurrell.co.uk/summaries/alan-cooper-how-to-plan-advertising)) | Decide what the ad must *do* | Strategy card |
| **Creative brief** | Planner, briefed in person | One page; one proposition; a desired response | A springboard for creatives. "The strategy is the idea before the idea" ([IPA/ISBA *Judging Creative Ideas*](https://ipa.co.uk/media/4580/judging_creative.pdf)) | Brief (§4 below) |
| **Idea** | Creative team (2–3 minds) | Scamps, scripts, key lines | The leap. Breakthroughs come from "1, 2 or possibly 3 minds" (George Lois, [Murrell](https://www.alexmurrell.co.uk/summaries/george-lois-damn-good-advice)) | 15 Stage 1 generation |
| **Internal review** | CD | Kill or keep | CD is "100% decisive, but only 80% right" (Leandro Barral, ex-W+K, via [LinkedIn](https://ar.linkedin.com/in/leandro-barral)) | Gates + rubric + tournament |
| **Client presentation** | Agency | Brief recap → idea → scripts/key frames/mood film | Sell one idea, or show routes | 3 storylines → client pick |
| **Director's treatment** | Production company (Smuggler, Biscuit, MJZ…) | 5–12 pp: approach, casting, camera, edit, music, sound, locations, art direction, production issues ([Piree](https://robinpiree.com/blog/how-to-write-a-directors-treatment-for-a-tv-commercial), [NoFilmSchool](https://nofilmschool.com/commercial-treatment)) | Turn the agency's idea into a film | Treatment page per storyline (§6) |
| **Pre-production → shoot → edit** | Director, producer, agency, client | Pre-production meeting (PPM), shoot, offline edit, online/finishing | Execution. Treatments now carry moving images, audio and mood films (a "nuclear arms race", [shots](https://shots.net/news/view/96770-are-directors-treatments-becoming-a-nuclear-arms-race)) | Stills → animatic → video (creative-brain §7) |

Directors win jobs by call → treatment in 2–7 days (often ghost-written, with an image researcher) → agency recommends one to the client ([Simian](https://gosimian.com/?p=503), [Piree](https://robinpiree.com/blog/commercial-treatment)). **The lesson for us** `[inf]`: the treatment is a *second creative act*. "Relatively ordinary scripts" become great films "in the hands of an inspired director" ([IPA guide](https://ipa.co.uk/media/4580/judging_creative.pdf)). Our agent must play that director too, not just transcribe the script into prompts.

## 2. House signatures worth stealing

- **Wieden+Kennedy: "Walk in stupid every morning."** The mantra hangs on a mannequin at the W+K London entrance. The agency's own essay warns that proprietary tools and insight workshops produce campaigns "that look like everyone else's" ([B&T](https://www.bandt.com.au/innovation-importance-walking-stupid/)). Karl Lieberman (Global CCO) says "good briefs are like buckets": spaces to work in, not answers. His habits ([Method Breakdown](https://methodstudio.substack.com/p/method-breakdown-karl-lieberman)):
  - Research a brand on eBay and Etsy to see what fans actually treasure.
  - Reframe the product around a theme *you* have opinions on (Old Spice → masculinity and adolescence).
  - Commit to a premise you doubt and play it straight. Dos Equis: "a beer can't make you interesting".
- **BBH: "When the world zigs, zag."** The motto dates from the first Levi's ad, a black sheep walking against the flock ([Stash](https://www.stashmedia.tv/44-years-later-bbh-doubles-down-on-the-zag)).
  - **Laundrette** started from a cultural diagnosis: no UK youth tribe wore jeans. The reported result was +800% 501 sales ([Marketing Week](https://www.marketingweek.com/levis-laundrette-sales-boost/)).
  - Hegarty found **"Vorsprung durch Technik"** on old publicity at the Audi factory. It made a vague "Euro-car" German, and he thought it "could be very funny." Focus groups disliked it; the client kept it ([Creative Review](https://www.creativereview.co.uk/07-vorsprung-durch-technik/), [Marketing Week](https://www.marketingweek.com/audi-category-conventions/)).
  - Hegarty's rules ([Murrell](https://www.alexmurrell.co.uk/summaries/john-hegarty-hegarty-on-creativity)): "Collaboration can easily turn into consensus"; "Selling is one of the most undervalued skills of any creative career"; make the idea explainable in a short line. Against tissue meetings: picking a direction halfway "inhibits magic" ([Campaign TV](https://youtu.be/7TS76gaiM9Q)).
- **Droga5 (David Droga): think the process backwards.** First decide what the audience should feel or do at the end, then pick the format: a stunt, a film, a QR code. "There's always deep strategy behind what we do" (Cannes 2025 coverage via [markt-kom](https://www.markt-kom.com/en/?p=236792); [Creative Review](https://www.creativereview.co.uk/how-i-got-here-david-droga/)).
- **Mother London: no middlemen.** Founded because ideas got "lost in translation" between clients and makers, so creatives talk directly to clients. The agency's ordered "holy trinity" is (1) the best work, (2) have fun, (3) make a living ([shots](https://shots.net/news/view/95946-sitting-with-a-prophetic-agency-at-the-mother-of-all-tables), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mother_(advertising_agency))).
- **AMV BBDO (David Abbott): words as "the servants of the argument"**, "plain, simple and familiar". Example: The Economist's "I never read The Economist. Management trainee. Aged 42." ([Campaign](https://www.campaignlive.co.uk/article/best-ads-50-years-economist-poster-defined-brand-agency/1519024), [Campaign Brief](https://campaignbrief.com/?p=104992)).
- **Ogilvy: the Big Ideal.** Cultural tension × the brand's best self, completing "The world would be a better place if…" (Dove: a beauty industry that harms self-image; [Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/2679902/idealism-sells), [Sketchplanations](https://sketchplanations.com/the-big-ideal)).
- **Anomaly: solve the business problem.** Sometimes the answer isn't an ad (a pop-up store, say), and part of the fee is paid only on results ([Anomaly](https://anomaly.com/philosophy), [ICMR](https://icmrindia.org/casestudies/catalogue/Business%20Strategy/anomaly-not-just-another-ad-agency-case.htm)).
- **Apple marcom (Tor Myhren, ex-Grey).** Apple works with TBWA\Media Arts Lab plus a large in-house team ([CampaignAsia](https://www.campaignasia.com/article/apple-marketing-vp-simplicity-is-not-simple/452651), [WARC](https://www.warc.com/newsandopinion/news/apples-radical-candour-with-agencies/42287)):
  - Simplicity as "the brutal art of reduction".
  - "Radical candour": decisions are made at the table, with no meeting-after-the-meeting.
  - The AirPods holiday film had muffled sound for its first 40 s, "an idea no algorithm would have greenlit".
  - Ken Segall (*Insanely Simple*): "small groups of smart people", and the "simple stick" ([Penguin](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/310710/insanely-simple-by-ken-segall/), [New Statesman](https://newstatesman.com/?p=129738)).
- **Goodby Silverstein: deprivation research.** Jon Steel's groups went without milk for a week first; the agency filmed its own staff at an emptied fridge. Running out was the insight → "the consequences of going without" → *got milk?* ([MediaVillage](https://www.mediavillage.com/article/historys-moment-in-media-launching-the-got-milk-campaign/amp/), [Mental Floss](https://www.mentalfloss.com/food/got-milk-ad-campaign-turns-25)).

**Common thread** `[inf]`: each shop has a *device for escaping the obvious*: walk in stupid, zag, work backwards, deprivation, the cultural tension. Our agent needs one too, and it is §7 S1.

## 3. Account planning: how the insight is found

**What an insight is.**
- Pollard's Four Points ([template](https://www.selfstorming.com/downloads/framework-templates/4-Points-Strategy.pdf)):
  - **Problem:** the human tension behind the business challenge, in the customer's voice, with no brand name or metric.
  - **Insight:** "the unspoken why that reorganises the problem". A behavioural truth "a little too honest to say out loud".
  - **Advantage:** the specific thing the brand can do today that the insight makes relevant.
  - **Strategy:** one sentence under 15 words that collides the insight with the advantage.
- The most powerful insights are **tensions** between or within human and cultural truths. Example: teenagers want to belong *and* to stand out ([IAA](https://iaaglobal.org/leadership-home/blogs/brand-building/insight-and-conflict-resolution-the-universal-lynchpin-of-brand-relevance)).
- Counterweight from Ogilvy planner Zac Martin: "we unnecessarily fetishise insights". Sometimes clarity about the product is enough ([B&T](https://www.bandt.com.au/opinion/not-every-brief-opportunity)).

**The triangle of truths** ([Marketing Society](https://marketingsociety.com/the-gym/are-consumer-insights-really-dead)). Jack Daniel's:
- **Brand truth:** traditional methods, Lynchburg.
- **Human truth:** men in sedentary jobs want to reconnect with rugged roots.
- **Cultural truth:** nostalgia for the Old West individualist.

The idea lives where all three meet.

**Laddering (Reynolds & Gutman means-end chains).** Ask "why does that matter?" repeatedly: **attribute → functional consequence → emotional consequence → personal value**. MECCAS maps each rung to a part of the ad ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ladder_interview), [arXiv review](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1607.07796)).

**Insight-hunting methods (all doable by an LLM agent)** `[inf where unsourced]`:
1. **Blind brainstorm first.** Before any research, write down everything you already assume about the product, brand and market. "This is the only time you're actually in the same place as the consumer" (Trott, [Murrell](https://www.alexmurrell.co.uk/summaries/dave-trott-predatory-thinking)).
2. **Backward telescope.** The brand team sees the brand huge; to the consumer "the brand is a tiny part of their lives" (ibid.). Write the product's *real* size in the buyer's day.
3. **Deprivation.** What happens in the moment the product is missing (Got Milk)?
4. **Fan archaeology.** What do people keep, collect, mod, review or complain about (Lieberman's eBay/Etsy; review mining in `15` §1.2)?
5. **Category convention audit.** List what every competitor shows. That is the zig, and the zag is its negation (BBH).
6. **Cultural tension scan.** What's unresolved or argued about around this category right now (Big Ideal)?
7. **Assumption test.** "Know that you don't know" (Trott). "Never call a behaviour irrational until you really know what the person is trying to do" (Sutherland, via [Buteau](https://www.antoinebuteau.com/lessons-from-rory-sutherland/)).

## 4. The brief: rules and our template

**Rules from the sources**
- **Single-minded:** one proposition, the "single most persuasive or compelling statement we can make" ([PLU brief](https://www.plu.edu/marcom/wp-content/uploads/sites/705/2016/10/creative-brief.docx)), short enough for a billboard ([Arcalea](https://arcalea.com/tools/creative-brief)). "If the idea is not simple and single-minded, then perhaps the brief was not simple and single-minded either" ([IPA](https://ipa.co.uk/media/4580/judging_creative.pdf)).
- **Make the binary choices first.** Trott's three questions: brand share or market growth? New or current buyers? Product (rational) or brand (emotional)? The binary brief "forces people to make uncomfortable decisions before the ads are written" ([Murrell](https://www.alexmurrell.co.uk/summaries/dave-trott-creative-mischief)).
- **Specify the response, not the stimulus.** JWT's T-Plan (King, 1964) described the target, then the responses the ad should produce in sense, reason or emotion ([4A's](https://aaaa.org/blog/timeline-event/stephen-kings-t-plan-target-plan)).
- **Talk to everyone in the category, assume low involvement, and prefer distinctiveness to differentiation:** "think about how you can say something differently" (Binet & Carter, [Murrell](https://www.alexmurrell.co.uk/summaries/les-binet-and-sarah-carter-how-not-to-plan)).
- **The missing line:** "People must notice the advertising" (Trott, via [Growing Viral](https://growingviral.beehiiv.com/p/dave-trott-predatory-marketing-principles)).
- **Brief in person, and completely.** No "brilliantly brief" form that needs a 40-page appendix (Russell Davies, [blog](https://www.russelldavies.com/typepad/russelldavies.typepad.com/planning/2006/10/the_perfect_cre.html)). Write it from the consumer's "I", not the brand's "we" ([Goodreads blog](https://www.goodreads.com/author_blog_posts/15935354-why-i-is-better-than-we-on-a-creative-brief)).

**Studio brief (one page; fill every box; ≤250 words)** `[inf: synthesis of the above]`
```
BRIEF · <product> · v1
0  BUSINESS PROBLEM   To … (objective)  By … (strategy)        [Cooper]
1  BINARY CHOICES     share|growth · new|current · product|brand  [Trott]
2  WHO (a person, not a demo) + the moment they enter the category (CEP, see 01)
3  PROBLEM in their words ("I …")                             [Pollard]
4  INSIGHT: the unspoken why (a tension, too honest to say)
5  ADVANTAGE: the product truth that the insight makes matter (filmable)
6  PROPOSITION ≤7 words: single-minded, billboard-length
7  WHY BELIEVE: one mechanism, number, material or demo
8  DESIRED RESPONSE: they will notice → get → feel → do       [T-Plan + ICP]
9  ZIG: what every competitor shows → our ZAG                   [BBH]
10 BRAND CODES to use: colour, shape, sound, device (fame/uniqueness, §5)
11 TONE in 3 words + 1 film reference that is NOT an ad
12 MANDATORIES & NO-GOs (legal, claims, AI-label, platform spec)
13 HOW IT WORKS: salience/fame | emotional involvement | persuasion  [IPA]
```

## 5. How great creatives think: the canon, as operating rules

| Thinker | Core idea (sourced) | Operating rule for our agent `[inf]` |
|---|---|---|
| **James Webb Young** (1939/40) | An idea is a new combination of old elements; the skill is seeing relationships. Five steps: gather (specific + general), digest, incubate, the idea appears, then develop it and expose it to criticism ([Murrell](https://www.alexmurrell.co.uk/summaries/james-webb-young-a-technique-for-producing-ideas), [James Clear](https://jamesclear.com/book-summaries/a-technique-for-producing-ideas)) | Gather *general* material too (other arts, other categories). Force **collisions**: product truth × an unrelated domain. Separate generation and judgment into different passes |
| **Bill Bernbach** | "Our proper study is simple, timeless human truths" (quoted by Trott). Persuasion "is not a science but an art"; "imitation is commercial suicide"; "You must get attention… How you get attention is an ever-changing thing"; "Research can trap you in the past" ([B&T](https://www.bandt.com.au/ddb-co-founder-bill-bernbachs-best-quotes-campaigns-more-a-principle-is-not-a-principle-until-it-costs-you-money/)) | Execution *is* content: the same proposition can be dull or famous. Never copy a reference ad's idea, only its craft |
| **Rosser Reeves** (*Reality in Advertising*, 1961) | A USP must make a proposition ("buy this, get this specific benefit"); one competitors cannot or do not offer; strong enough to "move the mass millions" ([Bly](https://bly.com/Pages/DMNCOL12.htm)). Feldwick: no real evidence for it ([Dialogue](https://dialoguereview.com/usp-three-letters-one-dodgy-idea/)) | Use for the **activation cut** (offer/proof). For the brand film, prefer *distinctive* to *unique* (Binet & Carter) |
| **Dave Trott** | Upstream thinking: "taking a problem you can't solve, getting upstream of that problem, and changing it into a problem you can solve". **Impact → Communication → Persuasion**, "and it must work that way round". 89% of UK ads are "not noticed or remembered". Gestalt: one X among 19 circles takes ~50% of the mind. "If you control the context… you control the question" ([TEDx transcript](https://youtu.be/t1sYh9itUvs)) | Before ideating, write **3 reframes of the problem** (e.g., "make people want our water" → "make tap water feel like a risk at a dinner party"). Score impact first: does frame 0 look like nothing else in the feed? |
| **Rory Sutherland** | Psycho-logic: "logic always gets you to exactly the same place as your competitors"; "the opposite of a good idea can also be a good idea"; change perception rather than reality; costly signals: "what looks like waste is often a signal of commitment and confidence" ([Buteau](https://www.antoinebuteau.com/lessons-from-rory-sutherland/), [Invest Like the Best](https://investlikethebest.libsyn.com/rory-sutherland-moonshots-and-marketing-invest-like-the-best-ep191)) | Always generate **one inverted concept** (do the opposite of the category). For AI ads, visible craft is the costly signal: cheap-looking AI reads as low commitment `[inf]` |
| **Ogilvy** | "Unless your advertising contains a Big Idea, it will pass like a ship in the night." The five questions: Did it make me gasp when I first saw it? Do I wish I had thought of it myself? Is it unique? Does it fit the strategy to perfection? Could it be used for 30 years? "I doubt if more than one campaign in a hundred contains a big idea" ([Buteau](https://www.antoinebuteau.com/lessons-from-david-ogilvy/), [Copy Chief](https://copychief.com/big-idea/)) | The five questions become review items R1–R5 (§7 S4). "30 years" = is it a **platform** that can carry episode 2, 3, 4? |
| **Sharp / Romaniuk (Ehrenberg-Bass)** | Distinctive assets are measured by **fame** (share of category buyers who name the brand from the asset alone, unprompted) × **uniqueness** (few wrong guesses). Quadrants: Use-or-lose / Invest / Avoid (famous but shared) / Test-or-ignore. Prompted tests inflate fame by up to ~20 pts ([Marketing Architects](https://www.marketingarchitects.com/blog/v41-n3-your-brand-assets-may-be-working-against-you), [Contagious](https://contagious.com/io/article/jenni-romaniuk-on-distinctive-assets)). An ad about a CEP must be branded with a famous, unique asset or the link isn't built ([Glocalities](https://glocalities.com/news/glocalities-approach-to-growing-brands)) | New AI brands start in "Test/ignore", so **pick 1–2 assets and repeat them in every film** rather than inventing new ones per ad. Never build the idea on a category-shared code (a generic condensation shot = "Avoid") |
| **Binet & Field** | ~60:40 brand:activation is "a principle", not an iron rule, and varies by category (FS ~80:20); fame and emotional involvement drive long-term effects ([CMA](https://www.thecma.ca/topic/articles/2023/08/04/the-long-the-short-and-the-reality-of-b2b-marketing-part-1), [WARC](https://www.warc.com/content/paywall/article/event-reports/new-lessons-from-binet-and-field---media-in-focus-marketing-effectiveness-in-the-digital-era/en-gb/113293)) | Each storyline names its job: **brand film** (emotion, fame, broad reach) or **activation** (proof, offer). Our 3 storylines should include at least one pure brand film |

### The ladder: product truth → human truth → the idea
Studios climb from a product fact to something a person feels, then drop back down to a filmable proof. `[inf: built on Reynolds & Gutman laddering + the triangle of truths]`

| Rung | Question | Example (fictional "NORDA" glacier water) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Product truth | What is literally true and filmable? | Filtered through 400 m of glacier ice; ships at 2 °C |
| 2 Functional consequence | So what does it do? | Colder and cleaner-tasting than tap |
| 3 Emotional consequence | So how does that feel? | The one moment of the day that feels untouched |
| 4 Human truth / tension | What's the unspoken why? | "Everything I consume has been through a hundred hands" |
| 5 Cultural truth | Why now? | Fatigue with processed, over-handled everything |
| **Idea** | Collide 4 with 1 | "The only thing nobody has touched": a world where every object carries fingerprints, except the bottle |
| Proof back down | Which shot proves rung 1? | Macro: a drop sliding over the logo; no fingerprints, only frost |

**Rule:** an idea that stops at rung 2 is a demo, and one that never returns to rung 1 is a vague mood film. Great ads touch rung 4 *and* rung 1 `[inf]`.

### Judging and killing ideas: what CDs actually ask
- **Envy:** "the envy factor is often how I judge creative ideas"; the first reaction is emotional, so test it afterwards ([Creative Review](https://www.creativereview.co.uk/tools-to-evaluate-creative-ideas/)). This matches Ogilvy's "wish I'd thought of it".
- **Simon Richings (D&AD):** Why would anyone care? Would you do it yourself? Does it hit the goal even if it goes off-brief? Is it right for the brand? Originality matters least, and a gut "yes" overrides the rest ([D&AD](https://www.dandad.org/insights/learning/how-to-evaluate-an-idea-new-blood-advice)).
- **Idea vs execution:** "The idea in every Tango ad is the quest for the full hit of the fruit… every ad is a different expression." If you can't separate the idea from its execution, there may be no idea ([IPA](https://ipa.co.uk/media/4580/judging_creative.pdf)).
- **IPA execution checks:** distinctive, relevant, credible, **campaignable** across media, **durable** over repeat viewing, deliverable on budget (ibid.).
- **"Would I remember it tomorrow?"** We found no source for this exact phrase. It is Trott's Impact test (89% not remembered), and we operationalise it as a next-day recall check `[inf]`.
- **Kill fast.** Chasing certainty means "wasting creatives' time working on ideas you should have killed" (Barral). Toby Talbot: distance yourself from your own idea ([LBB](https://lbbonline.com/news/toby-talbot-award-juror-advice)).
- **Don't build by committee.** "Collaboration can easily turn into consensus" (Hegarty). Sutherland calls slow committee approval the method by which "the idea bleeds to death slowly" (IPA guide).
- **Bold is safer than it looks:** no campaign in 30+ years of IPA data showed a negative sales effect; indifference is the bigger risk (Binet & Carter, [Murrell](https://www.alexmurrell.co.uk/summaries/les-binet-and-sarah-carter-how-not-to-plan)).

## 6. Presenting concepts and handling feedback

**Formats, from cheapest to closest to film** ([Animatic Media](https://animaticmedia.com/service/boardomatics), [LBB/Bandido](https://lbbonline.com/news/Bandido-mood-film-studio-pitch-maqueta)):

| Format | What it is | It sells… |
|---|---|---|
| Script + key line | Words only | The idea |
| Scamps/key frames | 3–6 frames | The image and the twist |
| Mood board / photomatic | Stills on a timeline | The feeling |
| **Ripomatic / mood film** | A cut of existing footage. "Almost unthinkable to win a pitch without" one in Spain | Tone and pace (but carries rights risk) |
| Boardomatic | Drawn frames + VO + temp music | The story |
| Animatic | Moving frames, real timing | The emotional read; production sign-off |

For us `[inf]`: our stills plus an ElevenLabs animatic (creative-brain §7.5) *is* a pitch-grade boardomatic or animatic, made with our own stills and never with ripped footage.

**How the presentation should run (sources)**
- **Restate the agreed brief first.** The client and the agency must both judge against it ([IPA](https://ipa.co.uk/media/4580/judging_creative.pdf), [Design Week](https://www.designweek.co.uk/business-advice-impress-presenting-work-clients/)).
- **Show one execution at a time** ([PR Daily](https://prdaily.com/?p=324036)).
- **Agree the format beforehand:** how many ideas, who presents, and whether the meeting is a tissue session or a single recommendation. There is "no right or wrong way" (IPA). Mother puts creatives in the room; other agencies route everything through account people.
- **Pressure-test the script with an outsider.** Trott has someone other than its author present it: confusion exposes an unclear idea ([Murrell](https://www.alexmurrell.co.uk/summaries/dave-trott-creative-mischief)).
- **Explain how the idea will work.** By salience/fame, emotional involvement, persuasion or price (IPA). Name its "sort": demo, comparison, problem–solution, slice of life, borrowed interest…

**How good clients give feedback, and how we should ask for it** (IPA guide):
- The brief's author attends, and the senior person orchestrates the responses.
- Use open questions: "What inspired this? How would it work elsewhere? How could we make it bigger?"
- Use the four-part response: "What inspires me… what works… what's missing… what could make it bigger".
- **P&G's "huddle":** the agency leaves the room, the client team agrees one view, and one person debriefs. Written feedback follows that is honest, objective, detailed against each objective, constructive, and "marshalled by one decision maker".
- **Before refining, ask: "Why change anything? Will these small changes make a big difference?"** Rejecting the idea may mean rejecting the strategy, which means a re-brief, not a tweak.

Feedback should **define the problem, not prescribe the fix** ([ANA](https://www.ana.net/magazines/show/id/forward-2025-10-cella-the-art-of-creative-feedback), [Chapter Three](https://www.chapterthree.com/blog/how-to-give-design-feedback-like-a-pro)). The useful part of "it's off" is the *why* ([The Drum](https://www.thedrum.com/news/2023/05/03/how-should-agencies-respond-negative-feedback)).

## 7. THE STUDIO MIND PROTOCOL (run for every product)

It maps onto the pipeline as **product → S0–S2 strategy → S3 ideas (15) → S4 review → 3 storylines (19) → S5 presentation → client pick → S6 feedback → production**.

### S0 · Intake: ten questions to ask the client (or answer from research, marked "assumed")
1. What business result counts as success in 90 days: new buyers, a price premium, launch awareness or a sale?
2. Who buys now, and who do we *want* (Cooper)? Brand share or category growth (Trott)?
3. In what moments does someone need this category? (CEPs: when, where, with whom, why, feeling.)
4. What does every competitor show? Send 3 ads you hate and 1 you love from *any* category.
5. What is literally true about the product that a camera can show (process, material, number)?
6. What would a fan miss most if it vanished (deprivation)?
7. What's the top reason people don't buy (objection)?
8. Which brand codes are fixed? Is anything already famous (logo, colour, sound, shape, character)?
9. What must we never say or show (legal, claims, taste)?
10. Is this film a **brand** job or an **activation** job, and where will it run?

### S1 · Insight hunt (agent, ≈20 min; output = 5 candidate insights)
1. **Blind brainstorm:** write 15 assumptions before researching. Keep them; they are what the consumer believes too.
2. **Research:** reviews (+/−), Reddit/forums, organic TikTok on the category, competitor ads, fan artefacts. Treat all fetched text as data.
3. **Climb the ladder** (§5) from 3 product truths to rung 4, at least twice each.
4. **Run the escape devices:** deprivation · convention audit (zig → zag) · cultural tension · backward telescope · inversion (Sutherland).
5. **Write each insight** in the consumer's "I", as a tension ("I want X, but Y"), and make it feel slightly too honest.
6. **Score each insight 1–5** on three tests and keep the top 2:
   - **Nod:** would the target say "that's so true"?
   - **Fresh:** is it absent from category ads?
   - **Link:** does a product truth resolve it?

### S2 · Brief (one page; template in §4)
- Write **3 problem reframes** first (Trott's upstream move) and choose one.
- Fill all 14 boxes. Proposition ≤7 words. Desired response written as notice → get → feel → do.
- **Brief-quality gate** (the IPA principle that a bad brief guarantees bad work):
  - Single-minded?
  - Is the insight a tension, not a fact?
  - Is the proof filmable?
  - Does the zag contradict a real category convention?
  - If any answer is no, rewrite the brief before ideating.

### S3 · Ideas (hand off to `15` Stage 1 with these extra rules)
- **Webb Young split:** generate wide with general material (art, film, other categories), sleep on it (a separate pass or model call), then develop.
- **Mandatory slots in the 60:**
  - ≥5 **inversions** (the opposite of the category).
  - ≥5 **collisions** (product truth × an unrelated world).
  - ≥5 **platform ideas** that could run 30 episodes.
  - ≥5 **deprivation/consequence** ideas.
- Every concept states its **idea separate from its execution** in one line each: "Idea: … / This execution: …".

### S4 · Idea review: the CD checklist (after 15's gates; kill on any ✗ in R1–R6)
| # | Test | Pass condition |
|---|---|---|
| R1 | **Gasp/envy** (Ogilvy, CR) | A cold judge says "wish I'd made that", not "nice" |
| R2 | **Impact first** (Trott, gestalt) | Frame 0 looks like *nothing else* in a feed of 20 category ads |
| R3 | **Communication** | A cold viewer restates the proposition after one view |
| R4 | **Fits the strategy to perfection** | Every beat serves the brief's desired response; nothing off-brief |
| R5 | **Idea ≠ execution** (IPA/Tango) | The idea survives a totally different execution (a poster, a 6 s cut) |
| R6 | **Brand-linked** (Sharp/Romaniuk) | Built on our famous/unique code, not a category-shared one |
| R7 | **Platform/30 years** | Can make episodes 2 and 3 without repeating the joke |
| R8 | **Ladder touch** | Hits rung 4 (human truth) *and* rung 1 (filmable proof) |
| R9 | **Next-day recall** `[inf]` | A synthetic viewer shown 10 ads recalls the brand *and* the moment |
| R10 | **Costly signal** `[inf]` | Craft looks expensive and intentional; no "AI slop" tells |
| R11 | **Bold check** | If it scares us slightly, good. If it could offend, run the Crush test (`19`) |
| R12 | **Swap test** (creative-brain) | It dies with a competitor's logo |

Review protocol: judge alone first, write the emotional reaction before any reasons, then reason (IPA "question yourself"; avoids Asch-style conformity). One decider gives a **verdict**: keep, push (good idea, weak execution) or kill. Record **why** in one line. Never "fix" a killed idea into a compromise.

### S5 · Presentation to the client (per storyline; extends `15` §3.1)
1. **Brief recap (1 slide, shared by all three):** problem → insight → proposition → desired response. The client approves this *before* seeing the work, so the work is judged against it.
2. **Per storyline, in this order:**
   - (a) name + one-line idea;
   - (b) "the sort of idea" and how it works (fame / emotion / persuasion);
   - (c) the ladder in 3 lines (product truth → human truth → twist);
   - (d) 3 key frames (hook / turn / payoff);
   - (e) treatment page: casting or no people, camera, light/colour world, edit rhythm, music and sound, brand code per shot;
   - (f) a 20 s **animatic** with VO and temp music, labelled "temp" where it is;
   - (g) episode 2 and 3 in one line each (platform proof);
   - (h) risk, cost and AI-difficulty.
3. **Order:** safe → balanced → bold. Show **one at a time**.
4. **Recommend one, with a reason.** Rank is revealed after the client's first reaction (`15`).
5. **Ask for feedback in the IPA four-part form** (inspires / works / missing / bigger), consolidated by **one decider**, in writing.

### S6 · Handling feedback
1. **Classify each note:**
   - **Strategy** (wrong problem/audience) → re-brief at S2.
   - **Idea** (doesn't land) → back to S3/S4.
   - **Execution** (tone, casting, shot) → fix in the treatment or prompts.
   - **Taste** (personal preference) → discuss; change only if it's cheap and harmless.
2. **Translate each prescription into a problem.** "Make the logo bigger" becomes "brand not noticed early enough", and we pick the best fix, which may be a stronger brand code at 0–2 s.
3. **Ask "why change anything?"** Push back once, with a reason, on any change that kills the twist or the swap test. Then do what the client decides: the client owns the brand.
4. **Log every note** with its class and outcome in the lab NOTEBOOK, so recurring notes become rules in this file.
