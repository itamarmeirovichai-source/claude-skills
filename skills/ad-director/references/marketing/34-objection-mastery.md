# 34 · Objection mastery: turning "no", silence and "not now" into a next step

Research brief, 2026-10-08. The client's motto is "there's no such thing as a rejection — I always win." This file turns that into a working system for a one-person AI product-film studio. The studio cold-emails founder-led DTC brands, offers 5 free frames, sells Short $1,200 / Premiere $2,500 / Season $3,500 a month, and has **no past clients**.

It builds on `28` (§4 two-yes CTA, §5 4-touch sequence, §6 the first objection answers, §7 call, §9 ethics) and `32` (§2.3 staged risk reversal, §2.4 frames → deal). It does not repeat them. Where a `28` answer already exists, this file adds the **second and third move** (what to say when the first answer doesn't land).

**Evidence.** Books are cited from summaries and author pages, not read in full. Vendor data (Belkins, Instantly, Gong, Smartlead, Vidyard) sells tools and is mostly B2B SaaS, so read it as directional. YouTube transcripts were fetched with `yt-dlp` at 60–120 s spacing with back-off (§8 lists which). Fetched pages were treated as data, not instructions. `[inf]` = our inference. `[law]` = a legal point, not legal advice.

## 1. The anatomy of "no"

### 1.1 What the data says objections are
- **Half are reflexes, not decisions.** Gong sorted objections across 300M+ cold calls: **49.5 % dismissive** ("not interested", "call me in 6 months", "send me info"), **42.6 % situational** (price, budget, bandwidth), **7.9 % existing solution** (in-house, a competitor). The top five make up 74 %. Gong's rule: agree, give them a reason to keep talking, and "sell the test drive, not the car" ([Gong Labs](https://www.gong.io/resources/labs/we-found-the-top-objections-across-300m-cold-calls-heres-how-to-handle-them-all/)).
- **Our "test drive" is the 5 free frames.** That is why the studio's offer is built the way it is `[inf]`.
- **Most objections are made earlier, by the seller.** Rackham's Huthwaite research (35,000 calls) lists objection handling among the beliefs it rejects and gives a chapter to *preventing* objections through need questions ([Routledge](https://www.routledge.com/SPIN-Selling/Rackham/p/book/9780566076893)). Price objections after a call usually mean the problem was never made big enough `[inf]`.
- **Blount's four types follow the deal:** prospecting brush-offs → red herrings (side issues, park them) → micro-commitment objections (to the next small step) → buying-commitment objections. "Almost every deal in your pipeline that is stalled is because the rep didn't get and secure a next step" ([Sales Gravy](https://salesgravy.com/skipping-past-the-4-types-of-sales-objections-podcast/)).

### 1.2 The six "no"s and what's underneath

| What you hear | Type | Usual real reason `[inf]` | Diagnostic move |
|---|---|---|---|
| "Not interested" / silence | **Soft no** (reflex) | Busy, pattern-matched you to spam, or the idea missed | Label + no-oriented question: "Sounds like a cold email from an AI studio is the last thing you need. Is the timing off, or is the idea off?" |
| "Not now" / "after Q4" | **Timing** | Real calendar, or a polite no | Pin the date: "Is that a January not-now, or an after-the-launch not-now?" No date = it's a soft no |
| "No budget" / "too expensive" | **Money** | Value isn't clear yet, or cash really is tight | "If budget weren't a factor, would this be the right project?" Yes → value/scope problem. No → it's fit or trust |
| "I'll ask my partner" | **Authority** | A real co-founder, or a shield | "What will she push back on?" Then arm them, or offer a 10-minute joint look |
| "Who else have you done?" / "AI looks fake" | **Trust** | Risk: no proof, and AI fear | Accusation audit first, then make the risk theirs to test (frames, balance on approval) |
| "We're fine" / "in-house" | **Need** | No pain felt, or status quo works | Poke-the-bear question about a gap they haven't priced: "When the hero ad fatigues, what replaces it?" |

**Hormozi's lens:** people blame **circumstances** (time, money, fit), then **other people** (partner, team), then **themselves** (fear of a bad decision), and each layer has to be peeled before they can decide ([The Game recap](https://podup.substack.com/p/peeling-back-the-onion-of-blame)). Recaps list the five surface forms as time, value, fit, authority, avoidance. His stance: "I want you to decide for you, not for me" ([summary](https://podcastsrecapped.substack.com/p/harsh-truths-about-human-nature-alex)). Many "budget" answers are really layer three: "I've been burned by an agency" `[inf]`.

### 1.3 The diagnosis toolkit, by practitioner

**Chris Voss** (*Never Split the Difference*, 2016):
- **No-oriented questions.** "Yes" makes people guard; "no" makes them feel safe. In the Swarfcast clip he describes a Georgetown student's side-by-side script test in which the "no" version "performed 23 percent more effectively" ([transcript](https://youtu.be/FVVOB7vqYAU)). That is one evening, one anecdote, not a study.
  - Examples: "Is now a bad time?" "Is it a ridiculous idea to…?" "Have you given up on…?"
- **"Have you given up on this project?"** is his one-line email for a stalled deal: nobody likes admitting they gave up, so it either restarts the thread or gets you out of limbo ([Big Think](https://bigthink.com/videos/chris-voss-on-walking-away-from-a-negotiation/)).
- **"That's right" vs "you're right".** "That's right" means they feel understood. "You're right" is "a brush off that we use when we want someone to leave us alone" ([Mindtools](https://www.mindtools.com/blog/run-negotiation-like-fbi/)). Summarise their situation until you hear "that's right".
- **Labels** ("It seems like…") and an **accusation audit** (say the worst they think of you before they do, playfully). Then silence: "wait six seconds". A slightly wrong label is fine, because "people love to correct you" and the correction is the real story ([Black Swan transcript](https://youtu.be/ZG0S-4oNNuI)).

**Sandler, negative reverse:** stay one step *behind* the buyer's enthusiasm, so they pull towards you. "Is it fair to assume this isn't a priority?" makes a bluffing prospect correct you or gives you an honest no ([HubSpot](https://blog.hubspot.com/sales/negative-reverse-selling), [Sandler](https://uk.sandler.com/?p=17270)). Not to be confused with "negative selling" (fear) `[inf]`.

**Jeb Blount, the Ledge → Disrupt → Ask turnaround** for prospecting objections. His own example: "That's perfect [ledge]… because none of my customers do outsourcing [disrupt]… Wouldn't it make sense for us to take a few minutes to see if this could help you? [ask]" ([Ask Jeb](https://salesgravy.com/?p=17841)).
- The ledge is a rehearsed, calm first phrase that buys your brain a beat.
- The disrupt breaks the pattern the prospect expects from a salesperson.
- Write one ledge per top objection and drill them aloud ([summary](https://pulserevops.com/knowledge/bs0254); secondary, unconfirmed against the book).
- Don't argue with a reflex. Arguing makes it their primary concern ([Ask Jeb](https://salesgravy.com/?p=17841)).
- In the same episode ([transcript](https://youtu.be/EppsCQnYWu0)) he warns that leading with "no risk, you don't pay until…" raises the antenna ("does that sound too good to be true?… are you creating objections by using that language"). Save the risk terms for the price conversation. Lead with the problem, and use "narrators" (staff who'll tell you what's really happening) before the decision-maker.

**Josh Braun, detach from the outcome:** the goal is to find out whether a problem exists, not to book the meeting. "If your intent is to overcome objections and book meetings with everyone, you are going to… sound in ways that feel rushed." Reps with the same talk track opened more conversations when they were slower and "relaxed and chill". Pause two beats before answering. For "send me information": "Sure. Just so I don't do you a disservice by sending you irrelevant information, would it be okay if I asked you a couple of questions first?" ([transcript](https://youtu.be/GwNPM6YcFvI)). Mirror "not interested" to learn whether it's relevance, timing or a bad moment; ask one sharp question that shows a gap they haven't noticed ("poke the bear") ([Cognism](https://cognism.com/blog/cold-calling-examples), [Something Inc](https://somethinginc.com/blog/poke-the-bear-vs-ai-volume-outbound-debate/)).

**Alex Hormozi, CLOSER** (secondary summaries; [11x](https://www.11x.ai/guides/alex-hormozi-sales-training-method)): **C**larify why they're here → **L**abel the problem ("So it sounds like… does that sound right?") → **O**verview past pain (what they tried, how it went) → **S**ell the vacation, not the flight (under 3 minutes) → **E**xplain away concerns → **R**einforce the decision after the yes. Use it as the frame-call skeleton (§2.7).

**Grant Cardone, filtered.**
- **Keep:**
  - Most objections are complaints, not real objections. Acknowledge, don't over-justify ([Shortform](https://www.shortform.com/summary/the-closers-survival-guide-pbp18613-b-summary-grant-cardone)).
  - Agree first. His handout sequence is listen → acknowledge → trial close → isolate the real objection → handle → close ([LightspeedVT handout](https://courseware.lightspeedvt.com/files/5/115A8903D0440D9BDF00C75B9CF661FB/9%2E%2DObjection%2DHandling.pdf)).
  - Show price early and spend the time justifying it ([NBC](https://www.nbcnews.com/news/amp/wbna45378976)). That fits our "price at ~75 % of the call" only loosely, so we keep "no surprise price" `[inf]`.
- **Drop:**
  - Pressure closes.
  - "Never take no."
  - Volume-at-all-costs follow-up.
  - Unsourced statistics such as "80 % of sales need 5+ follow-ups" (traced at best to a 1942 survey of under 40 people, [Usecarly](https://www.usecarly.com/blog/sales-follow-up-statistics/); "nobody can trace that stat", [Prospeo](https://prospeo.io/s/follow-up-statistics)).

## 2. Turning each "no" into a next step

**The rule** `[inf]`: every reply, including a no, gets one of four outcomes logged: **sale, referral, future date, learning**. If you can't name which one you got, you haven't finished the conversation.

### 2.1 The downsell ladder (cut scope, never price for the same scope)
Season $3,500/mo → Premiere $2,500 → Short $1,200 → **Pilot $750** (one 15 s hero + one 6 s cut, one ratio, one round; 100 % credited to a Premiere booked within 30 days) → **frames only, held for a future date** → **referral**. The $750 floor is from `28` §6. The credit makes the pilot a step up, not a discount `[inf]`.

A paid pilot beats a second free round: a small paid "yes" is the classic foot-in-the-door (Freedman & Fraser 1966), and paying clients engage. No study compares paid pilots with free trials for creative services, so this is `[inf]`.

### 2.2 Risk reversal
Use `32` §2.3 word for word: frames free → 50 % deposit → balance only on approval of the final cut. Add only one lever: **"If frame 1 doesn't look like your product, I stop and you've lost nothing."** Never "100 % risk-free" and never a ROAS guarantee. **When:** only once trust or price comes up, never in message 1 (Blount, §1.3). The free frames are the opener; the balance-on-approval terms are the closer `[inf]`.

### 2.3 Changing the decision-maker
- From a manager: "Who besides you would want to see the frames before anything's spent?" Then offer to make the frames *for* them to forward, with a 3-line summary they can paste.
- From a founder who defers to a partner: "What will [partner] push back on first?" Answer that objection in writing, in advance.
- Go around nobody. A cc to the founder over a manager's head burns the thread `[inf]`.

### 2.4 Timing plays (real dates only)
- **Q4 is real this year.** Meta's 2026 holiday notes: Black Friday is **27 November**, "only 28 days until Christmas"; "start ads by mid-October to let Meta learn before the peak" ([Social Media Today](https://socialmediatoday.com/news/meta-shares-holiday-2026-tips-for-small-businesses/826785)). CPMs typically rise 20–35 % on peak days ([Flighted](https://www.flighted.co/blog/black-friday-cyber-monday-meta-ads-strategy-for-dtc-brands)).
- **The honest maths** `[inf]`: a Premiere takes 7–8 working days. To give an ad 2 weeks to learn before 27 Nov, it must be live by ~13 Nov, so kick-off by **~3 Nov**. Say exactly that, once, with the reasoning. It is a real constraint, not a countdown.
- Other real anchors: Valentine's (fragrance, jewelry, chocolate), Mother's Day, a product launch they announced, a new SKU on their site.
- After 13 Nov, flip it: "Q4 is set. Want frames ready for the January reset, when CPMs drop?" (lower post-holiday CPMs are common vendor advice, unverified) `[inf]`.

### 2.5 Break-up, re-engagement and referral
- **Break-up email** (`28` §5 day 14 is ours). Vendor data puts final "close the loop" emails at ~10–14 % replies, above mid-sequence steps ([Optifai via search](https://optif.ai/learn/questions/follow-up-email-response-rate/), [BounceZero](https://bouncezero.io/cold-email-reply-rate-guide-2026)). These are unaudited, but consistent: a clear last message frees people to answer `[inf]`.
- **30/60/90 re-engagement** (only for prospects who replied, or asked for a date):
  - **Day 30:** new value, no ask. One frame from a *new* spec in their niche, or a note on their new launch.
  - **Day 60:** a fresh idea for a new product of theirs and a two-yes CTA.
  - **Day 90:** "Have you set product film aside for this year?" (Voss), then dormant. Silent cold prospects get **no** re-engagement; they get a single new first email after 6+ months, only if there's a real trigger `[inf]`.
- **The referral ask** (after any clear "no"): "Totally fine. Is there a founder you know whose product sells on texture or scent who'd want the frames?" Ask once. Founders know founders.

### 2.6 Converting ghosting
Ghosting is usually priority, not rejection `[inf]`. The sequence: (1) value nudge, 3 days: the one frame tweak they mentioned; (2) Voss one-liner, 7 days: "Have you given up on the [product] film?"; (3) a channel switch, day 10: a short IG or LinkedIn note; (4) close the loop, day 14, with a date. Belkins recommends switching to LinkedIn after a silent 3-step email sequence rather than sending a fourth email ([Belkins](https://belkins.io/blog/sales-follow-up-statistics)).

### 2.7 The "free frames delivered → close" sequence
1. **Before:** book a 15-minute slot when they say yes to frames ("Easier Thursday 10 or Friday 2 to see them?"). If they refuse a call, send them as a private link and ask for a 1–10 score.
2. **Accusation audit (30 s):** "You're probably thinking: new AI studio, no clients, this'll look plastic. Fair — judge the frames."
3. **Show, then stop talking.** "What jumps out?" (Clarify.)
4. **Label** their reaction until "that's right" (Label).
5. **Two questions on pain:** "What runs in that slot today? What happens when it fatigues?" (Overview.)
6. **Sell the vacation in under a minute:** "Frame 3 becomes a 20 s hero and six cuts live before [real date]." (Sell.)
7. **Price, top-down, recommend one, silence** (`28` §8). Concerns → §3 scripts (Explain).
8. **Two-yes close:** "Premiere or the Short to test first?" If no: pilot → future date → referral.
9. **Same-day proposal**, then reinforce at signing: kickoff date, what happens on day 2 (Reinforce).

## 3. Scripts: 18 scenarios, escalating responses

Format: **R1** = diagnose (label, no-oriented question), **R2** = reframe or a smaller step, **R3** = a graceful exit that still earns a date, referral or learning. E = email, D = IG/LinkedIn DM, C = call. Move to the next R only if the previous one got a reply that isn't a stop; silence follows the §4 cadence instead. Fill [brackets]; keep their words.

**1. Silence after message 1 (E).** Use `28` §5 steps 2–4. Then switch channel once:
- D: "Hi [name], sent you a film idea for [product] by email last week ([the guava splitting]). Easier to reply here?"
- After that: stop. Log the learning (line 1 or the idea missed).

**2. "Not interested." (E/D)**
- R1: "Fair, appreciate the straight answer. Was it the idea, or is film just not on the list this year?"
- R2 (if "the idea"): "Got it. If it had been [scent turning into the room] instead, would that have landed, or is it the whole AI thing?"
- R3: "All good. I won't follow up. If a founder friend ever needs a product film, I'd be grateful for the intro."
- C (ledge-disrupt-ask): "Totally fair… most founders I call aren't looking for video. The ones who say yes just want to see their product on screen for free. Is it a crazy idea to send you 5 frames anyway?"

**3. "Not now / maybe next quarter." (E)**
- R1: "Understood. Is that a January not-now, or an after-the-[launch] not-now?"
- R2: "Then I'll make the frames whenever suits. Do you want them ready to look at in January, or should I hold off completely until then?"
- R3: "I'll write on [date] and not before. If it's a no by then, just say so — that's a fine answer too."

**4. "No budget." (E/C)**
- R1: "Totally fair. If budget weren't a factor, would a [product] film even be on the list?"
- R2 (yes): "Then let's make it small. The pilot is $750: one 15 s hero, one cut, and it's fully credited if you go bigger within 30 days. Pilot now, or frames now and the budget talk in [month]?"
- R3: "Got it. When does the next budget window open? I'll come back then, with frames if you want them."

**5. "Too expensive" (after a quote). (E/C)**
- R1: "It sounds like $2,500 is more than you pictured. What were you expecting to spend?"
- R2: "We can cut scope, not quality. The Short is $1,200: one hero and three cuts. You lose the six variants. Does that version fit?"
- R3 (Sandler): "Honestly, it may not be worth it for you yet. Is it fair to say film isn't a priority until [launch]?" Let them correct you, or accept it and log a date.

**6. "Send me more info." (E)**
- R1: `28` §6 (one 40-second reel link, then the frames ask).
- R2: "Rather than a deck, can I show you one frame of [product]? It'll tell you more than anything I'd send."
- R3: "Sure — and so it's not a black hole, what would you need to see to decide either way?"

**7. "We do it in-house / we have UGC / we have an agency." (E/C)**
- R1 (`28` §6), then: "What's the hardest kind of shot for your team to get right now?"
- R2: "That's perfect, because I don't replace that. I make the shots a phone can't: [the pour in macro]. They run next to your UGC as the hero. Want the frames aimed at that gap?"
- R3: "Makes sense. Can I check in when your next launch is in production? Even a 'no' then is useful to me."

**8. "AI looks fake / our customers hate AI." (E/C)**
- R1 (accusation audit): "You're right to worry. Most AI ads look waxy. Is it the product looking fake you're worried about, or customers knowing it's AI?"
- R2 (fake look): "That's exactly what the frames test. If your bottle doesn't look like your bottle, we stop there." (customers knowing): "Fair. Meta may label it; I'll flag every generated shot. Many product-only films don't read as AI at all, but you decide from the frames."
- R3: "Then it's not the moment, and I'd rather you be sure. Can I send one frame in the spring to see if the bar's moved?"

**9. "Who have you worked with?" / no proof. (E/C)**
- R1: `28` §6 (honest: no paid clients, spec films for invented brands).
- R2: "So the risk is mine, not yours: free frames, then you pay the balance only when you approve the final cut. You'd also be one of five founding clients (one extra variant for a 15-minute debrief)."
- R3: "Totally fair to want a track record. Can I come back once I have three live projects to show you?" (Honest only if you will.)

**10. "I need to talk to my co-founder." (E/C)**
- R1: "Of course. What do you think she'll push back on first?"
- R2: "Want me to write a 3-line summary you can forward, with frame 3 attached? Or a 10-minute look with both of you?"
- R3: "What's a good day for me to check what she thought?" Log that date.

**11. Wrong person / "talk to our marketing lead." (E)**
- R1: "Thanks, that helps. Who'd be the right person, and is it OK to say you pointed me their way?"
- R2 (to the new person): "[Founder] suggested I reach out. I made a film idea for [product]: [one line]. Want the 5 frames for [A] or [B]?"
- R3 (no name given): "No problem. Is there a better way to reach the team than this inbox?"

**12. Ghosted after the frames. (E/D)**
- R1 (day 3): "Tweaked frame 3 as you'd asked ([lighter label]). Better, or worse?"
- R2 (day 7): "Have you given up on the [product] film?"
- R3 (day 14): "I'll close this out. The frames are yours to keep. Should I check back after [Q4/launch], or leave it there?"

**13. Ghosted after the proposal. (E)**
- R1 (day 2): "Any part of the proposal unclear, or is it the price that needs a rethink?"
- R2 (day 6): "Have you set the film aside for this quarter?"
- R3 (day 12): "Releasing the [date] slot I'd held for you. If timing changes, the proposal holds until [date+14]." (Only if the slot is real.)

**14. "Let me think about it." (C/E)**
- R1: "Of course. Usually that means one thing's not sitting right. Is it the price, the look, or the timing?"
- R2: "If it's [the look], we can do one more frame before you decide. If it's [timing], the Short fits [date]."
- R3: "Take the time. Shall I check in Thursday, or would you rather message me when you've decided?"

**15. "The frames aren't quite us." (E/C)**
- R1: "Thanks for being straight. What's off — the light, the world, or the product itself?"
- R2: "One redirect is on me. If it's still not you, we stop there, no hard feelings."
- R3: "Understood. Could you tell me what would have felt like you? It helps me more than you'd think." (Learning logged.)

**16. "Can you do it cheaper?" (E/C)**
- R1: "It sounds like the number matters more than the scope here. Is that right?"
- R2: "I keep the price fixed and move the scope: the pilot at $750, or the Short at $1,200. Which is closer?"
- R3: "I'd rather not discount, it wouldn't be fair to the next brand. If $750 is still too much, let's talk again when [budget window]."

**17. "Our current ads work fine." (E/C)**
- R1 (Sandler): "Then you probably don't need me. How long has the current hero been running?"
- R2 (poke the bear): "When it fatigues, what's next in line?"
- R3: "Got it. Can I send one frame when you launch the next product? That's the moment film usually earns its keep."

**18. "Stop" / "remove me" / "unsubscribe" (any wording, any channel).**
- Only response, same day: "Done — you won't hear from me again. Sorry for the noise."
- Add them to the do-not-contact list on **every** channel. No R2, no referral ask. CAN-SPAM requires opt-outs to be honoured within 10 business days ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business)) `[law]`.

## 4. Response-rate maximisation across the funnel

### 4.1 What the data says about touches
| Finding | Source | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Replies are spread out: step 1 gives 41.4 %, steps 2–6 give 58.6 %. Step 3 gives 35.6 % of email meetings. "3–5 steps" is the sweet spot; "beyond step 5… diminishing returns set in fast" | [Belkins](https://belkins.io/blog/sales-follow-up-statistics) | 4 email steps max (`28` §5) |
| "Sending 4+ emails in a sequence more than triples your unsubscribe rate" (Belkins, 16.5M emails). Founders' replies peak at follow-up 2 (6.94 %) and drop to 3.01 % by follow-up 4 | Belkins 2025 via [Prospeo](https://prospeo.io/s/follow-up-statistics) | For founders, never a 5th email |
| Cold calls connect 18.6 % vs LinkedIn 5.04 % vs email 0.45 % (meetings per prospect reached) | [Belkins](https://belkins.io/blog/sales-follow-up-statistics) | A phone/DM touch is worth trying when the number is public |
| Multichannel lift claims range from +37 % to +287 %; Snov.io found multichannel won in Brazil but **email-only did better in the US** (53.9M emails) | [Snov.io](https://snov.io/blog/web-summit-multichannel-outreach-research/), [Cleverly](https://www.cleverly.co/blog/multichannel-outreach-vs-cold-email-only) | Add channels to be *found*, not to be loud. Test |
| Personalised video: +92 % replies in a 9-person, 120-video test; 12 % reply rate in a 45k-video case study | [ReviewTrackers](https://www.casestudies.com/company/vidyard/case-study/reviewtrackers-doubles-new-meetings-in-60-days-with-vidyard), [1Huddle](https://www.casestudies.com/company/vidyard/case-study/1huddle-achieves-a-68-email-open-rate-with-vidyard) | Our version is a frame of *their* product, not a face video |

### 4.2 The 21-day cadence (fit 8+ prospects; fit 6–7 skip the video and DM)
| Day | Channel | Touch |
|---|---|---|
| −3 | LinkedIn / IG | Follow, and one genuine comment on a post (no pitch) |
| 0 | Email | `28` §2 message 1 (60–90 words) |
| 1 | LinkedIn | Connection request, ≤200-character note, no pitch: "Your [product] launch caught my eye. I make product films in Boca." (Free accounts get few personalised notes a month; reports vary, [Salesforge](https://www.salesforge.ai/blog/linkedin-connection-request-limit)) |
| 3 | Email | Step 2: a new idea |
| 7 | Email | Step 3: **one real mock frame of their product** as a link, labelled "AI concept, not for use" |
| 10 | IG DM *or* LinkedIn (not both) | "Made a frame of [product] for you, link's in your inbox. Easier to talk here?" |
| 14 | Email | Step 4: close the loop with a date |
| 21 | — | Stop. Log status and learning. 90-day dormant |

**Seven touches across 3 channels in 3 weeks, then silence** `[inf]`: inside the 4–7 touch range from `28`, never more than 4 emails, and nobody gets more than one DM without a reply. Optional for top-10 prospects: a 20–30 s screen recording ("here's frame 1 of [product], here's why it works") sent at step 3 instead of the still. Use an image plus voice, never a face if the brand stays faceless `[inf]`.

**The visual hook rules.**
- Build the mock from their public product photo.
- Watermark it "concept · AI-made".
- Host it privately (an unlisted link) and never post it.
- It proves craft and shows you did the work; it's the strongest reason to reply `[inf]`.
- **Timing:** Tue–Thu, 8–10 am their time (`28` §1).
- **IG DMs:** may land in message requests. Instagram's spam rules cover "unwanted commercial outreach" and repetitive messages, and limits aren't published ([Metricool](https://metricool.com/is-instagram-dm-automation-safe-rules-limits-and-best-practices/)). Send DMs by hand, under 10 a day `[inf]`.

### 4.3 Funnel levers by stage `[inf]`
- **Send → reply:** line 1 and the idea (`28` §2).
- **Reply → frames:** answer within 2 hours, and offer two products.
- **Frames → call:** book the call at the moment of the yes.
- **Call → proposal:** §2.7.
- **Proposal → paid:** same day, one recommendation, a real date.

Measure each stage weekly. Fix the leakiest stage first.

## 5. Honest limits

1. **"Stop" ends it, everywhere.**
   - CAN-SPAM covers B2B email. Opt-outs must be honoured within 10 business days, with no fee or extra steps to opt out, and an opted-out address must never be sold or passed on ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business)). Penalties are up to $53,088 per email (`28` §1.2) `[law]`.
   - We honour opt-outs **the same day** and across all channels. "Please don't" on a DM counts.
2. **Harassment hurts the brand.** Founder circles are small. Each spam report on a 50-person list is a 2 % complaint rate (`28` §1.1), and 4+ emails triple unsubscribes (Belkins). A pushy thread gets screenshotted `[inf]`.
3. **No fake scarcity or fake proof.**
   - Deadlines must be real (Meta's dates, their launch, our genuinely booked calendar).
   - No "only 2 spots left" unless it's true that day. No invented clients or testimonials (the FTC's 2024 rule, `28` §9).
   - Never imply the spec films were client work.
4. **How top closers persist without crossing the line:**
   - each touch adds something new;
   - each says what happens next;
   - they change channel instead of repeating themselves;
   - they stop on a schedule they set in advance;
   - they come back only with a reason.

   Braun's detachment is the safeguard: if you need the deal, it shows `[inf]`.
5. **What "always win" really means** `[inf]`: you cannot make every founder buy, and trying is what turns persistence into harassment. You *can* make every contact end in one of four wins:
   - **Sale:** any rung of the ladder.
   - **Referral:** a name.
   - **Future date:** a specific one, written down.
   - **Learning:** why they said no, logged, so it changes the next email.

   A "no" that teaches you why line 1 missed is a win. A "no" you didn't ask about is the only real loss.
