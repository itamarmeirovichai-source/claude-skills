# 40 · Growth levers outside the ad: offer, distribution, outbound, proof, funnel, partners

Research brief, 2026-10-08. The founder asked for "more things that really change results: purchases and views." This doc ranks the levers that sit **outside the ad creative**. It builds on `28` (cold email and calls), `29`/`33` (site), `32` (positioning, tiers, risk reversal, lead magnet) and `34` (objections and cadence), and does not repeat them. Where a lever is already covered, this doc points to the section and adds only what is new. Docs `35`–`38` (punchline, emotion, execution, hooks) cover the creative itself.

**Evidence.** Most growth numbers come from vendors that sell outreach or video tools, so they are directional. Platform rules (Instagram, FTC) are cited from the source where it could be fetched. Anything not sourced is tagged `[inf]` (our inference). No stat here is invented. Where a figure could only be found second-hand, the doc says so.

**Context we plan around.** One founder, roughly 2 hours a day, almost no budget, no paying clients yet, about 80 researched hot leads, an Instagram account with Otto and Vee, a site in progress. Today is Thursday 2026-10-08. **Black Friday is Friday 2026-11-27 and Cyber Monday is 2026-11-30**, seven weeks away. That date is the single biggest outside force on DTC buying in the next 60 days `[inf]`.

## 1. Offer engineering (adds to `32` §2)

`32` §2 already sets Hormozi's value equation, three tiers, the conditional staged guarantee, founding-client terms and price anchoring. Four additions change results:

### 1.1 Name the outcome, not the deliverable
Hormozi's value equation puts the **dream outcome** on top (value = dream outcome × perceived likelihood ÷ time delay × effort; [notes](https://www.maxmednik.com/blog/notes-on-100m-offers-by-alex-hormozi)). A "30 s single-take film" is a deliverable. The outcome a DTC founder buys is "a scroll-stopping hero for your Q4 ads, without shipping product to a studio." Lead every offer line with the outcome and put the deliverable second `[inf]`.

### 1.2 The BFCM offer (highest-leverage single change)
A real date beats fake scarcity. Hormozi's scarcity and urgency only work when they are true (same notes), and DTC brands lock Q4 creative before the peak `[inf]`.

- **Name:** "BFCM Premiere" (the Premiere tier with a calendar promise).
- **Promise:** frames in 48 h, first cut day 5, final files **by Friday 2026-11-13**, two weeks before Black Friday, so the brand has time to load and test.
- **Honest capacity cap:** "I can take 4 BFCM films. Booking closes 2026-11-02 or when 4 are booked." Four is our estimate of what one founder can deliver in that window `[inf]`; change it to the real number and keep it true. Show "2 of 4 booked" only if it is true.
- **Bonus, not discount:** a 6 s "Cyber Monday" cut-down and an end card with their offer text (cheap for us, valuable on the day). Hormozi's advice is to add bonuses rather than cut price (same notes).
- **Price:** unchanged ($2,500). The Short at $1,200 stays available as the smaller yes.
- After 2026-11-02, the same structure becomes "January reset" (new-year campaigns) `[inf]`.

### 1.3 The Short as a credited pilot
The biggest fear for a first buyer is "what if it looks fake?" (`34` §1.2). `32` §2.3 already lets them see frames free and pay the balance only on approval. Add: **"If you move to Premiere or Season within 30 days, the full Short fee is credited."** This turns the Short into a pilot that costs nothing extra if it works, which raises perceived likelihood without lowering price `[inf]`. Many agencies use "paid pilot" language for the same reason; we found no rate data on it, so measure it ourselves.

### 1.4 Options we considered and rejected
- **"Pay only if you love it" (full payment on approval, no deposit).** Strongest risk reversal, but a solo founder cannot absorb several walk-aways and it invites free-riding `[inf]`. Use only for a hand-picked pilot client whose logo or case study is worth the risk, and say so openly (`32` §2.3, Enns quote).
- **Performance guarantee (ROAS/CTR).** Ruled out in `28` §9: we don't control their targeting, offer or site.
- **Countdown timers that reset.** Fake urgency. It misleads buyers and burns trust `[inf]`; never use.

### 1.5 Packaging checklist (what a buyer should see on one card)
Outcome line → what they get (counted) → turnaround date, not "fast" → the guarantee in one sentence → price → one button. Keep the per-asset maths from `32` §2.6.

## 2. Distribution for views

### 2.1 What the platform says it rewards (Instagram)
- Instagram's own ranking page lists, for Reels: your activity (likes, saves, reshares, comments), your history with the poster, information about the reel, and information about the poster. It says it makes **less visible** "low-resolution or watermarked reels, reels that are muted or contain borders" and reels "that have already been posted on Instagram" ([Instagram ranking explained](https://about.instagram.com/blog/announcements/instagram-ranking-explained)).
  - **So:** never cross-post a TikTok-watermarked file. Export a clean master and post natively on each platform. No borders, no letterbox bars, sound on.
- Mosseri's public comments in 2025 named **watch time, likes per reach and sends (DM shares) per reach** as the main Reels signals, with sends weighted most for reaching non-followers (reported by [Fanpage Karma](https://www.fanpagekarma.com/insights/?p=9293); Mosseri published no weights). A stats roundup says a December 2025 update gave sends more weight; that is second-hand ([autofaceless](https://autofaceless.ai/blog/instagram-reels-statistics-2026)), so treat it as unconfirmed.
  - **So:** design posts people *send to a co-founder or their marketer* ("send this to whoever runs your ads"). Spec remakes of a brand people know, and "how this frame was made" breakdowns, are the most sendable formats `[inf]`.
- **Originality.** In 2026 Instagram extended to all formats its policy of not recommending accounts that mostly repost others' content to non-followers ([PetaPixel](https://petapixel.com/2026/04/30/new-instagram-policies-target-reposted-content/)). Our content is original by default; never repost other AI creators' clips "for inspiration."
- **Trial Reels** let a professional account show a reel to non-followers first, then share it to followers if it does well ([Lindsey Gamble](https://lindseygamble.com/blog/instagram-launches-trial-reels-giving-creators-the-ability-to-share-reels-with-non-followers-before-sharing-with-everyone); Instagram newsroom, Dec 2024). **Use it to test two hooks for every spec film** without cluttering the grid. Check current eligibility in the app.
- **Collab posts** put one post on both profiles with pooled engagement ([Planable help](https://help.planable.io/hc/en-us/articles/21715384533148-Instagram-Collabs-posts)). Sources disagree on the limit (one to five collaborators), so check the app. Use them with every pilot client and with any AI-creator peer we team up with.

### 2.2 Formats that grow AI-creative studios (evidence and inference)
- **Spec work that travels.** The Liquid Death AI spec (Veo 3, no brief, no budget) spread after a consultant reposted it ([DesignRush](https://news.designrush.com/liquid-death-ai-spec-ad-goes-viral-passion-projects-ip-nightmares)). The same piece flags the risk: some brands see unofficial ads as muddying their identity. PJ Accetturo's Genre.ai built its profile on the Kalshi spot that aired during the NBA Finals; the Wrapbook host says it "cost just $2,000" (PJ doesn't confirm), and Genre's clients since include Google, Qatar Airways and Popeyes ([Wrapbook podcast](https://www.wrapbook.com/on-production-podcast/the-real-economics-of-ai-native-production-featuring-pj-accetturo)). Visible traction on something you made was the pitch.
- **Our version** `[inf]`: "We made a spec film for [real DTC brand on our lead list]." Rules: (1) ask the brand first, by DM, with the frames ("can I post this?"); if they say no or don't answer within 7 days, post it only with the brand removed (invented label) or not at all. (2) Label it "unofficial spec, not commissioned" on screen and in the caption. (3) Never use their logo in a way that implies endorsement. This turns every outreach asset into content, and every post into outreach.
- **"Famous ad, remade in one take."** Remaking an iconic commercial's *idea* with an invented product gets the recognisability without trademark use `[inf]`. Don't copy shots frame for frame or use the original brand, music or footage.
- **Build in public.** Process clips (prompt → frame → film, the 3 failed takes before the good one, the price breakdown) are cheap to make and show craft and honesty at once `[inf]`. Otto and Vee host them (`30`); the founder's voice explains.
- **Cadence.** We found no audited data on AI-character account cadence. Vendor guides range from 3–5 posts a week ([nandbox](https://nandbox.com/?p=82372)) to "consistency beats volume." Plan: **1 reel a day for 14 days** (most as Trial Reels), then keep the formats that earn sends `[inf]`.

### 2.3 Cross-posting
Post the same master natively to Instagram, TikTok, YouTube Shorts, X and LinkedIn, each without another platform's watermark (§2.1). On X and LinkedIn, add a 2–3 line "what I learned making this" caption; that is where DTC founders and marketers talk shop `[inf]`. Scheduling via each platform's own scheduler or Meta Business Suite is fine; avoid unofficial auto-posters that need your password (§8).

### 2.4 Communities and newsletters
- **DTC Fam** is a free Slack group run by Kristen LaFrance with a weekly roundup newsletter ([ReferralCandy](https://www.referralcandy.com/blog/ecommerce-communities), [DelightChat](https://delightchat.io/ecommerce-forums-facebook-groups/the-dtc-fam-by-klf)). **DTCers** (The DTC POD) is invite-only by application ([Trend](https://www.trend.io/podcast/dtc-community)). **eCommerceFuel** is a paid, revenue-gated community for store owners (a 2025 roundup gives $149/month; [Flowium](https://flowium.com/blog/dtc-ecommerce-communities/)); we are not its audience, so skip it. We could not find 2PM's sponsorship terms; check its media kit before assuming anything.
- **Reddit:** each subreddit sets its own rules. r/ecommerce bans self-promotion, including DM solicitation, and requires a 10-day-old account with 10 comment karma (rules mirror, March 2025, via [redlib](https://redlib.ssps.io/r/ecommerce/hot)). The common heuristic is ~90 % genuine participation, ≤10 % self-reference, with disclosure ([redship](https://redship.io/blog/reddit-self-promotion-rules)).
- **How to show up** `[inf]`: answer creative questions ("why is my CTR dropping?", "is AI product video worth it?") with a useful paragraph and, where the rules allow, one example frame. No links unless asked. Goal: 2 real conversations a week, not traffic.
- **Newsletter sponsorships:** skip until there is revenue; the cost per qualified lead is unknown and the budget is near zero.

## 3. Outbound that converts (adds to `28` and `34`)

`28` covers cold email copy, deliverability, CAN-SPAM and the 4-touch sequence; `34` §4 covers the 21-day cadence. New here:

### 3.1 The spec-frame walkthrough (the core asset)
Send each hot lead **one frame of their own product** plus a **45–60 s screen recording** (Loom or similar) of the founder's voice over the frames: "here's your bottle, here's the single move I'd build the film around, here's why it stops the scroll."
- **Evidence on video in outreach is vendor-only and inconsistent:** Sendspark claims 2–3x replies vs text-only ([Sendspark](https://sendspark.com/resources/video-prospecting-guide)); HippoVideo cites a SalesLoft figure of +26 % responses ([HippoVideo](https://www.hippovideo.io/blog/6-steps-to-make-a-prospecting-video-for-email/amp/)); Loom's own claims run higher ([SyncGTM](https://syncgtm.com/blog/personalized-video-emails-sales)). No independent controlled study found. **Run our own split:** half the leads get frame + video, half frame + text, and compare after 7 days.
- **Our structural reason it should work** `[inf]`: we sell visuals, so the "personalisation" *is* the product demo. Generic agencies can't do this at our cost.
- **Format:** a still thumbnail of their product (the frame) as the video's first frame, so the preview itself is the hook. Keep the email/DM text under 60 words (`28` §1).

### 3.2 Warm-up before the DM
Instagram's own ranking page says your history of interacting with someone shapes what they see ([Instagram](https://about.instagram.com/blog/announcements/instagram-ranking-explained)). A DM from an account they already noticed is also less likely to sit unseen in message requests `[inf]`.
- **3-day warm-up per lead:** Day A follow the brand and the founder; Day B leave one specific comment on a recent post (about the product or the shot, never "great content!"); Day C a second comment or a story reply; Day D send the DM with the frame. Manual only (§8).
- Space it so the 80 leads move through in waves of ~15–20 a day of comments `[inf]`.

### 3.3 Which channel for which founder
| Channel | When | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Instagram DM | The brand's main channel; founder posts there | Our primary; no reliable public benchmark found |
| Email (contact form or founder email) | Always as the backup touch, per `28` | `28` §1 (Instantly baseline 3.43 % reply) |
| LinkedIn | Founder posts on LinkedIn at least monthly | Expandi, 13.2M attempts: ~28.5 % connection acceptance, ~3.0 % reply to connection notes, ~10.4 % reply to messages; Apparel & Fashion accepts least (19.9 %) ([Expandi](https://expandi.io/blog/linkedin-outreach-benchmarks-2026/)) |
| X | Founder is active on X | No benchmark found `[inf]` |

**Rule** `[inf]`: one channel at a time, the one where the founder is most active, then the backup channel after 4–5 days with no reply. Never hit three channels on the same day; that reads as pressure. On LinkedIn, send the connection request **without** a pitch note (note replies are low per Expandi), then message after acceptance.

### 3.4 Follow-up
Use `34` §4.2 (21 days). The extra touch this doc adds: **a value follow-up**, not a "bumping this." Example: on day 7, send a second frame in a different setting or the 6 s loop of frame 1. New work is a reason to reply `[inf]`.

### 3.5 Legal and platform guardrails
- **CAN-SPAM applies to B2B** ("The law makes no exception for business-to-business email"). Accurate headers, honest subject, a postal address, a working opt-out honoured within 10 business days; penalties up to **$53,088 per email** ([FTC guide](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business)). `28` §1.2 has our footer.
- **Instagram:** its terms bar collecting information "in an automated way without our express permission" (archived terms copy, [Open Terms Archive memo](https://opentermsarchive.org/en/memos/instagram-blocks-data-retrieval-and-clarifies-what-information-it-retrieves/)). Meta's messaging API only allows automated messages after the user interacts first, within 24 h ([ManyChat](https://manychat.com/blog/instagram-dm-automation-rules/)). **So:** no scrapers, no logged-in bots, no automated cold DMs. Claude can research public pages and draft messages; the founder sends each one by hand.
- No published Instagram DM quota exists; vendor numbers are guesses (Metricool, BooSend). Keep it human: ~15–25 new DMs a day, all personalised `[inf]`.

## 4. Trust and proof without clients

### 4.1 The proof ladder (build in this order)
1. **Spec films for invented brands** (already in progress) → craft proof.
2. **Spec frames for real leads**, shown only with permission (§2.2) → relevance proof.
3. **Process transparency** (a 60 s "how one shot is made", the failed takes, the timeline) → reliability proof `[inf]`.
4. **Pilot case studies** from the first 1–3 clients (`32` §2.3 founding terms): problem → frames → film → what they ran → what happened, **only with numbers they share and approve**.
5. **Testimonials**: ask after delivery, never in exchange for value. The FTC's 2024 rule bans buying or selling fake reviews and testimonials, and fake social-media indicators such as followers and views ([BCLP analysis](https://www.bclplaw.com/en-US/events-insights-news/what-the-ftcs-final-rule-means-for-fake-indicators-of-social-media-influence-and-fake-review-websites.html)).

### 4.2 Case-study format (one screen)
"Brand / product / goal" → before (their current ad still, with permission) → our frame → the film (autoplay muted, captions) → turnaround in days → one quote → (later) one metric with its source and date. No logos of brands we haven't worked for.

### 4.3 Founder face or voice
A real person behind an AI studio lowers the "is this a bot?" worry `[inf]`. If the founder won't show their face, use their voice on every walkthrough (§3.1) and a short signed "why I started VXO" note on the site. Otto and Vee host content but **never** sign off prices or promises (`29` §2.3, `30`).

### 4.4 Guarantee display
Show the `32` §2.3 guarantee verbatim next to the price and again in the booking confirmation. Conditions stay visible.

## 5. Funnel and retention

### 5.1 Landing page message match
Unbounce defines message match as the landing page matching the ad or link the visitor came from ([Unbounce](https://unbounce.com/conversion-rate-optimization/gmail-ad-fail-how-poor-message-match-can-kill-your-conversion-rate/)). Its 2024 benchmark puts the median landing-page conversion at **6.6 %** across industries ([Unbounce benchmark](https://unbounce.com/conversion-benchmark-report/)). We found no reliable figure for the lift from message match alone.
- **Build one page per entry point** `[inf]`: `/bfcm` (the BFCM offer), `/frames` (the free 5 frames, already planned in `29`), and `/for/[brand]` private pages for the top 20 leads showing *their* frame, the walkthrough video and a booking button. The DM links to their page; the headline repeats the DM's words.
- Speed and form rules stay as in `29` §2.

### 5.2 Speed to reply and booking
The best-known lead-response figures (contact within 5 minutes vs 30, "7x within an hour") trace to the 2011 HBR/InsideSales work; secondary sources disagree on the multiples, and the data are observational ([Search Influence](https://www.searchinfluence.com/blog/internet-form-response-times/), [LeadSigma](https://leadsigma.com/resources/blog/five-minute-lead-response-stat)). The direction is consistent: faster is better.
- **Rules** `[inf]`: reply to any positive DM/email within 60 minutes during US business hours (phone notifications on); every reply carries a booking link with 15-minute slots (Cal.com or Calendly free tier) in the lead's time zone; the form's thank-you page offers the booking slot immediately.
- Claude drafts reply templates for the five likeliest replies (`34` §3) so the founder only edits.

### 5.3 Nurture and retention
- **Non-buyers after frames:** a monthly "one new frame of your product" touch for 3 months (a new season, a new setting). Low effort with our pipeline, high relevance `[inf]`. Stop at the first "no thanks."
- **Retainer upsell:** `32` §2.5 (at approval and at day 21). Add a **"fatigue check" message** at day 21 with their own ad numbers if they share them.
- **Email list:** collect only opted-in emails (the frames form with a clear checkbox). A short monthly "what we made" note is enough.

### 5.4 Referral
Agency referral fees typically run 5–10 % of revenue, often of the first year, with 10 % the most common single figure in one consultant's experience ([Sakas & Company](https://sakasandcompany.com/agency-sales-referral-fees/)). For us `[inf]`:
- **Partners** (freelancers, media buyers, Shopify devs): **10 % of the first project**, paid when the client pays.
- **Clients:** one free variant for a referral that books, stated openly. Not tied to a review.
- Put one line in every delivery email and on the invoice: "Know a founder who needs this? Intros are rewarded."

## 6. Partnerships

### 6.1 White-label supply to agencies (highest-impact partner play)
Media-buying and performance agencies need a steady flow of fresh creatives and often outsource production; vendors sell white-label UGC and creative supply to them ([Twirl for agencies](https://usetwirl.com/for-agencies), [Webtonic roundup](https://www.webtonic.io/blog/best-ugc-production-agencies)). Upwork reports AI video generation and editing as one of its fastest-growing skills (+329 % year on year, reported second-hand by [Uncle](https://tryuncle.com/learn/ai-at-work/how-ai-is-changing-freelance-platforms-like-upwork-and-fiverr)), and Video & Animation demand from SMBs rose 27 % in August 2025 ([Upwork](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2025/09/02/3142746/0/en/Upwork-Monthly-Hiring-Report-High-Value-Work-Grew-31-Among-Large-Businesses-as-AI-Amplifies-Demand-for-Human-Skills.html)).
- **Offer** `[inf]`: "Your brand, our films. 1 hero + 6 variants per client concept, unbranded, NDA signed, 7-day turnaround." Wholesale price ~20–25 % below retail so the agency keeps a margin (e.g., Premiere scope at ~$1,900–2,000).
- **Targets:** 1–10 person Meta/TikTok media-buying shops serving DTC (find them via LinkedIn "Meta ads agency DTC," agency founders posting on X). Pitch with one finished film made for one of *their* public clients' products, unbranded.
- **Why it ranks high:** one agency can bring several brands a month; it replaces 20 cold leads `[inf]`.
- **Risk:** agencies own the client relationship and we can't show the work. Keep a clause allowing private portfolio use or a fee premium for full anonymity.

### 6.2 Shopify ecosystem
The Shopify Partner Directory is a curated list with categories including "visual content and branding," and listing appears to be by invitation via the Partner program ([Shopify Help](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/partner-directory), [Shopify Community](https://community.shopify.com/t/joining-the-shopify-partner-directory/336503)). It is slow and needs proof. **Action:** create a free Partner account now, apply when 3 client projects exist. Meanwhile partner with Shopify theme/dev freelancers on the referral terms in §5.4 (they hear "our product pages need video" often `[inf]`).

### 6.3 Marketplaces
Upwork and Contra profiles take an hour and catch inbound demand for "AI product video" `[inf]`. Price at retail, link to the site, never undercut the site. Low priority.

## 8. What to avoid

- **Bought followers, views, likes or comments**, engagement pods with fake accounts: banned under the FTC's 2024 rule when used to misrepresent influence for a commercial purpose ([BCLP](https://www.bclplaw.com/en-US/events-insights-news/what-the-ftcs-final-rule-means-for-fake-indicators-of-social-media-influence-and-fake-review-websites.html)), and Instagram's ranking treats engagement quality as the signal anyway.
- **Logged-in scrapers, auto-DM bots, password-sharing growth tools** (Instagram terms; Meta API rules, §3.5).
- **Mass identical DMs or emails**, three channels on one day, more than the `34` cadence.
- **Spec work implying a brand endorsed it**; logos, music or footage from famous ads.
- **Watermarked or bordered cross-posts** (less visible per Instagram).
- **Fake scarcity** ("only 2 left" that resets), fake "most popular," invented client counts.
- **Posting a lead's frames publicly without permission.**

## 10. The 10 highest-impact changes to make now

1. **Launch the BFCM Premiere** with a real delivery date (final by 2026-11-13), a real capacity cap and booking close (2026-11-02), and a Cyber Monday cut-down as the bonus. Put it at the top of the site and in every DM this month.
